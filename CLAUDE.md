# Infinite Canvas

Native macOS infinite canvas (an AppKit document app, SwiftUI windows around an AppKit canvas), built from compiled-in plugins.

## Build and test

- `./build.sh && open build/InfiniteCanvas.app`. It calls `swiftc` directly and wraps the binary in an `.app`, because SwiftPM (`swift build`) is broken with this machine's Command Line Tools. Each module is compiled with only its dependency closure on `-I`, as many at once as there are cores (`BUILD_JOBS=N`), each once its dependencies are built, and only when what it's built from changed: its sources, its command, its dependencies' `.swiftmodule`s. A module's `.swiftmodule` is a job of its own (function bodies skipped, as in the job swiftc splits off itself), so what imports it starts compiling long before its code is done, and in release other modules can't inline its functions whatever cross-module optimization makes of them (nothing is `@inlinable`, and the benchmarks measured no difference). A module's code compiles to an object per source at fixed paths (`-num-threads 4`, through an output file map), so the same inputs make the same bytes, and what comes out the same isn't replaced: an edit inside a function recompiles only its module and links the executables again, and a touch or a checkout that changes nothing links nothing. In debug an executable's object job emits its module too, which its link hands the debugger (`-add_ast_path`), as a one-step `swiftc -g` does. A build fails, saying so, if a module's sources change while it builds (an interface and an object from different edits would link). Builds of one configuration wait for each other (`build/.debug.lock`), and however build.sh or test.sh ends (^C, a TERM), what it started stops with it. The app bundle's files change only when they do: macOS checks each new executable the first time it runs (about 0.3 s each, one at a time). The app carries its command line, `canvas` (`Contents/Helpers/canvas`; `build/canvas` links to it): with the app open, `build/canvas help`.
- `./build.sh bench` builds BenchmarkTests in release and runs it: saving, opening, validation's first read (every shape, checked against its kind's schema too) and its read after a drag event, after one of a subboard 100 notes mention (their mention rule looked it up, and ignores moves) and after moving every shape, a hover and a drag frame (titles' pills and locks in view), an empty board's frame on Retina at 100%, 70%, 35% and 10% (the background and the dot grid, the camera still, panning and pinching), a drag that snaps with every shape of the board in view (at 10%: its first event, which reads them, in a frame; each after within a drag event's input budget), a hover on a note's port dot and a drag from it, a hover onto a title's pill and onto a mention's, reading the table of contents' rows after a commit, a keystroke in the `@` picker (the `@` that opens it too, on a file of two boards typed inside the subboard, and on a board of rectangles where only 3 notes can be mentioned), a rename of a subboard mentioned 100 times (the rows and validation's read after it too), the panel of that subboard, its Mentioned In tab showing, read after a drag event of another note and after a mentioner's rename, what's connected to a shape with 100 edges (`topology.neighbors(of:)`), a completion near the end of a schema card's JSON as long as the model writes (34,000 bytes), a `canvas draw` of 20 notes each pushed along its row of the grid, the minimap's first picture (a frame at a time) and a drag frame with it, ten subboards' previews filling in (the longest frame, and how many), and undoing and redoing a drag (with its frame), a tenth of the board's move and deletion, and every shape's deletion, on generated boards of 1,000 and 10,000 shapes (three notes in ten mention a subboard and a shape that isn't there, so frames draw pills), against their budgets (the persistence plan's: open 10,000 shapes in under 1 s, Topology's index and Mentions' catalog within that in 50 ms, a frame in 16 ms, which undo and redo of a drag fit with its frame, and of a tenth of the board's move or deletion without one; an empty board's frame in 2 ms, the thread's CPU time (`Budget.emptyFrame`); validation's first read of 10,000 in 100 ms, and undoing or redoing their deletion; the rows, validation and the panel's layout after a commit, in 1 ms (`Budget.panelAfterCommit` the last); a picker keystroke, and a schema card's completion, in 2 ms; a rename of a shape mentioned 100 times, its commit and validation's read after it, in 1 ms more than one mentioned by none; the neighbors in 1 ms; and the draw in 150 ms, time the window doesn't draw). Across boards, on generated files (`generatedFile(root:children:depth:)`, the shipped subboards): opening 10,000 shapes spread over nested boards (under 1 s); entering a subboard of 10,000 cold with ⌘↓ (the switch, a frame, the rows and validation's read, together, under 250 ms, `Budget.enterCold`; who mentions whom, Topology's, and Mentions' catalog are the file's, read as it opens, so entering reads none of them), and going back up with ⌘↑ (with the root listed whole after it, too) and down again warm (the same, in a frame); a hover, a drag and the rows after it beside a subboard of 10,000 and inside one while the root holds as many (a frame; 1 ms), and the drag event's input alone (1 ms, and at most 3× what it costs on a file of that board alone, so a commit's upkeep of the whole file shows); the minimap's picture of the board entered (a frame at a time); a crumb's menu inside a subboard, the root's rows after a move on the board shown (1 ms: a move makes none again) and after a rename there (a frame: every note's row up there is made again); and deleting a subboard whose boards hold 10,000 (in a frame), and undoing or redoing that (100 ms). It only reports the first frame and the next zoomed out to fit the board (at 10,000 shapes, as far as the 10% zoom goes), the Mentioned In tab's lines for the subboard mentioned 100 times (`backlinks(of:)`, about 1 ms: read only while the tab shows and a mentioner changes), the inspector's list replaced on a switch between boards of 10,000 (a 50 ms target it misses by far: every row is replaced, about a second at 10,000, which the switch budgets leave out; "Not built yet"), a crumb's menu's first read of the root's rows (some 23 ms at 10,000) and the menu shown in the window (some 35 ms the first time, 10 after), and a chain of 1,000 boards written, read and walked. `./test.sh` runs it small, in debug, without the budgets.
- `./test.sh` builds every module but the app (debug, `-D DEBUG`) and runs the test executables, as many at once as there are cores (`TEST_JOBS=N`), slowest first, printing each one's output whole, in order. `TEST_FILTER=substring ./test.sh` runs the matching tests, and fails if none match. `TEST_UI_MENUS=1` adds the tests that open real (on-screen) menus, and runs the executables one at a time. Run by hand, a test executable takes `TEST_TIMES=1` (each test's seconds, on standard error). A time budget in a test measures the test thread's CPU time (TestKit's `threadCPUTime`), never the wall clock, which counts whatever else the machine runs. Every `Suite` a test target declares must be passed to `runTests` in its `main.swift`; test.sh fails otherwise.
- `Package.swift` is the module graph: one target per line, each tagged `// layer:`. Its header lists the layers and what each may use (wire, foundation, ui, horizontal, vertical, bridge, composition, app, cli, testsupport, test). Non-test modules live in `Sources/X`, or `Examples/X` with `path:`; test modules in `Tests/X`.
- Every build.sh mode audits first; `./build.sh audit` does only that. What it reads of each module is kept in `build/audit` and read again only for a module that changed, and a pass is kept by every input's name, size and mtime, so auditing what already passed costs one stat. It fails on a cycle or a forbidden layer edge; on any difference between that table and SwiftPM's own evaluation of `Package.swift`; on a module whose imports, as the compiler parses them, aren't exactly its declared dependencies; on an import inside `#if`, `canImport(<project module>)`, `@_exported`, `@_implementationOnly` or `@testable`; on a public type named like a module, or `Task` (it would shadow Swift's in every importer); and on a module writing another module's `ShapeKind`, `PluginID`, `ToolID`, `PropertyKey`, `ChannelID` (Ports') or `EdgeTypeID` (Topology's) string without depending on it (a string two modules declare is both's, so an id that must equal another module's is made from that module's constant, as `ArrowEdge.id` from `Arrow.kind`; a name in a property list, `Property("…")`, is exempt, and so is a JSON member named by a JSON Schema keyword, a subscript's key, `json["title"]`, or a key before a colon where a member's name starts, but not in a `case`'s patterns or `where` or a ternary, even across lines: the `title` keyword is also Titles' property key; any other member's name, `json["locked"]`, counts). The graph (the `-I` closure plus MemberImportVisibility) keeps the core from referencing plugin types; the id check keeps any module from writing another module's kind, tool, plugin, property, channel or edge type id as a literal. The editor's host API (input, cursor, drawing, delegate) is `@_spi(Host)`, and only the ui, testsupport and test layers may import it.
- `./build.sh graph`, `dot`, and `api <Module>` (a module's public interface) are for reading the graph.
- If the build fails with "this SDK is not supported by the compiler", a stale newer SDK is being picked. Prefix the command with `SDKROOT=/Library/Developer/CommandLineTools/SDKs/MacOSX26.5.sdk`.
- Sources compile in Swift 6 language mode. Keep them free of warnings (build.sh passes `-warnings-as-errors`).

## Architecture

Read `docs/plugin-system.md` first; it's the guide to the plugin system, with the examples.

- `Canvas` (the core) knows no particular shape or concern (its one tool is Hand), and must stay that way. A concern like validity is declared by a plugin (an `ExtensionPoint` in its own module) and other plugins opt in; if the core seems to need to know about one, add a generic extension point instead.
- A plugin is a value (`CanvasPlugin`) that registers into a frozen `Registry`, which refuses a plugin list with a duplicate plugin, a contribution whose owner isn't installed or whose kind nobody registers, or two claims to one key. A rule spanning a point's contributions that keys can't express goes in that point's `static func check(_ registry:)`, which throws `RegistryError.invalid` or any error (reported as `invalid` for the point; the core runs it without knowing what it checks). A kind is its props type (`static let kind`, defined by its list of properties); its behavior is a stateless `ShapeDefinition` (only `render` is required for box kinds, and `geometry` drives hit testing and outlines); more comes from capability protocols (`Editable`, `CustomTranslation`, which moves a kind by its own rules through `beginTranslation` when a `TranslatingSession` moves it, `PaintOrderAnchored`, `DerivedProps`, whose `derive` puts what follows from the rest of the props in step, as the kind's setters would, in a shape made from properties, `registry.shape(_:properties:)`: a schema card's height, a note's from its text, and `BoardOwning`: each shape of the kind owns a board, a subboard, with nothing to implement; boards in a file, below). Escape on a `TranslatingSession` puts back only what the drag moved (a box's origin; a `CustomTranslation` kind's whole record from before its begin), so a plugin's write to a shape while it moves (a model's answer landing) stays. A shape just placed (`editor.place`) follows the pointer while the press that placed it lasts, whatever its placement (`PlacingSession`, a `TranslatingSession` inside), an editing placement's shape is edited on the release, and Escape before that takes the shape away and puts back what the undo participants kept before the press (what was selected), so the press comes to nothing; placed with no press, an editing placement's is edited at once. Selection declares two more: `HandleProviding` (handles on the only selected shape), and `BrushHitTesting`, which the marquee asks only when it overlaps a shape without strictly containing it (default: `ShapeGeometry.isHit(byBrush:)`, from `geometry`). Behavior intrinsic to a kind is a capability; behavior any plugin can add is an extension point. Conform to `ShapeDefinition` (and capabilities), `Tool`, `Session`, `EditorService`, `MentionScope` and any extension point that implements `check` or `summary` (how the registry's report shows a contribution) in an extension: only there does the compiler flag a near miss on a defaulted requirement.
- A kind's props, and any struct stored in them, are a list of properties (`SchemaDefined`): `static let properties = Properties<StickyNote>(title: "Sticky note") { Property("frame", \.frame).required(); Property("text", \.text).description("…") }`, with `init()` giving the defaults. The list is the type's `Codable` (a member missing from a board reads as its default; a `required()` one has none) and its JSON Schema (2020-12): each property's type from its member's (`SchemaDescribed`: the core's geometry and `ShapeID` as `core.*` definitions, `SchemaCases` for a string enum, a nested struct in place or, with `definition:`, shared and named after its module, `canvastesting.memo-note`), its default, and what its modifiers add (`PropertyModifiers`: `minimum`, `maxLength`, `pattern`, `examples`, `keywords` for any other). A type that codes itself by hand writes `schema(in:)` by hand (`ArrowTerminal`). The registry describes every kind's and added property's schema as it's built, and refuses a list of any struct those reach that misses a stored member (a board wouldn't save it), names a non-member or has a key path that isn't its member (a computed view), lists one twice, writes one member twice, has an optional that's required or defaults to non-nil, or holds optionals in a collection (nor may an added property's value); the first problem is the one reported, a property value's against its declarer; and two types under one definition name, or a struct that holds itself with no name. `registry.schema(for: kind)` is the kind's whole schema, its added properties included, and marked `x-canvas-board` for a `BoardOwning` kind. `registry.schemaViolations(of: shape)` checks a shape against it (its own and added properties as one object, `record.propertiesJSON`, through a fast `JSONValueEncoder`; each kind's schema compiled as the registry is built, `SchemaValidator`, which checks any JSON against any schema, strings code point for code point and patterns as ECMA-262 reads them; and a schema it couldn't check, a `$ref` to nowhere or back to itself before going into the value, a pattern ICU can't read, refuses the plugin list, a kind's or an added property's): typed props always have the schema's shape, so what breaks it are bounds (`maxLength`, `minimum`) and numbers that aren't finite. A bound is a guideline, never a refusal: Validation warns about each violation. Agents get the schemas from the command line (`canvas kinds --schema`).
- Added properties are what a plugin adds to the kinds that opt in: something the shapes know that their kind doesn't declare (a title). The type is the key: `enum TitleProperty {}` with `extension TitleProperty: ShapeProperty { static let key: PropertyKey = "title"; static var defaultValue: String? { nil } }` (a nil default: only the shapes it's set on have one), and `static var keywords: PropertyKeywords<Value>` for what its schema says beyond its type (Titles' title: `.minLength(1).maxLength(80)`), and `static func isNone(_:)` for a value that's no value at all (Titles' blank title), which no shape holds: a shape set to one, or read from a board with one, holds nil, or the default for a property that has one (which can't be none). A value a board writes as null, added, kept or handed over, is none too. Its owner declares it (`registrar.declare(P.self)`); a kind opts in through the owner's sugar (`ShapeRegistrar.optIn(to:)`, as `note.title()` does), a bridge's `registrar.optIn(Kind.self, to:)`, or `optInEveryShape(to:where:)`. The registry refuses an opt-in to a property nobody declares, a key declared twice, a kind opted in twice, and a key that's one of the kind's own property names; its report lists `property title` and `property title → sticky-note`. Read and write `shape[P.self]` (`record[P.self]`), in a transaction like the props; a write the shape can't hold (its kind doesn't opt in, nil for a property with a default, a value kept aside) is dropped. A placement sets one on what it places with `.setting(P.self, to:)`. Defaults are filled where shapes enter an editor (inserted or placed, `CanvasEditor.init` and `replaceFile` on every board, `replaceDocument`, a board read; `registry.fillingDefaults`); filling never marks a board edited by itself. In a kind's schema each one is a property marked `x-canvas-owner`. What a shape knows is a property; what a plugin keeps about a shape (the one it was copied from) is `ShapeMeta`, never a property.
- Layers: horizontals (Keyboard, Selection, Validation, Titles, DetailPanel, ContextMenus, TableOfContents, Adornments, Clipboard, Locking, Ports, Mentions, Topology, Automation, LanguageModels, Boards, Snapping) declare extension points and import no vertical, though one may build on another (Boards on ContextMenus, DetailPanel, Keyboard, Selection, TableOfContents and Titles; Clipboard on Keyboard, Selection and ContextMenus; Locking on Adornments, ContextMenus, Selection and TableOfContents; Ports on Selection and Topology; Mentions on ContextMenus, DetailPanel, Selection, TableOfContents, Titles and Topology; Titles on Adornments, DetailPanel and TableOfContents; Adornments, DetailPanel, ContextMenus and TableOfContents on Selection; Selection on Keyboard); verticals (Arrows, StickyNotes, Schemas, Subboards, Rectangles, Polylines, Eraser, Reordering) import no other vertical (StickyNotes and Schemas import Ports, each for a channel of its own, and StickyNotes and Subboards import Mentions, notes to hold mentions and both to be mentioned; Subboards builds on Boards); bridges (RectangleTitles, ValidationDetails, ValidationContents, MentionValidation, ArrowPorts, TitleNames, PortEdges, ValidationChecks) connect them, even two horizontals (ValidationDetails joins Validation and DetailPanel, ValidationContents Validation and TableOfContents, MentionValidation Mentions and Validation; TitleNames, PortEdges and ValidationChecks join Automation to Titles, Ports and Validation, for `canvas draw`), and ArrowPorts joins Arrows, Ports and Topology, the only code that knows both arrows and channels. The foundation is Geometry, Canvas and DrawScript (a pure library beneath the plugins: the language `canvas draw` reads, and its solver; it knows no canvas, registry or plugin). The shipped list is `ShippedPlugins.plugins(pasteboard:model:)` in Composition (and `registry(pasteboard:model:)`, `makeEditor(document:scheduler:pasteboard:model:)`); the app passes `ShippedPlugins.anthropicModel`.
- The detail panel (DetailPanel, a horizontal) lays out a header, tabs, sections and a footer; plugins fill them with `x.details { DetailSection("…", in: SomeTab.self) { $x in … } }` and `detailStatusForEveryShape`: status lines, the shape's state rather than its settings, in the footer, or with `in: SomeTab.self` in a tab that shows only while the shape has lines there (ValidationDetails' Issues tab), and whose title in the strip follows its worst line's symbol while that warns or errs (`layout.alert(in:)`). A tab is a type (`DetailTab`) any plugin may declare, in order bands: 0 a kind's own, 50 a horizontal's feature tab, 100 `StyleTab`, 300 a tab of the shape's state that comes and goes (Issues, Mentions' Mentioned In). A section of such state says when it shows with `.shownWhile { shape, context in … }` (Mentioned In's: while the shape `isMentioned`), asked on every commit beside the status lines, whatever the commit changed, so it reads a service's cached state, O(1), and only the sections that have one are asked (found once per kind); a tab shows only while it has a section shown or status lines, and the shape's panel draws again only when its record, its status lines or the sections `shownWhile` hides change. A horizontal's feature is opt-in sugar on `ShapeRegistrar` that contributes to its own point and calls `details`, so opting in brings its tab (`x.opensBoard(…)`, Boards' Board tab). An added property's sections are over its value: `x.details(of: P.self) { DetailSection(…) { $value in … } }`, or on `PluginRegistrar` for every shape that has it; a kind's own sections and header read added values through `$x.shape`, the whole record (`$note.shape[TitleProperty.self]`), and bind one, nil while the shape has none, with `$x[P.self]` (a `Binding<P.Value?>`, as `TitleHeader($x)` does; the bindings one `$x` makes of its added properties read back the last write through any of them; on a kind that doesn't opt in, it reads nil and drops writes, and `$x.holds(P.self)` says which, so `TitleHeader($x)` on a kind that doesn't opt into titles stops a debug build). `DetailCommandButton(title, systemImage:, command:)` runs a registered command (`editor.perform`), dimmed while `editor.canPerform` says no: the panel's counterpart of a menu item (it reads the editor from the panel's environment, `\.detailEditor`). A number being typed is committed in `boardWillChange` (`DraftTracker.commit`), so it lands on its shape before another board is shown. Sections write only through `$x.field` or `$x.update` (one transaction each, on the live shape, which the binding reads back until the panel redraws; a field's writes, one after another, are one undo step, and `$x.update(\.field) { … }` runs with them for a field written through a method, as the schema card's `setSchema`), bind the narrowest key path, and use `LabeledContent` and DetailPanel's primitives, never their own layout or fonts: among them `DetailTextArea` (with `placeholder:`, and `onCommit:`, which makes Return commit, ⇧↩ a new line, and the keyboard leaving commit too, a turn later if the view left the window with it, so a commit captures its service weakly), `DetailCodeEditor` (code typed as keyed: monospaced, every substitution, automatic completion, prediction and Writing Tools off; lines never wrap; as tall as its lines, from `DetailStyle.codeEditorHeight`, a text area's 76, to what the panel shows; ⌘↩ and the keyboard leaving commit; `coloring:` names its runs, `DetailCodeSpan`, and the panel colors them, red only for `.invalid`; `completions:`, a `DetailCodeCompleting`, says what can be typed at the caret, UTF-16 in, a `DetailCodeCompletions` out: the `range` a pick replaces, which holds the caret, the `query` typed so far, and every candidate as offered, unfiltered, each a `DetailCodeCompletion`, its `label`, `detail`, `insert`, the `selection` within it a pick leaves, `reopens` and `isRelevant`; ⌃Space, and AppKit's `complete:` (⌥⎋, F5), opens it in the one caret list, below, while the text can be written and something's offered, else nothing, no beep: under what's typed, each label in the code's font, matched in its semibold, its detail after it, VoiceOver saying both, ranked with `CaretListMatcher` (nothing typed: as offered; else by tier, then `isRelevant`, then as offered; what doesn't match left out); while it's open ↑ ↓ ⌃P ⌃N and Page Up and Down move, Return, Enter and Tab pick, Escape, ⌘., ⌃Space, ⌥⎋ and F5 close it, ⌘↩ closes it and commits, and the rest is the text's; it asks again after every keystroke and move of the caret, and closes once nothing's listed, the caret leaves the place it opened for (the range starts elsewhere), the text is shown from outside or can't be written, the keyboard or the window goes, the window resigns key, or what's typed scrolls out of view; a pick is `insertText` over the range between two `breakUndoCoalescing()`, one step of the text's undo that the box writes as typing, and one that `reopens` opens it again; `onCompleting:` hears it open and close (a pick that opens it again, not at all), for what waits on the person's choosing (the schema card's sync); without `completions`, F5 does nothing and ⌥⎋ leaves the panel: code gets no dictionary words), `.detailUpdating(_:)` (read-only and dimmed while something else writes the box; the words go on a status line where the person is, never over the text), and `DetailStatusLine` (a tone's symbol or, `isBusy`, a spinner, one width so the text doesn't move, then the text, which takes no tone and can be selected and copied (a line whose selected text has the keyboard as it goes hands it back to the canvas), with `DetailStatusAction` buttons under it). Text that isn't a string edits in a `DetailTextBox(value, height:placeholder:onCommit:make:)`, a box as a text area's (the panel's own, `DetailCodeEditor` and a `DetailTextArea` with `onCommit:`, are boxes over `PlainTextView`, on TextKit 1 as they always were; a `DetailTextArea` without `onCommit:` is still SwiftUI's `TextEditor`), over the view `make` returns: a subclass of `DetailTextView` (open: `onCommit`, `commitsOnReturn`, `onExit` and `placeholder` do nothing until set, so outside the panel it's a plain text view, and `commitsOnLeaving` off leaves only ⌘↩ to `onCommit`, as a note's editor on the canvas, whose ⌘↩ ends editing; it draws its placeholder in `drawBackground`, since AppKit gives a class that overrides `draw(_:)` TextKit 1; `showEnabled(_:)` dims it, by default by its text's color, the label's or the secondary label's) that adopts `DetailTextContent` (`value`, which the box writes to the binding on every change, and `show(_:)`, which the box calls only when the binding changed from outside, an undo or another plugin's write: the storage alone, never `didChangeText`, since the board may be applying a step, and then the box drops typing's undo), set in `DetailStyle.textFont` (the panel's text, 11 pt), as Mentions' `MentionTextArea` is. A subclass that shows a list at its caret keeps its window, `caretListWindow` (a `MentionTextView`'s `@` picker, a code editor's completions), and `DetailTextView` does the rest for every such list alike: while one's open it `holdsKeys`, so the list's commands (`CaretList.perform`) are the list's, before the delegate's turn (through the window's `pickHighlighted()` and `closeList()`, which run the owner's), and Return and Escape are never its commit's nor the panel's exit, while ⌘↩, where the text commits, closes the list and commits, as with none; the keyboard leaving, the view leaving its window and its being made read-only close it; what's left to the subclass is when it opens, what it lists and what a pick puts in (a subclass may `holdsKeys` for something else too). The panel's root hears Escape before the view does, and lets it through only to a view holding keys, and ⌥⎋ to a view that `offersCompletions` (a code editor with completions it can write), list or not; ⌘., which the window sends up the responder chain as `cancelOperation:`, closes an open list (the view's `cancelOperation(_:)`), else goes on up and leaves the panel from its root (`onExitCommand`). Tests read a list through `caretListWindow`. Such a list is the app's one caret list, DetailPanel's, whatever text it's over (Mentions' `@` picker; a code editor's completions): `CaretList`, the model, headless (`CaretListRow`s: headers, items and one empty state; a `CaretListItem` is a title with its `matched` characters semibold, in an optional `font`, an optional symbol and `tint` before it, a `trailingSymbol` after it, a `detail` at the trailing end, which takes what the title leaves, or `DetailStyle.caretListDetailShare` of the row where both are long, and what VoiceOver says, `spoken`, the title and detail by default; the first item highlighted, ↑ ↓ wrapping and a page, `rowsShown`, stopping at the ends; `perform(_:)` takes the text view's commands: ↑ ↓ ⌃P ⌃N, Page Up and Down, Return and Tab pick, but not with nothing highlighted, Escape, ⌥⎋ and F5 close), sized by its owner's `CaretListMetrics` (width, row height, rows shown, offset below the anchor; its look is `DetailStyle`'s), and shown in a `CaretListWindow(list, accessibilityLabel:over:anchor:pick:close:post:)`, a borderless child window that never takes the keyboard (not deferred: a click's events name it, shown or not, and a board's window leaves clicks in its children alone, so a pick with the mouse is part of the typing), placed under the anchor range's `firstRect` (its rows' text under the anchor's start, above the line where there's no room below, on screen), following every ancestor's frame and bounds, asking its owner to `close` once the anchor leaves the text's visible rect or the window resigns key, and telling VoiceOver through `post` (Mentions routes it through `MentionAccessibility`); its owner calls `listDidChange()` after each change and `dismiss()` as it closes, and decides when it opens and closes, what the query is and what a pick inserts. Both lists rank typed text with one matcher, `CaretListMatcher` (the tiers are `CaretListRanking`; `rank` is the two passes every list makes, over labels given by index, nil for one it leaves out, the typo tier only when fewer than 8 matched above, which Mentions' catalog makes over its keys and `matches(_:in:)` over a list of labels). Disabled (a read-only shape's section, `detailUpdating`), a box is read-only and dimmed by its view (`showEnabled(false)`), never by SwiftUI, which would take every click over it: its text is still selected and copied, and its clicks land (a pill followed), but it never commits (`onCommit`): nothing was written. A section reaches a service of its plugin's with `$x.service(S.self)` (the schema card's `SchemaSync`), never the editor, and `$x.shapeID` is the shape a binding writes, whatever its value. A read-only shape's panel (a locked one) shows everything with its sections' controls and its header disabled (`registry.isReadOnly(record)`, so the panel never reads the document), but a section's that changes nothing of the shape (`DetailSection(…).enabledWhileReadOnly()`: Boards' Open, Mentions' Mentioned In); Back, the tabs and collapsing still work. The header is a view made from the shape's binding too, `x.details(header: { $x in DetailHeader("…") }) { … }`: a `DetailHeader`, or a view of the kind's own built on `DetailHeaderLabel` (with `DetailHeaderField` for a title renamed in place; `isEditable: false` shows it as text at full strength, for a title that can't be written just now), so every header looks alike; the panel hands it the kind's name and symbol through the environment, and tests read it back with `layout.header` or `layout.header(as:)`. - Validation (a horizontal) is ESLint's model: rules (`x.validate { shape, context in … }`, `registrar.validate(P.self)`, `validateEveryShape`) find issues on a shape, each `.error` or `.warning`, two channels of one thing; an error makes a shape invalid. Validation's schema rule ignores moves (and keeps each shape's verdict until the shape changes other than by moving, for the re-checks a lookup brings), and so does MentionValidation's, so a drag of a subboard 100 notes mention re-checks none of them. It checks the board shown (a board never shown isn't checked: its issues show once you enter it), and keeps what it found (issues, lookups, what's left to check) per board in a `BoardStash`, so going back to a board checks nothing. `registry.validationIssues(of:in:)` runs a shape's rules against any `ShapeContext` (a transaction's: `canvas draw`'s warnings, through ValidationChecks), with schema verdicts of its own. - The clipboard (Clipboard, a horizontal): ⌘X, ⌘C and ⌘V (Keyboard), the `.cut`, `.copy` and `.paste` commands, and Cut, Copy and Paste in every context menu (items for the canvas), on the selection; the menu's Paste lands centered on its click (`editor.paste(centeredAt:)`, the menu's `point`), where ⌘V keeps its rule, below. The pasteboard holds a small file (`BoardCodec` JSON, the app's own type; a `CanvasFile`, format 4 only when a copied shape's board has shapes, else as a board); anything else on it is ignored for now. Copying a shape that owns a board copies what it holds, all the way down, under the original ids: on each board the shapes that can be copied, let go of what's left out on their board (a shape left out takes the boards under it), and only boards of kinds that own boards. Shapes bound to what a cut deletes (`ShapeReferencing`: an arrow's end) let go of it at the cut, as a delete does, and a move doesn't bind them again; only names survive it. Cut deletes only what it copied whole: a shape whose boards hold one that couldn't be copied stays, and pastes as a copy, since its id is in use. Every kind is copyable unless it opts out (`x.uncopyable()`, `x.copyable { … }`; the `ShapeCopyability` point). Every kind whose props are bound to shapes adopts the core's `ShapeReferencing` (arrows, CanvasTesting's `Tag`): deleting a shape asks it of every shape bound to it, after `willDelete` handlers (arrows let go where they're drawn), and a copy is bound to the copies of the shapes copied with it and lets go of the rest; it returns nil when it can't let go of a shape it can't find, and a copy then leaves it out. Shape metadata (`AnyShapeRecord.meta`, typed by a plugin's `ShapeMetaKey`, saved under the owner's id, then the name) is the core's generic place for what a plugin keeps about a shape beside its props; what the shape itself knows is an added property. A per-kind rule table is `registry.contributionByKind(to:)`, and the union of shapes' bounds `shapeContext.bounds(of:)`.
- References between shapes have two forms behind one entry, `record.remappingReferences(_:in:)` (`docs/plugin-system.md`, "Values that name shapes"). Names (`ShapeNaming`, a value: a mention) may be of a shape on any board of the file (ids are unique across it: `editor.file.record(id)`), and may outlive their shapes: a copy renames those of shapes copied along and keeps the rest (nil keeps a name; across a copied tree of boards, `remappingReferences(_:naming:in:)` passes names through the whole tree's map and bindings through their board's, as Clipboard's paste does), a cut and its paste move the shapes under their ids, so a name of one from outside what moved names it again (a binding to one was let go at the cut), a delete never looks for them (it asks only `referencingKinds`, so deleting a mentioned shape writes nothing), and `draw` keeps a name of a shape that's in no board of the file (or one the script deletes), or on a board the app can't show, and warns ("<shape>: cite names shapes that aren't in the file: …; they show as missing", "… names shapes on boards this app can't show: …"), and refuses a binding to one on another board as to one in none (`invalid_value`: "… is bound to a shape on another board of the file"). - Locking (a horizontal, on Adornments, ContextMenus, Selection and TableOfContents): any shape can be locked, from the context menu only (Lock, or Unlock once every shape the menu is for is locked, as tldraw's toggle; items for the canvas in `LockMenuGroup`, order 300; no key), and unlocked there or with a click on its chip (`LockChip`, a clickable adornment). Once a shape is unlocked, however, its chip shows the lock open for 1.5 s, more while the pointer's on it, then fades over 0.3 s; a click on the open lock locks the shape again, so a click by mistake can be taken back. Locked is something the shape knows: `LockedProperty` (`locked: true`, nil when unlocked), an added property every kind opts into (`optInEveryShape`), read as `record.isLocked`, set with `editor.lock(ids)` and `editor.unlock(ids)`; a locked shape's copy is locked. Locking tells the core locked shapes are read-only (`registrar.readOnlyShapes`), so nothing changes, moves, restacks, edits or deletes one (a drag moves the rest of the selection, Delete and Cut take the rest, the panel is disabled, there are no handles), while it's still selected, copied, listed and bound to. Each try shakes it (`LockState` hears `didRefuseChanges` and plays `editor.setDrawingOffset`: 5 points side to side, 3 times over 360 ms, dying away; a try while it plays doesn't start it over; a click doesn't count); every refused shape on the board shown shakes, so a shape whose board holds a locked shape, which isn't deleted either, shakes too. A locked shape that owns a board is entered like any other. On another board, `LockState` takes back its locked shapes as it kept them (a `BoardStash`, while the board's revision is unchanged; else it finds them again, one pass; `boardsScannedForLocks`, debug builds, counts the passes) and drops the shakes and open locks of the board left. - Ports (a horizontal, on Selection and Topology; data-canvas's) are dots just outside a shape's sides, from which a drag draws a connection (an arrow) that may end only on a shape that takes its channel. A channel is a kind of connection, a type (`Channel`: `static let id: ChannelID`, and `static var color` for its dots, computed, gray by default), declared by the plugin that owns what it means (`registrar.channel(NoteLink.self)`, the `Channels` point; one declaration per id), which declares Topology's edge type of the same name too, under the connector's family (a root without a connector). `ChannelID` is what a connection keeps, so it never changes once shipped, and the id audit holds its literal to the module that declares it. A kind has ports on a channel with `x.ports(C.self, sides:role:)` (`registrar.ports(K.self, on: C.self, sides:role:)` from a bridge; the `ShapePorts` point, one per kind and channel, and a kind may have ports on several): a dot at the middle of each of `sides` (`PortSide`, all four by default), and a `PortRole`, `.both` (the default), `.source` (dots; nothing ends on it) or `.target` (connections end on it; no dots). `ShapePorts.check` refuses ports on a channel no installed plugin declares, or on the connector's own kind. One plugin draws connections, the connector (`registrar.connects(K.self, edgeType: F.self, canConnect:make:ends:begin:)`, the `Connectors` point, at most one): `F` is the family of edges connections are (an `EdgeType` it declares; with `ends`, a connection bound at both ends is one edge, of its channel's type, else `F`'s, and `F`'s too for a channel that names a declared type that isn't a channel's; a free end, none), `K` opts into `ChannelProperty` (key `channel`, nil by default, so only a connection drawn on a channel has one; one `canvas draw` makes with `channel: none` has none), `canConnect` says which shapes one may start from and end on in a `ShapeContext` (the editor's for the dots, a transaction's for a connection made by name: `registry.canConnect(_:in:)`), `begin` gets the `Connection` (`shape`, `side`, the dot's `channels` in the port table's order, `origin`, where it was pressed, world units) and returns the session the press hands the drag to (nil: nothing), and the optional `make` and `ends` connect shapes without a drag: `registry.connection(_:existing:in:)` makes a `ConnectionRequest` (ends, optional sides, channel, both heads) into a connection, not inserted, or changes `existing` (only named sides and the heads; `existing` itself when nothing would change, so `==` tells unchanged), and `registry.connectionEnds(of:)` is which shapes one joins. Without a connector no shape shows ports. Every question about ports reads one port table per kind (`KindPorts`, PortTable.swift: plugin order, a kind's first contribution on a channel its only one there), the registry's lookups too (`connectorKind`, `makesConnections`, `dotChannels(of:side:)`, `takes(_:_:as:side:)`, …), so a drag and a connection made by name pick channels alike. `editor.channel(of:endingAt:)` is the first of a connection's channels a shape's kind takes as a target (nil for the shape it starts from, or one kept aside); `registry.accepts(shape, on:as:)` and `registry.declares(_:)` are what rules for connections already drawn go by. The dots are Selection's canvas buttons, dragged (`PortDot`, from `PortState.dot(atViewPoint:)`), shown for one shape, the host: the one whose dot the pointer is on (any shape's, so a dot reached straight from empty canvas shows its shape's, as data-canvas's halo does), else the shape with dots under the pointer, kept while the pointer stays within reach of its dots (its view bounds grown by 24 points) over empty canvas or a shape without ports; none while a handle is under the pointer (it takes the hover), and none once the tool changes or the shape is deleted. A shape has dots when its kind has a side that isn't `.target`, it isn't kept aside, and `canConnect` allows it. They show in the select tool only, at 25% zoom and up (`PortStyle.minZoom`), while the pointer's over the canvas (`editor.pointerOverCanvas`) and, where it is now (`editor.pointerWorldLocation`), within reach of them, and nothing is under way but a dot's press: each 12 view points out from the middle of its side of the shape's bounds, a disc 8 points across in its first channel's color at 60% in a white 1.5-point ring, and hovered or pressed 10 points across at full color in a halo 24 points across at 24% (`PortStyle`'s radii: 4, 5, 12); constant on screen, following the host's drawing offset (a locked shape's shake). A dot shows, and is hit, only while no selectable shape painted above its own covers its center (an arrow leaving the shape there, a neighbor on top; `PortState.liveDots(of:)`), so a shape lying on a bigger one shows its dots over it. The nearest showing dot within 12 view points is pressed (none where the select tool hasn't seen the pointer move, just after a tool change or a first click into the window, since none shows there); past the drag threshold the connector draws, and while it does each shape in view the connection could end on is outlined faintly, as a hover (the one it would end on now gets the hint). `PortsPlugin` comes after `AdornmentsPlugin` in the shipped list, so a dot is found before a lock chip (a clickable adornment) and draws above it. Locked shapes keep their ports: connecting doesn't change them. ArrowPorts makes arrows the connector (its family `ArrowEdge`, `arrow`, its id made from `Arrow.kind`; `canConnect` is `shapes.canBindArrow(to:)`, `begin` is `editor.beginArrow(from:at:canBind:complete:)`, `canBind` a shape that takes one of the port's channels (`editor.channel(of:endingAt:)`), and `complete` sets the arrow's `ChannelProperty`; `make` binds both ends, an unnamed side at the center (imprecise) and a named one at its middle (precise, not exact), its channel set before `registry.arrowMayBind` asks the rules), and its `arrowBindingRule` keeps an arrow with a channel some installed plugin declares to shapes that take it at that end (the start as `.source`, the end as `.target`), re-aimed or moved; one without (`channel: none`'s), or with an undeclared channel, binds wherever an arrow can. - Topology (a horizontal on the core alone; `docs/plugin-system.md`, "Topology") is what connects and contains what, whatever is where: edges, a typed graph (an arrow bound at both ends, a mention), and containment, a tree (which board a shape is on); spatial relations (a note within a rectangle's bounds) are geometry, not Topology's. The carrier shape is the system of record (an arrow's bound ends and `channel` are its edge, a note's text its mentions): `TopologyIndex` (`editor.topology`, a service) derives the whole file's edges from the records, by carrier, by each end and by type, in memory, never saved (nothing in props, meta or the file: tldraw's binding records would be a second truth to keep in step, and a mention outlives what it names), and never transacts. An `Edge` is its `type` (the most specific), `source`, `target`, `carrier`, `slot` ("" for the carrier itself, else the property's name) and `count` (repeats in that slot); only a connection bound at both ends is one (no loose edges), and a shape kept aside, or on a board the editor can't show, carries none. Edge types are types (`EdgeType`: `static let id: EdgeTypeID`, `parent` nil by default) declared by the plugin that owns the meaning (`registrar.edgeType(T.self)`, or `edgeType(_:parent:)` with a parent resolved from the frozen registry; the `EdgeTypes` point, one per id, whose `check` refuses an undeclared parent and a loop), a tree (`EdgeTypeTree`: `registry.edgeTypes`, or `topology.types`, kept); shipped: `arrow` (ArrowPorts' `ArrowEdge`) with each channel's type (`note-link`, `schema-link`) under it, and `mention` (Mentions' `MentionEdge`, a root). A type nobody declares (a newer app's channel) is kept as written, with no parent. Queries filter by `EdgeTypeFilter`: `.all`, `.within(t)` (t and every type under it), `.only(t)`. Sources (the `EdgeSources` point; several per kind add up) say what a record carries: `x.edges { shape, registry, sink in … }`, `registrar.edges(K.self) { record, registry, sink in … }`, `edgesForEveryShape(where:)` (asked once per kind), and the property rule `registrar.edges(in: P.self, as: T.self)` (every shape a `ShapeNaming` added property's value names, slot its key, on the kinds that opt in; it doesn't declare `T`); `sink.emit(type, from:to:slot:)` counts repeats and keeps the order of first emission. A source is a pure function of one record and the registry (never other shapes, geometry, services or time, nor where a box is), so each commit reads again only what it removed, added, changed other than by moving (`ChangeSet.moved`), or touched `elsewhere`, and undo is exact; `registry.edges(of:)` is one record's. Queries cost what they return: `edges(of:_:types:)` (`.outgoing`, `.incoming`, `.either`; an edge to itself once; `hasEdges(of:_:types:where:)` stops at the first, making nothing), `neighbors(of:_:types:)` (the other ends by type: what's connected, and how), `edges(ofType:)`, `edges(carriedBy:)`, `end(_:)` (`EdgeEnd`: `.live(board)`, `.unavailable`, `.missing`), `revision(of:)` (observed per shape, counted from its first read) and `touched` (the last commit's; `touched(types:)`, of some types). Containment reads the board tree, no index: `parent(of:)` (the owner of its board; nil on the root), `children(of:)`, `ancestors(of:)` (nearest first), `isInside(_:_:)`, only on boards the editor can show; containers other than boards (frames, groups) would be a point, not built. It reads every board the editor can show as it opens (a kind with no source costs a lookup), again for `replaceFile` and `replaceDocument`, and nothing on a switch. `TopologyPlugin()` is right after `SelectionPlugin()` in the shipped list, so its service hears each commit before those that read it; a service that may hear first calls `topology.catchUp(changes)` (nothing once heard). Every plugin list with Ports or Mentions needs it. No command reads it yet, and `canvas draw` matches an edge by its ends, not its type.
- Snapping (a horizontal on the core alone; `docs/plugin-system.md`, "Snapping"): what a drag moves (a `TranslatingSession`: the selection dragged, a shape just placed) snaps to the frames of the shapes in view, as one rect (the frames it moves, together, as the drag began), along each axis on its own, to the nearest within 8 view points (tldraw's `snapThreshold`) of: an edge or its middle onto another frame's (tldraw's corners and center); its middle onto the middle of the space between two neighbors, if it fits there; its far or near edge where the space between two neighbors would repeat before or after them (tldraw's bounds snapping, but spaces only between neighbors, each frame's nearest past either edge that overlaps it across the axis, within 64 frames along it, and only for what shares their stretch across by more than the threshold, so a snap along the other axis never leaves it without its guide; `SnapTargets`, pure, world units). Ties go to edges and middles, then the near edge. It lands exactly on the line (the core's translation adjuster moves each box by exactly what it says on a changed axis), and along the other axis follows the pointer exactly. Always on, unlike tldraw's (on only while ⌘ is held), once the pointer is past the drag threshold from the press, so a click's lone box moves no more than the pointer; ⌘ or ⌃ held once the drag is under way lets what moves follow the pointer exactly, at once (Keynote's ⌘; either frees an arrow's end too; at the press ⌘ on a shape starts a marquee and ⌃ is the secondary click), and ⇧, which keeps the drag to one axis (the core's), snaps and guides along that axis alone (`SnapTargets.snap(_:movedBy:within:only:)`). Kinds with a frame (`BoxShapeProps`) snap and are snapped to, locked ones too (they stay put, and aren't part of what moves); arrows and lines neither (tldraw's arrows can't be snapped to, `canSnap`). What's in view is read when the drag first snaps, again once the camera moves or a shape the drag doesn't move changes (`SnapState`, a service); no view (`viewportSize` zero, headless), nothing to snap to. Guides (`editor.snapGuides`: `.alignment`, a line through the corners and centers on it with a ✕ at each; `.spacing`, equal spaces side by side with ticks, those in a row too; tldraw's look, in its snap red) draw with the session. Reading 10,000 frames in view costs some 6 ms, each event after 0.12 ms (`./build.sh bench`).
- The command line (`canvas`, for agents first, people too; `docs/plugin-system.md`, "The command line"): agents never read or write a board's file, only run `canvas`, which asks the running app. The wire is CanvasRPC (layer `wire`: knows no canvas, plugin or AppKit): JSON-RPC 2.0, a message a line, over a Unix-domain socket only this user can reach (`~/Library/Application Support/InfiniteCanvas/canvas.sock`, or `CANVAS_SOCKET`; the app that holds `canvas.sock.lock` listens, so a second app gives up and a crashed one's socket is taken over); `CommandSpec`, a command's description with its params as a JSON Schema, and how params are spelled as arguments (`spec.params(from:)`, `usage()`, `help()`); the blocking `RPCClient` and the `RPCListener`, which answers on the main actor. `canvas` is CanvasCLI (layer `cli`: the wire only, never the app) and knows no command but `help`: each run asks the app (`describe`) and makes the commands, their arguments and their help from what it says, so a plugin's command needs nothing of `canvas`, and it refuses an app of another `RPC.protocolVersion`. Results go to standard output (JSON when it isn't a terminal, text for people, `--format`), errors to standard error with a `reason` and a `hint`, and it exits 0 done, 1 the app couldn't, 2 usage, 3 unreachable. The app's end is CanvasUI's `CommandServer`, started by the app: `status` and `boards` are its own, and it runs the rest on the board a request names (`--board`: its number, `BoardDocument.number`, its file, or its name) or the one in front, and there on the board inside the file that its window shows (`editor.board`: `boards` names it, `shows` and `showsPath`, root first, as `BoardID`s, and `status` the front one's, `frontmostShows`; no command addresses another yet), so a command's change is an undo step on that board. Every other command is Automation's (a horizontal, which the app reaches through Composition's `commands(in:)`): `registrar.automationCommand("name", "summary", params: P.self, positionals:, examples:) { params, context in … }`, its params a `SchemaDefined` list (each property an option, `fooBar` as `--foo-bar`; `x-value-name` for help), checked and decoded before it runs, its output anything `Encodable`, its errors `AutomationError(_:reason:hint:)`, a board command's changes through `context.board()`'s `transact`. The registry refuses a name `canvas` can't type or that's kept (`RPC.reservedCommandNames`: `describe`, the server's `status` and `boards`, `canvas`'s `help`), a param named `board` or like `canvas`'s own options, and two commands of one name. A command's options may come before its name (`canvas --board 2 draw - < plan.txt`); text whose schema says `"x-stdin": true` is read from standard input when given as `-`; an error about a script carries `line` and `statement`, and several at once `errors` (protocol 2); CommandServer adds `board: {id, name, shows, showsPath}` to a board command's object result, as `boards` lists it, read before it runs. Automation's own: `draw` and `kinds`. `draw` reads a script in a language after reladraw's (`THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md`), one statement a line: a shape named by its title (a title the board has restates that shape, any other makes one), with attributes by its kind's schema and placements against shapes already named, never coordinates; an edge; a delete; a style. DrawScript reads it (`Script(parsing:)`) and places it (`LayoutProblem.solve()`, its obstacles filed once a run in a grid, `ObstacleIndex`, and each answer kept, so a draw costs what's near what it places: `./build.sh bench` holds 20 notes pushed along rows of 10,000 shapes to 150 ms); Automation's `Drawing` resolves it against the board shown as the run starts (new shapes from `placement.shape(at:)`, attributes through `registry.shape(_:setting:)`, JSON held to the kind's schema first, `registry.schemaViolations(of:kind:)`, so a member its type hasn't is `invalid_value`, not dropped; - Selection (a horizontal, after Keyboard in the shipped list) is built on those hooks alone: the selection state (a service, read as `editor.selectedShapeIDs` and `editor.selection`, the only selected shape, as tldraw's onlySelectedShape), the Select tool (the default) and marquee, handles, Delete/Return/⌘A/Back (their keys registered with Keyboard; Delete enabled while something's selected, a locked shape too, which shakes, ⌘A while the board has shapes), the arrow keys' nudge (1 world unit, 10 with ⇧, `nudgeSelection(by:)`, a `translate` in one "Move", no snapping, again and again while held, one undo step, while something's selected and nothing's under way or edited; a locked shape stays, refused, and shakes), Zoom to Selection (⇧2, `reveal` of the selection) and a trackpad's smart zoom (`.smartMagnify`, a two-finger double-tap: `reveal` of the shape under the pointer, where the gesture was, `editor.pointerLocated(at:)` (host API), since a window that isn't key hears no moves; and the next, while the camera's still there, back), and the double-click claim. Opening a shape is Selection's too: `x.opens("Open") { shape, editor in … }` (the `ShapeOpening` point; `registrar.opens(Kind.self, …)` for bridges) says what opening a kind's shape does (a board-owning kind shows its board); a double-click on a shape, Return, Enter and ⌘↓ (`.openSelection`, enabled while `editor.canOpen` the only selected shape and nothing is being edited, as ⌘↓ is then the text's; Return and Enter always take the key) run `editor.open(id)` (a double-click's `open(id, at:clickCount:)`, from where it's pressed): the kind's opener, else `beginEditing` for an `Editable` one (a read-only one counts as openable, so its refusal shakes; an opener runs whatever the lock). `editor.goBack()` is data-canvas's selection history: each change of the selection stacks the one it leaves, with its board and the camera as it was then (`editor.cameraTarget`, where it was headed if it was gliding; 50 steps; a select-tool press and its drag are one step; `canGoBack` only when going back would change the selection or the board); showing another board is a step too, even with nothing selected (Selection's `boardDidChange`: nothing is selected on arrival, not a step of its own, and the press, hover, handle and canvas buttons are let go), but not a file put in place, which no one took (`BoardChange.isFileReplaced`: a file opened on the board left open, a revert), and going back to a step on another board shows it, selects there and sets the camera (not animated), skipping a board that can't be shown any more; `editor.showBoard(_:selecting:)` switches and selects as one step (on the board shown, it only selects); the detail panel's header has the back button, and Go › Back (⌘[) works whatever has the keyboard. Every kind is selectable unless it opts out (`x.unselectable()`, or `x.selectable { ... }` per shape); the select tool sees through such shapes, nothing else does. A read-only shape (a locked one) is selected, hovered and outlined as any other, but shows no handles, so a press there presses the shape. A plugin can put buttons on the canvas (the `CanvasButtons` point: where they are, in view points, and what they do), clicked (`registrar.canvasButtons(at:click:)`, Adornments' clickable adornments: the lock chip) or dragged (`registrar.canvasButtons(at:drag:)`, Ports' dots): in the select tool only, they win over handles and shapes (the later plugin's first, as its overlay draws on top); a press and a release on a clicked one click it (dragged off, nothing), with the pointing hand over one (`CanvasCursor.pointingHand`); a dragged one's press, past the drag threshold, hands the drag to the session `drag` returns (given the button and where it was pressed, world units; nil ends the press), and released without a drag does nothing, with the open hand over one and the closed hand while it's pressed; Escape lets go of either. `editor.hoveredButton` and `editor.pressedButton` say what to draw hovered and pressed (both cleared as a drag hands over), a plugin whose buttons change without a commit calls `editor.canvasButtonsDidChange()`, and `editor.pointerOverCanvas` is where the pointer is over the canvas in the select tool (view points, as it last moved with nothing under way; nil once it's left the canvas or gone over the minimap or the editing view, another tool is current, or Escape let go of a dragged button's press, until it moves again), for buttons that show only while it's near (a port's dots). Something drawn for a shape outside it (`registrar.decorationHits`, the `DecorationHits` point: every adornment) is that shape for the select tool (a press, a drag, the release, a double-click), hover and the context menu: `editor.selectableShape(at:)` asks it first, the last plugin's first, with `isSelectable` as its `include`, so an unselectable shape's is seen through; buttons still come before it, and the core's `shape(at:)`, the marquee, the eraser and an arrow's end never see it. Unlike tldraw (and the POC), the selection stays outlined while it moves. `editor.hovered` is the shape the pointer is over, set by the select tool on the canvas or by any plugin where it shows shapes (the table of contents' rows), in any tool; changing tools clears it. `editor.isHovered(id)` is observed per shape (a key path per shape), so a view of one shape hears only that shape's hover change. Its outline is faint: wider and lighter than the selection's (2.5 points, the accent at 60%), unlike tldraw's, which strokes a hover like the selection. Code that reads the selection imports Selection; without its plugin, the selection is empty.
- Going to shapes is Selection's, one way for every plugin: `editor.reveal(ids)` only moves the camera (centers the shapes' bounds in `unobscuredViewport`, below the path over a subboard's canvas, fitted with 100 points of margin, no closer than 120%, over 200 ms: `SelectionStyle.reveal*`, data-canvas's zoomToBounds), and is what selecting table-of-contents rows does. `editor.goTo(ids)` is following a link (a mention): it ends editing, selects the selectable ones (a Back step only when that changes the selection; to what's selected already, or to unselectable shapes, it only moves the camera), and reveals unless they're all in view, where the camera is headed (`cameraTarget`), at a readable zoom: `SelectionStyle.readableZoom` (70%: a note's 16-point text reads at 11.2 points there, just over `NSFont.smallSystemFontSize`), or a kind's own, closer, for text set smaller (`x.readableZoom(_:)`, `registrar.readableZoom(K.self, _:)`, Selection's `ReadableZoom` point, above zero and at most 120%, `ReadableZoom.maximum`, as close as `reveal` brings shapes, `editor.readableZoom(of:)`: a schema card's rows read at 100%; the closest of the shapes' kinds counts), or as close as fitting them would bring them (a shape too big for 70% that's all in view stays put). The shapes may be on any board of the file: it goes to the board of the first of them on a board it can show, and to those of them there, showing that board and selecting them as one Back step (`showBoard(_:selecting:)`), then revealing them. `reveal` is the board shown's alone. Nothing mid-gesture, or when none of the shapes is on a board it can show. `editor.goInto(id)` is following a link into what it names (a mention of a subboard): `goTo`, then `open` there (`canOpen`), as one Back step to where it was followed from, whatever boards it went through (`SelectionState.asOneStep`: nothing inside records a step of its own); nothing for a shape whose own board is shown.
- Faces (Selection's `ShapeFaces` point, `registrar.shapeFaces(at:click:doubleClick:hovering:)`) are parts of a shape's content that answer a click without taking the shape's press: a mention's pill in a note, a link, a checkbox on a card. `at` is asked only for a plain press (no ⇧, ⌘ or ⌥, not a Control-click) on the shape the select tool would press, selected or not, at a world point on that shape itself (a point on what `DecorationHits` draws for a shape is no face's), after canvas buttons and handles, and looks at that one shape; the last plugin's first. The press is the shape's: it selects the shape as any press does and, past the drag threshold, moves the selection from the press; on a lone box it waits for the threshold too (an intended deviation from the lone box's press, pinned in SelectionTests). Released within the threshold, the shape is selected alone and `click` runs; Escape lets go first. A double-click whose press lands on one selects the shape alone and runs `doubleClick` in place of editing it, a read-only shape's too; without `doubleClick`, a double-click there edits an `Editable` shape and is otherwise one more click. The pointer over one hovers the shape `hovering` names (`editor.hovered`, so hover keeps one path), or its own shape when it names none, or one that isn't there or can't be selected; hover asks whatever keys are held, and the cursor stays the arrow. `click` runs once the press is over, so it may `goTo` or go back, and what it selects is a Back step of its own; it isn't given the press, so a face that needs a point carries it in its value (a pill edits its note from there, `editor.beginEditing(id, at:)`), and a click that begins editing leaves the pointer over the editing view, which ends the hover. CompositionTests' canvas view tests hold these pieces together with `pillsPlugin`, a pill-like face in a note: edited from the pill on a click, not hovered under its text, and gone to (`goTo`) from a locked note's double-click; Mentions' pills are faces built on them. A face that edits its shape checks `editor.isReadOnly` first (as `pillsPlugin` does): a refused edit is a try, which Locking shakes, and a click on a locked shape isn't one. A click on a face that goes somewhere (it changes the selection or moves the camera: a link's `goTo`) makes the rest of its double-click its own: the select tool ignores each press after it with a click count of 2 or more, until one of 1 (`ignoresPress`), so the camera goes on to where the click sent it and nothing is placed, edited or pressed where it has got to; a click that goes nowhere (a pill's) leaves the double-click to `doubleClick`.
- Boards are files, one per board, with the boards inside it (`docs/plugin-system.md`, "Boards on disk"): `BoardDocument` (CanvasUI, an `NSDocument`, autosaved in place) saves `editor.file`, every board of it, with `BoardCodec` (Canvas), and opens one with `replaceFile`. The app is AppKit-run: `InfiniteCanvasApp` sets `BoardDocument.configuration` (the shipped registry, the window's SwiftUI content) and installs `BoardMenus`; the Info.plist that `build.sh` writes exports the `.canvasboard` type (not `.board`: Freeform owns it). Order in the stack is each record's `stackingKey`. A kind's props are versioned by its append-only `static let migrations`: changing how they encode needs a migration and a new fixture in `Tests/CompositionTests/Fixtures/kinds` (never edit a fixture), even a new member with a default (`PropsMigration("name")`, which rewrites nothing). An added property's value is versioned by its own (`PropertyMigration`, over the value's JSON), whatever kinds opt in, with fixtures in `Fixtures/added` (`expectPropertyFixtures`); `PropsMigration.handOver("title", to: TitleProperty.self)` hands a kind's own member over to one. Shapes the app can't use are kept aside (`AnyShapeRecord.isOpaque`) and saved back as they were; a horizontal that goes through every shape skips them. A record's metadata is saved beside its props (`meta`), members no plugin knows included, and its added values in `added`, by key; those the app can't use (not declared, not opted into, newer, unreadable) in `kept`, with their versions, saved back as they were and listed in `LoadReport.keptValues` (the alert never offers to delete them). A board is written in the oldest format that holds it: 4 with any board besides the root (`BoardCodec.encode(CanvasFile)`: `boards`, one flat map by owner id, never nested (Foundation's JSON reader stops at 512 levels), each `{ "shapes" : [...] }`, under the root's `versions`; read only from format 4), else 3 with added values (the envelope's `versions` has `kinds` and, when any, `added`), else 2 with metadata, else 1 (both with `schema`), so an older app refuses what it would save without. `LoadedBoard.file` is every board (`document` its root); reading walks the boards in tree order with one set of ids, so a repeated id is the first holder's, with the board it owns, and keeps the boards it can't show (`LoadReport.keptBoards`, `KeptBoard`: an owner kept aside, of a kind that owns none, inside such a board, unreachable, or an entry that isn't a board, kept verbatim in `CanvasFile.unreadableBoards`), writing them back; `KeptShape` and `KeptValue` name their `board`. The camera on each board, the board left open, and whether the details and the minimap show are remembered per file (`BoardViewMemory`: `View`'s `board` and `boards`, read with `decodeIfPresent`, so an older entry is the root's; saved for the boards the file can still show, `editor.boardCameras`: the one shown and those above it, then those shown most lately, up to `BoardViewMemory.boardLimit`, 50, by `editor.boardsLeft` and the last session's `recent`), never in the file; a file opens on the board left open if it can still show it, through `replaceFile(with:showing:)`, so it's no step back nor an edit, each board with its camera (`rememberedCameras`). A board document has no undo manager (`hasUndoManager = false`): it counts its changes by its history's events (`undoHistory.didChange`, host API: a step began, came to nothing, was undone or redone), at once, so undoing to what was saved reads unedited. Every save closes the open step (`breakStep`), so undo returns to exactly what was saved; a periodic autosave during a press, or while a key is held whose repeats are one step (`undoHistory.isKeyHolding`: an arrow key's nudges), gives way (`userCancelled`) and runs once it's let go (`snapshot(implicitlyCancellable:)`). Its window is a `BoardWindow`, which answers Edit › Undo and Redo: the text's own while text has the keyboard (its `textUndo`, the only undo manager text views see, or a field's; forgotten when the text loses the keyboard; after each, the text says it changed, so a note or a field's binding writes it), else the board's `.undo`/`.redo`, named ("Undo Move"); it seals a run of panel writes on a click, a key on a control, or the keyboard moving, and every board's window seals on a click in a window that isn't a board's (the color panel), but not in a board's child window (the `@` picker beside a panel's text, whose pick is part of the typing).
- Change documents only with `editor.transact`, and name the step a user would call it (`transact("Name")`, `undoHistory.group("Name")` around several). A body that throws commits nothing and opens no step, and the error comes out untyped. Tools see `Pointer` values, never `NSEvent`; timers go through `editor.scheduler`. Model positions are world units; overlay sizes (handles, outlines, hit margins) are view points, divided by `zoom` in world space.
- Boards in a file (`docs/plugin-system.md`, "Boards in a file"): a file is a tree of boards, `CanvasFile`: the root board, and `children`, a board for each shape of a `BoardOwning` kind, keyed by the owner's `ShapeID` (a board is named by a `BoardID`, `.root` or `BoardID(owner:)`, written "root" or the id), never in the owner's props (its schema is marked `x-canvas-board`). So the tree is a function of where shapes are: a board has one owner, a copy (a new id) has a board of its own, and nothing done in the app makes a loop or an orphan (only a file edited by hand, which reading keeps and reports). Shape ids are unique across the file (`file.board(containing:)`, `file.record(_:)`); a board with no shapes isn't kept, so placing an owner makes only the shape; each board has a revision (`file.revision(of:)`) that changes with its shapes and never comes back, which is what anything kept about a board (an index, rows, issues, locks, a preview) is checked against. One editor holds a whole file (`editor.file`) and shows one board of it (`editor.board`) as `document`: what's local to a board comes from reading `editor.document`; never list shapes from `editor.file`. `editor.contents(of:)` and `editor.owner(of:)` read any board. A transaction is on the board shown and reads the file through the editor, never holding a copy; deleting a shape takes its board and every board under it in the same commit (the cascade, `ChangeSet.boardsRemoved`, in `Transaction.delete`, one undo step that undo puts back whole, every id), unless a read-only shape is on any of them, which keeps the owner from being deleted; `transaction.insert(_:contents:)` brings a shape in with its boards (a paste, `boardsAdded`). A commit says what it did beyond its board in `ChangeSet.elsewhere` (every shape of the boards it took or brought, those it changed on another board, and, for a file put in place over the board shown, those its other boards changed), for what reads shapes anywhere in the file. Only `editor.transact(on: board, "Name") { … }` edits a board that isn't shown (what lands late: a sync's answer): its commit changes that board in the file, is an undo step of its own made on that board (undoing it shows the board first; a step open on the board shown stays open, after it), and services hear it as `boardsChanged` with its shapes `elsewhere`; it can't take the board shown or one above it, shakes nothing, and returns nil for a board `canShow` refuses. Core and horizontal tests nest boards with CanvasTesting's `PocketsPlugin` (a `pocket` kind that's `BoardOwning`, apart from `FixturesPlugin` so the reports tests pin don't change), never with Subboards.

Arrows stop aiming in `boardWillChange`. `replaceFile(with:showing:)` stays on the board shown if the new file can show it, else goes to the nearest board above it, or the root, as a switch does (and `CanvasEditor(registry:file:showing:)` opens on one). A `BoardStash<State>` is a service's state per board, taken back only while the board's revision is unchanged; `BoardChange.isFileReplaced` says a `replaceFile` came with the switch, so what a service keeps across the file by shape id is taken afresh. Damage marks are per index (`DamageMark`, host API; `editor.isCurrent(mark)`), so a picture kept for a board (the minimap's) is current only while the editor kept that board's index. A render may read `context.shapeCount(inBoardOwnedBy:)` and `context.shape(inFile:)` (a shape on any board the editor can show: a mention's pill; `editor.shape(inFile:)` elsewhere), and draw `context.drawBoardPreview(ofBoardOwnedBy:in:)` (only on `RenderContext`: geometry and hit tests never read another board). The command names `.openSelection` and `.enclosingBoard` are the core's, for plugins to claim (Selection claims `.openSelection`, Boards `.enclosingBoard`). A switch isn't an undo step: it closes the one under way, and undoing or redoing a step made on another board shows that board first. Every shape there is a silhouette of its geometry in one muted gray (`Silhouettes`, the core's, host API, which the minimap draws with too), no text or colors, in a mask at most 256 pixels on a side. A preview is keyed by board and `file.revision(of:)`, so one of a board as it was before an edit or an undo is dropped, never drawn; the 64 drawn last are kept, never evicting one the frame before drew (more owners in view than that: the rest show their count, and nothing thrashes). Boards asked for are built at the start of each frame, those asked for longest ago first, a chunk of shapes at a time within 2 ms (`frameBudget`; at least 16 shapes), and the editor asks for frames (`editor.scheduler`) while any wait; what the frame just drawn didn't ask for stops waiting (`endFrame()`, and a switch of board drops it all). Keep them that cheap: `./build.sh bench` holds ten 10,000-shape subboards in view to a frame while they fill.
- A frame costs what's in view and what changed, never the whole board (`./build.sh bench` holds 10,000 shapes to 16 ms). The dot grid under the shapes (`DotGrid`, the core's) is drawn from tiles of dots filled once, copied at whole device pixels through a clip of the pixels the dots touch, in the context's own pixel layout and space (sRGB for one no 8-bit bitmap takes, an extended range's): pixel for pixel the path of an ellipse per dot it replaced wherever a whole number of dots spans whole pixels (100%, and the POC's goldens at 60% and 15%, which hold it), and elsewhere each copy within an eighth of a pixel (a tile for each quarter-pixel step its copies need); kept while the camera doesn't move, some 1 to 1.5 ms of a frame at 1200 × 800 on Retina (the path took 3 to 7), CanvasTests' GridTests against that path (CanvasTesting's `drawGridAsEllipses`, which the benchmark reports beside it). The editor keeps each shape's geometry and extent, and the paint order (`ShapeIndex`), and redoes them for what each commit's `ChangeSet` names; it keeps the board's bounds too, and where each commit moved or reshaped shapes (damage: where the shapes it touched, and those whose geometry followed them, were and are), for pictures of the board kept from frame to frame (host API: `shapeBounds`, `damageMark`, `damage(since:)`). So a definition looks at other shapes only through its `ShapeContext` (`shape(_:)`, `geometry(of:)`, `hitTest(_:shape:)`: they're tracked, all of `document` or `camera` isn't); a `hitTest` that reaches past the outline declares `hitMargin`; a paint anchor follows from its shape's props and the document's order; per-frame code (overlays) goes through `editor.paintOrder(in:)` with `context.visibleRect`; and a service keeps per-shape state and redoes the shapes a change set names, as `ValidationEngine`, `ContentsState` and `AdornmentState` do.
- The minimap (CanvasUI's `MinimapView`, which `CanvasView.showsMinimap` hosts in the canvas's bottom-left corner; View › Show Minimap, ⌃⌘M, remembered per board in `BoardViewState` and `BoardViewMemory`; hidden, its corner the canvas's, while the board has nothing to show, `shapeBounds` null, and faded in with the first shape, once the press that placed it under the minimap, or its editing, is done, at once on another board) works as tldraw's: the board and the viewport fitted together; a click beside the viewport eases the camera there over 320 ms, a drag moves it within tldraw's clamp, a double-click centers exactly. It draws every shape as a rough silhouette of its geometry in one color (the core's `Silhouettes`), never its render (no text, no colors), into a bitmap of tiles kept from frame to frame (`MinimapPicture`): damage makes tiles stale, and each frame draws again those stale longest, within its budget (4 ms, and a tile at least), so a frame spends at most that on the picture however big the board, and whatever goes stale every frame, every tile has its turn. The selection shows in the accent color, through `editor.outlines` (host API). On another board it starts afresh, never from the old board's picture, and keeps up to 8 boards' pictures (those on the path longest, as the editor keeps their indexes), taken back while `editor.isCurrent` says their marks are. Keep it that cheap.
- The views `CanvasView` hosts, the minimap and the editing view, have the pointer where they are: over one the pointer isn't over the canvas, though AppKit tells the canvas it moved (its tracking area covers them), so the tool hears it leave (`pointerExited`), once, as it moves or is released there (the press that began editing), and nothing more until it's back on the canvas. So the select tool hovers no shape under a note's text (`editor.hovered` is only what another plugin sets there), `editor.pointerOverCanvas` is nil there, and the canvas leaves the view's cursor alone. Once editing ends, where the editing view was is the canvas again. So do the views a host puts over the canvas (the path's pill and a crumb's menu): the canvas asks the window what's at the pointer (`hitTest`), and where that isn't the canvas or a view it hosts the pointer is off it, under the arrow cursor, and back on the canvas the tool's cursor is set again.
- Arrows see other shapes only as `ConnectableShape` (a closed shape's core geometry). Keep specific shape kinds out of arrow code.
- UI constants live in each module's `@MainActor` style enum (`Style`, `ArrowStyle`, `NoteStyle`, ...). Pure geometry (Geometry, `ArrowGeometry`) is nonisolated, so its constants live on those types.

## Arrows follow tldraw

- Arrow behavior and geometry deliberately mirror tldraw's arc arrows: binding precision and timers, clipping, arrowhead gaps, handles and hint overlays. The constants come from tldraw's source.
- Check tldraw (`packages/tldraw/src/lib/shapes/arrow/`, `bindings/arrow/`, `overlays/`) before changing arrow behavior, and call out any intentional deviation.
- Moving arrows is `ArrowDefinition: CustomTranslation`. Alone (or with unrelated shapes) it is the POC's translation exactly; moved with a shape it's bound to, it keeps every binding, as tldraw. Its ends probe at the full delta (tldraw: half), a known deviation the transcript pins.
- What an end may bind to is tldraw's canBind, from any plugin: `ArrowBindingRules` (`registrar.arrowBindingRule { query in … }`, over `ArrowBindingQuery`: the arrow as it is, the end, a shape under it, the registry). An end binds only to a shape every rule allows, looking through the rest to the shapes behind, wherever an end binds: `ArrowTargeting.bindTerminal` (a port's drag, re-aims, the precise timer, a modifier's change) and a lone arrow's translation probe (`beginTranslation`); rules are asked only of the shapes the point hits, and with none nothing is filtered. Arrows knows no channel: ArrowPorts' rule does, and lets an arrow without one bind to anything, as arrows always did (the transcript holds them). `shapes.canBindArrow(to:)` is whether an arrow can bind to a shape in a `ShapeContext` (there, closed, not an arrow; `editor.canBindArrow(to:)` in the editor's), `registry.arrowMayBind(arrow, end, to:)` asks the rules for an arrow made without a pointer (ArrowPorts' `make`), and `editor.beginArrow(from:at:canBind:complete:)` draws one for another plugin's gesture (a port's drag): it inserts an arrow, its start bound to the shape at its center (imprecise) and its end free at `origin`, and returns an `ArrowHandleDragSession` dragging the end (`connecting`), which binds by tldraw's rules (fast to the center, slow or after a 320 ms dwell precise, ⌥ exact, ⌘ or ⌃ nothing, ⇧ snaps the angle) only to shapes `canBind` and the rules allow, seeing through the rest; released bound, `complete(&record, target)` finishes it in one transaction. `BindingHintsOverlay` draws for the arrow being dragged (the session's `arrowID`), else the selection, so re-aims and a port's arrow hint alike. A port's drag deviates from tldraw on purpose, as data-canvas's does: released free (or on Escape) the arrow is deleted, where tldraw keeps a free end; it scrolls the canvas at its edges (`scrollsAtEdges`), where tldraw's handle drags don't, so a shape off screen can be linked to without zooming out; its start is the port's shape's center, with none of tldraw's arrow tool's hover and press timers; and the selection and the tool are left alone (no selecting the new arrow, no step of the selection's history).
- CompositionTests holds the app to the proof of concept (commit 804db97): an arrow transcript compared byte for byte, and renders (outlines, binding hints, handles) compared pixel for pixel. `Tests/CompositionTests/Replay.swift` drives both the POC recording and the test. The fixtures come only from the POC, via `scripts/record-poc-fixtures.sh` (re-running it must change nothing). Never regenerate them from the app to make a change pass; an intended change to arrow behavior needs an explicit note. A scenario that ends differently on purpose gets an entry in `intendedDeviations` (TranscriptTests): the golden line, the app's, and why. The one so far: a press doesn't bring a shape to the front (the POC's did; tldraw's doesn't). Scenarios are only ever appended, recorded from the POC; the one exception: retiring the arrow tool (A; arrows are drawn from ports alone now) took the scenarios and renders that drew with it, and re-aims of an arrow's end the same ways took their place, appended. The POC's timers run on real time, so keep an event 50 ms or more from when one fires, or it records either way. The "pan, then re-aim" scenario presses H and holds still 100 ms before `.up` (both no-ops in the POC), and the welcome renders use the POC's note text, `pocWelcomeTexts`.

- For the table of contents, read `editor.tableOfContents?.entries` headlessly (each row's text, symbol and badge through `entry.label`, its marks through `entry.marks`), and drive `select(_:)`, `showAll()`, `hover(_:)` and `endHover(_:)`. `rowsRedrawn` (debug builds) counts how often the list has asked a row for its view, so a test holds a change to redrawing the rows it changes (HoverTests), and `rowsMade` how many rows it has made (BoardTests: going back to a board makes none). Headless, `open(_:)` is a row's primary action; in a window, `pressReturn()` with the list focused runs it (a synthesized double-click doesn't reach the list). SwiftUI's hover doesn't answer synthesized events, so check a row's tint by rendering the list with `snapshot(view)` (CanvasTesting; highlights draw, a source list's vibrant text may not).
