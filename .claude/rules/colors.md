# Colors

Colors: the color a shape of a kind that opted in holds (`color`: a named color or a hex value), the built-in names and a file's own, the book that says what every color looks like (`editor.colors`), how a kind draws its color, where one is picked (the panel's row, `/color`, Color ▸), what `canvas draw` and `canvas query` say of it, and what each shipped kind does with its color.

Related: [palette](palette.md), [properties and schemas](properties-and-schemas.md), [detail panel](detail-panel.md), [slash commands](slash-commands.md), [context menus](context-menus.md), [validation](validation.md), [command line](command-line.md), [queries](queries.md), [types](types.md), [subboards](subboards.md), [frames](frames.md), [arrows](arrows.md), [plugins and kinds](plugins-and-kinds.md), [boards on disk](boards-on-disk.md) ([every topic](../../CLAUDE.md#map)).

In the guide, `docs/plugin-system.md`: [Colors](../../docs/plugin-system.md#colors).

## Architecture

- Colors (a concern, on Canvas, Palette, ContextMenus, DetailPanel, Draw, Queries, SlashCommands and Validation; plugin id `colors`; loosely tldraw's color system: named colors, each drawn in roles per appearance, and a document's own colors) knows no kind. A shape of a kind that opted in has a color; every other shape has none. What a color looks like is [Palette](palette.md)'s `Swatch`, seven roles in the light appearance and the dark, worked out from the one value the same way for a name and for a hex; which names there are, and what each shape's color is, is the book's. What a kind does with its color is the kind's.
- **A color** (`ShapeColor`): `.named(String)` or `.value(RGB)`, one string on a board (`blue`, `Brand Blue`, `#4465E9`). `ShapeColor(text)` reads one as it's written: a hex in either case and in three or six digits, else a name folded to one line with no spaces at its ends; nil for nothing and for what starts `#` and isn't a hex. Decoding never refuses a board: a string that isn't a hex is a name, which the file has or lacks. A name is kept as written and found whatever its case (`ShapeColor.key(of:)`). Its schema is a string with `contentMediaType` `text/x-canvas-color` (`ShapeColor.mediaType`), which draw and queries read it by. No alpha.
- **The property** (`ColorProperty`, key `color`, nil on a shape with no color of its own; `record.ownColor`; fixture `Fixtures/added/color.v0.json`; `color` is an ordinary id to the audit, `ORDINARY_IDS`, a word any data may hold). A kind opts in inside its registration: `note.colorable(default: .yellow)`, or `frame.colorable()` for a kind whose shapes look as the kind does on its own with no color (`registrar.colorable(K.self, default:)` for another plugin's kind): the opt-in to the property and one contribution to `ColorDefaults` (a `KeyedExtensionPoint`, one a kind; its `check` refuses a default that names a color no file is sure to have, anything but a built-in name or a value; the report's `colorable (yellow) → sticky-note`). `registry.isColorable(kind)`, `registry.defaultColor(of: kind)`. A list with a kind that opts in needs `ColorsPlugin` (the registry refuses a contribution whose owner isn't installed), and `ColorsPlugin` needs `SlashCommandsPlugin` and `ContextMenusPlugin`, as Locking does.
- **The built-in names** (`BuiltInColors.all`, in the order every list shows them): gray, red, orange, yellow, green, teal, blue, indigo, purple, pink, each a `NamedColor` (`name`, `value`, `isBuiltIn`, `swatch`, `color`). Each value is one sRGB color chosen from renders of its roles on both canvases, dark enough that white reads on its solid (yellow's and orange's take black), and its roles are `Swatch(value)` but for three pins that keep what the app drew before colors had names: yellow's paper is the note's yellow (`BuiltInColors.notePaper`), and indigo's line in the dark (`#5E5CE6`) and its surface (`cardLight`, `cardDark`) are the subboard's. ColorsTests hold every role of every one to its floor on both canvases.
- **The book** (`ColorBook`, `editor.colors`, a service; in a render `context.service(ColorBook.self)`): `names` (the built-in ones, then the file's), `named(_:)`, `canonical(_:)` (a name in its list's spelling, nil for one the file lacks), `has(_:)`, `swatch(for: color)`; `color(of: record)` is the color a shape is drawn in, its own where the file has it, else its kind's default, else nil (a name the file lacks is drawn as the default, and warned of), and `swatch(of: record)` its roles. It keeps nothing per shape or per board (its `boardDidChange` reads no shape): what it keeps is per color, the roles and their `CGColor`s in each appearance (`SwatchColors`), made the first time a color is asked for, let go of when the file's colors change, and at most 4,096 (a picker dragged across its wheel asks for thousands of values); the color and the kind asked for last are kept apart, since shapes drawn one after another mostly share both, and whether the appearance being drawn in is dark is asked once an appearance, not once a shape. `onChange { … }` is called when the file's colors change, for a plugin that keeps something made from a color.
- **Drawing** (`context.colors(of: shape)`, a `RenderContext` extension): the seven roles as `CGColor`s in the appearance being drawn, or nil for a shape drawn as its kind looks on its own. One lookup a shape, read in a render and a skeleton alike. A kind draws the role that suits each part: `paper` for a note's sheet, `solid` for a line or a border, `surface` for what holds shapes, `ink` for text in the color.
- **Writing**: `editor.setColor(_ color: ShapeColor?, of: [ShapeID])`, one step, "Change Color", on whichever boards the shapes are; nil takes the color away; a name is stored in its list's spelling; kinds with no colors are left out; a read-only shape is refused by the core and shakes; a change that changes nothing is no step.
- **A file's own colors** (`FileColors.swift`): a list of a name and a value, kept on the file as a file value (`FileColorsProperty`, key `colors`, a `FileColorList` of `FileColor`s, each `{ "name": …, "value": "#RRGGBB" }`, in the order added; [properties and schemas](properties-and-schemas.md), File values; fixture `Fixtures/file/colors.v0.json`, held by PersistenceTests through CanvasTesting's `expectFilePropertyFixtures`), listed after the built-in ones in that file only. A name is one line (`ShapeColor.folded`), 1 to 40 characters (`ColorsStyle.maxNameLength`), never starting `#`, unlike any other of the file's whatever the case, and never a built-in one: `ColorNameRefusal` (`empty`, `startsWithHash`, `tooLong`, `builtIn`, `taken`) is why one is refused, as a value with no words (`ColorNameRefusal.check(_:takenKeys:)`, `book.refusal(ofName:renaming:)`). Reading is lenient, so a board written by hand is never refused for them: an entry that isn't a name and a hex, or whose name the rules refuse, is dropped. The book follows the file: `fileColors` is read as the book is made, on a commit that changed the value (`ChangeSet.fileValues`: an edit, an undo, a redo) and on a file put in place (`BoardChange.fileValues`), all through `fileColorsDidChange(to:)`, which lets go of what was made from the old colors, calls the `onChange` handlers and redraws; a switch of board reads nothing. On `CanvasEditor`, each one step, returning whether it did anything: `defineColor(named:value:)` ("Add Color"); `setColorValue(of:to:coalescing:)` ("Change Color": the list's entry alone, so every shape that names it is recolored and none rewritten; with a key, a run is one step, a drag in the color panel); `renameColor(_:to:)` ("Rename Color": the entry, and `color` on every shape of every board that holds the name, whatever its case); `removeColor(_:)` ("Remove Color": the entry, and each shape that held it takes its value as a hex, so it looks as it did); `nameColor(of:_:)` ("Add Color": the value a shape holds becomes a color of the file's and the shape names it). A rename, a removal or a naming that would rewrite a read-only shape refuses whole (nothing changes, no step, those shapes shake): done shape by shape it would leave a locked shape under a name the file lacks, drawn as its kind's default. A shape on a board that can't be shown is not reached. A name the file lacks (a shape pasted from another file) draws as its kind's default and is warned of.
- **The panel's row** (`ColorDetailsPlugin`, `ColorPanel.swift`, `ColorPanelModel.swift`): one line, `Color`, in the Style tab (`StyleTab`, DetailPanel's, after Connections), on every kind that has colors: a headerless section named for its steps (`DetailSection(in:id:).named("Color")`, [detail panel](detail-panel.md)), shown while the shape's kind has colors (`shownWhile`, asked of the book, `hasColors`, since a section is handed the editor's services and not its registry; the section is over every shape, since one with no color of its own holds no value for a section over the property to show). The row is a dot of the color itself (its value, as a swatch shows one; its roles are what a shape draws) and what it's called (`ColorRowModel.title`: a named color's `NamedColor.title`, a built-in name capitalized and a file's as written, as Color ▸ says it; a value as its hex; a shape with no color of its own shows its kind's default dimmed, or `None` dimmed, and so does one that names a color the file lacks). A click opens the grid (`ColorGrid`, a popover): the named colors as dots, seven a row, the built-in ones and under them the file's, the shape's own ringed, each dot its name as tooltip and accessibility label; then `None`, `Other…` (the system color panel, `ColorPanelTarget`, started at the color itself, never a role of it: each color it reports written as a hex in sRGB, a drag one step; the panel floats on after the row shows another shape or goes, so the row lets go of it then, `release`, and what is picked after is written nowhere) and, while the shape holds a value of its own, `Name…`, which shows a field in the grid's place (`ColorNameForm`: the button dimmed while the name is refused, Return gives it) and makes the value a color of the file's. A dot of the file's has a menu: `Rename…`, `Change…` (the color panel, a drag one step) and `Remove`. Writes of the shape's color go through the section's binding (`ColorRowModel.apply(_:to:)`, `ColorChoice`), so the step is "Change Color" and a locked shape's row is disabled; the file's colors' through the editor's calls above. `ColorRowModel` has no view, so tests drive it.
- **`/color`** (`ColorCommand.swift`, registered by `ColorsPlugin` on every kind that holds `color`): a command that takes one choice ([slash commands](slash-commands.md)). Its rows are the named colors in the book's order, each a chip in its `solid` with its title in `onSolid` (`SlashChoice.chip`, `chipInk`: black on yellow and orange), the built-in ones in one group and the file's in the next, then `None` while the shape has a color of its own; what is typed as a hex value is offered as its one row, as it's stored (`/color #46e`: `#4466EE`). A pick is `editor.setColor`, one step. A read-only shape is offered no command.
- **Color ▸** (`ColorMenu.swift`, an item for the canvas in `StateMenuGroup`, after Is a ▸ in plugin order, no line between): a submenu in the menu of a selection whose shapes all have colors: the built-in names capitalized, a line, the file's, a line, `None`; a check by the color every selected shape holds (none where they differ, `None` where none holds one); a pick colors the whole selection in one step; never dimmed (a read-only shape is refused and shakes while the rest change). Plain titles: `MenuItem` shows no image.
- **Draw and queries** (`ColorWords.swift`, each a plugin value of its own): `ColorNamesPlugin` gives `canvas draw` a `drawTextFormat` for the color's media type: a name is kept in its list's spelling (`BLUE` is `blue`), a hex as `#RRGGBB`, and a name the file lacks, or a `#…` that is no hex, is `invalid_value` on its line, listing the names (`canvas kinds` says the attribute takes `text (a color's name or #RRGGBB)`). An edge takes `color:` for its arrow. `ColorQueriesPlugin` gives `canvas query` a `queryTextFormat` that reads the stored text (a hex normalized): `[color="blue"]` matches as written, `[color]` a shape with a color of its own, and the engine compares the literal as written, so another case needs `i` (`[color="BLUE" i]`). The canvas skill says both ([command line](command-line.md)).
- **The warning** (`ColorValidationPlugin`, `ColorValidation.swift`): a shape whose own color is a name the file lacks carries `Color “brand” isn’t in this file.` (a warning, `field` `color`); a hex and a built-in name never do. The rule reads the shape and the book, so a commit costs the shapes it names; when the file's names change (one added, renamed or removed; a color given another value changes no warning, so a drag in the color panel looks through nothing), `ColorWarnings` has every shape of the file that holds a name checked again (`validation.recheck`), a pass over the file that happens only then.
- **A listed shape's symbol** (`ColorTints.swift`): `registrar.shapeTints` (the core's point, [plugins and kinds](plugins-and-kinds.md)) answers a shape's own color's `solid` where the file has it, and nil otherwise, so a shape with no color of its own is listed in its kind's tint as it always was (`book.tint(for:)`: the same `NSColor` each time, so a list that compares appearances sees no change). The book tells the editor when the file's colors change (`editor.shapeTintsDidChange()`), which every list that shows a tint hears.
- **What each kind does with its color**: a note's paper is `paper`, yellow without one, and a type's note stays white ([editing](editing.md)); a type's color is the color its note holds, else one of eight names by its id, drawn as its pill, its notes' chips and `/is-a`'s rows ([types](types.md)); a frame's surface is `surface` and its border `solid`, none without one ([frames](frames.md)); a subboard's card is `surface` and its border and symbol `solid`, indigo without one ([subboards](subboards.md)); an arrow's line and heads are `solid` and its label `ink`, none without one ([arrows](arrows.md)). Each says its title's cover surface from its own color (`x.titleCoverSurface`, [titles](titles.md)), and each keeps its color zoomed out. A reference has no color.

## Testing

- ColorsTests are on the tests' own kinds (`Support.swift`: `Sheet`, colorable, yellow by default, drawn as its paper; `Pane`, colorable with no default, drawn as its solid, gray with none; CanvasTesting's box has no colors; `colorPlugins()`, `colorHarness()`, `render(_:dark:)`, which draws in either appearance where CanvasTesting's `renderBitmap` draws in the light one, and `expectPixel`). `Imports.swift` imports every module the target's line names, so the line holds whichever suites are filled. The suites: `shapeColorTests` (written and read, what is no color, a board's string), `builtInColorTests` (the ten, every role at its floor on both canvases rounded to 8 bits, white on every solid but yellow's and orange's, the pins), `colorRegistryTests` (the opt-in, the report, the schema, the refusals), `colorBookTests`, `setColorTests`, `colorRenderTests` (a default, a color, a value, the dark appearance, a name the file lacks), `fileColorTests` and `fileColorsOnDiskTests` (each action and its undo, the refusals, a rename across two boards, a removal keeping each shape's look, the read-only refusal, a file put in place, the codec and a real `BoardDocument`), `colorPanelTests` and `colorPanelFileTests` (the row's model; writes through the binding the panel hands a section, caught by a probe section, since SwiftUI's buttons can't be found in an unshown window; the color panel faked by an opener), `colorCommandTests` (through `SlashCommandState`), `colorMenuTests` (a secondary click), `colorDrawTests` and `colorQueryTests` (the engines installed, with `CardsPlugin` for a kind with no colors), `colorValidationTests` and `colorTintTests`.
- CompositionTests' `colorsShippedTests` hold the shipped pieces: which kinds have a color and their defaults, `/color` typed in a note's editor in the app's canvas view, Color ▸ in a note's, an arrow's and a frame's menu and not a reference's, and a script drawn and queried through `canvas` over a socket of its own. Each kind's own tests hold its look (StickyNotesTests' `coloredNoteTests`, FramesTests' `frameColorTests`, SubboardsTests' `subboardColorTests`, ArrowsTests' `arrowColorTests`, TypesTests' `typeColorsTests`), and each list's its tint.
- The gallery's `colorStates` (`GalleryColors.swift`): a board with a note in each built-in color, a colored frame, subboard and arrow, in full and at 30%; the panel's row on a plain note, a blue one and a frame; the grid with two of the file's colors, and for a value; `/color`'s list, narrowed, and for a hex; a type with a chosen color beside one with its id's; and a note's menu as text. `GalleryStage.viewPicture` draws a SwiftUI view alone (the grid, which is a popover in the app).
