# Types

Types: a shape that holds a JSON Schema is a type, named by its title (making one with `/is-a type` and taking it back with Remove Type, the file's types in `TypeIndex`, the Schema tab's JSON, its completions and issues); what a shape is (`is`, the `is-a` edge, `/is-a`, the chips and the Types tab); a note that is one: its values (`fieldValues`), what its types give it (the chain, the field book), the rows it and its type have under their words and the typing in them, the card a row with more in it opens, the form, the panel's Fields and the one writer that changes a value; the check (needed and wrong), pointers to other notes, the title from a field (`$title`); what agents read and write of all of it (draw, queries, `--ask`); the words and the schema kept saying the same thing by the model (`TypeSync`), its highlights and their popovers; and their tests.

## The parts

Types is one module in nine parts, each a folder of `Sources/Types`, and a part names only what is in the parts its line in `Package.swift`'s table of parts says it uses: the build type-checks each with those sources alone, so a file that reaches into another part doesn't compile ([build](build.md), "The parts of a module"). The sections below follow them.

- In the table's order, each using only parts above it: `JSON` (the reader, caret, completer, pointer, meta-schema, `SchemaDocument`, `SchemaValue` and `SchemaDirectives`: it knows no type and no shape) uses nothing; `Type` (the two properties, `schema` and `is`, the readings, references, the graph and the file's `TypeIndex`, a type's colors, and `TypesStyle`) uses `JSON`; `Fields` (the chain, `FieldBook`, kinds, paths, values, text, summaries, pointers, the title from a field, `FileFields` and the one writer) uses those; `Checks` (`FieldCheck`, `FieldChecker`, `FieldChecks`, `FieldSchemas`) adds `Fields`; `Sync` (`TypeSync`, its prompts, the highlights) uses only `JSON` and `Type`; `IsA` (what a shape is, shown and changed: the commands, `/is-a`'s rows, Is a ▸, the chips, the panel's Types and Schema tabs with the JSON's completions) uses `Fields` and `Sync` and theirs; `Form` (the sheet, `FieldFormView`, the cards, `LinkPicker`, the panel's Fields) uses all of those; `Rows` (`RowBook`, the drawing, faces, editing and `RowsView`) uses `Form` too, since a row opens a card and never the other way; `Engines` (what draw, queries and Find are given) uses every part. `TypesPlugin.swift`, beside the folders, holds the plugin values and installs all of it.
- A new file goes in the part whose line already names everything it uses. What two parts share lives in the lower one: `TypesStyle` is declared in `Type` and each part adds its own constants in an extension (the inks a card and a row both draw with are the form's, `FieldFormStyle.swift`, since `Rows` is above `Form`); `PluginID.types` is with `TypeWords`, under the points that name it as their owner; `typeable()` is in `Fields` because it opts a kind into `fieldValues` too; the link media type's home is `MetaSchema.linkMediaType`, which `FieldKind.pointerMediaType` is.
- A lower part that must tell a higher one something takes a closure and is registered for, as `TypeIndex.onChange` and `TypeSync.onSpotChange` are: `FieldChecks.onTypingChange` is how the cards hear that the caret moved. A use that has no such answer means the thing is in the wrong part, or the line in the table is wrong: change that, never the check.

## A type

A shape that holds `schema` is a type: its words say what the notes of that type have, and its JSON is the same thing written out. This section is the type itself; what a shape is of a type, and what it holds for the type's fields, follow.

- Types (a concern, on Adornments, Canvas, ContextMenus, DetailPanel, Draw (for the words it gives `canvas draw`, and for its placement for plugins, which puts a new type down: [placing](placing.md)), Find (a note that is one has its values found: `FieldFindPlugin`), LanguageModels, Mentions, ProseMaps, Queries (for the words it gives `canvas query`), Selection, SlashCommands, TextEditing (the one text editor of the rows' one-line field and the form), Titles, Topology and Validation; `docs/plugin-system.md`, "Types") knows no kind. A kind opts in with sugar its plugin calls: `typeWords(_:drawnIn:fitting:)` hands Types its words (a key path to its `MentionText`, the placement its render draws them in, and what a write of them changes besides: a note's height), a contribution to the `TypeWords` point (one per kind, its `words`, `placement` and `write`, the last a transaction's update that fits the shape as the kind fits it), and `canBeType()` opts the kind into `TypeSchemaProperty` (key `schema`, a `SchemaText`), which the registry refuses without `typeWords` (`TypeWords.check`). StickyNotes calls both, so a note is the shipped type; TypesTests' `Memo`, a box of words of the tests' own, shows Types needs no note. A shape is a type exactly while it holds `schema` (`record[TypeSchemaProperty.self] != nil`); nothing else marks it, and Type is in no list of a shape's types. Its name is its title (Titles' `TitleProperty`, `registry.title(of:)`): a type's schema keeps no root `"title"` of its own, and one the person types in the JSON is kept as typed and never the name. `SchemaText` is one string in a board, kept as typed (its order and half-typed text survive), and `ShapeNaming` through `urn:uuid:` `$ref`s (`SchemaReferences.refs(in:)`, `uri(of:)`, `target(of:)`, read in any case, whole or with a fragment), so a copy of a type with the types it refers to refers to the copies and one alone to the originals, and `canvas draw` warns of a `$ref` to a shape not in the file; the key `schema` is an ordinary id to the audit (`ORDINARY_IDS`), a board's own old member. `Fixtures/added/schema.v0.json` is its fixture.
- Making one: `/is-a type` (the `Type` row of `/is-a`'s list: [What a shape is](#what-a-shape-is)) or `editor.makeType(_:)` (`makeTypes(_:)` for several, one transaction), one step "Make Type", on a shape that holds the property and none yet. Its schema is the starter (`TypeSchemaProperty.starter`, an object with no properties) unless the shape's words are a JSON object (`TypeWordsSchema.schema(fromWords:)`: nil if the words hold a mention, are empty, or trimmed are not an object; an array, a number or text get the starter; `{}` is a schema), in which case the schema is the words, trimmed of whitespace at both ends and otherwise as written, with no model call, and the sync then writes the words from the schema: `TypeSync.changed`, on a shape made a type by the person, sets the last-synced sides to the words and `{}` when the words equal the schema, so the next commit finds the schema ahead of the words and asks for the words (the `toWords` direction), and with `{}` words, which say nothing, nothing is asked. A type does not start public (it is found by `/is-a` and by its title; `/public` is the person's to say, on a type as on any shape). `editor.removeType(_:)` (`removeTypes(_:)`), one step "Remove Type", takes the schema away and leaves the words, the title and whether it's public; undo brings the schema back. Each plural form leaves out the shapes that cannot take the change and transacts nothing when none is left; a read-only shape stays in, so the core refuses it and it shakes while the rest change. Is a ▸ in a shape's menu is the other way. An agent makes a type with draw's `schema:` (an added property's attribute, as every one is): a note drawn with it, named by its title.
- A type's note is white paper (StickyNotes': `NoteStyle.typePaper`, white in the light appearance and 0.92 in the dark, the plain yellow note unchanged in both; the text's ink is the note's on both): its render and its skeleton fill it, the skeleton resolving the dynamic color once for the appearance drawn (`NoteStyle.typePaperCG`; [benchmarks](benchmarks.md)), and its cover's ink is chosen for it (`titlePillLook` with `coverSurface`). It is yellow again once the schema goes.
- A type's color (`TypeColors`, public; the table is `TypesStyle.colorTable`): one of eight by the id, never stored, so two types can share one; the index is the first eight bytes of the id's UUID, each summed with its position, modulo eight (never `hashValue`; TypesTests' `typeColorsTests` hold a table of ids that never changes, the spread, and the validator: white on each `base` at 4.5:1, each ink on its 14% tint over the light canvas (0.965) and the dark (0.11) at 4.5:1, none within 0.05 of red's hue, the eight distinct). `base` is fixed in both appearances; the ink follows the appearance. `TypeColors.solid(id)` is the pill's tint, `light(id)` the chips', `base(id)` the color alone, `gray` (0.40, `TypesStyle.grayChip`) what has no color. `/is-a`'s rows are chips (`SlashChoice.chip`, [slash commands](slash-commands.md)): `Type` and an untitled type gray, the others in their color, white on it, the `{}` first. A type's pill is Titles' (`registrar.titlePillLook(forShapesHolding: TypeSchemaProperty.self)`, [titles](titles.md)): its title after `curlybraces`, solid in its color; untitled, a dimmed gray `Untitled` placeholder (the neutral look every placeholder pill has); a note that holds no schema keeps the plain pill; Remove Type and its undo switch it.
- The file's types: `TypeIndex` (`editor.types`, an `EditorService`) reads every board's types as the file opens (the kinds that `canBeType` only, a lookup a shape), then keeps them by each commit's `ChangeSet` alone (added, removed, changed, `elsewhere` with the kind first, `replacedDocument`, and a whole file put in place, `boardDidChange`'s `isFileReplaced`): `isType(_:)`, `types`, `name(of:)` (its title), `revision(of:)` (moves when its schema or its name does) and `onChange(_:)` (the ids that came, went, or whose schema, name or own types changed, after each commit that did), `schemaText(of:)` and `chainRevision` (internal: the stamp the field book keeps its resolutions by, moved when a type comes, goes, its schema's text changes or its own `is` list does, at one integer add; not for a rename, an instance's list or another shape's text). It reads nothing per frame and nothing on a move. `referenceTargets(from:)` is a `$ref`'s completions: every other titled type, by name, its fields counted, in the order of the names. `popover(at:on:origin:)` is an editing view's way to its highlights' popovers.
- Readings: `SchemaReadings` keeps a reading per shape and text (`versionsPerShape`, 4; past 4,096 those used longest ago go; `TypesPlugin.schemasRead` counts reads in debug builds), its `shown` the last that was a JSON object while the text isn't one; `issues` are Validation's (`TypeValidationPlugin`, `validateEveryShape`): not JSON or not an object an error; `required` naming no property, a `type` that isn't JSON Schema's, and whatever else the meta-schema refuses (`MetaSchema` through the core's `SchemaValidator`, its own sentences, a `type` left to the rule of its own), warnings, 20 at most. `SchemaDocument`, `JSONReader`, `JSONCaret`, `JSONCompleter`, `JSONPointer` and `MetaSchema` are the reading, caret, completion, pointer and meta-schema machinery under it. `SchemaValue` is a schema as it was written: `JSONValue` with an object's members in the order written (a name twice: the last value at the first's place; equality is order-sensitive, as a property moved is a change to its rows), `SchemaValue(node)` from a reading, `.json` for the core's validator (the one function, where `SchemaReadings` and the chain each had a copy). `SchemaPositions` (`named`, `definitions`, `single`, `lists`) is where a schema holds a schema: what walks one (`SchemaWalk`) and what rewrites one (the chain's reading of a level) go by it.
- The panel (`TypeDetailsPlugin`): every shape that holds the property has the Schema tab (`TypeSchemaTab`), its JSON section (`TypeSchemaCode`): `SchemaCodeEditor` (public, for any schema kept as a shape's text: colored by `JSONReader.tokens`, `SchemaCodeColors`; completed by `TypeCompletions.completions(in:at:types:extra:)`, the meta-schema's keywords with no root `title`, a `required` list's names, a `$ref`'s targets, its own definitions then the file's other titled types, the root's `$title` directive ([A title from a field](#a-title-from-a-field)), a `contentMediaType`'s examples with a link to a note first (`FieldKind.pointerMediaType`, which is `MetaSchema.linkMediaType`, the constant the meta-schema is written from: with a pointer's `contentSchema` `$ref`, the file's types by name, at any depth, that is how a pointer is made in the card with no template), and what a caller adds where a value goes, `extra`), its issues a status line each, and the sync's line (`TypeSyncLine`: Retry, Open Settings…, or nothing). Writes through it are one step a run, "Change JSON". The header is the kind's (a note's title).
- StickyNotes: the note's render fills the highlights under its text; its editor (`NoteEditor`) follows the sync, read-only and faint while the model writes the words, showing what lands (`MentionTextView.show`, typing's undo dropped), ⌘↩ ending editing while read-only, and asks for the highlights' popovers as the pointer rests. Every list that installs `StickyNotesPlugin` installs `TypesPlugin` (the registry refuses an owner not installed).
- Composition: `TypesPlugin(), TypeValidationPlugin(), TypeDetailsPlugin()` right after `PublishingPlugin()`, so a note's commands list `title`, `lock`, `public`, `is-a`, then Moving's. The schema card, its tool, its `schema-link` channel and the Schemas module are gone: a board that holds a card keeps it aside ([boards on disk](boards-on-disk.md)).

## What a shape is

A shape is of the types its `is` list names; the chips above it, the menus and `/is-a` read and write that list.

- What a shape is: `is`, an added property (`IsAProperty`, key `is`, an ordered `[TypeLink]`, none twice: `uniqueItems`; empty is none, so emptying it takes the property off), on the kinds that call `typeable()` (it needs `typeWords`, `TypeWords.check`): a note and an arrow. `TypeLink` is a one-field wrapper of a `ShapeID`, `ShapeNaming`, coded by hand as the bare uuid string, `contentMediaType` `text/x-canvas-type` (`TypeLink.mediaType`, what draw and queries read the entries by): a name, not a binding, so a copy of an instance copied with its type is an instance of the copy, one copied alone stays an instance of the original, and a type that is deleted leaves its name behind (its chip dashed) until the delete is undone. `ShapeID` itself must not be `ShapeNaming` (an arrow's bindings are ids). The order is the order added: a later entry wins where two say the same (the chain reads it so: [A note that is one](#a-note-that-is-one)). `Fixtures/added/is.v0.json` is its fixture. Types declares the `is-a` edge type (`IsAEdge`, a root, "from a shape to each type it is, in the order of its list") and derives it with Topology's property rule (`registrar.edges(in: IsAProperty.self, as: IsAEdge.self)`: slot `is`, from the instance to each type, a name of a pill forwarded to what it stands for, a move reading nothing). `build.sh`'s `ORDINARY_IDS` holds `is`. A kind that is `typeable()` says where its words are, as one that `canBeType()` does: an arrow's `typeWords` places its label against the shapes around it (`typeWords(_:drawnIn:fitting:)` has an overload whose `drawnIn` is given the `ShapeContext`; the contribution's `placement` takes it).
- `/is-a` (`registerIsACommand`, every shape that holds `is`: a note, an arrow in its label; Types' one command, after `title` and the other plugins' every-shape commands): a slash command that takes one choice (`SlashCommand(_:argument:choices:step:perform:)`, [slash commands](slash-commands.md); `argument` `type`). Its rows (`TypeChoices.rows(for:in:)`, in `TypeChoices.swift`, asked once when the list opens) are `Type` (group 0, title `TypesStyle.typeChoice`, symbol `curlybraces`, step "Make Type") only while the shape can be a type (`registry.canBeType`) and is not one, never on an arrow's label or a type, then the file's types the shape may be (`TypeIndex.choices(for:)`, group 1, the same symbol, step "Add Type"): every type for which `TypeIndex.admits(_:for:)` is true (a type, never the shape itself, one it is already, one that reaches the shape (`reaches`: no cycles), a pill (a stand-in kind), a shape that isn't there), on any board, ordered by name (lowercased once and compared as plain text, the id the tie: the locale's collation cost most of opening the list), an untitled one last and called `Untitled`; the first type is `isPreferred`, so with nothing typed it is the highlight, and `Type` is when there are none. A row's id is `type` or the type's uuid (`TypeChoice`). A pick runs at once: `Type` calls `makeType`, a type `addTypeLink(_:to:)` (the type goes at the end of the list, a later line wins, one step, "Add Type", on a shape on any board; `addTypeLink(_:to: [ShapeID])` for several shapes in one step); a read-only shape is offered nothing (the state), and the core refuses and shakes the one that became so. `editor.removeTypeLink(_:from:)` is the one way an entry goes (one step, "Remove Type", the property off with the last; the plural form for several), the chips' and the panel's Remove call it, so what follows from a type being given or taken back (a note's title from a field: [A title from a field](#a-title-from-a-field)) happens inside these, in the person's own step. `@` typed on `/is-a`'s line is a letter (Mentions asks `listsChoices(at:)`).
- New type (`/is-a company`, TypesStyle `newType`, "New Type"): for what was typed, `/is-a`'s `otherwise:` offers the row `New type “Company”` (`TypeChoices.newRow(typed:owner:editor:)`, `TypeChoice.new(name)` in the row's `id`), which the list puts last, after the ranked matches, and not at all where a row is titled what was typed (`/is-a type` on a shape that has the `Type` row, `/is-a company` with a type called Company: case aside, spaces folded) or nothing is typed ([slash commands](slash-commands.md)); where nothing matches it is the only row and highlighted, with matches the best match stays the highlighted row (`/is-a co` with a Company lists Company and then `New type “Co”`, and Return picks Company). The name is the typed text folded to one line (`TitleProperty.folded`) with its first letter capitalized and the rest as typed, in the typographic quotes; none for nothing typed, a name over a title's 80 characters, the list's own word (`type`, case aside: on a shape that is a type already `/is-a type` offers nothing), no kind that can be a type, or one with no placement (`registry.placement(for:)`, read from a kind's tool). The pick is `editor.newType(named:besides:)` (public, discardable result): in `undoHistory.group("New Type")` it takes the kind (the owner's if it can be a type, else the first in registry order that can and has a placement), reads the board shown once for the landing beside the owner (`Surroundings(on:moving:in:).near(owner)`, as `/move-near` places one: right if free, then below, left, above), puts the placement down titled with the starter schema (`Placement.setting`, and `mapping` to learn the new record's id: the core's `place` is unchanged) and then links the owner (`addTypeLink`). The note is selected and edited as any placed note is, which ends the owner's editing, and the step stays open while the new note is edited, so words typed into it before that ends are in New Type and one undo takes them with the note and the link; a read-only board or a container that refuses it puts nothing down (the core refuses; nil, no link, no step, and the board's owner or container shakes), as does an owner not on the board shown. The new type is not public and its words are empty, so the sync has nothing to read until the person writes. TypesTests' `newTypeTests` (`IsALine`, Support.swift: `IsAView`, a text view that hosts slash commands over a memo, in an unshown window, so the real list is typed into and picked from) and CompositionTests' `typesShippedTests` hold it, and the gallery's `Flow2-p4-list-match-and-new-type`; `Budget.newTypePick` ([benchmarks](benchmarks.md)).
- Is a ▸ (`TypeMenus.swift`, `registerTypeMenus`: an item for the canvas, `MenuItems(in: StateMenuGroup, id: "is-a")`, so in Lock's group with no line between them, after it in plugin order): a submenu `Is a` in the menu of every shape the selection holds, made when the menu opens (`IsAMenu`). Items: `Type` (group `ShapeMenuGroup`, only when every selected shape can be a type), a line (the group `IsATypesMenuGroup`, order 1), then the types some selected shape is or may be (`TypeIndex.menuChoices(for:)`, in `choices(for:)`'s order), each titled by its name or `Untitled`. A check by an item every selected shape has (`Type`: all are types); there is no mixed state, so a type some have is unchecked and choosing it gives it to the rest. An unchecked item calls `makeTypes` or `addTypeLink(_:to:)` (steps "Make Type", "Add Type": one step for the whole selection), a checked one `removeTypes` or `removeTypeLink(_:from:)` ("Remove Type"). Never dimmed: a read-only shape is refused by the core and shakes while the rest change in the one step. The submenu is left out for a selection with a shape that holds no `is` (a frame, a subboard), and where it would hold nothing (an arrow in a file with no types). An arrow's menu has it with the file's types and no `Type`. A list that installs `TypesPlugin` installs `ContextMenusPlugin` too (the registry refuses a contribution whose owner isn't installed).
- Chips (`TypeChips`, an `Adornment` and `ClickableAdornment`, aboveTopLeft, order 100, on every kind that holds `is`; a group, tagged): one chip per entry of `is`, in order, light (Adornments' `tinted(_:)`) in the type's color (`TypeColors.light`) with its type's live title (`TypeIndex.name(of:)`; an untitled type a gray placeholder "Untitled", no color), `.missing()` (dashed, italic, gray) for a type that is gone or no longer a type, with the last title it was known by, else "Deleted"; the ones that don't fit whole fold into Adornments' "+N" (gray). A type's note shows its own pill and then the chips of the types it builds on; there is no Type chip (Is a ▸ Type in the menu makes and unmakes one). The index tells Adornments to make the chips of a type's instances again when it is renamed, deleted, brought back or no longer one. A click (`TypeChipMenus.menu(for:in:)`, popped up below the chip's rect with `editor.popUpMenu`): on a type's chip `Go to “Person”` (`editor.goTo`, left out for a type that isn't in the file) and `Remove “Person”` (`removeTypeLink`, left out while the shape is read-only); on "+N", those for every type, a separator between types. Nothing is shown on a shape drawn as a skeleton. On an arrow the row sits above its label (Adornments' anchor).
- The panel's Types tab (`TypesTab`, order 50, a section "types" over `IsAProperty`, `enabledWhileReadOnly`): a row per type in order, a click on it goes to the type (`editor.goTo`, on the scheduler), a minus removes it (`removeTypeLink`; off while the shape is read-only), the row dimmed with the last title once the type is gone. A type that has supertypes has the Schema tab and this.
- Duplicating a typed shape (⌘D, a ⌥-drag, copy and paste, cut and paste: [clipboard](clipboard.md)) keeps `is`; copied with its type, the copy's names are renamed among the copies by the paste's one map (`TypeLink` is `ShapeNaming`), so the copies are the copy's instances and the original keeps its own; pasted into another file the names point at shapes it lacks (dashed chips).
- What is what: `TypeIndex` (`@Observable`, `editor.types`) also keeps `links(of:)` (a shape's types, in the order of its list; a name of a shape that is gone included), `instances(of:)` (the shapes whose list names it, directly), `lineage(of:)` (what a shape's types lead to, in the one order they are read in, `TypeOrder.linearized`: a type before every type it builds on, and where a list leaves a choice the later added first, which is C3, Python's method resolution order; where C3 has no order, as when a note was given Employee and then Person, a depth-first walk breaks the ties and the rule still holds, so Person never beats Employee; each once, never the shape itself, a cycle stopping where it comes back; a list is read latest first, `TypeGraph.parents(of:)`), `TypeGraph.reaches(_:from:)` (reachability alone, no order: what `admits` and the query ask), `affected(by:)` (the instances of the shapes, near and far, each once: what a change to a type changes) and `name(of: TypeLink)` (`TypeLinkName`: `.live(title)` while the shape is a type, else `.missing(lastKnown:)`, the last title it was known by in this session; observed per type). Kept by each commit's `ChangeSet` as the types are (a shape that only moved reads nothing: `recordsRead` in debug builds), read again for a file put in place and for a commit heard out of turn (`heardRevision`, as Titles' index does; `boardDidChange` always catches up), and it tells Adornments (`editor.adornmentsDidChange(for:)`) to make the chips of the instances of every type again whose name or being a type changed (renamed, deleted, brought back, no longer one).

## A note that is one

A shape of a type holds `fieldValues`, and what its types give it is the chain of them: which fields, in which order, from which type, and what each field's kind is. The vocabulary under the rows, the card, the panel's Fields and the check is in `Sources/Types/Fields` (and the check's answer in `Checks`), so they cannot disagree. All pure but the services; each file is small.

- What a shape holds for its types' fields: `fieldValues`, an added property (`FieldValuesProperty`, a `FieldValues`: one JSON object over `JSONObject`, coded as the bare object) on the kinds that call `typeable()` (it opts in beside `is`; `isTypeable` still means "holds `is`"). A value that is empty is not kept, at any depth (`FieldValues.pruned`, the one rule: null, text with no characters, a list left with no items and a group left with no members once their own empties are dropped; `false`, `0` and text of spaces are values), and every way in prunes (`init(_:)`, `init(from:)`, the subscript; `values` is `private(set)`), so `isNone` is `isEmpty` and a board read, a draw or a set that is only empties holds none. Written by people and agents, never `readOnly`; `canvas draw`'s `fieldValues: {…}` is joined with what the shape holds, member by member at every depth (a list replaced whole, `null` clearing the member it is written at; [Agents](#agents)), an edge takes it; a query reads a path into it (`[fieldValues.status="paid"]`). Nothing reads a type for it: it knows no field, so a value no type names is kept. A pointer in it names its shape ([Pointers](#pointers)). `Fixtures/added/fieldValues.v0.json` is its fixture. Beware `JSONValue` is `ExpressibleByNilLiteral`: a `nil` beside a `JSONValue` in a ternary is `.null`.
- The chain (`Chain.swift`, pure: `Chain.resolve(types:in:)` over a `ChainCatalog` of `ChainNode`s, so one answer serves a shape, every shape with the same types, and a test with no editor): what an instance's types give it, as a `Resolved`. `order` is `TypeOrder.linearized` of the instance's list read latest first, less the shapes the catalog has nothing for (a gone name, or a shape that is no longer a type, ends its branch: its notes keep their words and show a dashed chip, while `:is-a` still follows its list). A field's winner is the first type in `order` to name it, and its whole `ChainField` is used (nothing merged, `required` with it, which is the root's own; a `required` name with no property names no field). `fields` is in row order: `order` reversed, each type's fields in the order written, a name taking the place it first appears at, so a field a more specific type restates keeps the more general type's place with the restater's definition (`source` the winner, `introducedBy` the type whose block the row sits in). A type cannot take a field away. `rules` are every type's root keywords across fields (`allOf anyOf oneOf not if then else dependentRequired dependentSchemas`, none overridden, in row order of their types), `directives` the root's `$title`-like properties, raw, one per name, the first in `order` (so an Employee gets Person's `$title`; `TitleSource` reads `title`, and `SchemaDirectives` is the one place a root `properties` key is told a field from a directive). The root's `patternProperties`, `$ref`, `additionalProperties`, `unevaluatedProperties`, `propertyNames`, `minProperties` and `maxProperties` are none of these: they speak of the whole object, which holds every type's fields and values no field names, and those are never wrong. `schema` is the as-written composition (`properties`, the winners' `required`, `allOf` of the rules; what is empty is left out) for the check. A level is read from a type's JSON (`ChainLevel(schema:)`) with every `#` reference written out at the reading (`SchemaInliner`, over `SchemaPositions` alone: `enum`, `const`, `default`, `examples`, `contentSchema` and what is no schema are copied as written; siblings win over the target, the target's order kept; a reference to nowhere is dropped; one that leads back into itself or past six deep is dropped and the field's `holdsItself` set; `$defs`, `definitions`, `$schema`, `$id` and `$anchor` are left out of what is written). A `urn:uuid:` `$ref` (another type, bare or into a part of it) is kept as written: a bare one is "another type's fields", one with a fragment gives no rows (rows read it as Other), and the check answers both with the type's own composed schema. `Resolved.Field.field.schema` is a `SchemaValue`, so a group's rows and a type's card have their order.
- The field book (`FieldBook`, `editor.fieldBook`, an `EditorService` registered right after the index; it reads nothing as it is made, so `typeIndex10k` does not see it): `fields(types:)` (and `fields(of:)`, a record's `is` list) is the chain's `Resolved` for a list, resolved once for each distinct list as the shape holds it (the same types in another order are another list: the order decides who wins) and kept while `revision` (the index's `chainRevision`) stands. A list is resolved when first asked, never eagerly, so a type's edit costs the lists asked afterwards, not all: each valid keystroke in a type's Schema tab drops every kept list (held by `Budget.fieldListsResolved`'s second figure, [benchmarks](benchmarks.md)). A type's own note asks `fields(types: [itsID])`, the list an instance of it asks too. `members(of:)` gives the fields a group holds in order (its `properties`, its own `required`) or, for a field that is a bare `urn:uuid:` `$ref` to a type, that type's fields with their own sources, inherited first (empty for a type that is gone, a part of one, a pointer, anything else; a `$ref` with `properties` beside it shows the type's only), and `item(of:)` the field for a list's `items`, named "" (nil for none and for tuples); both are given a `Resolved.Field`, so a reader that unwraps a schema (a "may be null" dropped) can ask of what it built. `ChainBook` (internal) is the catalog under it: each type's level read once per text through `SchemaReadings`' `shownRoot`, so a type whose JSON is broken mid-edit keeps the rows of its last good JSON (this session's: a file opened with a broken schema has none until it's mended, and undo back to a good text is that text's rows) and a type whose text was never an object has none; `prune()` forgets the levels of shapes no longer types. `resolutionsMade` (debug builds) counts resolutions, as `TypesPlugin.schemasRead` counts reads. It keeps nothing per shape or per board, so it hears a board switch with no change set (`boardDidChange` empty; the default would work one out, which BoardsTests refuses of every shipped service) and follows a file put in place through the index's stamp. A type that is gone, its name and its color are the index's, not the book's. Nothing here draws; the check is [Checks](#checks).
- `FieldPath` (`steps`: `.key` by the field's key, never its title, `.index` by the *stored* index; the list is dense, empties are pruned, so a card maps its display line to the stored index by counting the non-blank lines before it): `field`, `parent`, `isPrefix(of:)` (equal counts), `isRoot` (a problem across fields), `pointer` (JSON Pointer's escapes), and `init(violation:)` from a `SchemaViolation`'s path (the root is `FieldPath()`; `FieldPath([])` is ambiguous). `FieldName`: `words(of:)` is the field's own `title` trimmed of surrounding spaces, else its key in lower-case words (`lineItems`, `line_items`, `postalURL` is `postal url`), `stepName(of:)` the undo step of every write of that field, whatever the surface: `Change Line Items`. `FieldWords`: every string a row, card or panel says (`add gift note`, `No fields`, `+12 more`, `from Person`, `from 2 types`, `4 fields`, `2 items`, `edited as JSON in the panel`), beside `FieldKind.typeLabel`'s.
- `FieldKind` (`FieldBook.kind(of:)`, a pure function of the field's schema: it reads no other type, so a gone type still reads as `typeFields(target)`; a reader that wants it often keeps it): the first that fits, after a "may be null" is dropped: `holdsItself` is `other`; a pointer (`type: string`, `contentMediaType: "text/x-canvas-link"`, `contentSchema.$ref` a bare `urn:uuid:`; an array of them is `pointers`); a bare `urn:uuid:` `$ref` is `typeFields` (a fragment is `other`); `enum` of scalars or a `const` is a choice (a non-scalar one is `other`); an array (`choices` for `uniqueItems` over an `enum`, `pointers`, `listOfTypeFields`, `listOfGroups`, `list(of:)`, else `other`: no `items`, tuples, `prefixItems`, a list of lists); an object (`group` for `properties`, then `other` for `patternProperties`, `allOf`, `not`, then `pairs` for a plain `additionalProperties`); `boolean`, `integer`/`number`, a `date` format as `date`, a `date-time` one as `dateTime`, any other string as `text`; two branches (a `type` list, `anyOf`, `oneOf`) each a kind but `other` as `either`, three or more `other`; anything else `other`. `hasMore` is whether a row on a note ends in `›` and opens a card (everything but `plain`, `pointer` and an either of those); `opensOnType` is the type's note's own column (`Choices`, `List of Text` and the links have no `›` there). `typeLabel(memberCount:)` is the kind's words on a type (`Either` past 22 characters or with a branch that is not plain). `field(at:types:)` names the field of a value path (`members(of:)`, `item(of:)`, `pairValue(of:)`, an either's first branch that has the step), `branches(of:)` an either's.
- `FieldValues.value(at:)` and `setting(_:at:)`: the pruning rule at any depth (setting empty takes the element out and the rest shift up; an index at or past the count appends; a parent that holds the other shape of value is replaced, but removing from under one changes nothing, and a path that starts with an index changes nothing). `keys(unnamedBy:)` is the members no field names, the panel's dimmed values.
- `FieldText`: what typed text is stored as, and how a value shows; every function takes a defaulted `locale:`. A number is stored as one only when it reads back as typed (`9.50` is 9.5, `12.00` is 12; `02134`, `1e3`, `1.`, `-`, a 17-digit id are text); a date is a full date however it is written (`2026-03-03`, `Mar 3, 2026`, `3 Mar 2026`, `3/3/26` in the locale's order), stored `2026-03-03`, and in a `dateTime` field (`format: date-time`) as UTC midnight, `2026-03-03T00:00:00Z`, which a date-time format check accepts; both forms show as the day; a part of a date stays text; nothing is refused. The locale's formatters are made once per locale and kept (a `DateFormatter` costs far more to make than to use), since rows draw dates every frame in view. `shown` of a value of another JSON type shows it as its own type would (the check is what calls it wrong). `value(from:asOneOf:)` and `shown(_:asOneOf:)` are for a field that takes either of two plain kinds.
- `FieldSummary.of(_:field:in:types:)`: what a row with more in it reads (a group leaves out a member edited as JSON: a sentence is not a value) (`empty`, `value`, `items`, `count`, `pair`, `json`, and `pills`, the names a list of pointers shows now, for a group's summary and Find: its row draws pills from the values), `text` the same as one string. A pointer reads `.value` of the name it shows (`FieldBook.shown`), a token's live name, else the text as stored. `FieldCheck`, `FieldProblem` and `FieldClass`: the check's answer as a value (`mark(at:)` the worst at or under a path, `problems(at:)` exactly there, `leaving(out:)`). `RowList` (`init(_:own:)`, `count(of:own:)`): nine fields or fewer all show; past nine, two rows or more inherited (introduced by a type not on the shape's own list) fold into one `from Person` row in the place of the first, and of what is left nine or fewer show, else eight and `+N more`: a fold shows only when it hides two rows or more, so a note has at most ten rows.
- The services that keep what is written, checked and opened (`FieldWriter`, `FieldChecks`, `FieldCards`; the writer and the cards keep nothing per board, so their `boardDidChange` is empty: the default works out a switch's change set, which BoardsTests refuses of every shipped service) are registered after `TypeSync`, each reached through an accessor that is nil without `TypesPlugin`: `editor.types`, `fieldBook`, `typeSync`, `fieldWriter`, `fieldChecks`, `fieldCards`, `rowBook` and `fileFields`. `FieldWriter` is [the one writer](#the-one-writer), `FieldChecks` the check ([Checks](#checks)), `FieldCards` and `FieldForm` the card and the panel's form ([Cards and the form](#cards-and-the-form)). `FieldCardRequest` is the card's whole request, a public init with `folded` and `rowRectNow`.

## Rows on a type's note

A type's note has, under its words, a hairline and one row for each field its chain gives it (`fields(types: [id])`, so an Employee with none of its own has Person's); a note that is one has its fields there with its values ([next](#rows-on-a-note-that-is-one)). Types owns it, a kind opts in, and the core learns nothing.

- A kind says where its rows go: `registrar.typeRows(drawnIn: { shape, context in … })` (`TypeRowsPlaces`, a `KeyedExtensionPoint`, one contribution a kind, `Registry.typeRows(of:)`; `check` refuses a kind that holds neither `schema` nor `is`) and its render calls `context.drawTypeRows(of:in:)` (StickyNotes: `NoteLayout.block`, after the highlights, before the editing guard, so the rows draw under the editor too). Only such a kind is given `TypeRows.extent`: an arrow says nowhere and has no rows.
- `RowList` is the fold, pure. `RowBook` (`editor.rowBook`, a service registered last in `TypesPlugin`, made with the others, reading no shape as it is made) keeps a template for each distinct (list of types, isType): the `RowList`, and for each row its name (`FieldName.words`), `FieldKind`, `typeLabel` and chip, whether it is needed, its tick and its measured name, and the name column's width; stamped with the index's `chainRevision` and dropped whole when it moves (a rename moves no size: only a template that says a type's name, `from Person`, is made again, by a rename count). `templatesMade` and `rowsShown` count in debug builds. `rows(of:)` is the rows as drawn with their rects (world units; tests, the gallery), `rowsRect(of:)` the kind's rect, `spot(of:)` what a type's note says where its rows go.
- The spot (`RowSpot`), in order, for a shape that holds `schema`: its template has rows: `.rows`; the sync's status is `.updating(.schema)`: `.reading` (three bars); `.failed`: the reason with `.retry`, `.settings` (a key missing or refused) or no action (`TypeSyncFailure.Remedy.editByHand`: too long, declined, no model); its words are empty: nothing (blank, 200 wide, no hairline); an edit is waiting and no read has answered (`isWaiting`, `hasAnswered`): nothing, so `No fields` never flashes under the first words typed; else `.noFields`. Rows win over a read in progress: a type with rows read again keeps them, the last good ones, and no bars show. A read that failed is said under them, as the plan has it ("the reason and Retry when it failed", "a type too long for the model to write says so in the rows' spot, as any failure does"; too long only ever happens to a type that has rows): `RowBook.foot(of:)` is a `.failed` for a type with rows whose sync failed to write the schema from its words (`TypeSyncFailure.writing == .schema`: no key, a refusal, an error, too long), drawn as one row more at the foot of the rows in the empty spot's own look (`FailureLayout`: the reason, then `Retry` or `Settings…`, none for too long), and nil for a failure to write the words, under which the rows say what the schema says (the panel has that one). A frame asks it of every type's note in view, full or skeleton, so it reads `TypeSync.failure(of:)`, which does not observe (`status(of:)` makes a key path a call: 0.5 ms on `Budget.typeRowBars`' 350 skeletons, which put it over) and is one compare while nothing is syncing or failed.
- The extent is `RowsExtent(minimumWidth: TypesStyle.rowsMinimumWidth (320), height: (hasWords ? rowsLead : 0) + rows × rowHeight (22))`, rows 3 for reading, 1 for `No fields` and a reason, a type's rows and one more while a failed read is said at their foot (so the laid-out note is tall enough for it; mid-transaction too, since the foot is the sync's, by the shape's id); none for a shape that is no type and has no types with fields. The provider (`RowBook.provide`) tests the cheapest first (no `schema` and no `is`: nil, two added-value lookups, which is every plain note), then the book's kinds; answered from the committed index when `reading.isCommitted`, else (a draw, a paste, a duplicate, `/is-a` itself, a type and its instance written together) resolved over `reading.shape(inFile:)` with no template and no text measured: a type's level is the field book's own (`FieldBook.level(of:)`) while the text is the committed one, so a type whose JSON is broken mid-edit gives a pasted instance the rows of its last good JSON, else read from the text once, by (id, text).
- Announcements: `RowBook` is told by `TypeIndex.onChange` and `TypeSync.onSpotChange`. A type's change that moves `chainRevision` drops the templates and calls `editor.shapesDidChangeLayout` once for the changed types, their instances near and far (`affected(by:)`) and the instances the commit itself added or changed (the index has filed them before it tells): a service ahead of Types may have asked for their frames against the index of the file before. A rename redraws. A status or wait changing lays a type's note out again with no commit (`spotChanged`): one with no rows has another spot; one with rows only when the failure at their foot came, went or reads otherwise (`feet`, the failure each was last laid out for), so a wait or a call announces nothing for it. An edit to a note's own record needs none: the core lets its size go. A file put in place drops the tables.
- The sync reports for the spot (`onSpotChange`, `isWaiting`, `hasAnswered`: [The model](#the-model)); a shape the person makes a type shows the bars at once, with no 3 s wait.
- Drawing (`RowsDrawing.swift`): clipped to the block widened at the left by the tick's room (`tickInset` + `tickWidth`, in the paper's padding): the hairline (black at 12%), then the spot (bars at 10%, one row of text) or the rows: tick, name (the note's ink, 45% for an inherited row, 55% on a note that is one), the amber dot 5 after it, and from the right `›`, the chip, the label (an inherited row is faint all through: its label and `›` at the name's 45%, its chip drawn at that alpha). Text is Core Text lines kept by (text, style, width cut to) in `FieldLines` (8,192, then the lot is let go), fixed colors since the paper is light in both appearances; the chip is Adornments' (`AdornmentLabel.draw(at:in:)`, drawn as in the light appearance), built with `TypeChips.label` so untitled, gone and renamed types read as above a note. `RowGeometry` is the one place that says where a row's parts are (the name column is the longest name, at most `nameShare` (45%) of the rows' width, the same on a type's note and on a note that is one). Constants are `TypesStyle`'s (`rowsMinimumWidth`, `rowHeight`, `rowsLead`, `nameGap`, `nameShare`, `tickWidth`, `tickInset`, …).
- Clicks (`RowFaces.swift`, one `shapeFaces` Types registers; `RowFace { shape, row, part, at }`): nothing in a type's rows is edited. A row whose kind `opensOnType` (`›`) and a fold ask `editor.rowBook?.openCard` for a read-only card (a `FieldCardRequest`: `mode: .kinds`, `isReadOnly`, the row's rect, a fold's `folded` rows, `rowRectNow`, which finds the row again by its field or by which fold it is and answers nil once it is gone); the book does the asking for every door (`opened`, debug builds, is what it asked), and `FieldCards.open` is the card's: a click or Return on a `›` row, on the fold, on a type's note and on a locked note opens the real card, read-only on the last two. A click on the value of a `Choice` or `Choices` row pops `RowMenus.options` (the options in the schema's order, every item disabled) under it; `Retry` is `typeSync.retry`, or `refuseChanges` (the shake) on a read-only type; `Settings…` is `AnthropicSettingsView.showSettings()`, locked or not. Anywhere else a press is the note's. A face is asked on every move over a shape and costs the provider's lookups (a note that is one has the values' faces, [Typing in a row](#typing-in-a-row); never for the shape being edited or a skeleton). `doubleClick` runs the same for `›` and options, so the second press of a fast pair does not begin an edit, and does nothing for Retry and Settings…. `FailureLayout` is where the reason and its action's words go, for the render and for a click, in the spot's only row or in the row past a type's rows (the foot); `RowBook.actionRect(of:)` is the words' rect for tests and the gallery, in either.
- While the words are edited (`RowsView`, `Sources/Types/Rows/RowsView.swift`): the note's editing view covers the paper, so a press over the rows would put the caret at the end of the words. A kind's editing view lays a `RowsView` over its rows (`RowBook.makeRowsView(for:host:)`, a `RowsHost` of the editing frame's origin, `end`, `keepInView`, `focusWords`; StickyNotes' `NoteEditor` adds it above its text view). It draws nothing but the focus outline (the render under it draws the rows), shows the arrow, does what a click on the canvas does for the part under the press, and nothing elsewhere in the rows: a press there moves no caret. On a note that is one it is the rows editor ([Typing in a row](#typing-in-a-row)). The book keeps one weak (the session's) and calls `reposition()` after each commit and each layout change it announces; the note's editor does after a size change and a keystroke.
- Zoomed out (`fillTypeRowBars`, `showsTypeRowBars`, `RowBook.bars`): a kind's skeleton asks `context.showsTypeRowBars` (zoom at least `skeletonRowsZoom`, 14%, where a row is three pixels tall) before it works out where its rows are, then fills one bar a row (`skeletonRowBarHeight` 8, as wide as the name's columns by `SkeletonText.columns`, `FieldRowTemplate.barWidth`, never the measured width: no skeleton shows a letter, and SkeletonsShippedTests holds two boards that differ only in letters to one picture), a fold as three dots, the reading state as its three bars, `No fields` and a reason as one bar (`spotBarShares[0]` of the block; a reason at the foot of a type's rows is that bar under theirs), through `fillSkeletonText`. The cover title of a titled note is drawn by Titles over the bars, as it is over any skeleton.

## Rows on a note that is one

The same rows, with the note's values at the right (`RowShown.swift`, `RowsValuesDrawing.swift`): the name muted at the left, the value left-aligned in the column after it (`›` follows a summary), as the gallery draws them.

- `RowBook.rows(of:)` for a note that is one pairs its template with its `fieldValues` (`ShownRows`, kept by shape, 2,048 at most then the lot is let go; rects from the block's top-left so a drag lets go of nothing and `rows(of:)` after it adds the block's origin; let go of for the shapes a commit added, removed or changed other than by moving, and those of `changes.elsewhere`, whole with the templates, and when the width, the lead or the rename count it was built for moved; `rowsShown` counts the builds). Each row's `content`, by the field's kind: a plain value `.text` (as `FieldText.shown`/`FieldSummary` say it, kept to `longestShown` characters; the cut to the column is the drawer's), a yes or no `.box` (unticked for none and for `false`), a choice `.choice(label)` (`nil` for none, a value no option names as itself), either of two plain kinds `.text`, a pointer `.links([ShownLink], more:)` (each element that is a token a pill, `ShownLink { link, text, x, width, isLive }` from the value column's left, `text` the token as mention text, read once with the row so a frame neither writes nor reads a token, the width of Mentions' pill on `MentionSurface.outlined` (cut to the column), `TypesStyle.linkGap` (6) apart, as many as fit with room for `+N`, which counts the rest and every element that is no token; a string that is no token is `.text` of itself, red by the check; with the pointer's type gone it is `.gone(type)`), anything with more in it `.summary` (a list or choices cut to the column with the rest counted, `gift, mugs +2`, by measuring; the rest as `FieldSummary.text`), `.empty("add gift note")` for none (no `›` while empty), `edited as JSON in the panel` for what no control suits (empty or not), and `.gone(type)` for another type's fields, or a list of them, whose type is gone (its dashed chip, the value kept and not checked). A default fills nothing.
- Marks are not kept: each draw reads `check(of:)` once a note (`FieldChecks.check`, or `RowBook.checks` in debug builds, which tests set to draw marks before and beside the check, with `typing`'s path taken out of its answer as the real one does) and `mark(at:)` once a row (a fold: the worst of its folded fields). `RowLook.of(content:mark:)` is the whole of what a mark does to a row: `.wrong` is the red ink with a wavy line (1 point, a 4-point wave, 1.25 high, under the text), `.needed` on an empty row the same words in amber, `.neededInside` a summary or fold in amber, a box is never needed, a gone type is never marked. The text inks are the dot's amber and the wrong red darkened until each reads at 4.5 to 1 on a note's yellow and on white (`neededTextInk`, `wrongInk`; the dot stays `neededInk`). The value of the row `FieldChecks.typing` names for this shape is not drawn (the row's own field draws it).
- The outline of the row whose card is open (`FieldCards.openTarget`, a fold by `openFolded`) is drawn while the note is not edited: 1.5 points in the accent, round the value as it hugs it (`hug(_:)`, at least `fieldFocusMinimum`). A row skips drawing its value only while its own field has the caret (`typing` a path of one step); a caret in a cell of the card it opened is a deeper path, and the row's summary stays drawn behind the card.
- Zoomed out a filled row has a second bar, `skeletonValueShare` of the value column (a box's width for a box), never as wide as what it says; none for an empty one, red (`skeletonWrong`) for a wrong one; a fold's dots are red when it carries a wrong one.
- **⌘F finds a value** (`FieldFind.swift`, `FieldFindPlugin` after Find and Types; Types depends on Find): a note that is one gives Find a text for each field with a value, as its row shows it, a row with more in it every text under it; a hit marks the row's value (the fold for a folded field; a pointer by the name it shows now, `FieldFind.lines` through `FieldBook.shown`, and `reached(by:)` adds the link carriers, so renaming a Person reads again the notes that point at her). `RowBook.reached(by:)` is its `affected` and has the index hear the commit early; the rules for it are in [find](find.md).

## Typing in a row

A note that is one has two places for the caret in its one editing session: the words, and a row. Types' `RowsView` (`makeRowsView`, which StickyNotes' `NoteEditor` adds over the rows) is the rows editor; Types imports TextEditing for it.

- **A typed value** (text, a number, a date, either of two such kinds) is edited by a `RowField` (`RowField.swift`): an `EditingTextView` on TextKit 2 with a container that never wraps, no autocorrect, smart quotes or dashes, replacement or spelling marks, no mentions and no slash commands (`@` and `/` are letters), laid over the value in a clip view as wide as the text hugs it (`fieldFocusMinimum` to the value's column, 4 points out each side) and slid so the caret stays in it. The view draws the outline (`fieldFocusWidth`, the accent). Its text is a text value as it is, a number or a date as the row shows it (`RowsView.text(of:kinds:)`); while it has the caret it holds what was typed (`9.50`), and the row shows the stored form (`9.5`) once it leaves. A value written from outside (an agent's draw, an undo) while the caret is there is shown in the field (the view remembers what it wrote and compares it to the stored value at each commit), and drops the field's own undo.
- **A line break** is never in the field's text: where a value has one (`\n`, `\r\n`, `\r`, `\u{2028}`, `\u{2029}`) the field has one space that carries `RowField.lineBreak`, the break it stands for; `RowField.value` puts each back. Three rules keep it apart: `typingAttributes` always answers the row's plain attributes (nothing typed beside a stand-in takes the attribute), every real break in the storage (only a paste or a drop brings one) is swapped for a stand-in after each change, and the field's undo carries attributes (it is plain text, and still does). A stand-in is deleted, selected and stepped over as the one space it shows as.
- **Each keystroke is a commit** through the one writer (`RowBook.write`: `FieldWriter.setTyped`, or `set` of `FieldText.value(from:asOneOf:)` for an either), so typing in one field is one undo step named for it (`Change Email`), another field another; the writer's run key does it. A row's commit ends the words' `Typing` step and the next word starts another (`undo.md`): accepted. Nothing is refused, clamped or changed (`FieldText`).
- **The check is told** which field is typed in: a field's becoming first responder sets `editor.fieldChecks?.typing = (shape, path)`; its resigning clears it, and only if `typing` still names that shape and path (the panel's boxes set it too, and only whoever sets it clears it), so does the view's going out of its window. A box, a choice and a summary never set it. The render skips the value of the typed row (the field draws it); `RowBook.check(of:)` takes the typed path out of an overridden answer as `FieldChecks.check` does of its own, so nothing under the caret is red.
- **Where a session starts**: `NoteEditor` takes `EditContext.startPoint` into the rows view (`begin(at:clickCount:)`): a press on a typed value gives the field the keyboard with the caret at the character pressed (a double-click, the word), one on a row with more in it focuses the row and asks for its card on the scheduler's next turn (only if the session is still this one), anywhere else (a name, the paper, a box, a choice) the words take it. `EditingView.firstResponder` is that responder. A click on the canvas that begins it is the faces' (`RowFace`, below).
- **Keys** (the table is in `RowsView.swift`): Tab and ⇧Tab, ↓ and ↑ walk every row, typed or not, folds included (Tab onto a row with more only focuses it); off the last row Tab ends editing, ⇧Tab and ↑ from the first go back to the words, ↓ on the last does nothing; ↓ from the words' last line (the caret first moves as it always has: if it stayed on its line there was no line below) focuses the first row; Return and Escape end editing, ⌘↩ too, but Return and Space on a row with more ask for its card (`openCard`, whose `closed` focuses the row again and whose `tabbedOut` goes on); Space toggles a focused box and pops a focused choice's menu (`RowMenus.choice`: the options in the schema's order, a check by the stored one, a separator, `None` last). A row that is not typed is focused with the view itself as first responder, outlined (a choice with `▾` after its value), and **swallows every key it has no use for**: up the responder chain are the canvas's shortcuts, where Backspace deletes the selection, which is the note being edited (`noteRowEditingTests`).
- **While the note is edited** a press in the rows is the rows': on a typed value it moves the caret there, on a box it toggles it and focuses the row, on a choice it focuses the row and pops its menu, on a row with more it focuses the row and asks for its card; on a name, the lead or the paper nothing, and the caret stays where it was. The pointer is the I-beam over a typed value and the arrow over the rest.
- **If the rows change under a focused row** the focus stays with the row of the same field (a fold by which fold it is); if that row is gone or folded the caret goes back to the words. A note that becomes read-only ends the session (the core's) and the view goes with it.
- **A click on a value acts at once** (`RowFaces.swift`: `RowFace.Part.value`, `.name`; `valuePart(at:on:)` finds them from the shape's kept rows alone, so a hover builds nothing it does not find kept). It selects the note alone and then, by the row's `RowClass`: a typed value, a row with more in it and the fold begin the note's session (`editor.beginEditing(shape, at: face.at)`, so `NoteEditor` starts it in that row as above); a box writes the other of ticked and not (`RowBook.toggle`); a choice pops `RowMenus.choice` below its value; a row that reads `edited as JSON in the panel` asks `openCard` and begins nothing. A click on a field's name, or anywhere in a row off its value, only selects the note, also when it is the only one selected (where a click elsewhere on it edits its words); its double-click is `editor.open(…, clickCount: 2)`, which edits the words. A box, a gone type's chip and a link row's value are value faces only on the thing itself, and a gone type's chip and a list of links (`.still`) have no value face (a click there is the note's); a link row's value (`.link`) begins the note's session in the row and opens its list. Their names, like every name, are `.name` faces, so a click on one only selects, also on the only selected note (a plain click on the note would edit its words). On a read-only note a typed value or a box or a choice shakes it and writes nothing (`beginEditing` and the writer refuse, `refuseChanges` for the menu), but a row with more opens its card to be read (`isReadOnly: true`, `closed` and `tabbedOut` nothing) and does not shake: reading is no try. `doubleClick` on a value is the click again (two clicks on a box are two writes), so the second press of a fast pair begins no edit by accident. The cursor over a value stays the arrow on the canvas; it is the I-beam only once the row is edited.

## Cards and the form

A row with more in it opens a card, the form it holds is values first, and the panel holds the same form.

- The form a card and the panel hold is values first: `FieldSheets.sheet(path:field:kind:values:check:book:types:mode:isReadOnly:presentation:drafts:open:back:branch:locale:)` and `foldSheet(_:…)` (`FieldSheet.swift`, pure; the view is `FieldFormView`'s) answer a `FieldSheet`, one level: `layout` (`rows`, `column`, `table(names)`, `pairs`, `checks`), `back` (`‹ shipping address`), the type's `chip`, an either's `branches` and the `branch` picked (the one the value fits, the first with none; `branch:` picks the other and its control is empty, even when the stored value is another shape: an object stored and Text picked shows nothing, not the object as JSON), `lines` and `stops` (reading order: the branch picked as a stop at line -1, then each line's cells that take the keyboard, or the line as a whole, column -1, for a check line, a triangle and a row with more that is its line's only cell; words alone, a pointer's or a kind's on a type's note, are none). A `FieldLine` has its `name`, the amber dot of a needed member on a type's note (`isNeeded`), a member's `chip`, its `cells`, the panel's `depth` and `disclosure` (nil none, false closed, true open) and, for a line of a list, a table or pairs, `list` (its path), `slot` (its place in the `FieldDraft`) and `isPair` (the list is pairs, as drawn: the form's draft for it is made the same way, which an either of pairs and text with nothing stored cannot say by its kind). A `FieldCell` has its `path`, `field`, `control` (`text(PlainKind)`, `oneOf` for either of two plain kinds, `yesNo`, `choice`, `more(FieldSummary)`, `check(Bool)` and `clear` for several choices and their `None`, `label`, `link(type)` for a pointer, `json` for the panel's box), the stored `value`, `shown` (`FieldText.shown`, or a summary's text), `placeholder` (`add city`; `add` on the waiting line, and on an empty needed cell of a table), `mark` (`FieldCheck.mark(at:)`, none for a line that is not stored), `isStored`, `isEditable` and `alignsRight` (a number in a table). A list is dense, so the lines of a list, a table and pairs come from a `FieldDraft` (`slots`: `stored(key:)` for an item or a pair by its key, `blank` for the waiting line and a line whose text was deleted, `pair(name:value:taken:)` for half a pair or a name another has), which the form keeps for each level by its path: `storedIndex(ofLine:)` is the stored slots before it, so a line that is not stored has the path of the item it would be, `reconcile(with:)` follows what something else wrote (a list whose count differs is all stored again with one blank waiting; pairs lose a gone key's line and gain a new key's as the last stored line, before the waiting one; a half-typed pair stays), pairs show in name order as made and `init(_:waiting:pairs:)` has no blank when read-only (an empty read-only level reads `None`). On a type's note (`mode: .kinds`) a level's lines are its members' names and kinds (`typeLabel`), a `›` (`more`) only where `opensOnType`; a list of groups or of a type's fields shows its item's members as rows, pairs two rows (`name`, `value`). In the panel (`presentation: .panel`) a field is a line with a triangle and its summary; open (`open[path]`, else open while a problem is at or under it) the next level is under it a depth down, a member with more has its own triangle, a list of groups is a line an item (summary and triangle, opening to its rows) and the `add` line, an either shows the branch its value fits, `Other` is the JSON box. `FieldSheets.draft(at:kind:values:isReadOnly:drawnAsPairs:)` is the default draft. A pointer is a `link(type)` cell (its stored token, `shown` the name it shows now or the text as stored for a value that is no token, `add customer` when empty, editable while its type is a type: a gone type's cell is the type's chip and takes nothing, a gone type's list of pointers `label` words); a list of pointers (its type a type) is the column of a list of plain values with `link` cells, a line a pointer and a waiting line (`linkColumnLines`), a triangle in the panel and a `more(.pills)` cell as a member of a group, whose level is that column; a `link` cell is a stop only while editable.
- `FieldFormView` (`FieldFormView.swift`, `FieldFormKeys.swift`, `FieldFormWrites.swift`, `FieldFormLayout.swift`, `FieldGridView.swift`, `FieldCellEditor.swift`, `FieldFormStyle.swift` the numbers, `FieldFormWords.swift` the words) is the form drawn and keyed: AppKit, one `NSView`, so the card hands it to the core directly (nothing the canvas hosts is SwiftUI) and the panel's `FieldForm` is a thin face of it. It draws a `FieldSheet`: a header that stays put (the line back, the type's chip, an either's branch names, a table's names), under it the lines in a scroll view that draws only those in view with string drawing (a wrong cell red and wavy, a needed empty one amber, an items summary cut to what fits and ended `+N`, a number set right in a table); it owns the one text editor (`FieldCellEditor`, an `EditingTextView` of one line in a clip view of the cell's size, so a long value scrolls sideways under the caret, with every substitution off, no spelling and no list; a pasted line break is kept and the cell shows its first line), which it moves over the focused text cell and forgets the undo of each time it moves (`undoManager?.removeAllActions()`, since the window clears a text's undo only when the keyboard changes hands). `focus` is a `FieldStop`: a text cell has the caret (the editor first responder, its text selected), any other stop the form itself, and `Tab`, ⇧Tab, Return, Space, Esc, Up, Down, Left and Right on the branch names and Backspace on an empty line are the form's, reached as the editor's text commands (`textView(_:doCommandBy:)`: `insertTab:`, `insertBacktab:`, `insertNewline:`, `cancelOperation:`, `moveUp:`, `moveDown:`, `deleteBackward:`) or as `keyDown` on a stop that is not text (a test's ⇧Tab is `\u{19}`, what a real one types). Off the first stop or the last at the top the form asks to close (`onClose(.tabbed(forward:))`; Esc `.escaped`; Return on a blank last line `.returned`) and the card's owner does it; inside a level they go back to the stop after (or at) the one it was opened from; in the panel they go on to the next or previous key view. A row with more opens in the same card (`levels`, a line to go back, Esc goes back, Return on a blank last line goes back), a fold's card holds a field's step while a row is open in it (`setCardStep`); `Other` and a read-only try are the panel's tab (`FieldCards.revealInPanel`) and `refuseChanges`. Every write is the one writer's with the level's step (`.card(key:)` in a card, `.ownRun` in the panel and at the fold's own level): each keystroke is a commit; a cell of a line that is not stored writes the whole list with the item put in at the line's stored index, never an index of its own, a stored line emptied turns blank and takes its item out, an item emptied by its last cell the same; a pair is stored only with both halves and a name no other pair has. It follows the file through `FieldCards` (each form tells it of itself and is kept weakly): a commit to its shape reloads it at once, and any commit schedules one pass a turn later (`editor.scheduler`) in which a form reloads if the book's or the check's revision moved, since a type's change is a commit to the type's record and the index and the check may hear it after the service does; the editor keeps its typed text unless its own cell's stored value was written by something else. Sizes are the sheet's, kept until it changes (`fittingSize(anchorWidth:room:)`: 240 to 480 wide by what its names and values need and no wider than the room, 72 more for each column of a table past four, 360 tall, the fold's 320, then the lines scroll; the row it hangs from sets no width). The cell with the caret tells `editor.fieldChecks?.typing` and is not marked while it does. A link cell (`FieldFormLinks.swift`) draws Mentions' outlined pill (`MentionTextStyle`, the rows' dark ink: it is white in either appearance; a wrong one red with the wave, a gone note's dashed) and is a stop that has the form's keyboard: a click on a live pill goes to the note (`mentions.follow`: editing ends, so a card closes; also in a read-only form, which has no stop), a printable key, Space, Return or a click off the pill opens `LinkPicker`'s list under the cell with the one cell editor laid over it holding the query, nothing written and the check told nothing until a pick; Return or a click on a line picks (one `put` of the token: a waiting line stores a new item), Tab and ⇧Tab close with nothing stored and move on, Escape closes keeping the focus (a second goes back a level or closes the card), Backspace on the empty query closes it, and Backspace or Delete on the cell clears a value (a list's line goes). `FieldCellEditor` (`linkPicking`, `caretListWindow`) has its own `doCommand`: it asks `LinkPicker.perform` before `EditingTextView`'s, which would hand Tab to the list as a pick. A press on the grid closes an open list first.
- `FieldCards.open(_:)` (`FieldCards.swift`) shows the card a row with more in it opens, the core's `CanvasCard` ([editor](editor.md)) about the note with a `FieldFormView` in it, hung from the request's `rowRect`: false for a plain field, a pointer (a list of them opens, its type a type: its card is the column of pills), a path no field names, a fold with nothing in `folded`, a shape not on the board shown and, in a type's note, a kind with no `›` there (`opensOnType`); true with no card for `Other`, which selects the note and chooses the panel's tab where its JSON is (`revealInPanel`: the Types tab of a note, the Schema tab of a type); and, asked for the target that is open, closing it and telling that request's `closed`, true (a click on the row of an open card). The card's id is `FieldCardID { shape, target, folded }` (`folded` the first folded field's key, so a note's two folds are two cards) and the key of the step its writes join (`.card(key:)`); its step is `FieldName.stepName` of the *top-level* field of the path, whatever depth it is at, none for a fold, a type's note or a read-only note (`isReadOnly`, `mode: .kinds`, `editor.isReadOnly`). The form's `onClose` maps Esc, Return on a blank last line and ⇧Tab off the first stop to the request's `closed()` and Tab off the last to `tabbedOut(true)`, always after the card is taken down; a press elsewhere (`.pressedElsewhere`) is told `closed()` too, and no other closing is (editing, the board, an undo, the zoom, the row leaving the view, the shape gone, `close()`): the row's editor may be gone then, so the card forgets its session (`openTarget` nil) before anything hears. The form's `onGone` (the field left its type, or its kind has no card; every level is resolved again from the type, on a type's own card too, so a member added shows and one lost goes) closes the card, `onReadOnly` (a commit froze the note) lets the card's step go, `onResize` tells the core the card wants another size. In the pass a turn after any commit the card is hung from its row again: `request.rowRectNow` (nil closes it) or, with none, the offset from the note's frame it opened at; `openTarget`, `openFolded` and `openForm` are the card's state for the row that outlines it, a note's two folds, tests and the gallery. The line back from inside a fold's card names the fold as its row does (`from Person` or `from 2 types` when the rows are inherited, `+12 more` otherwise). A form for a shape a commit changed reloads at once, so what was just typed shows. The check moving with no commit (`FieldChecks.typing` set or cleared by a surface that is no form of this shape's) reaches the forms in the same pass (`FieldCards.checkDidMove`, told through `FieldChecks.onTypingChange`, which the cards register for as they are made: the check is in a part under the form's and names nothing of it), and a form that set it itself already shows it (`showsCurrentCheck`, kept current across its own set) and is not taken apart for it. A row's view asks the service whether a card of its shape is open (`openTarget`) and not a flag of its own: a card goes down in ways that tell the row nothing, and a row that thought one open would never let go of its focus.
- `FieldForm(_ context: FieldFormContext)` (`FieldForm.swift`) is the same form in the panel, `presentation: .panel`: a SwiftUI face (`FieldFormHost`, an `NSViewRepresentable` over a `FieldFormView(context)`; `updateNSView` hands it the context and reloads it) as tall as what it holds (`contentHeight`; the form's `onResize` sets a `@State` height a turn later, since SwiftUI may not be told in the middle of its own update). A section places it whole on a line of its own under the field's name, never in a `LabeledContent` control column; it draws no name of its own and works from 140 points ([detail panel](detail-panel.md) holds the exception to the panel's own-primitives rule). Closed until opened: one line with a triangle and the summary; what is open is the person's, kept for the session by shape and path in `FieldCards.panelOpen` (not in the file; a deleted shape's entry goes), and while they have not touched a node it opens by itself when a wrong value is at or under it (a needed field left empty is no error: it has the amber dot by its name, and opening it would only put an `add` line under it). The form says no problem in words (the Fields section's lines under the field do): the cell is red or amber, and a node with a wrong value in it is open. Tab off the last stop and ⇧Tab off the first are `window.selectKeyView(following:)` and `(preceding:)` (SwiftUI's hosting gives the next form its keyboard, tested), Esc is the panel's (the form answers no `cancelOperation:` there and the editors set no `onExit`), a triangle is a stop (Space and Return open and close it). A field `Other` is a box of its JSON (`FieldJSON.pretty`: indented, members in key order): monospaced `DetailStyle.textFont`'s size, 76 tall and growing with its lines to twelve (`TypesStyle.formJSONMinHeight`, `formJSONMaxLines`), the one `FieldJSONEditor` (an `EditingTextView`) over the focused box as the cell editor is over a cell and the box drawn as static text when it is not; a member that is `Other` has the same box under its own name's row, so a line may be taller than a row (`FormMetrics.offsets`, only while a box is in the sheet, so a table of thousands is still a division). What is typed stays a draft (`jsonDrafts`, shown even after a reload from elsewhere) until it is done: ⌘↩, or the keyboard leaving, which `EditingTextView` does for a view with `onCommit`; JSON that parses (`FieldJSON.reading`: a bare number or string too) is stored as one step named for the field, empty clears the field, and JSON that does not is kept in the box, nothing is written, and `Not JSON` is said under it until it is typed in again. The cell with the caret in the box tells the check, as a cell does; clicking back into a box or cell the keyboard has left (a control beside the form) takes the keyboard back (`focus(_:)`). A form's field leaving its type, or its kind having no form, makes `reload` stop (`onGone`).

## The panel's Fields

The Types tab's second section (`FieldsPanel.swift`; id `fields`, title `Fields`), registered by `TypeDetailsPlugin` after the Types rows (`registerFieldsPanel`, right after `registerTypePanel`, which is in a part under the form's). For a shape that holds no schema and is some or holds a value (`shownWhile`: `FieldBook.givesFieldsOrValues`, a kept lookup), so a note, an arrow, a shape that was some and is none now; a type's own note has none. `.enabledWhileReadOnly()`, so a locked shape's form can be opened to be read; the plain controls dim themselves. Several shapes selected: the summary, no Fields.

- `FieldsPanelModel.rows(of:book:)` decides everything with no view: a row for each field in the chain's order (`FieldsPanelRow`: `.plain` for `FieldKind.plain` and an either of two plain kinds, `.form` for every other kind, with no fold), then `.unnamed` for each root key no field names (`FieldValues.keys(unnamedBy:)`, key order). A value no field names inside a group or an item is stored and not listed.
- The control: text a half-height `DetailTextArea(asTyped: true)` (line breaks kept), a number, a date and an either one line (`TypesStyle.fieldLine`), parsed as a row's is (`FieldText.value(from:asOneOf:)`); yes or no a `DetailCheckbox` (a click writes `true` or `false`, no way back to absent); a choice a `Picker` of the options by label, a stored value no option holds before a divider, `None` last (clears; written as `Optional<JSONValue>.none`, a bare `nil` there is `.null`); a `FieldForm` (`presentation: .panel`) under the name, full width, for the rest. A read-only shape is handed `isReadOnly: true`.
- Typing: each keystroke is a write (the writer's run for the field: one step, `Change Price`). The box holds its draft while it has the caret and takes the stored form only when the value changed from outside (undo, an agent), on Return and when the keyboard leaves; nothing is refused, clamped or changed. The first keystroke tells `FieldChecks.typing` (this panel has no focus event), and only the box that set it clears it (Return, the keyboard leaving, the box going away). An undo in the same turn as the keyboard leaving can beat SwiftUI's redraw and leave the box showing its draft; it takes the stored form at the next change.
- Values no field names: `DetailShapeRow(symbol: unnamedValueSymbol, dimmed: true)` with a minus after it (`Remove Nickname`, `removeUnnamed`). No header.
- Problems: under a field, once, a `DetailStatusLine` for each wrong problem at its own path (`check.problems(at:)`, an error), with the place in the message; a needed field left empty is no error and is said nowhere in words: it has the amber dot by its name, as a type's row has (`FieldNameLabel`, the dot a `FieldNeededDot` view of its own so a test can find it; `FieldsPanelModel.isNeeded`: the worst mark at the field or under it is needed, so a field with a wrong value inside is red there and has no dot). A problem deeper, in a group or an item, is said at its own row by the form (the lines above it carry the mark too, as a row above a wrong cell does), not here. A rule across fields has no field here; it is said in the Issues tab.
- `FieldsList` reads `TypeIndex.observeChanges()` (an observed count `tell` bumps for each commit that changed a type), so a field added to a selected instance's type shows without a commit to the instance; a form must read it too. It reads `editor.isReadOnly` in its body as `TypesRow` does, so it runs again on every commit while its shape is selected, O(fields); the panel itself draws again for its shape's commits only (`panelsDrawn`).

## The one writer

`editor.fieldWriter` (`FieldWriter`, `FieldWriter.swift`) is the only thing that changes a shape's `fieldValues`: a row, the card, the fold and the panel all go through it, so a field is changed one way and its step is named one way. Called from key and mouse handlers, never inside a transaction or while an undo is applied (`transact` refuses both).

- Its door. `set(_:at:of:step:)` (`setTyped` is `set(FieldText.value(from:as:), …)`, `remove(at:)` is `set(nil, …)`), `rename(_:to:under:of:)` (a pair's value moves to its new key in one commit; false for an absent, empty, same or taken name and for the root) and `removeUnnamed(_:of:)`. All five end in one `apply`: a path that starts with no key (the root included), a shape not on the board shown and a kind that is not `typeable()` are false; a shape that can't change is `editor.refuseChanges(to:)` (services hear it, Selection shakes it) and false, before any transaction, so no empty step; else one `transact` with one `update` of the record. A write that changes nothing is true, and `Transaction.update` keeps a record only if it differs, so there is no commit, no step, and what there was to redo stays. `editor` is internal: the panel builds a `FieldFormContext` from it.
- Its steps. `.ownRun` is named `FieldName.stepName` of the field the key names on the shape's types (`Change Shipping Address`; a key no field names by the same word split, `Change Nickname`) and coalesces by `FieldRunKey { shape, field }` of the top-level field: typing in one field, however many commits and at whatever depth under it, is one step; another field, `UndoHistory.seal()` (the host seals on every mouse-down and move of the keyboard) or a commit from elsewhere ends the run. `.card(key:)` is nameless and joined by the card's key. `removeUnnamed` is `Remove Nickname` (the key's words, capitalised) and never coalesced.
- The title is set in the same `update` (`titling`, last): see [A title from a field](#a-title-from-a-field). Nothing else belongs in it.

## Checks

What a note's values are checked against, and what the check says. The whole answer is a `FieldCheck` ([A note that is one](#a-note-that-is-one)); `FieldChecks` (`editor.fieldChecks`) makes and keeps it; two rules in `TypeValidationPlugin` and the word `:incomplete` read it. Needed is amber and is no issue (no ring, no Issues line, no table-of-contents dot, not `:invalid`); wrong is red and an error; a warning (a pointer to a note that is gone) is an orange ring and an Issues line, no red and not `:invalid`.

- The one pure part is `FieldChecker` (one for each distinct list of types, over a `FieldBook`, no editor: a type's name and schema text are the book's, `FieldBook.name(of:)` and `schemaText(of:)`): it runs the core's validator (`SchemaValidator`, `formats: .standard`) over the list's composed schema without its `allOf` (`main`) and over each type's rule object (one validator each, so a problem on the root names the rule's type), each made checkable first, and says each violation as a problem of a class. A `$ref` to a type is served by a resolver (`Resources`): `urn:uuid:T` is T's checkable resolved schema with T's own root `$defs` and `definitions` laid in (read from the last good JSON), so a ref with a fragment and the `#/` refs inside are checked too; a type that is gone is served as nothing and recorded as gone (a type that comes back makes the checker stale). `isCurrent()` re-reads the list's resolution, each served input and whether each pointer's type is a type, and compares them: an edit that changes nothing the check reads (a root `description`, whitespace, text broken mid-edit) leaves the checker and every answer it made standing.
- `FieldSchemas.checkable` (public, pure) rewrites a schema on a copy, at every place that holds a schema: T1 drops a `pattern` or a `patternProperties` entry ICU cannot read (the type's fault, not the note's); T2 drops `additionalProperties`, `propertyNames`, `patternProperties`, `unevaluatedProperties` and `maxProperties` from an object with `properties` (a value no field names is never wrong, at any depth; `minProperties` stays, as an unnamed member can only help meet it), and T2b an `additionalProperties: false` from anywhere (a pairs field's schema `additionalProperties` and its counts are its own rules); T3 turns `dependentSchemas` into an `if/then` (the core ignores it); T4 rewrites an `if` that has `properties` into `{if: presence, then: {if: X, then: T, else: E}}`, so a condition asks only once the fields it tests have values (an empty Order is not asked for a gift note, and its `else` is not applied to a note that never said; an `if` with only `required` is the author's); T5 turns an `anyOf` or `oneOf` of required-only branches into "if none holds, ask for every field of them" (and for a `oneOf`, the author's `oneOf` only when one holds, so two is the validator's own violation at the node). Limits: an `if` through `anyOf`, `not` or `$ref`, and an applicator's branch under an object that has no `properties` of its own, keep the validator's reading.
- Classes. `required` and `dependentRequired` are needed, at the missing member, dropped when no field is there (a name no type has, a path under an `Other`) and for a yes or no (an unticked box is a value); inside an absent group nothing is asked. `type`, `enum`, `const`, the bounds, lengths, `pattern`, `format`, the list limits, `minProperties`, `propertyNames`, `not`, `anyOf`, `oneOf` and `false` are wrong, at the value's path (the list's, the object's, the member's); a path no row has is clamped to the deepest row above, and dropped when even its first step names no field. A needed list is needed while empty (never `minItems`: an empty list is no value); one filled short of `minItems` is wrong. A violation at the root is a rule across fields: wrong, keyword `rule`, no row. `$ref` (a gone type, a loop) is dropped. Problems are deduped (a field asked for by the root and by a rule is one; two wrongs at one path are two), capped at 100 keeping at least one of each class (so `isWrong` and `isIncomplete` never change), and sorted by row, then path (a key by its text, an index by its number, the root last).
- Words (`FieldCheckWords`): a wrong problem is its place and the validator's own tail (`line items › Plate › quantity is 0, and should be at least 1`: one wording across the app; the place is the rows' names, an item named by its first plain value cut to 24 characters, else `item 2`, a pairs key as written), a needed one `<place> is needed`, a root one `Order rule: the note <tail>`, a refused pair name `labels › Size 2 isn’t a name labels allows`. `SchemaViolation.detail` is the tail.
- `FieldChecks` (`@Observable`, `revision` is what a view tracks; an `EditorService` in an extension with its own `boardDidChange`): `check(of:)` is the answer with `typing`'s path left out, `fullCheck(of:)` the whole (queries); both `.clean` for no `is`, a type's own note, an opaque shape, and a list none of whose types is a type. It keeps one `FieldChecker` for each distinct list (at most 1,024, then all dropped) and one answer for each shape asked. An answer is never invalidated; it is trusted while the record's own list and values are the ones it was made from (two compares that end at the storage's identity) and its checker is current (`FieldBook.revision` against the checker's stamp, rebased when a re-read finds it equal). A note with no values takes the checker's one check of no values, kept with the rename count it was made at: one that holds a root problem (a rule across fields that fails on an empty note) says its rule's type by name, so a rename makes it again. `documentDidChange` reads no record and checks nothing: it forgets what left the file, moves `revision` if a changed shape has an answer (a move changes no answer), and clears everything for a document put in place.
- `typing` (`(shape, path)?`): set by whoever has the caret in a field when it gets it and cleared by them when it leaves, one at a time. The field's path and below are left out of `check(of:)`, so a half-typed value is never red and never rings. Setting it moves `revision`, has Validation check the old and the new shape again (`ValidationEngine.recheck`) and redraws. It is also cleared when its shape goes from the file, when that shape's editing ends (the ring is Validation's, drawn once editing ends) and on a board switch or a file put in place; a board switch tells Validation too (`recheck`), which files the shape under the board it keeps, so the answer stashed with that board, made with the path left out, is made again when the board is taken back.
- A type's change reaches shapes through `TypeIndex.onChange`, which runs after the index has taken the commit in (a rule's `affected:` would hear it first, from an index a commit behind): if only names changed (`chainRevision` stands) only the answers that name a type (a root problem) are checked again, so a rename costs what its chips do; otherwise `TypeReferences` (the types each type's text refers to, both ways, a gone target kept so its return reaches the types waiting for it) is updated, and the reached set is the changed types, their instances, the types that refer to them, and so on to a fixpoint; Validation is told with `recheck` (and the table of contents rows are made again), but not of an instance whose answer's checker is still current once asked (the edit changed nothing the check reads of it: a description, whitespace, a field it doesn't have) and whose answer names no type: each reached list is made current inside the commit and the instance keeps its answer, so an edit that reads nothing costs the types alone (2.7 ms over 10,000 instances, against 57 to 73 when all were named). Which shapes are reached matters only to Validation: the answers are found stale by themselves. `goneReferences(of:)` is a type's refers-to-a-gone-type list.
- The check of a run (`FieldChecks.check(of: record, reading:)`, public; `FieldClass.word` says a class to an agent): a shape's values against its types as a `LayoutReading` has the file, which for a transaction's `shapes.reading` is the file as the transaction leaves it (draw's script, a paste, a dry run), where the committed answers would be of the file before. The committed answer (`fullCheck`) when the reading is the editor's, and when neither the shape nor any type it reads is `reading.isChanged` (the list, what each builds on and the types their JSON refers to, over and over, from the committed index and `TypeReferences`, kept per list until the chain moves; a type the file never had and the reading lacks too differs in nothing): a restated note costs a lookup. The kept checker when only the shape's values are new, checking them and keeping no answer (`typing` is not left out: it is the full check). Else a `FieldBook` over the reading (`init(reading:record:shared:)`: it resolves each list over `ReadingTypes` for its own life, reads a type's name from Titles and its text from the shape, shares the editor's book's levels, whose `level(readFrom:of:)` is `RowBook`'s read of a type mid-transaction, lifted) and a `FieldChecker` over it, kept in `scratch` by list with what it read (each type's text, title and `is` list, and the types its `$ref`s met) and found again while the reading still has those the same, at most 16, let go by the next commit or board change; `scratchMade` counts them in debug builds. A type the reading deleted is a gone type's: nothing. The pointers' part of a run's check is over the reading too (`linkState(_:as:reading:record:)`, handed to `linkProblems`): a target the reading lacks is `.gone` (`.elsewhere` when the committed file can't read it either: kept aside, on a board it can't show), one it holds is `.one` while it holds no schema and its `is` lists, as the reading has them, reach the type, else `.notOne`; so `@[Ann Lee]` with Ann drawn in the same script is one, and a target the script deletes gone. The committed answer is taken only while no pointer's target, nor a type it is or builds on (`index.graph.lineage`), is `reading.isChanged`; else its pointers' part is said again over the reading, the validator's part (`base`) kept.
- The rules (`TypeValidationPlugin`) have no `affected:`: a wrong value is an error and a pointer to a note that is gone a warning (`FieldClass.warning`), each with the problem's message, the row's key as `field` and its JSON Pointer as `path` (both nil for the root); a needed one is none; a type whose JSON refers to a gone type carries a warning, `Refers to “Address”, which is gone.`, never the instances, and so does one whose `$title` titles nothing ([A title from a field](#a-title-from-a-field); it asks the `FieldBook`, so draw, which has none, says nothing of it). Where the context has no editor (draw's check, which `registry.validationIssues(of:in:)` runs over the transaction's shapes) the value rule asks the service through `context.reading` and `check(of:reading:)`, so a wrong value and a pointer to a note that is gone or isn't of the type are in draw's warnings, against a type and a target on the board or drawn in the same script.
- Known limit: the Issues tab refreshing when a caret leaves a row (the ring and the dot do, immediately; the tab on the next time the panel is made).

## Pointers

A field that points at a note. Where it shows: a pill in its row ([Rows on a note that is one](#rows-on-a-note-that-is-one); each drawn by `context.drawMentionText` as a text of one pill in `LinkPills.style`, fixed lines set from the left, which Mentions draws straight into the context with the name the note has at that draw, no attachment's image: [mentions](mentions.md); a style in the font's own lines or centered would send every pill through string drawing again, six times the cost, which `Budget.linkRowsFrame` holds), in a card's cell and the panel's form ([Cards and the form](#cards-and-the-form)), picked in with the list below; found by ⌘F with the name it shows now; completed in the Schema tab (a `contentMediaType`'s examples); drawn by `canvas draw` as `@[Title]` ([Agents](#agents)); asked by `--ask` as `link to Person`; lit by the neighbors' `field-link` color, and listed in their Connections tab under Mentions and Mentioned By ([neighbors](neighbors.md)).

- A pointer is a field whose schema is `{"type": "string", "contentMediaType": "text/x-canvas-link", "contentSchema": {"$ref": "urn:uuid:<the type>"}}` (`FieldKind.pointerSchema(to:)` writes it, `FieldKind.pointerMediaType` is the media type, both public; a list of them is an array of these; a `$ref` with a fragment, or to anything but a type, is Text). Its value is one mention token in a string (`FieldLink`: `target` and the `stored` name, `init?(_ value:)` reading `MentionToken`, `value` writing it, `FieldLink.pointing(at:in:)` with the name a mention of the note shows now). `FieldValues` names the shapes of its tokens to the core (`ShapeNaming`, [shape references](shape-references.md)), and each is a `field-link` edge, a child of `mention` ([topology](topology.md), [neighbors](neighbors.md)). `FieldLinks.admits(_:as:in:)` is what a target must be (never a type's own note; its chain reaches the type: an Employee is a Person) and `targets(of:in:)` every one, file-wide. `FieldBook.shown(_:)` is the name a pointer shows now (a mention's, else the name it holds, else "Deleted") and `resolution(of:)` its resolution.
- **A link to a note** (a pointer's row, `RowClass.link(type)`; a list of pointers is `.still` until its card) is focused as a box is (this view first responder, outlined) and picked in with `LinkPicker` (`LinkPicker.swift`, headless as `ReferencePicker` is; `RowsView.startPicking`): typing on the focused row (any key with no ⌘ or ⌃ that makes a printable character) or Space opens the list under the value, a `RowField` over it taking the characters (nothing is written, nor `FieldChecks.typing` set, until a pick; `RowBook.picking` has the render skip the value under the field); a click on the value of a note not edited begins the session in the row and opens the list a turn later (`RowsView.begin`, guarded as the card is), of a read-only note shakes it, and a press while the list is open closes it first. The list is Mentions' own candidates in `among:` the type's notes through the chain (`FieldLinks.targets(of:in:)`, made again at each keystroke, never a type's own note, the owner left out), any board of the file, ranked, recent and near as the `@` picker's: a line is the name, matched letters in semibold and, off the board shown, the board's name at the right (`MentionChoice.items`, with no kind's symbol, as the gallery draws it), `No Person notes` (`No notes` for an untitled type) or `No matches` for none. **Only Return or a click picks**; `LinkPicker.perform` is the one rule of the keys and both text views that host the list call it before their own handling (`RowField.doCommand`; `EditingTextView`'s would give Tab to the list): ↑ ↓ ⌃P ⌃N and Page keys move the highlight, Return picks the highlighted line and with none is taken and does nothing (so nothing typed is stored and no newline goes in), Tab and ⇧Tab are never a pick (the list closes, the field goes with what was typed, the value is as it was and the key goes on: the next row, the previous or the words), Escape closes it (the row stays focused; a second Escape ends editing), Backspace on an empty query closes it. A pick writes the token through the one writer (`FieldLink.pointing(at:in:)`, `didPick`; `.ownRun`, `Change Customer`, one undo step), the list and the field go, and the row stays focused with the new pill. Delete or Forward Delete on a focused link row with a value clears it (`writer.set(nil, …)`, one step).
- The check of a pointer: the validator ignores `contentSchema`, so a second pass reads the values. `FieldChecker` (with `init?(types:book:)`, `check(_:)` and `linkProblems(in:state:)`; whether a pointer's type is a type and its name are the book's, so a checker over a reading reads them as the run leaves them) holds `linkSites`, made once for a list: where its schema holds a pointer, at any depth (a field, a member of a group or of another type's fields followed once, an item of a list of groups or of pointers; an `either`'s branches are not followed; a pointer to a type that isn't one is no site, but the checker keeps its type and whether it was one, `linkTypes`, so a pointer's type that goes or comes back makes the checker stale and the answers are made again: a note first checked while Person was gone is checked once Person is back, and a wrong one is clean once Person goes). `linkProblems(in:state:)` is pure over a `LinkState` (`.one`, `.notOne`, `.gone`, `.elsewhere`) handed in for each target and the type it points at: a value that is not exactly one token is wrong (keyword `link`: `customer isn’t a link to a Person`), a token whose target is `.gone` a **warning** (`customer links to “Ann Lee”, which is gone`, the name it holds), `.notOne` wrong (`… which isn’t a Person`, the live name; `of its type` for an untitled type), `.one` and `.elsewhere` nothing; at the pointer's own path (`.index(n)` for a list's item), placed as every place is (`line items › Plate › product …`). A type that has gone since the sites were read is skipped. `FieldClass` is `warning = 0, needed = 1, wrong = 2`: a row's mark is the worst class at or under it, so a warning never hides a needed mark and draws nothing on a row (its look is the dashed pill; `RowLook.of` makes `.warning` plain); `hasWarnings`; the cap keeps one problem of each class present.
- The states come from the editor (`FieldChecks.linkState`: no board holds the id `.gone`, a kept-aside shape `.elsewhere`, else `FieldLinks.admits`), and an answer stays right while its targets do: `FieldBook` keeps what each target was when something answered from it (`remember`, called by every reader: `shown`, `resolution(of:)` and the check; `TargetState`: its resolution, its `is`, whether it holds a schema) and `linkCarriers(touchedBy:)` reads the state again of each id a commit added, removed, changed (not by moving) or touched elsewhere that has an entry, and only where it differs updates it, moves that target's `linkRevision(of:)` (whether or not any note points at it) and adds the sources of its incoming `field-link` edges (after `topology.catchUp`; kept by the commit's change set and `editor.revision`, so the ask is made once a commit, and a commit heard out of turn, after one a service ahead of Types made while hearing it, is read for its own ids: its delete is found, and so is its undo). So deleting, undoing, cutting and pasting, renaming, taking `Person` off a note or making it a type are found; moving it to another board, typing in its words below the first line, a keystroke in a note no one points at, and a drag are not (a dictionary miss or one state read). `FieldChecks.documentDidChange` asks it before its early return, has Validation recheck the carriers and moves `revision`; an `Answer` with pointers keeps `base` (the validator's part), the `targets` it read with their revisions and the chain's revision then (`LinkPart`: `base`, `targets`, `chain`), and is current only while all hold (one nil compare for an answer with none; the chain is compared first, so an answer whose values name no target, `customer: "not a link"`, still follows its type going and coming back), else its pointers' part is made again over `base` (`linkChecksMade`, debug), the validator not asked; a chain change (`typesChanged`) also rechecks the carriers of the targets it reaches (Employee no longer building on Person reaches the Orders that point at an Employee) and an answer with pointers is not kept by an unchanged checker, though its values name no target (TypesTests' `fieldLinkChecksTests` hold both directions). A file put in place drops the book's states. A field keyed `stands-for` has no edge (Topology's slot): its answer still follows the target's revision, but Validation is not told when only the target changes (pinned).

## A title from a field

A type's `"$title": {"x-canvas-from": "name"}`, a member of its root `properties`, says which field titles a note that is one. The most specific type's wins whole (an Employee gets Person's; one that writes its own replaces it, even naming a field only Person defines). The note's real title is a copy of that field's value as its row shows it, never read live from the field.

- `SchemaDirectives` (`JSON/SchemaDirectives.swift`: the readings under the chain use it too): `isField(key)` (no `$` first) and `name(key)` (`title` for `$title`, nil for a field and a bare `$`); `Chain` uses it, and so does everything that counts or lists fields as the slices go on, so a `$` key is never a field.
- `TitleSource.of(resolved)`: the `title` directive's `x-canvas-from` field, found through the chain (`resolved.field(named:)`), when its kind can title: `titles(_:)` is text, a number, a date, a date-time or a choice (`PlainKind`), never a yes or no or anything that is not plain (a pointer, a list, a group, another type's fields, pairs, an either even of two plain kinds, what no control suits). Nil for a `$title` that is no object, names nothing, names no field (a name only `allOf` or `patternProperties` gives is none) or names one that can't title: nothing is then titled and no pill hides, never a crash or a partial title. `problem(_:writtenBy:)` says why for the type that wrote the winning directive, and nil for a child that inherits it. A `$title` inside a group's `properties` is a member of that group, never a directive. A field a more specific type restates as something that can't title makes the source nil for its notes, silently.
- `FieldBook.titleSource(types:)` and `titleSource(of:)`: `TitleSource.of` the list's resolution, kept beside `kept` and emptied with it. `titleSource(of:)` is nil for a type's own note, **before** it reads the `is` list: Employee's type note holds `[Person]`, and without the guard it would be titled by Person's `$title`.
- `$title` is never a field. The chain, `RowList`, `FieldsPanel` and the schema the check validates read `Resolved.fields`, which has none. At the root, `SchemaDocument.fields` filters the resolver's (so "N fields" and TypeSync's two `fields.isEmpty` tests don't count it: a type holding only `$title` and words was never answered), `SchemaReadings` leaves `$` keys out of the names a root `required` may use (`“$title” is required, but isn’t a property.`), the `required` completions (`TypeCompletions.requirable`) skip them at the root, and `SchemaTarget.locate` answers nil for a pointer into `/properties/<a $ key>` so a highlight of it is dropped (the net under the prompt telling the model to link nothing there). In a group's `properties` a `$` key is an ordinary member, and a `"$title"` member of `fieldValues` is a value no field names (dimmed, never wrong).
- `FieldTitling` (`FieldTitling.swift`, pure, the locale passed): `shown` (the value as its row shows it, folded to one line; nil for none and for a list or an object under the key), `fits` (80 code points, as Validation counts), `retitle(held:old:new:_:)` and `firstTitle`. `retitle` in order: the field's own value did not change, leave (a write to another field never titles); no title held, write the new value if it fits; the title held is the old value as shown, write the new one if it fits and else take the title off (a title never outlives the value it was copied from); anything else is a title set by hand, leave. A title typed by hand that equals the value when the field is next written is the same as a copy, and follows from then on.
- The write: `FieldWriter.apply`'s one `update` reads the old values from the record inside the closure (never `values(of:)` before it), changes them, then asks `titling`, which answers nil (leave) unless the kind holds a title (`registry.properties(for:)` has `TitleProperty.key`: a set on another kind would be dropped silently, and an arrow is typeable and holds none) and `fieldBook.titleSource(of:)` is there (never a type's own note); then `FieldTitling.retitle(held:old:new:_:)` with the stored title (`TitleProperty.folded(record[TitleProperty.self])`: a title a kind derives is not a title held). A row, the fold's card, the panel's Fields and the form all come through `apply`, so all of them title with no code of their own, in the field's own step (`Change Name`, or the card's held step): undo puts back the value and the title together. A read-only shape is refused before the transaction, title included. Draw never calls the writer and titles by statement; paste and duplicate copy a title with its values (both then carry Titles' duplicate warning); a script or test that sets `FieldValuesProperty` titles nothing; a type's `$title` or its field changing re-titles nobody (a title is a copy).
- `/is-a`, Is a ▸ and `newType` all end in `addTypeLink`, which in its one `update` titles a note once: when its title is empty, it is no type's own note, its kind holds a title and the new list's source (`fieldBook.titleSource(types:)`, from the committed index, which has the type added) gives a value that fits (`firstTitle`). The same step (`Add Type`), on any board, once for each shape of a selection. A note with a title is left alone, whatever it is; a type added to a titled note changes no title. `removeTypeLink` writes `is` alone: the title stays, and with no type to source it, it reads as set by hand.
- A `$title` that titles nothing is a warning on the type that wrote it (`TitleSource.problem(_:writtenBy:)`: the winning directive's `source` is this type, so an Employee inheriting Person's bad one says nothing and Person's note does), `field: "schema"`, joining the third rule of `TypeValidationPlugin` beside the gone-reference warning (so no new line in the registry report). It is asked of `fieldBook.fields(types: [type])`, the type with what it builds on, so a field Person defines counts for Employee's own `$title`; a change to a type it builds on asks again through `FieldChecks.typesChanged` (a child is an instance of its parent). Words (`FieldCheckWords.title`): `“$title” names no field.`, `“$title” names “nme”, which isn’t a field.`, `“$title” names “address”, which isn’t text, a number, a date or a choice.` Without an editor (draw's check) there is no `FieldBook` and it says nothing; an instance shows nothing.
- The Schema tab completes the directive at the root (`TypeCompletions`, over `JSONCompleter`): a key in the root's `properties` (`path == [properties]`) offers `$title` when there is none, written `"$title": {"x-canvas-from": ""}` with the caret in the quotes and the list asked again; in `properties/$title` only `x-canvas-from` belongs (`belongs` drops the keywords of a property's schema the meta-schema would offer), written `"x-canvas-from": ""`, asking again; its value (`offers(at:)` beside `required` and `$ref`: the schema lists no values there, and the engine asks the client where it offers nothing) is the root's fields that can title (`TitleSource.titles` of `FieldKindReader.kind`, a `#` reference read through one step), each with its type (`SchemaFieldType.label`), none from a group's `properties`. A field inherited from another type is not offered (the tab sees this type's text); typed by hand it works through the chain. The seam is the engine's one generic hook for a key the schema doesn't list, `JSONCompleter(keys:)` returning `JSONCompletionKeyOffer`s (key, detail, the value it starts as and where the caret lands in it, `reopens`), appended after the schema's keys, those the object holds left out, written as `keyItem` writes one (the key alone where its colon is there).
- No second pill. A note that is one shows no title pill for a title copied from its field: the type's chip above it and the name in its row. `FieldBook.fieldTitled(record, title:)` is the test: `titleSource(of:)` is there and its title is that field's value as shown (a lookup by the list, one `FieldTitling.shown`, a compare; nil for a title set by hand and for a type's own note). Types registers a second `titlePillLook(forShapesHolding: IsAProperty.self)` (the three-argument form, [titles](titles.md)) that hides the pill when the record is no type's note, has an `is` list, its kind draws rows (`RowBook.kinds`, the kinds with `typeRows`: a property of the kind alone, so **a folded name's pill stays hidden**: the fold is a place rows show, and a type gaining a ninth row must not bring the pill back on every instance) and `fieldTitled` answers. A kind that draws no rows keeps its pill: nothing else says the name. The guards that cost nothing come first, since Find's build asks the look of every titled shape. A cover is asked with no services, so zoomed out the cover still says the name. A title by hand shows its pill as any title does; typing the name into the field to match hides it again and the title follows from then on. Find leaves out a hidden pill's title text (the row's hit, `FieldFindPlugin`, is the same text, marked on the fold where the name is folded); a type edit that drops `$title` or changes its field names the instances (`FieldFindPlugin.affected`), so the title's text comes back with no commit on the note.
- The label is kept by shape and made again only for a shape a commit added or changed other than by moving; a type's change that names no instance (its `$title`, or the kind of the field it names) leaves labels stale, so `RowBook.typesChanged`, where it has the instances the chain's move reached (`index.affected(by:)`), calls `editor.adornmentsDidChange(for:)` on them: it forgets by id and reads no record, and the labels in view are made again at the next draw. Nothing per frame.

## Agents

What draw, queries and `--ask` read and write of Types, through Types' own `TypeNamesPlugin` and `FieldValuesPlugin` (`TypeNames.swift`, `FieldValuesDraw.swift`), which fill Draw's points, and `TypeQueriesPlugin` (`TypeQueries.swift`), which fills Queries' ([command line](command-line.md), [queries](queries.md)): each a plugin value of its own, so a list without the draw engine or the query engine leaves its words out, and neither engine knows Types.

- In draw and queries (`TypeNamesPlugin`, `TypeQueriesPlugin`): `is: "Employee card"` on a shape or an edge is a `drawTextFormat` for `TypeLink.mediaType`: the title names a type as draw finds titles (`context.shape(called:admitting:)`, file-wide, or drawn in the same run, `writing` carrying the keys the statement writes, so a note given `schema:` there is a type), and what it admits is `TypeIndex.canBeNamed(_:registry:writing:)`, the one pure function over a record and the registry that says which shapes a script may name in `is` (a type that is no pill), anything else refused "which isn't a type or a shape"; it writes the id (the entry's coded form), and refuses the shape itself (`DrawTextContext.owner`, the shape whose attribute it is: "can't be its own type") and a shape already built on the owner (a cycle through shapes of the file, by the index's `graph.reaches`, read through `DrawTextContext.service`: "is already built on this shape, which would make a cycle"; a cycle between shapes a single script draws isn't checked, `reaches` is cycle-safe). A quoted value for a list of text is a list of one (Draw's `KindVocabulary.value(of:)`), several are JSON. A pointer in `fieldValues: {…}`, at any depth, is the string `"@[Ann Lee]"`, written as the token of that shape by `FieldValuesPlugin` (no type is asked: whether the note is of the field's type is the check's, [command line](command-line.md)). Queries: `:is-a(S)` is `queryRelation(… reads: .file, transitive: true, namesTypes: true)` over `TypeFacts`, a `QueryFileFact` of `TypeIndex.graph`, a `TypeGraph` (`links` and `names`, copy on write, kept by the index beside its own tables, so a query takes it up in O(1)); `related` is `TypeGraph.parents(of:)` (a shape's types, later first), and `TypeIndex.lineage(of:)` is the same graph's `lineage(of:)`, the order rows are read in, where `:is-a` asks only reachability (`graph.parents`, transitive: no order). `:edge-to(S, is-a)` stays direct. A `queryTextFormat` for `TypeLink.mediaType` reads an entry as its type's title (`TypeGraph.names`; a gone type stays its id), so `[is~="Person"]` and a row's `is` read titles; `.Person` is refused toward `:is-a("Person")`.
- `FieldChecks.check(of:reading:)` ([checks](#checks)) is what a run's shapes are checked by. `FieldChecks.drawn(_:written:reading:)` adds what a statement wrote: `DrawnFields` is the `check`, the `unnamed` members and the types' titles in the list's order (`Untitled` for one with none). `FieldBook.unnamed(in:types:)` (public) walks a written object beside `fields(types:)`, `members(of:)` and `item(of:)`: a key no field names is an `UnnamedMember` (its `FieldPath` and the row it sits under, nil at the top; a list's item names its list's row), never looked into, so a member under it is not listed twice; it looks into a group, another type's fields and a list's items and no further (pairs, `Other`, an either, a pointer, a plain value, a `$ref` to a gone type or to a part of one); a member written `null` is never one. `drawn` leaves `unnamed` empty while any of the shape's types is gone (its values are kept and not checked) and for a shape with no types; `FieldValuesPlugin` says that one itself.
- `FieldClass.word` is how a problem's class is said (`needed`, `wrong`, `warning`). A `FieldProblem` becomes a Queries `ShapeProblem` in the word plugins, `FieldValuesPlugin` and `TypeQueriesPlugin` (`field` the path's first key, `path` its JSON Pointer, both nil for a rule across fields). `:incomplete` lists its needed ones as its rows' `problems` (`TypeQueriesPlugin`, from `fullCheck`).
- `FileFields` (`editor.fileFields`, `FieldBook.fileFields`, kept until `chainRevision` moves) is what every type of the file says of the members a note's values can hold, by key at each depth: the union of each type's own fields (`fields(types: [t])` with `source` `t`; a `$`-key is a directive, never one), a group's members, another type's fields' and a list's items'; a step past a plain value or a pointer leads to nothing, past pairs, `Other`, an either, a `$ref` to a type that is gone or to a part of one, or beyond six levels, to anything. `unknown(_:)` is what `TypeQueriesPlugin` answers `queryPathCheck(attribute: fieldValues)` with: the first member of a path nothing of the file can hold and the members that could be there, nil while any note lists a type that is gone (`TypeIndex.listsAGoneType`: its fields are unknown, and its notes' values are kept), so a typo in `[fieldValues.stauts]` is the query's `unknown_field` warning.
- `FileFields.text(for: question)` is what `--ask` is told, as `<fields>` (`TypeQueriesPlugin`, `queryAskFact(tag: "fields")`): a line for each titled type (made once, kept with `FileFields`): `Title: key "name" (kind), …`, `Title builds on Parent: …`, `Title: no fields`; the quoted name only where the field's own `title` is another than the key's words (`FieldName.words`); the kinds `text`, `date`, `date-time`, `number`, `whole number`, `yes/no`, a choice's stored values `a|b|c` (eight, then `|+N`), `any of a|b`, `list of text`, `list`/`group` with their members in braces one level (deeper `{…}`, twelve, then `+N more`), `fields of Address`, `list of fields of Room`, `link to Person`, `links to Person`, `pairs of text`, `number or text`, `other`, a gone type `a gone type`; at most 60 fields a type (`, +N more`) and 8,000 characters, types ranked by a word the question shares with the title, a field's key or its name, then by how many notes are one (`TypeIndex.instances(of:)`), then by title, whole types dropped from the end and the rest named on `and N more types: A, B` while that fits, then `…`. Never a value. Made again when `TypeIndex.stamp` moves (a type's schema, name or own types).
- `canvas measure` and `canvas draw`'s own handling of a note's values are [command line](command-line.md)'s: draw joins a statement's `fieldValues` with what the note holds, member by member, and its result and warnings say what each shape's values still need and what is wrong (`FieldChecks.drawn`, above); `canvas measure` reads the types of the board in front, so a tool can size a typed note with its rows before it draws it (`namesBeyond:` and `alsoReading`, with a warning where an `is:` names nothing).

## The model

Types asks the model for two things, kept in step with the person's text: the schema from the words and the words from the schema, and which words say which part of the schema (the highlights).

- The sync: `TypeSync<Schema>` (`editor.typeSync` for a type's, `TypeSync<TypeSchemaProperty>`) keeps a shape's words and a `SyncedSchema` property (one whose value is a `SchemaText`: the property it writes, the prompts it sends, `SchemaSyncPrompts`, its update step's name, and whether the shape's name is the schema's, `namesTheShape`) in step through `editor.languageModel`: only the person's edits sync (not its own write, a board read again, a move, a lock, a rename, an agent's write, `ChangeSet.byAgent`, which is taken as in step), on a commit (editing the words ended however it ended, `editingDidEnd`, an edit still waiting committed the next turn; ⌘↩ or the keyboard leaving in the panel's JSON) or 3 s after the last (`TypeSync.idleDelay`, `editor.scheduler`), the wait held while the JSON's completion list is open (`completing`); words that changed rewrite the schema (whole: past 34 KB never sent, "Schema too long to rewrite", `TypeSyncFailure.tooLongMessage`), else a schema changed in meaning (its root `"title"` left out of the comparison) rewrites the words; never for text that isn't a JSON object, a read-only shape (`editor.isReadOnly(inFile:)`, asked as the sync starts and as an answer lands), or a shape with no fields and no words; with no model at all (a plugin list without LanguageModels) a side to write fails, saying `No language model` (`TypeSyncFailure.noModelMessage`, no Retry). A shape seen for the first time is in step, but words over a schema with no fields, never answered, are ahead; a shape the person makes a type (`/is-a type`) waits as an edit does and is committed on the scheduler's next turn (an undo or a draw in the make's turn lands first, and a shape that is no type then asks nothing): the read starts at once, with no 3 s wait. A rows' spot reads the sync through `onSpotChange` (told a shape's id for each status change and each flip of the wait), `isWaiting` and `hasAnswered` (its words at the last sync are not empty: a read has answered). The name: the type's title goes in as the root `"title"` of each schema sent (`TypeNaming.titled`, unless the schema has one the person typed), the model's comes out of what it writes back (`TypeNaming.untitled`), and an untitled shape is named by it in the same step. While a call is out the side it writes is read-only (the JSON, `detailUpdating`; the note's editor, read-only and faint, showing what lands); its answer is dropped if that side changed. Its write is a step of its own ("Update Schema", "Update Text"), on the shape's board shown or not (`transact(on:)`), the words through the kind's `TypeWords.write`; undo and redo start nothing (`restoredStep`) and the pair last synced goes back with the steps (`UndoParticipant`). Its status (`TypeSyncStatus`, `TypeSide` `.words`/`.schema`, `TypeSyncFailure` and its remedy) shows under the JSON ("Updating schema…", "Updating text…"). What it asks is `SchemaPrompts` (the file name, constant names and prompt literals byte for byte, since `scripts/schema-prompts` parses them; they still say "prompt" for the words): Haiku 5.5 at `medium`. A system prompt is `SchemaPrompts.system(_:schemas:)`: the direction's base, then what it says of titles (`toSchemaTitle` and `toSchemaNewTitle`; `toPromptTitle`), on every request, then what it says of other schemas (`toSchemaReferences`, `toPromptReferences`), only in a request that names some, each a blank line apart; highlights get no titles. A rename keeps the field's key and puts the new name in the field's `"title"` (the words are written by it); a pointer is a string with `contentMediaType` `text/x-canvas-link` and a `contentSchema` that is the `$ref` to the type's word, written for "a link to", "points to", "refers to", where a bare `@` word is the type itself; a `$title` (`{"x-canvas-from": key}`, last in `properties`) is written on request, and for a new type whose first field is plainly its name, and the words say nothing of one the person never said; an `if` names its field under its own `properties`. Tests pin the text without a model (`promptTests`: each JSON example in it parses, the pointer's example reads as `.pointer(id)`). The model never sees an id: each type the texts name is `@` and a word (`SchemaReferenceNames(words:schemas:name:isType:)`), a mention of a type its word and a `$ref` that word as its URI; a mention of a shape that isn't a type reads as its label, words. Words that are a JSON object never go to the model as a prompt (at every edit and when a note first becomes a type): when words that changed parse as one (`TypeWordsSchema.schema(fromWords:)`: no mention in them) they are the schema as written, in one "Update Schema" step, none if it already says so (`adopt`), and the words are then written from it, as when a note becomes a type, the pair said to have been these words over `{}`; that request has no `<prompt>` (the model writes the words from nothing), and its answer's preamble is judged against no words. JSON still being typed (`{`, then `"` or `}`, or only `{`) asks nothing; words in braces that are no JSON (`{name, age} for each guest`) are a prompt. With no key the schema is the JSON and the rows show, the status asks for a key, and one saved asks for the words. The words are answered inside `<answer>` and `</answer>` (`SchemaPrompts.answerTag`; the schema is answered bare, as ever), and only what is inside them is the words, since Haiku sometimes opens with a remark and asking it not to made that worse (`parse`, `tagged`): the last complete pair, trimmed; after an opening tag never closed, what follows it; with no tags the whole answer, as before; `NO_CHANGE` judged outside the tags (first word or last line) and inside them; tags with nothing in them a failure. `<answer>` and not `<prompt>`, the request's own block, which a remark names ("There is no <prompt>, so I write one"). A rewrite of the words that reads like a preamble, a fence or `NO_CHANGE` inside text is unusable, unless the words it replaces read so themselves (`SchemaPrompts.parse(_:_:words:)`): a note that begins "Here are the fields" is a note.
- Highlights: a prose map of the words into the schema (`SchemaTarget`, its guide `SchemaPrompts.highlightsGuide`, `highlightsReferences` its context when the words name types), asked once the shape is drawn in full and in step, read through `ProseHighlights` (generic over a target whose addresses are JSON Pointers, keyed by shape and target: the texts as the model reads them, made once per text, and the map moved onto the drawn text, `MentionReading.drawnRange`; `forget(_:)`, `forgetEverything()` in debug builds). A kind's render calls `context.fillTypeHighlights(of:in:)` with the placement it draws its words in, between its fill and its text (a note's), which costs one added-value lookup on a shape that isn't a type: a field's pale blue, a rule's pale amber (`TypesStyle`). Resting the pointer on them shows that part of the schema, or of the type a `$ref` names, in the core's popover: on the canvas through Selection's `shapePopovers` (`TypeHighlights.popover(at:on:in:)`), in a note's editor through `EditContext.hoverPopover` (the editor asks `editor.types`). `TypeHighlights.placed(for:in:)` and `lines(for:in:)` read them for tests.
- `scripts/schema-prompts` (`evals.py`, `highlight_evals.py`, their cases, a README that says how to run them) are the lite evals of the prompts: read the Swift, so there is one copy, and never run by a test or without the lead (no key is printed: `--check`, which `evals.py` runs with a self-test of every check, returns before one is loaded). A prompt's change reruns every case; a new check is shown able to fail in `selftest()`; `evals.py`'s `parse` copies `SchemaPrompts.parse` (the words read from their `<answer>` tags, the tag's name out of the Swift), and `selftest()` reads the answers `promptTests` reads, so a change to how an answer is read changes both; the new cases go in `gen_cases.py` (then `python3 gen_cases.py`) and `highlight_cases.py`, never in `cases.csv`.
