# Types

Types: a shape that holds a JSON Schema is a type, named by its title (making one with `/is-a type` and taking it back with Remove Type, the file's types in `TypeIndex`, the Schema tab's JSON, its completions and issues); what a shape is (`is`, the `is-a` edge, `/is-a`, the chips and the Types tab); a note that is one: its values (`fieldValues`), what its types give it (the chain, the field book), the rows it and its type have under their words and the typing in them, the card a row with more in it opens, the form, the panel's Fields and the one writer that changes a value; the check (needed and wrong), the title from a field (`$title`); what agents read and write of all of it (draw, queries, `--ask`); the words and the schema kept saying the same thing by the model (`TypeSync`), its highlights and their popovers; a note's fields filled from its words by the model (`FieldFill`), and whether a shape and a type's shapes do (`autofill`, `$autofill`); and their tests.

## The parts

Types is one module in nine parts, each a folder of `Sources/Types`, and a part names only what is in the parts its line in `Package.swift`'s table of parts says it uses: the build type-checks each with those sources alone, so a file that reaches into another part doesn't compile ([build](build.md), "The parts of a module"). The sections below follow them.

- Which part uses which is `Package.swift`'s table of parts, and the comment over the table says what each holds; `Rows` uses `Form`, since a row opens a card and never the other way.
- A new file goes in the part whose line already names everything it uses. What two parts share lives in the lower one: `TypesStyle` is declared in `Type` and each part adds its own constants in an extension (the inks a card and a row both draw with are the form's, `FieldFormStyle.swift`, since `Rows` is above `Form`); `PluginID.types` is with `TypeWords`, under the points that name it as their owner; `typeable()` is in `Fields` because it opts a kind into `fieldValues` too.
- A lower part that must tell a higher one something takes a closure and is registered for, as `TypeIndex.onChange` and `TypeSync.onSpotChange` are: `FieldChecks.onTypingChange` is how the cards hear that the caret moved. A use that has no such answer means the thing is in the wrong part, or the line in the table is wrong: change that, never the check.

## A type

A shape that holds `schema` is a type: its words say what the notes of that type have, and its JSON is the same thing written out. This section is the type itself; what a shape is of a type, and what it holds for the type's fields, follow.

- Types (platform, a horizontal and a shape trait; what it imports and why: [build](build.md), Who builds on whom) knows no kind. A kind opts in with sugar its plugin calls: `typeWords(_:drawnIn:fitting:)` hands Types its words (a key path to its `MentionText`, the placement its render draws them in, and what a write of them changes besides: a note's height), a contribution to the `TypeWords` point (one per kind, its `words`, `placement` and `write`, the last a transaction's update that fits the shape as the kind fits it), and `canBeType()` opts the kind into `TypeSchemaProperty` (key `schema`, a `SchemaText`), which the registry refuses without `typeWords` (`TypeWords.check`). StickyNotes calls both, so a note is the shipped type; TypesTests' `Memo`, a box of words of the tests' own, shows Types needs no note. A shape is a type exactly while it holds `schema` (`record[TypeSchemaProperty.self] != nil`); nothing else marks it, and Type is in no list of a shape's types. Its name is its title (Titles' `TitleProperty`, `registry.title(of:)`): a type's schema keeps no root `"title"` of its own, and one the person types in the JSON is kept as typed and never the name. `SchemaText` is one string in a board, kept as typed (its order and half-typed text survive), and `ShapeNaming` through `urn:uuid:` `$ref`s (`SchemaReferences.refs(in:)`, `uri(of:)`, `target(of:)`, read in any case, whole or with a fragment), so a copy of a type with the types it refers to refers to the copies and one alone to the originals, and `canvas draw` warns of a `$ref` to a shape not in the file; the key `schema` is an ordinary id to the audit (`ORDINARY_IDS`), a board's own old member. `Fixtures/added/schema.v0.json` is its fixture.
- (Relationships, their ends and the service that keeps them, the mark `relationship` and an arrow's `endRule`: [relationships](relationships.md#what-is-stored).) The mark is not in the schema's text, so the sync never sees it and no prompt changes for it.
- The graph (`TypeGraph`) also keeps `types` (every shape that holds `schema`, titled or not) and `relationshipTypes` (those that hold the mark too); `TypeIndex.canBeEmbedded(_:)` is a type that is no relationship type (a field may hold its fields in place), and the one question the sync (`TypeSync.referenceNames`), the request for highlights (`TypeHighlights.placed`) and the `$ref` completion (`referenceTargets(from:)`) ask of a mention: a mention of a relationship type is plain words to the model (not in `<schemas>`, read as its label, never a highlight's context) and the completion leaves it out, while a `$ref` to one written by hand still reads as its word (the `name:` closure is unchanged); a relationship type's own words, naming its ends, are synced as any type's (`mentionedTypeTests`) once it has some, and one with empty words is never synced either way (`TypeSync.sides(of:)` is nil for it: no call, no status, no failure; a read out when its words are cleared is dropped; its schema is written by the field list and its words are not drawn; words typed later are ahead of the schema it has, and wait as an edit does: its panel's Description is where they are typed, [A relationship type is a pill](#a-relationship-type-is-a-pill)). `TypeIndex.onChange` delivers a `TypeChange` (each set is said on it) after each commit that changed anything it keeps; a flip of the mark alone moves the type's `revision`, not the chain, and with `everything` the other sets hold the union of before and after. `FieldChecks` and `RowBook` act on `types` alone, so what they do is what they did.
- Making one: `/is-a type` (the `Type` row of `/is-a`'s list: [What a shape is](#what-a-shape-is)) or `editor.makeType(_:)` (`makeTypes(_:)` for several, one transaction), one step "Make Type", on a shape that holds the property and none yet. Its schema is the starter (`TypeSchemaProperty.starter`, an object with no properties) unless the shape's words are a JSON object (`TypeWordsSchema.schema(fromWords:)`: nil if the words hold a mention, are empty, or trimmed are not an object; an array, a number or text get the starter; `{}` is a schema), in which case the schema is the words, trimmed of whitespace at both ends and otherwise as written, with no model call, and the sync then writes the words from the schema: `TypeSync.changed`, on a shape made a type by the person, sets the last-synced sides to the words and `{}` when the words equal the schema, so the next commit finds the schema ahead of the words and asks for the words (the `toWords` direction), and with `{}` words, which say nothing, nothing is asked. A type does not start public (it is found by `/is-a` and by its title; `/public` is the person's to say, on a type as on any shape). `editor.removeType(_:)` (`removeTypes(_:)`), one step "Remove Type", takes the schema away and leaves the words, the title and whether it's public; undo brings the schema back. Each plural form leaves out the shapes that cannot take the change and transacts nothing when none is left; a read-only shape stays in, so the core refuses it and it shakes while the rest change. Is a ▸ in a shape's menu is the other way. An agent makes a type with draw's `schema:` (an added property's attribute, as every one is): a note drawn with it, named by its title.
- A type's note is white paper (StickyNotes': `NoteStyle.typePaper`, white in the light appearance and 0.92 in the dark, a note that isn't one is its color's paper, yellow without one, unchanged in both appearances, and a type's color doesn't show on it; the text's ink is the note's on both): its render and its skeleton fill it, the skeleton resolving the dynamic color once for the appearance drawn (`NoteStyle.typePaperCG`; [benchmarks](benchmarks.md)), and its cover's ink is chosen for it (`titlePillLook` with `coverSurface`). It is its color's paper (yellow without one) again once the schema goes.
- A type's color (`TypeColors`, public; Colors', [palette](palette.md)): the color the type's shape holds (`record.ownColor`, kept in `TypeIndex`'s entry) where the file has it (`ColorBook.swatch(for:)` answers; a name the file lacks, or no `ColorsPlugin` in the list, is not held), else one of eight built-in names by its id, so two types can share one: `TypeColors.names` (orange, yellow, green, teal, blue, indigo, purple, pink, in that order; never gray, which reads as no color, nor red, which reads as wrong) and `TypeColors.index(of:)`, the first eight bytes of the id's UUID, each summed with its position, modulo eight (never `hashValue`; TypesTests' `typeColorsTests` hold a table of ids that never changes and the spread). A type whose note holds no color is its id's color wherever its color shows: its pill and chips, its notes (`TypeColorsPlugin`, below), and its own symbol in a list and the panel's row (dimmed, as a default is), its note staying white. `editor.types?.look(of: id)` is how it is drawn, a `TypeLook` made once a swatch (so tints made again are equal, as Adornments keeps its fills by tint) (its members say what each draws; chips drawn on a note's paper are drawn light too, [adornments](adornments.md)); `TypeColors.gray` (`TypesStyle.grayChip`) is what has no color: `Type` and an untitled type. `TypeIndex` asks for the book as it is made (so `ColorsPlugin` may come after Types in the list) and tells Adornments to make the chips of a type's instances again, and the readers of its name (the card's chip) hear it, when a commit changes the type's `color` (a color alone moves no revision and tells `onChange`'s observers nothing) and, through `ColorBook.onChange`, for the types that hold a named color when the file's colors change; the type's own pill is its shape's, which that commit names. `/is-a`'s rows are chips (`SlashChoice.chip`, [slash commands](slash-commands.md)): `Type` and an untitled type gray, the others in their color, the `{}` first. A type's pill is Titles' (`registrar.forShapes(holding: TypeSchemaProperty.self) { $0.titlePillLook { … } }`, [titles](titles.md)): its title after `curlybraces` (`arrow.right` for a relationship type, one that holds the mark), solid in its color; untitled, a dimmed gray `Untitled` placeholder (the neutral look every placeholder pill has); a note that holds no schema keeps the plain pill; Remove Type and its undo switch it.
- A type's color in its schema (`TypeColorDirective`, Type `TypeColorDirective.swift`, public): `"$color": {"const": "blue"}`, a directive in the root's `properties` beside `$title` (`SchemaDirectives`: no field, no row, nothing a note of the type holds), its `const` a literal as a shape's `color` holds one (`ShapeColor(_:)`: a name, a hex). It says what the type's shape's own `color` says (which `TypeColors` draws, unchanged), and the core's `keepsInStep` rule that `canBeType()` adds (`TypeColorDirective.keep`, [properties and schemas](properties-and-schemas.md), Added properties) keeps the two saying it through every write, whoever writes, in the write's own commit and step: a write that changes the member (or a type that comes, or a note made one, with one) gives the shape its color (a built-in name in its list's spelling; none when the member goes; nothing for one that isn't a color: `reading(of:)` is `.none`, `.color` or `.notAColor`, nil for text that isn't a JSON object, which changes nothing); else a write that changes the color (or a type that comes, or a note made one, with one: `makeType` on a blue note writes it into the starter) writes it into the text (`text(_:saying:)`: the `const` replaced, else the member put first in the root's `properties` on a line of its own, indented as the member after it, or a `properties` put in the root; none takes every `$color` out with its comma, so the starter comes back exactly; the rest of the text as typed); both changed, the member wins. Two that disagree with neither changed (a board from before) are left until either changes. So the panel's row, `/color`, Color ▸, draw's `color:`, and a file's color renamed or removed (Colors rewrites each shape's `color`) write the JSON; typing the member in the Schema tab, draw's `schema:`, words that are JSON and a paste set the color. A `$color` that isn't a color is a warning on the type, `“$color” isn’t a color.` (`SchemaReadings.issues`); a name the file lacks is held as the name, and Colors warns of it. A schema written whole keeps the `$color` of the one it replaces, as written, unless it says one (`HeldDirectives.carried(from:into:)`): draw's `schema:` (Types' `drawValueWriter` for `schema`, in `TypeNamesPlugin`), so a script run again says `unchanged`, and the model's answer and JSON words taken as the schema (`TypeSync`). The model is never sent it (`HeldDirectives.stripped`: the sync's schemas, and the highlights' through `SchemaReadings.shownModelText`), and the sync takes a change to it alone as no change (`TypeSync.changed`, `means`). It is the type's own: a type built on it keeps its own color (the chain's `directives` carry it, and nothing reads it there). ColorDirectiveTests (`colorDirectiveTests`, `typeColorStepTests`, `colorSyncTests`) hold it.
- A type's color on its shapes (`TypeColorsPlugin`, Type `TypeColors.swift`, a plugin value of its own after Colors and Types, so a list without Colors leaves it out; the report's `inherited color → every shape holding is`): a shape of a type that holds no color of its own (a note's paper, a relationship's arrow) is drawn in its type's color, the one its type's pill shows, through Colors' `InheritedColors` ([colors](colors.md)), wherever its color shows (its render and skeleton, its title's cover, its symbol in a list, the panel's row, dimmed as a default is). `TypeIndex.color(inheritedBy:)`: the first color the file has of those its chain's types' shapes hold, in the order its fields resolve (a type before what it builds on, a later type in its list first, through types alone as the field book's chain goes: a name that is gone, or a shape that is no type now, ends its branch, so a note of a type taken away takes nothing of what that was built on, where `lineage(of:)` and `:is-a` go on through it), else the one its first type's id says (the later type's, of several), so a color set anywhere up the chain comes before one an id says (Cy, an Employee, is blue from a blue Person while Employee's pill keeps its id's color); for a type itself, the one its id says (what its pill, its symbol in a list and the panel's row show while it holds none; its note stays white, and a type built on another keeps its own); nil for a shape of no type. Nothing is written to the shapes: a type given another color, its undo, a type deleted, a link taken away recolor them with no step of their own. What a list's chain gives is worked out once a list (`chainColors`: the colors its types hold and the color its first type's id says, kept by the list as the shape holds it while `chainRevision` and `colorRevision` stand: a type's color coming, going or changing moves the second), so a frame asks a dictionary lookup a shape (`chainColorsRead`, debug builds); the index tells Colors (`inheritedColorsDidChange()`, in `onceHeard`) after a commit in which a type that has shapes of it (or went) was recolored, came or went, or was given another list of its own (which can put another type first for a shape of it and of a type it is built on now), after the file's colors change while a type that has shapes of it holds a color by name (which they take, or pass over while the file lacks it), and after a file put in place. The owner's asks: "if you set a type's color, it applies to the instances as well" (2026-10-21), and of a type's notes left yellow while its pill had its id's color, "this is a bug. it should adopt color from the top" (2026-11-04); a shape's own color wins. TypesTests' `instanceColorTests` (`InstanceColorTests.swift`) hold it.
- Whether a type's shapes fill their fields from their words, in its schema (`TypeAutofillDirective`, Type `Autofill.swift`, public): `"$autofill": {"const": false}`, a directive in the root's `properties` beside `$title` and `$color`, its `const` true or false. It says what the type's shape's own `autofill` says (`AutofillProperty`, below), kept in step by a second `keepsInStep` rule `canBeType()` adds (`TypeAutofillDirective.keep`), as `$color` and the color are: a write that changes the member (the JSON typed, draw's `schema:`, words that are JSON) gives the shape its say (none when the member goes; nothing for one that isn't true or false: `reading(of:)` is `.none`, `.autofills(Bool)` or `.notABool`), else a write that changes the say (the panel's box, draw's `autofill:`) writes the member (`text(_:saying:)`), both changed the member winning. One that isn't true or false is a warning on the type, `“$autofill” isn’t true or false.` (`SchemaReadings.issues`); the Schema tab completes it (`$autofill`, written saying false, selected, then `true` or `false`, `TypeCompletions`). What `$color` and `$autofill` share is one implementation (`HeldDirective`, Type `HeldDirective.swift`: the member read, written, carried and stripped as typed text, the rest of the text as typed; `HeldDirectives`, the two together, and those other plugins hold in a schema: `registrar.typeDirective(name)`, the `TypeDirectives` point, a word once and none of Types' own or `title`, LanguageRules' `$rules` ([language rules](language-rules.md)); `registry.heldDirectives` is the list, Types' two then the plugins' in plugin order, which the sync takes as it is made, the index and the highlights' request read, and draw's `schema:` writer is handed with the registry): a plugin writes the one it holds with `HeldDirective(name:).text(_:setting:)` (the `const` replaced or the member put first, nil taking it away, the rest as typed: LanguageRules' `RuleWriter`); the model is never sent either (`HeldDirectives.stripped`: the sync's schemas, the highlights' through `SchemaReadings.shownModelText`), a schema written whole keeps those of the one it replaces unless it says them (`carried(from:into:)`: draw's `schema:`, the model's answer, JSON words), and a change to them alone is no change to the type (`sameButHeld`: no revision moves in `TypeIndex`, the sync takes it as in step, and `means` reads neither). A type built on one says the same unless it says otherwise (`TypeIndex.autofillInherited`, below). AutofillTests' `autofillDirectiveTests` and `autofillStepTests` hold it.
- The file's types: `TypeIndex` (`editor.types`, an `EditorService`) reads every board's types as the file opens (the kinds that `canBeType` only, a lookup a shape), then keeps them by each commit's `ChangeSet` alone (added, removed, changed, `elsewhere` with the kind first, `replacedDocument`, and a whole file put in place, `boardDidChange`'s `isFileReplaced`). It reads nothing per frame and nothing on a move.
- Readings: `SchemaReadings` keeps a reading per shape and text, its `shown` the last that was a JSON object while the text isn't one; kept for every type of every file open: `capacity` is what the open files' indexes say they hold (`SchemaReadings.expect(types:from:)`, told by each `TypeIndex` as its `entries` change and as it goes: `isolated deinit`) and a quarter more for the texts typed and the copies of a file beside its original (which count twice), never fewer than `minimumCapacity` (4,096), so a file of 11,000 types (the generated board of 100,000 shapes has 11,392) is read once as it opens and a second window over it reads none, where a fixed 4,096 read every one again each time the sync or a panel came round to it (and sorted every reading kept each time it passed); past `capacity` the reading used longest ago goes, one at a time (the readings are a list in the order of use, each use moving one to the front: O(1)), and closing a file cuts what is kept to what is left; a shape keeps up to `versionsPerShape` (4) texts; `issues` are Validation's (`TypeValidationPlugin`, `forShapes(holding: TypeSchemaProperty.self) { $0.validate { … } }`): not JSON or not an object an error; `required` naming no property, a `type` that isn't JSON Schema's, and whatever else the meta-schema refuses (`MetaSchema` through the core's `SchemaValidator`, its own sentences, a `type` left to the rule of its own), warnings, 20 at most. `SchemaValue` is a schema as it was written: `JSONValue` with an object's members in the order written (a name twice: the last value at the first's place; equality is order-sensitive, as a property moved is a change to its rows), `SchemaValue(node)` from a reading, `.json` for the core's validator (the one function, where `SchemaReadings` and the chain each had a copy). `SchemaPositions` (`named`, `definitions`, `single`, `lists`) is where a schema holds a schema: what walks one (`SchemaWalk`) and what rewrites one (the chain's reading of a level) go by it.

Writes through it are one step a run, "Change JSON". The header is the kind's (a note's title).

Every list that installs `StickyNotesPlugin` installs `TypesPlugin` (the registry refuses an owner not installed).
- Composition: `TypesPlugin(), TypeValidationPlugin(), TypeDetailsPlugin()` right after `PublishingPlugin()`, so a note's commands list `title`, `lock`, `public`, `is-a`, then Moving's. The schema card, its tool, its `schema-link` channel and the Schemas module are gone: a board that holds a card keeps it aside ([boards on disk](boards-on-disk.md)).

## What a shape is

A shape is of the types its `is` list names; the chips above it, the menus and `/is-a` read and write that list.

- What a shape is: `is`, an added property (`IsAProperty`, key `is`, an ordered `[TypeLink]`, none twice: `uniqueItems`; empty is none, so emptying it takes the property off), on the kinds that call `typeable()` (it needs `typeWords`, `TypeWords.check`): a note and an arrow. `TypeLink` is a one-field wrapper of a `ShapeID`, `ShapeNaming`, coded by hand as the bare uuid string, `contentMediaType` `text/x-canvas-type` (`TypeLink.mediaType`, what draw and queries read the entries by): a name, not a binding, so a copy of an instance copied with its type is an instance of the copy, one copied alone stays an instance of the original, and a type that is deleted leaves its name behind (its chip dashed) until the delete is undone. `ShapeID` itself must not be `ShapeNaming` (an arrow's bindings are ids). The order is the order added: a later entry wins where two say the same (the chain reads it so: [A note that is one](#a-note-that-is-one)). `Fixtures/added/is.v0.json` is its fixture. Types declares the `is-a` edge type (`IsAEdge`, a root, "from a shape to each type it is, in the order of its list") and derives it with Topology's property rule (`registrar.edges(in: IsAProperty.self, as: IsAEdge.self)`: slot `is`, from the instance to each type, a name of a pill forwarded to what it stands for, a move reading nothing). `build.sh`'s `ORDINARY_IDS` holds `is`. ## A relationship type is a pill

A relationship type (the mark on a shape that holds `schema`) is drawn as a pill on the line between its two types, not as a note. Nothing of it is stored: the title and the schema are the shape's own, and the pill is a layout input, read by the kind that draws the type's note (Types' Rows part: `Rows/TypePills.swift`, `Rows/TypePillEditor.swift`; StickyNotes' `NotePill.swift`).

- The input is `TypeRows.pill` (`LayoutInputKey<TypePill>`: `title` (nil while untitled), `fields` (what `FieldBook.fields(types: [it])` gives, its chain's) and `size`, the text measured), provided by `RowBook.providePill` for every shape that holds `schema` and the mark, of a kind that says where its rows go (`typeRows(drawnIn:)`), whatever it holds (words and fields are kept and not drawn); none for any other shape, so a plain empty type stays a full note. Mid-transaction its fields are resolved over the shapes as the transaction leaves them (`ReadingTypes`), as the rows' extent is. It replaces `TypeRows.isCompact`, which is gone. A change of the title or the schema is the shape's own commit, and a change that comes from a type it builds on is announced with that type's layout (`RowBook.typesChanged`: the changed types and their instances, near and far, which a relationship type that builds on it is).
- The size is `TypePills.pill(title:fields:)`: the text (the name, or `Untitled`, then ` · N` with fields) in `TypePills.font` (13 semibold) measured once for each distinct (text, count) (`TypesStyle.pillWidthsCapacity` kept), the padding either side (`pillPadding` 12), `pillMinimumWidth` 40 at least and `pillMaximumWidth` 240 at most, `pillHeight` 24. Drawn past 240 the line ends in an ellipsis, its count going first (`TypePills.draw`, Core Text).
- The note lays itself out at that size, about the middle of the box it stores: the core's `LaidOutBox` answers an anchor (`laidOutAnchor`, a point of the box in unit coordinates that stays where it is, (0.5, 0.5) here, (0, 0) for everything else) besides its size, kept with it (`LaidOutSize`), so a longer name grows both ways and the line through it stays put. Everything that asks how much room it takes reads the capsule: its geometry, hit testing, the area query, an arrow's end, the outline (`StickyNoteDefinition.indicatorPath`: `NoteStyle.capsule`) and the editing frame. It has no resize handles: a note resizes, but a pill's size is its name's, so the note's `CustomSizing` says it doesn't (`isResizable`: `NoteLayout.pill` is nil). `canvas draw` places it by its laid-out frame and writes the box that puts the frame there (`ShapeItem.roomOffset`).
- It is drawn by the kind from the input alone (`renderPill`): a capsule filled with the type's own color (`TypeIndex.look(of:).row`, its title pill's, as that read; the text in `rowInk`), its name in that ink (the placeholder dimmed) and the count softer after a dot; no paper, words, rows or hairline. Zoomed out it is the capsule in its color with no letters (`NoteSkeleton`, a skeleton asks no text). A relationship type shows no title pill above it (`titlePillLook`: `hidesPill`) and no type chips (`TypeChips`). (A hidden pill is no pill for Find to ring, so Find does not list a relationship type by its title.)
- Its name is typed in the pill: [The release of a port's drag](#the-release-of-a-ports-drag).
- **The card that defines it** (Form's `DefinitionCards`, `DefinitionView`, `DefinitionModel`; the face in Rows' `DefinitionFace.swift`): a click on the pill (a Selection face over the whole pill: the press is the shape's, so a drag still moves it, and a double-click edits the name in place and takes the card down) opens the core's `CanvasCard` about the type, 4 points under the pill's laid-out frame, which it follows (`moveCard` after each commit); Tab in the name opens it at its first line past the name (`RelationshipHooks.openDefinition`, `enteredByKey`), and so do the pill's part (⌥Tab, `DefinitionFace`, "Fields") and Open Fields (at the name). A second click on the pill closes it. From the top: the name (the title, one coalesced step `Name Relationship Type`, placeholder `Untitled`), a line for each field (its name, and its kind at the right: Text, Number, Date, Yes or no, Choice; the list of kinds opens under the line, ↑ ↓ and Return pick, a click picks), a Choice's options as indented lines and an empty indented line that adds one, `add field`, a hairline, and a line for each end arrow (from ends first): the end type's chip, the row's name (the arrow's words' first line, one coalesced step `Name Row`, the rest of the words kept; the placeholder is the row's derived name), Needed and Only one (`editor.setEndRule`, its steps). Fields and options are written only through `editor.fieldDefiner`, each a step (Add Field, Rename Field, Change Kind, Remove Field, Add/Rename/Remove Option): a name or option is written when the caret leaves it, and one emptied and left (or Backspace on an empty line) removes it. A held ⌫ or ⌦ is one press (`HeldKey`; `removeFocusedLine(repeating:)`), here and in the form: the repeats neither take out the line the keyboard moved on to nor the name it lands on. Keys are the form's: Tab and ⇧Tab move (off either end the card closes and the card's owner says where the keyboard goes: [relationships](relationships.md#the-pills-keys)), Return goes on (on a typed `add field` it adds the field as Text and lists its kinds; picking one goes on to `add field`, or to a Choice's options), Esc closes the list of kinds, else writes what is typed and closes, ↑ ↓ go by lines; the one cell editor is `FieldCellEditor`. It holds no step of its own (`stepName` nil). A commit from elsewhere reloads it (`DefinitionModel.read`, compared); it closes when the type goes or loses the mark, on a press elsewhere (keeping what was typed), an undo, editing. A locked type (or board) opens it read-only: it can be walked (Tab, ⇧Tab, ↑ ↓, ← → take any stop, which draws its mark; the card has the keyboard itself, with no cell editor), while Return, Space, Delete and typing shake the type and nothing is written; a locked end arrow refuses its row's name. By keys (keyboard navigation's P1 to P9, on `KeyMeaning`): ← and → on a stop that is no text (a kind, a check) go to the previous and next stop on the same line and stay at its ends, in a name they are the caret's; Backspace or Delete on a field's kind, and ⌘⌫ in a field's name or an option, take the field or the option out in one step (`Remove Field`, `Remove Option`), the keyboard going to what slid into its place; ⌘↩ (the cell editor's `onCommit`, and at a stop that is no text) closes the list, writes what is typed and closes the card, as Esc at the top does; Escape closes the list of kinds, then the card. VoiceOver: the card is a popover element (`CanvasCard.accessibilityLabel`, "Fields of <the type's title>") whose children are the stops in Tab's order (`DefinitionAccessibility.swift`: name, field, "Kind of <field>" as a pop-up button with the kind as its value, option, "New option", "add field", "Row name, <type>", Needed and Only one as check boxes); the shared cell editor is named for the stop on every `focus(_:)`. A string field with an empty `enum` (what a Choice is written as before its first option) reads as a Choice of none (`FieldKindReader`), not `Other`.
- **Its words, in its panel** (StickyNotes' `NoteHeader.swift`, the note's own panel, since the pill draws none of its words): a Description section in the type's Schema tab (`TypeSchemaTab`), above the JSON (a kind's own sections come before every shape's), shown while the note holds the mark and `schema` (`shownWhile`, the record alone), the note's words in Mentions' `MentionTextArea` (pills, the `@` picker; each run of typing one step, "Change Description"), read-only and dimmed while the sync writes the words (`detailUpdating`). It is the way to say a relationship type in natural language: the type's sync reads the words as it reads a type's words on its note (with the relationship type's literal, `toSchemaRelationshipType`), once typing pauses (`TypeSync.idleDelay`) or at once on Return or the keyboard leaving the box (`onCommit`, `TypeSync.commit`), and the fields it writes show in the JSON under it and in the pill's card; a field the card changes rewrites the words, as a schema edit does for any type with words. A relationship type's first words wait as an edit does (`TypeSync.changed`: it was not followed while it had none, so it is seen anew, its last-synced words said empty and its read left to the wait or the commit, never the next turn, so the box's first letter asks nothing). StickyNotesTests' `relationshipDescriptionTests` (the section above the JSON, none for a plain type; typing and Return writing the words in one step and the model's fields arriving, told it is a relationship type), TypesTests' `syncTests` (the first words waiting), and the gallery's `Rel3-panel-description`.
- Tests: TypesTests' `relationshipTypePillTests` (the input with and without a title, words and fields, none for a plain type and an instance, the size, the follow of a parent's field, the editing line), `rowSpotTests` (no rows whatever the read says); CompositionTests' `notePillTests` (the layout about the middle and growing both ways, the stored box untouched, mid-transaction, the bitmap and the skeleton, the outline, hit testing, no pill or chip, an arrow's end) and `notePillEditingTests`; CanvasTests' `laidOutTests` hold the anchor. The gallery: `Rel1-p3-landed`, `Rel1-p5-named`, `Rel1-p6-fields`, `Rel1-p12-zoomed-out`, `Rel1-p19-long`; the card's: `Rel3-card-empty`, `Rel3-card-kinds`, `Rel3-card-kinds-list`, `Rel3-card-fields`, `Rel3-card-only-one` (`GalleryDefinition.swift`), and its tests TypesTests' `definitionCardTests`, CompositionTests' `notePillCardTests`.

## A note that is one

A shape of a type holds `fieldValues`, and what its types give it is the chain of them: which fields, in which order, from which type, and what each field's kind is. The vocabulary under the rows, the card, the panel's Fields and the check is in `Sources/Types/Fields` (and the check's answer in `Checks`), so they cannot disagree. All pure but the services; each file is small.

- What a shape holds for its types' fields: `fieldValues`, an added property (`FieldValuesProperty`, a `FieldValues`: one JSON object over `JSONObject`, coded as the bare object) on the kinds that call `typeable()` (it opts in beside `is`; `isTypeable` still means "holds `is`"). A value that is empty is not kept, at any depth (`FieldValues.pruned`, the one rule: null, text with no characters, a list left with no items and a group left with no members once their own empties are dropped; `false`, `0` and text of spaces are values), and every way in prunes (`init(_:)`, `init(from:)`, the subscript; `values` is `private(set)`), so `isNone` is `isEmpty` and a board read, a draw or a set that is only empties holds none. Written by people and agents, never `readOnly`; `canvas draw`'s `fieldValues: {…}` is joined with what the shape holds, member by member at every depth (a list replaced whole, `null` clearing the member it is written at; [Agents](#agents)), an edge takes it; a query reads a path into it (`[fieldValues.status="paid"]`). Nothing reads a type for it: it knows no field, so a value no type names is kept. `Fixtures/added/fieldValues.v0.json` is its fixture. Beware `JSONValue` is `ExpressibleByNilLiteral`: a `nil` beside a `JSONValue` in a ternary is `.null`.
- Whether a shape of a type fills its fields from its words: `autofill`, an added property (`AutofillProperty`, Type `Autofill.swift`, a Bool, `false` a value) on the kinds that call `typeable()` (beside `is` and `fieldValues`; a type's note is one, and there it is the type's say for its shapes, kept in step with its `$autofill`). None means the shape does what its types say: `TypeIndex.autofills(_:)` is its own say, else `autofillInherited(by:)`, the first a type of its chain holds in the order its fields resolve (`lineage(of:)`, as a type's color is taken; for a type, what the types it builds on hold), else true. The index keeps each type's say in its entry (no revision moves for it) and tells the views that show it once a commit changed one (`observeAutofills`). Its one writer is `FieldFill.setAutofill(_:of:)` (the panel's box): the shape holds its own say only where it differs from what its types say, so one that agrees follows them, one step `Change Autofill` on the shape's board, a read-only shape refused (the core shakes it). Draw's `autofill:` writes it on a note or an edge. `Fixtures/added/autofill.v0.json` is its fixture (`false`).
- The chain (`Chain.swift`, pure: `Chain.resolve(types:in:)` over a `ChainCatalog` of `ChainNode`s, so one answer serves a shape, every shape with the same types, and a test with no editor): what an instance's types give it, as a `Resolved`. `order` is `TypeOrder.linearized` of the instance's list read latest first (each type once, the first, as the index and `canvas draw` read a list that names one twice), less the shapes the catalog has nothing for (a gone name, or a shape that is no longer a type, ends its branch: its notes keep their words and show a dashed chip, while `:is-a` still follows its list). A field's winner is the first type in `order` to name it, and its whole `ChainField` is used (nothing merged, `required` with it, which is the root's own; a `required` name with no property names no field). `fields` is in row order: `order` reversed, each type's fields in the order written, a name taking the place it first appears at, so a field a more specific type restates keeps the more general type's place with the restater's definition (`source` the winner, `introducedBy` the type whose block the row sits in). A type cannot take a field away. `rules` are every type's root keywords across fields (`allOf anyOf oneOf not if then else dependentRequired dependentSchemas`, none overridden, in row order of their types), `directives` the root's `$title`-like properties, raw, one per name, the first in `order` (so an Employee gets Person's `$title`; `TitleSource` reads `title`, and `SchemaDirectives` is the one place a root `properties` key is told a field from a directive). The root's `patternProperties`, `$ref`, `additionalProperties`, `unevaluatedProperties`, `propertyNames`, `minProperties` and `maxProperties` are none of these: they speak of the whole object, which holds every type's fields and values no field names, and those are never wrong. `schema` is the as-written composition (`properties`, the winners' `required`, `allOf` of the rules; what is empty is left out) for the check. A level is read from a type's JSON (`ChainLevel(schema:)`) with every `#` reference written out at the reading (`SchemaInliner`, over `SchemaPositions` alone: `enum`, `const`, `default`, `examples`, `contentSchema` and what is no schema are copied as written; siblings win over the target, the target's order kept; a reference to nowhere is dropped; one that leads back into itself or past six deep is dropped and the field's `holdsItself` set; `$defs`, `definitions`, `$schema`, `$id` and `$anchor` are left out of what is written). A `urn:uuid:` `$ref` (another type, bare or into a part of it) is kept as written: a bare one is "another type's fields", one with a fragment gives no rows (rows read it as Other), and the check answers both with the type's own composed schema. `Resolved.Field.field.schema` is a `SchemaValue`, so a group's rows and a type's card have their order.
- The field book (`FieldBook`, `editor.fieldBook`, an `EditorService` registered right after the index; it reads nothing as it is made, so `typeIndex10k` does not see it): `fields(types:)` (and `fields(of:)`, a record's `is` list) is the chain's `Resolved` for a list, resolved once for each distinct list as the shape holds it (the same types in another order are another list: the order decides who wins) and kept while `revision` (the index's `chainRevision`) stands. A list is resolved when first asked, never eagerly, so a type's edit costs the lists asked afterwards, not all: each valid keystroke in a type's Schema tab drops every kept list (held by `Budget.fieldListsResolved`'s second figure, [benchmarks](benchmarks.md)). A type's own note asks `fields(types: [itsID])`, the list an instance of it asks too.  `ChainBook` (internal) is the catalog under it: each type's level read once per text through `SchemaReadings`' `shownRoot`, so a type whose JSON is broken mid-edit keeps the rows of its last good JSON (this session's: a file opened with a broken schema has none until it's mended, and undo back to a good text is that text's rows) and a type whose text was never an object has none; `prune()` forgets the levels of shapes no longer types. `resolutionsMade` (debug builds) counts resolutions, as `TypesPlugin.schemasRead` counts reads. It keeps nothing per shape or per board, so a switch is nothing to it (`boardDidChange`'s default) and follows a file put in place through the index's stamp. A type that is gone, its name and its color are the index's, not the book's. Nothing here draws; the check is [Checks](#checks).
- `FieldPath` (`steps`: `.key` by the field's key, never its title, `.index` by the *stored* index; the list is dense, empties are pruned, so a card maps its display line to the stored index by counting the non-blank lines before it): `FieldName`: `words(of:)` is the field's own `title` trimmed of surrounding spaces, else its key in lower-case words (`lineItems`, `line_items`, `postalURL` is `postal url`), `stepName(of:)` the undo step of every write of that field, whatever the surface: `Change Line Items`. `FieldWords` holds every string a row, card or panel says, beside `FieldKind.typeLabel`'s.
- `FieldKind` (`FieldBook.kind(of:)`, a pure function of the field's schema: it reads no other type, so a gone type still reads as `typeFields(target)`; a reader that wants it often keeps it): the first of `FieldKindReader`'s rules that fits, in the order said there; `hasMore`, `opensOnType`, `typeLabel(memberCount:)`, `field(at:types:)` and `branches(of:)` are said on them.
- `FieldValues.value(at:)` and `setting(_:at:)`: the pruning rule at any depth (setting empty takes the element out and the rest shift up; an index at or past the count appends; a parent that holds the other shape of value is replaced, but removing from under one changes nothing, and a path that starts with an index changes nothing). `keys(unnamedBy:)` is the members no field names, the panel's dimmed values. A note with no title and no words is named by its values: `FieldValues.name(of:)` (record-only, for the note's `untitled:` closure: the filled top-level text and number values and a ticked box's field name, in key order, joined by ` · `, 60 characters at most; nil for a type's own note and for a note with none), so the first-minute Layer reads `1 · expose foobar · foundation` wherever a shape is named.
- `FieldText`: what typed text is stored as, and how a value shows; every function takes a defaulted `locale:`. A number is stored as one only when it reads back as typed (`9.50` is 9.5, `12.00` is 12; `02134`, `1e3`, `1.`, `-`, a 17-digit id are text); a date is a full date however it is written (`2026-03-03`, `Mar 3, 2026`, `3 Mar 2026`, `3/3/26` in the locale's order), stored `2026-03-03`, and in a `dateTime` field (`format: date-time`) as UTC midnight, `2026-03-03T00:00:00Z`, which a date-time format check accepts; both forms show as the day; a part of a date stays text; nothing is refused. The locale's formatters are made once per locale and kept (a `DateFormatter` costs far more to make than to use), since rows draw dates every frame in view. `shown` of a value of another JSON type shows it as its own type would (the check is what calls it wrong). `value(from:asOneOf:)` and `shown(_:asOneOf:)` are for a field that takes either of two plain kinds.
- `FieldSummary.of(_:field:in:types:)` is what a row with more in it reads (a group leaves out a member edited as JSON: a sentence is not a value). `RowList` (`init(_:own:)`, `count(of:own:)`): nine fields or fewer all show; past nine, two rows or more inherited (introduced by a type not on the shape's own list) fold into one `from Person` row in the place of the first, and of what is left nine or fewer show, else eight and `+N more`: a fold shows only when it hides two rows or more, so a note has at most ten rows.
- The services that keep what is written, checked and opened (`FieldWriter`, `FieldDefiner`, `FieldChecks`, `FieldCards`; the writers and the cards keep nothing per board, so a switch is nothing to them (`boardDidChange`'s default)) are registered after `TypeSync`, each reached through an accessor on the editor that is nil without `TypesPlugin`. `FieldWriter` is [the one writer](#the-one-writer) of values and `FieldDefiner` of field definitions ([the one writer of definitions](#the-one-writer-of-field-definitions)), `FieldChecks` the check ([Checks](#checks)), `FieldCards` and `FieldForm` the card and the panel's form ([Cards and the form](#cards-and-the-form)). `FieldCardRequest` is the card's whole request, a public init with `folded` and `rowRectNow`.

## Rows on a type's note

A type's note has, under its words, a hairline and one row for each field its chain gives it (`fields(types: [id])`, so an Employee with none of its own has Person's); a note that is one has its fields there with its values ([next](#rows-on-a-note-that-is-one)). Types owns it, a kind opts in, and the core learns nothing.

- A kind says where its rows go: `registrar.typeRows(drawnIn: { shape, context in … })` (`TypeRowsPlaces`, a `KeyedExtensionPoint`, one contribution a kind, `Registry.typeRows(of:)`; `check` refuses a kind that holds neither `schema` nor `is`) and its render calls `context.drawTypeRows(of:in:)` (StickyNotes: `NoteLayout.block`, after the highlights, before the editing guard, so the rows draw under the editor too). Only such a kind is given `TypeRows.extent` (and `TypeRows.pill`): an arrow says nowhere and has no rows. A relationship type has no rows: `RowBook.provide` answers none for a shape that holds the mark, and it is a pill instead ([A relationship type is a pill](#a-relationship-type-is-a-pill)).
- `RowList` is the fold, pure. `RowBook` (`editor.rowBook`, a service registered last in `TypesPlugin`, made with the others, reading no shape as it is made) keeps a template for each distinct (list of types, isType); stamped with the index's `chainRevision` and dropped whole when it moves (a rename moves no size: only a template that says a type's name, `from Person`, is made again, by a rename count).
- The spot (`RowSpot`), in order, for a shape that holds `schema`: its template has rows: `.rows`; the sync's status is `.updating(.schema)`: `.reading` (three bars); `.failed`: the reason with `.retry`, `.settings` (a key missing or refused) or no action (`TypeSyncFailure.Remedy.editByHand`: too long, declined, no model); its words are empty: nothing (blank, no hairline); an edit is waiting and no read has answered (`isWaiting`, `hasAnswered`): nothing, so `No fields` never flashes under the first words typed; else `.noFields`. Rows win over a read in progress: a type with rows read again keeps them, the last good ones, and no bars show. A read that failed is said under them, as the plan has it ("the reason and Retry when it failed", "a type too long for the model to write says so in the rows' spot, as any failure does"; too long only ever happens to a type that has rows): `RowBook.foot(of:)` is a `.failed` for a type with rows whose sync failed to write the schema from its words (`TypeSyncFailure.writing == .schema`: no key, a refusal, an error, too long), drawn as one row more at the foot of the rows in the empty spot's own look (`FailureLayout`: the reason, then `Retry` or `Settings…`, none for too long), and nil for a failure to write the words, under which the rows say what the schema says (the panel has that one). A frame asks it of every type's note in view, full or skeleton, so it reads `TypeSync.failure(of:)`, which does not observe (`status(of:)` makes a key path a call: 0.5 ms on `Budget.typeRowBars`' 350 skeletons, which put it over) and is one compare while nothing is syncing or failed.
- The extent is `RowsExtent(height: (hasLead ? rowsLead : 0) + rows × rowHeight (22), wordsLine:)` (no width: the rows read at the width the kind has and never widen it, so a note keeps the width it stores, [editing](editing.md)), `hasLead` being words over the rows or the shape being edited (`RowBook.hasLead`, which every place that lays a row out goes by: the faces, the drawing and its hairline, the rows shown), and `wordsLine` the shape being edited (`isEdited`: `editor.editingShapeID`; the core lays the edited shape out afresh as editing begins and ends), so its kind keeps its words a line however empty they are and the caret sits above the rows rather than under them (StickyNotes' `NoteLayout.wordsHeight`, one line `NoteStyle.oneLineHeight`, which the first letter typed fills without moving the rows); `RowBook.extent(ofShape:edited:)` is the extent at rest or edited, and a note's editor takes the difference of the two layouts' rows' tops (`NoteLayout.blockTop`) as how much further down the rows sit while edited, adds it to a press that began the session, made on the note at rest (`NoteEditor.movedByEditing`), so a press on a row's value still begins in that row, rows 3 for reading, 1 for `No fields` and a reason, a type's rows and one more while a failed read is said at their foot (so the laid-out note is tall enough for it; mid-transaction too, since the foot is the sync's, by the shape's id); none for a shape that is no type and has no types with fields. The provider (`RowBook.provide`) tests the cheapest first (no `schema` and no `is`: nil, two added-value lookups, which is every plain note), then the book's kinds; answered from the committed index when `reading.isCommitted`, else (a draw, a paste, a duplicate, `/is-a` itself, a type and its instance written together) resolved over `reading.shape(inFile:)` with no template and no text measured: a type's level is the field book's own (`FieldBook.level(of:)`) while the text is the committed one, so a type whose JSON is broken mid-edit gives a pasted instance the rows of its last good JSON, else read from the text once, by (id, text).
- Announcements: `RowBook` is told by `TypeIndex.onChange` and `TypeSync.onSpotChange`. A type's change that moves `chainRevision` drops the templates and calls `editor.shapesDidChangeLayout` once for the changed types, their instances near and far (`affected(by:)`) and the instances the commit itself added or changed (the index has filed them before it tells), so no size laid out against the index of the file before is kept. A rename redraws. A status or wait changing lays a type's note out again with no commit (`spotChanged`): one with no rows has another spot; one with rows only when the failure at their foot came, went or reads otherwise (`feet`, the failure each was last laid out for), so a wait or a call announces nothing for it. An edit to a note's own record needs none: the core lets its size go. A file put in place drops the tables.
- The sync reports for the spot (`onSpotChange`, told once the sync has the change being delivered, `editor.onceHeard`; `isWaiting`, `hasAnswered`: [The model](#the-model)); a shape the person makes a type shows the bars at once, with no 3 s wait.
- Drawing (`RowsDrawing.swift`): clipped to the block widened at the left by the tick's room (`tickInset` + `tickWidth`, in the paper's padding): the hairline, then the spot (bars, one row of text) or the rows: tick, name, the amber dot after it, and from the right `›`, the chip, the label (an inherited row is faint all through). Text is Core Text lines kept by (text, style, width cut to) in `FieldLines`, fixed colors since the paper is light in both appearances; the chip is Adornments' (`AdornmentLabel.draw(at:in:)`, drawn as in the light appearance), built with `TypeChips.label` so untitled, gone and renamed types read as above a note. `RowGeometry` is the one place that says where a row's parts are (the name column is the longest name, at most `nameShare` (45%) of the rows' width, the same on a type's note and on a note that is one). Constants are `TypesStyle`'s.
- Clicks (`RowFaces.swift`, one `shapeFaces` Types registers; `RowFace { shape, row, part, at }`): nothing in a type's rows is edited. A row whose kind `opensOnType` (`›`) and a fold ask `editor.rowBook?.openCard` for a read-only card (a `FieldCardRequest`: `mode: .kinds`, `isReadOnly`, the row's rect, a fold's `folded` rows, `rowRectNow`, which finds the row again by its field or by which fold it is and answers nil once it is gone); the book does the asking for every door (`opened`, debug builds, is what it asked), and `FieldCards.open` is the card's: a click or Return on a `›` row, on the fold, on a type's note and on a locked note opens the real card, read-only on the last two. A click on the value of a `Choice` or `Choices` row pops `RowMenus.options` (the options in the schema's order, every item disabled) under it; `Retry` is `typeSync.retry`, or `refuseChanges` (the shake) on a read-only type; `Settings…` is `AnthropicSettingsView.showSettings()`, locked or not. Anywhere else a press is the note's. A face is asked on every move over a shape and costs the provider's lookups (a note that is one has the values' faces, [Typing in a row](#typing-in-a-row); never for the shape being edited or a skeleton). `doubleClick` runs the same for `›` and options, so the second press of a fast pair does not begin an edit, and does nothing for Retry and Settings….
- By keys (keyboard navigation's P1 and P3): the same faces are parts of the shape (Selection's `ShapeParts`, `RowBook.parts(of:)`, listed by `registerRowFaces` in the scope of `schema` for a type's note and of `is` for a note that is one): a type's note's `›` rows and folds (their whole row), choices (the value) and relation rows, and Retry or Settings… (the words), each titled by its row's name (`FieldWords.retry`, `.settings`), its press the click's `perform`; and a read-only note that is one, where Return can't edit: its rows with more in them, its JSON rows and folds (the card, to be read), its relation rows' pills (the note) and `›` (the card). An editable note lists none: Return edits it and Tab walks its rows. None while it is edited or a skeleton. ⌥Tab walks them in the rows' order (Selection reads a row of parts as those within half the shortest one's height); Escape out of the card lands on the part, then on the note. Retry is also a command, `retryTypeSync` (`registerRetryCommand`: enabled while a selected type's last read failed with `.retry` and it can change; in the selection's menu, so the Shape menu), as the row's words and the panel's button run it. TypesTests' `typePartsTests` and CompositionTests' `typesKeysShippedTests` hold them.
- While the words are edited (`RowsView`, `Sources/Types/Rows/RowsView.swift`): the note's editing view covers the paper, so a press over the rows would put the caret at the end of the words. A kind's editing view lays a `RowsView` over its rows (`RowBook.makeRowsView(for:host:)`, a `RowsHost` of the editing frame's origin, `end`, `keepInView`, `focusWords`; StickyNotes' `NoteEditor` adds it above its text view). It draws nothing but the focus outline (the render under it draws the rows), shows the arrow, does what a click on the canvas does for the part under the press, and nothing elsewhere in the rows: a press there moves no caret. On a note that is one it is the rows editor ([Typing in a row](#typing-in-a-row)). The book keeps one weak (the session's) and calls `reposition()` after each commit and each layout change it announces; the note's editor does after a size change and a keystroke.
- Zoomed out (`fillTypeRowBars`, `showsTypeRowBars`, `RowBook.bars`): a kind's skeleton asks `context.showsTypeRowBars` (zoom at least `skeletonRowsZoom`, 14%, where a row is three pixels tall) before it works out where its rows are, then fills one bar a row (`skeletonRowBarHeight` 8, as wide as the name's columns by `SkeletonText.columns`, `FieldRowTemplate.barWidth`, never the measured width: no skeleton shows a letter, and SkeletonsShippedTests holds two boards that differ only in letters to one picture), a fold as three dots, the reading state as its three bars, `No fields` and a reason as one bar (`spotBarShares[0]` of the block; a reason at the foot of a type's rows is that bar under theirs), through `fillSkeletonText`. The cover title of a titled note is drawn by Titles over the bars, as it is over any skeleton.

## Rows on a note that is one

The same rows, with the note's values at the right (`RowShown.swift`, `RowsValuesDrawing.swift`): the name muted at the left, the value left-aligned in the column after it (`›` follows a summary), as the gallery draws them.

- `RowBook.rows(of:)` for a note that is one pairs its template with its `fieldValues` (`ShownRows`, kept by shape, 2,048 at most then the lot is let go; rects from the block's top-left so a drag lets go of nothing and `rows(of:)` after it adds the block's origin; let go of for the shapes a commit added, removed or changed other than by moving, and those of `changes.elsewhere`, whole with the templates, and when the width, the lead or the rename count it was built for moved; `rowsShown` counts the builds). Each row's `content`, by the field's kind: a plain value `.text` (as `FieldText.shown`/`FieldSummary` say it, kept to `longestShown` characters; the cut to the column is the drawer's), a yes or no `.box` (unticked for none and for `false`), a choice `.choice(label)` (`nil` for none, a value no option names as itself), either of two plain kinds `.text`, anything with more in it `.summary` (a list or choices cut to the column with the rest counted, `gift, mugs +2`, by measuring; the rest as `FieldSummary.text`), `.empty("add gift note")` for none (no `›` while empty), `edited as JSON in the panel` for what no control suits (empty or not), and `.gone(type)` for another type's fields, or a list of them, whose type is gone (its dashed chip, the value kept and not checked). A default fills nothing.
- Marks are not kept: each draw reads `check(of:)` once a note (`FieldChecks.check`, or `RowBook.checks` in debug builds, which tests set to draw marks before and beside the check, with `typing`'s path taken out of its answer as the real one does) and `mark(at:)` once a row (a fold: the worst of its folded fields). `RowLook.of(content:mark:)` is the whole of what a mark does to a row: `.wrong` is the red ink with a wavy line (1 point, a 4-point wave, 1.25 high, under the text), `.needed` on an empty row the same words in amber, `.neededInside` a summary or fold in amber, a box is never needed, a gone type is never marked. The text inks are the dot's amber and the wrong red darkened until each reads at 4.5 to 1 on a note's yellow and on white (`neededTextInk`, `wrongInk`; the dot stays `neededInk`). The value of the row `FieldChecks.typing` names for this shape is not drawn (the row's own field draws it). A relation row is marked by the note's check of that row (`check.mark(at: row.relation)`, [relationships](relationships.md#the-check-on-a-notes-rows)): an empty Needed row reads `add layer` in amber, a row that breaks Only one has all its pills red with the wave, and on a type's note a row whose end is Needed has the dot after its name (`RelationRowTemplate.isNeeded`, which the name column makes room for as a required field's).
- The outline of the row whose card is open (`FieldCards.openTarget`, a fold by `openFolded`) is drawn while the note is not edited: 1.5 points in the accent, round the value as it hugs it (`hug(_:)`, at least `fieldFocusMinimum`). A row skips drawing its value only while its own field has the caret (`typing` a path of one step); a caret in a cell of the card it opened is a deeper path, and the row's summary stays drawn behind the card.
- Zoomed out a filled row has a second bar, `skeletonValueShare` of the value column (a box's width for a box), never as wide as what it says; none for an empty one, red (`skeletonWrong`) for a wrong one; a fold's dots are red when it carries a wrong one.
- **⌘F finds a value** (`FieldFind.swift`, `FieldFindPlugin` after Find and Types; Types depends on Find): a note that is one gives Find a text for each field with a value, as its row shows it, a row with more in it every text under it; a hit marks the row's value (the fold for a folded field). `RowBook.reached(by:)` is its `affected`, asked once every service has the commit, so the index has told the book what it reached; the rules for it are in [find](find.md).
- **A row's first value in the issue walk's list** (`Engines/IssueRowValues.swift`, `IssueRowValuesPlugin`, id `issue-row-values`, a plugin value of its own listed right after `TypeDetailsPlugin`, so a list without Validation leaves it out; the report's `IssueRowValues → every shape holding fieldValues`): a flagged shape's row in Validation's list ends in the first thing it holds ([validation](validation.md)), the thing the issue is about. `registrar.forShapes(holding: FieldValuesProperty.self) { $0.issueRowValue { record, editor in … } }` fills Validation's scoped point: a note of a type gives the first text of `RowBook.findTexts(of:)` (the first field of its row order that holds a value, as its row shows it: a date `Mar 3, 2026`, a choice its label), an arrow of a relationship type (a kind that relates) the first of `findTexts(ofRelationship:)` (a Vote arrow reads `Nay`), and a shape with no value, or a type's own note, none, so its row is its name alone. A text equal to the shape's title (folded, as `TitleProperty.folded` compares) is skipped for the next field's, so a type whose `$title` is its first field does not repeat the name, and so is a text with nothing in it (spaces are a value, but fold to none), with a title or without; nothing is left when no other field holds a value. The point folds the answer to one line and caps it ([validation](validation.md)). Read from the record and the types alone, as Find reads them, so the row of a stop on a board that is not the one shown reads the same; asked for the rows the list draws, never the file. Validation owns the point and knows no value; Types knows no list.

## Relation rows

What a relationship type's ends give a note (the model is in [relationships](relationships.md)): shown, and set from the row (typing, Space or a click lists the notes and Return draws the arrow: `RelationPicker`; a pick in an Only one row replaces what is there; a pill is deleted from the row).

- **Which notes.** A note gets a row for each `RelationshipRow` of `relationships.rows(for: its types, isType:)`: every note of a type at a drawn end (or one that builds on it), on every board, and both type notes (a type's own note lists the rows of itself and of the types it builds on, those faint and ticked); never a relationship type's own note nor an instance of one. The template is a function of the types and the drawn ends, so a note's height is too: `RowsExtent` counts relation rows, the committed way through the template and mid-transaction through `relationships.graph(in: reading)` (for a transaction, the committed graph with what the run has written put back, so a transaction that writes an end and the notes of its types together sizes the notes with the new rows, as the commit then has them). The dashed line (`relationRuleHeight`, 8 points, `relationRuleDash`, drawn in fixed ink) shows only between a field or a fold and the first relation row, and is no row: `RowGeometry(block:hasLead:template:)` carries its offset to every site that places a row (`rect(_:)`, `row(at:)`, `ruleY`). Relation rows never fold and the fold counts fields only.
- **A row's name** is `RelationshipRow.name(in:)` (a typed name as typed; else on a from end or both sides merged the relationship type's title, else the one other type's title; on a to end the one other type's title, else the relationship type's title; else `untitled`, lower case: car's row of `owner` is `owner`, person's is `car`, and a type related to itself unnamed has one row called by the relationship type). A rename of the relationship type reaches rows, checks' words and templates as a rename of an end type does (`usesNames`, `RelationshipChange.rows`); the later of two relationship types' rows of one name adds ` · Employment`. A template that says a title is made again after a rename (`usesNames`); the template also goes when `relationships.revision` moves (two integer compares an ask), with the rows built from it.
- **On a note that is one** (`ShownRow.Content.related([ShownPill], more:)`, `.empty("add layer")` with none; `ShownRow.relation` is the row's key and `isFold` what `path == nil` used to mean): the pills of `relationships.relations(of:in:)` sorted by the name each shows (case aside, then the arrow's id), as many as fit with room for `+N`, the first always. A `ShownPill` is `{ target, via, arrow, state: EndReading, label, text, x, width, isLive, isWrong }`: every state of the far end is a pill, solid for `.note` and dashed for the rest (`.notAdmitted` the note's live name, the others the name the pill keeps, else `Deleted`, `Not available`, `Notes`, a blank), `isWrong` false until the check's slice. `destination` is where a click goes: the note while live, else `via`.
- **`›` and the row's card.** A row that holds a relationship ends in `›` after its pills and their `+N` (`RowBook.chevronRect(of:)`, public; `pills(of:among:width:)` leaves it `chevronRoom`; the row's `hug` includes it); an empty row has none. Return on a focused relation row opens the card of its relationships (`EditedRows.opensCard(_:)`: a row with more in it, or a relation row that holds a relationship) and the list when it holds none; Space and a click on the value elsewhere still list the notes the row may name. A press on the `›` is `RowFace.Part.chevron`: a read-only note opens the card to be read, any other begins the session in the row and `RowsView.begin` asks for the card on the scheduler's next turn (as `.more` does). The card is [below](#cards-and-the-form).
- **On a type's note** (`.relationType(direction:chips:more:)`): the name faint with the tick when inherited, and from the right `+N`, up to two chips, `→`, `←` or `↔`; `→ any` for an open side. Not edited.
- **What nothing announces.** `RowBook` observes `RelationshipIndex.onChange` (`relationsChanged`): `shown` is let go of for `notes` and for the entries whose `arrows` meet `arrows` (not `values`: the arrows only their own words, values or look changed, whose ends, types and fit are as they were, which `RelationshipIndex` names without their end notes, so a keystroke in an arrow's card or a row's card rebuilds no row); `layout` lays out `layout ∪ affected(by: layout)` (the end types, their instances near and far; a relationship drawn or deleted names none); `everything` lays out the end types before and after with their instances; a redraw for the rest. A pill is laid out with the name of the note it stands for, which nothing announces: `pillLabels` (by note) and `holders` (by note, the notes whose rows show a pill for it) are filled as rows are built, cleared with `shown` (capacity, a template dropped, a file put in place), and `letGo` compares what a commit changed with them, so a rename of a far note remakes exactly the rows that show it and a keystroke below its first line remakes none. The width of a pill is kept by what it is made of (`pillWidths`: the name it shows, whether it is live, the room the column gives it; the style is the relation pills' alone), so a hub's thousand rows measure its pill once and not once a row (an attachment, its font and its text, to read one number); it goes only over its capacity (`shownCapacity`). Every relation a row sorted is filed, the ones counted in `+N` too (any of their names decides which pills are shown). `viaHolders` (by pill, the notes whose rows read a far end through it) is filled and cleared the same way, and a commit that changes a pill lets go of those rows, though it changed no arrow. A type that comes or goes (`TypeChange.membership`), or a list that is no type's whose instances remain (a type taken away that notes still name), moves `RelationshipIndex.revision` (so the rows kept for a list go) and names its instances in `notes` and in `layout`, so they are laid out with the rows they have now.
- **Faces.** A click on a pill goes to `destination` (a read-only note goes too); on a type's relation row, anywhere in it, to the relationship type (`RowFace.Part.relationType`); on the value of an empty relation row begins the session in the row (`RowsView.Focus.relation`, which Tab and the arrows walk) and opens its list on the scheduler's next turn ([typing in a row](#typing-in-a-row)). A click on the dashed line is no face.
- **Zoomed out** a relation row is a name's bar (`RelationRowTemplate.barWidth`) and nothing else: a skeleton reads no relations, and the dashed line has no bar. Find does not search a relation row's pills.
- Tests: TypesTests' `relationRowTemplateTests`, `relationRowBookTests`, `relationShownTests`, `relationFaceTests`, `relationWriteTests` (over `RelationScene`: slips, bonds and relays; a row's presence writes no property of the note and no step), `rowValueTests`' pill cases; CompositionTests' `noteRelationRowsTests` (real notes and arrows, so not StickyNotesTests', which may not import Arrows: the arithmetic, the drawing, skeletons, a press on an empty row); the gallery's `relationStates` (`GalleryRelations.swift`); `Budget.relationRowsFrame`, `relationEmptyRowsFrame`, `relationSkeletonFrame` ([benchmarks](benchmarks.md)).

## Typing in a row

A note that is one has two places for the caret in its one editing session: the words, and a row. Types' `RowsView` (`makeRowsView`, which StickyNotes' `NoteEditor` adds over the rows) is the rows editor; Types imports TextEditing for it.

- **A typed value** (text, a number, a date, either of two such kinds) is edited by a `RowField` (`RowField.swift`): an `EditingTextView` on TextKit 2 with a container that never wraps, no autocorrect, smart quotes or dashes, replacement or spelling marks, no mentions and no slash commands (`@` and `/` are letters), laid over the value in a clip view as wide as the text hugs it (`fieldFocusMinimum` to the value's column, 4 points out each side) and slid so the caret stays in it. The view draws the outline (`fieldFocusWidth`, the accent). Its text is a text value as it is, a number or a date as the row shows it (`RowsView.text(of:kinds:)`); while it has the caret it holds what was typed (`9.50`), and the row shows the stored form (`9.5`) once it leaves. A value written from outside (an agent's draw, an undo) while the caret is there is shown in the field (the view remembers what it wrote and compares it to the stored value at each commit), and drops the field's own undo.
- **A line break** is never in the field's text: where a value has one (`\n`, `\r\n`, `\r`, `\u{2028}`, `\u{2029}`) the field has one space that carries `RowField.lineBreak`, the break it stands for; `RowField.value` puts each back. Three rules keep it apart: `typingAttributes` always answers the row's plain attributes (nothing typed beside a stand-in takes the attribute), every real break in the storage (only a paste or a drop brings one) is swapped for a stand-in after each change, and the field's undo carries attributes (it is plain text, and still does). A stand-in is deleted, selected and stepped over as the one space it shows as.
- **Each keystroke is a commit** through the one writer (`RowBook.write`: `FieldWriter.setTyped`, or `set` of `FieldText.value(from:asOneOf:)` for an either), so typing in one field is one undo step named for it (`Change Email`), another field another; the writer's run key does it. A row's commit ends the words' `Typing` step and the next word starts another (`undo.md`): accepted. Nothing is refused, clamped or changed (`FieldText`).
- **The check is told** which field is typed in: a field's becoming first responder sets `editor.fieldChecks?.typing = (shape, path)`; its resigning clears it, and only if `typing` still names that shape and path (the panel's boxes set it too, and only whoever sets it clears it), so does the view's going out of its window. A box, a choice and a summary never set it. The render skips the value of the typed row (the field draws it); `RowBook.check(of:)` takes the typed path out of an overridden answer as `FieldChecks.check` does of its own, so nothing under the caret is red.
- **Where a session starts**: `NoteEditor` takes `EditContext.startPoint` into the rows view (`begin(at:clickCount:)`): a press on a typed value gives the field the keyboard with the caret at the character pressed (a double-click, the word), one on a row with more in it focuses the row and asks for its card on the scheduler's next turn (only if the session is still this one), anywhere else (a name, the paper, a box, a choice) the words take it. `EditingView.firstResponder` is that responder. A click on the canvas that begins it is the faces' (`RowFace`, below).
- **Keys** (the table is in `RowsView.swift`): Tab and ⇧Tab, ↓ and ↑ walk every row, typed or not, folds included (Tab onto a row with more only focuses it); off the last row Tab goes on past the note (`RowsHost.tabbedOut`: the note's editor edits the next note, at its words, or with no other note comes back round to its own words), ⇧Tab and ↑ from the first go back to the words, ↓ on the last does nothing; Tab in the words focuses the first row, and ⇧Tab from the next note's words comes back to the last row (`focusLastRow`, the session `NoteHops` begins at its end), so Tab and ⇧Tab undo each other across notes; ↓ from the words' last line (the caret first moves as it always has: if it stayed on its line there was no line below) focuses the first row; Return and Escape end editing, ⌘↩ too, but Return and Space on a row with more ask for its card (`openCard`, whose `closed` focuses the row again and whose `tabbedOut` goes on); Space toggles a focused box and pops a focused choice's menu (`RowMenus.choice`: the options in the schema's order, a check by the stored one, a separator, `None` last), and Return pops it too (a box and a gone type's row keep Return as the fast way out: macOS checkboxes toggle on Space only); with a pill focused on a relation row (← → between the pills), Return or Space goes to its note, as a click on the pill does (`mentions.follow`), where Return with no pill focused opens the row's card; ⇧Return, in a row's field or on a focused row, adds a note beside the note and edits it (`RowsHost.addBeside`, which `NoteEditor` builds beside `tabbedOut` and the words' ⇧Return shares). Return, Space, Backspace and ⌘↩ act once for a press: `isARepeat` is ignored for them (a held Backspace on a two-pill row focuses the last pill and goes no further), where Tab and the arrows repeat. The keys are read through `KeyMeaning` (`RowsView.keyDown` as an event, `RowField.doCommand` as a text command), the one key table ([keyboard navigation](keyboard-navigation.md)). A row that is not typed is focused with the view itself as first responder, outlined (a choice with `▾` after its value), and **swallows every key it has no use for**: up the responder chain are the canvas's shortcuts, where Backspace deletes the selection, which is the note being edited (`noteRowEditingTests`).
- **VoiceOver** reads the rows: `RowsView` is a group (`Fields`) whose `accessibilityChildren` are one element per row, built when asked from `EditedRows`, each kept while its row is as it was made (`rowElements`; the frames made again) so the focused element is one of the children by identity, and the focused one made for its row alone (role by `RowClass`: a box a check box with 1 or 0, a choice a pop-up button, a typed row a text field, a row with more, a fold and a summary a button, a relation row a group with its pills as button children; label the field's name; value the shown value; help `needed` or `wrong` by the row's `RowLook`). `accessibilityFocusedUIElement` is the row's field, the focused pill, or the focused row, and `focusRow` and the pill moves post `focusedUIElementChanged`. A row's field is a `RowField` with the text field role and the row's name as its label ("price, text field, 12"). StickyNotesTests' key walk (`KeyWalk`) holds the stops, their names and the keys with no use at a stop.
- **While the note is edited** a press in the rows is the rows': on a typed value it moves the caret there, on a box it toggles it and focuses the row, on a choice it focuses the row and pops its menu, on a row with more it focuses the row and asks for its card; on a name, the lead or the paper nothing, and the caret stays where it was. The pointer is the I-beam over a typed value and the arrow over the rest.
- **If the rows change under a focused row** the focus stays with the row of the same field (a fold by which fold it is); if that row is gone or folded the caret goes back to the words. A note that becomes read-only ends the session (the core's) and the view goes with it.
- **A click on a value acts at once** (`RowFaces.swift`: `RowFace.Part.value`, `.name`; `valuePart(at:on:)` finds them from the shape's kept rows alone, so a hover builds nothing it does not find kept). It selects the note alone and then, by the row's `RowClass`: a typed value, a row with more in it and the fold begin the note's session (`editor.beginEditing(shape, at: face.at)`, so `NoteEditor` starts it in that row as above); a box writes the other of ticked and not (`RowBook.toggle`); a choice pops `RowMenus.choice` below its value; a row that reads `edited as JSON in the panel` asks `openCard` and begins nothing. A click on a field's name, or anywhere in a row off its value, only selects the note, also when it is the only one selected (where a click elsewhere on it edits its words); its double-click is `editor.open(…, clickCount: 2)`, which edits the words. A box and a gone type's chip are value faces only on the thing itself, and a gone type's chip (`.still`) has no value face (a click there is the note's); a relation row's value (`.relation`: none yet) begins the note's session in the row and opens its list. Their names, like every name, are `.name` faces, so a click on one only selects, also on the only selected note (a plain click on the note would edit its words). On a read-only note a typed value or a box or a choice shakes it and writes nothing (`beginEditing` and the writer refuse, `refuseChanges` for the menu), but a row with more opens its card to be read (`isReadOnly: true`, `closed` and `tabbedOut` nothing) and does not shake: reading is no try. `doubleClick` on a value is the click again (two clicks on a box are two writes), so the second press of a fast pair begins no edit by accident. The cursor over a value stays the arrow on the canvas; it is the I-beam only once the row is edited.

## Cards and the form

A row with more in it opens a card, the form it holds is values first, and the panel holds the same form.

- The form a card and the panel hold is values first: `FieldSheets.sheet(path:field:kind:values:check:book:types:mode:isReadOnly:presentation:drafts:open:back:branch:locale:)` and `foldSheet(_:…)` (`FieldSheet.swift`, pure; the view is `FieldFormView`'s) answer a `FieldSheet`, one level: `layout` (`rows`, `column`, `table(names)`, `pairs`, `checks`), `back` (`‹ shipping address`), the type's `chip`, an either's `branches` and the `branch` picked (the one the value fits, the first with none; `branch:` picks the other and its control is empty, even when the stored value is another shape: an object stored and Text picked shows nothing, not the object as JSON), `lines` and `stops` (reading order: the branch picked as a stop at line -1, then each line's cells that take the keyboard, or the line as a whole, column -1, for a check line, a triangle and a row with more that is its line's only cell; words alone, a kind's on a type's note, are none). A `FieldLine` and a `FieldCell` say their members on them (a line's `isPair`, the list being pairs as drawn, is what an either of pairs and text with nothing stored cannot say by its kind). A list is dense, so the lines of a list, a table and pairs come from a `FieldDraft` (`slots`: `stored(key:)` for an item or a pair by its key, `blank` for the waiting line and a line whose text was deleted, `pair(name:value:taken:)` for half a pair or a name another has), which the form keeps for each level by its path: `storedIndex(ofLine:)` is the stored slots before it, so a line that is not stored has the path of the item it would be, `reconcile(with:)` follows what something else wrote (a list whose count differs is all stored again with one blank waiting; pairs lose a gone key's line and gain a new key's as the last stored line, before the waiting one; a half-typed pair stays), pairs show in name order as made and `init(_:waiting:pairs:)` has no blank when read-only (an empty read-only level reads `None`). On a type's note (`mode: .kinds`) a level's lines are its members' names and kinds (`typeLabel`), a `›` (`more`) only where `opensOnType`; a list of groups or of a type's fields shows its item's members as rows, pairs two rows (`name`, `value`). In the panel (`presentation: .panel`) a field is a line with a triangle and its summary; open (`open[path]`, else open while a problem is at or under it) the next level is under it a depth down, a member with more has its own triangle, a list of groups is a line an item (summary and triangle, opening to its rows) and the `add` line, an either shows the branch its value fits, `Other` is the JSON box. `FieldSheets.draft(at:kind:values:isReadOnly:drawnAsPairs:)` is the default draft. A `note` cell is a stop only while editable; no field makes one yet.
- `FieldFormView` is the form drawn and keyed: AppKit, one `NSView`, so the card hands it to the core directly (nothing the canvas hosts is SwiftUI) and the panel's `FieldForm` is a thin face of it. ## The one writer

`editor.fieldWriter` (`FieldWriter`, `FieldWriter.swift`) is the only thing that changes a shape's `fieldValues`: a row, the card, the fold and the panel all go through it, so a field is changed one way and its step is named one way. Called from key and mouse handlers, never inside a transaction or while an undo is applied (`transact` refuses both).

- Its door. `set(_:at:of:step:)` (`setTyped` is `set(FieldText.value(from:as:), …)`, `remove(at:)` is `set(nil, …)`), `rename(_:to:under:of:)` (a pair's value moves to its new key in one commit; false for an absent, empty, same or taken name and for the root) and `removeUnnamed(_:of:)`. All five end in one `apply`: a path that starts with no key (the root included), a shape on no board of the file and a kind that is not `typeable()` are false; a shape that can't change (`isReadOnly(inFile:)`, on any board) is `editor.refuseChanges(to:)` (services hear it, Selection shakes it) and false, before any transaction, so no empty step; else one `transact` with one `update(inFile:)` of the record. The writer reads and writes whichever board the shape is on (`values(of:)` is `shape(inFile:)`'s), so a far arrow's values are written in the same step as the card that edits them (a row's card). A write that changes nothing is true, and `Transaction.update` keeps a record only if it differs, so there is no commit, no step, and what there was to redo stays. `editor` is internal: the panel builds a `FieldFormContext` from it.
- Its steps. `.ownRun` is named `FieldName.stepName` of the field the key names on the shape's types (`Change Shipping Address`; a key no field names by the same word split, `Change Nickname`) and coalesces by `FieldRunKey { shape, field }` of the top-level field: typing in one field, however many commits and at whatever depth under it, is one step; another field, `UndoHistory.seal()` (the host seals on every mouse-down and move of the keyboard) or a commit from elsewhere ends the run. `.card(key:)` is nameless and joined by the card's key. `removeUnnamed` is `Remove Nickname` (the key's words, capitalised) and never coalesced.
- The title is set in the same `update` (`titling`, last): see [A title from a field](#a-title-from-a-field). Nothing else belongs in it.

## The one writer of field definitions

`editor.fieldDefiner` (`FieldDefiner`, `Fields/FieldDefiner.swift`) is the only thing that edits the fields a type defines (its root `properties`, and `required` in step): a field's name, its kind and a choice's options. Nothing else writes a field definition by hand; the model writes a schema from the type's words, and the Schema tab is typed JSON. The relationship type's card is its face; any type's could be. Shaped like `FieldWriter`: called from key and mouse handlers, never inside a transaction or while an undo is applied.

- The edits are pure, in `SchemaFields` and `DefinedKind` (`Fields/FieldDefinitions.swift`; no editor): a schema as `SchemaValue` in, the schema after out (itself when nothing would change, nil when it can't be done). The five kinds are `DefinedKind` (`text`, `number`, `date`, `yesNo`, `choice`; `label` is `Text`, `Number`, `Date`, `Yes or no`, `Choice`, the words `FieldKind.typeLabel` uses) and each is written as the schema `FieldKindReader` reads back as it: `{"type": "string"}`, `{"type": "number"}`, `{"type": "string", "format": "date"}`, `{"type": "boolean"}`, `{"type": "string", "enum": [...]}`. A choice starts with an empty `enum`, which `FieldKindReader` reads as `Other` until the first option is added (`DefinedKind(reading:)` still says Choice). `DefinedField` (`key`, `name`, `kind`, `options`, `isRequired`) is a field as the card lists it; `definer.fields(of:)` reads them.
- The door: `addField(named:kind:to:)` (answers the key), `renameField(_:to:in:)`, `changeKind(of:to:in:)`, `removeField(_:from:)`, `addOption(_:to:in:)`, `renameOption(at:to:of:in:)`, `removeOption(at:of:in:)` (an option is named by its place among the field's options in the order written; a `null` in the `enum` is no option). Each is one `transact`, named `Add Field`, `Rename Field`, `Change Kind`, `Remove Field`, `Add Option`, `Rename Option`, `Remove Option` (`FieldDefiner.Step`), and false (nil for the add) when nothing was written: a shape that holds no schema; a schema that isn't a JSON object, or `properties` that isn't one (no shake: a no); an empty name or option; a field that isn't there; an edit that changes nothing (the same kind, name or option), which makes no commit and no step. A type that can't change is `editor.refuseChanges(to:)` before any transaction (services hear it, Selection shakes it).
- Keys and names. A typed name's key is camelCase in plain ASCII for a Latin script (accents dropped), a script's own letters otherwise, as the sync is told to write names (`SchemaFields.key(forName:)`: `since`, `primaryOwner`), unique among the type's properties (`since2`). The name typed is the field's `title` unless the key's own words (`FieldName.words`) already say it; a rename keeps the key and sets the `title`, taking it off when the key's words say the new name. A kind change replaces the field's schema with the kind's, keeping `title` and `description` (a choice that becomes another kind loses its options). Removing a field takes its name out of `required` (the member goes when that empties it); a note's values under the key stay, as a value no field names.
- The write is the whole schema, re-laid out (`SchemaValue.pretty`: two spaces, members in written order, an array of scalars on one line, the way the starter is laid out), as the shape's `schema`. Hand formatting elsewhere in the schema is not kept. Numbers are doubles. Rows, instances' rows, the field book and the check follow by the paths a schema edit already takes (`TypeIndex.onChange`); the sync hears it as it hears one typed in the Schema tab, and asks nothing of a type whose words are empty.

## Checks

What a note's values are checked against, and what the check says. The whole answer is a `FieldCheck` ([A note that is one](#a-note-that-is-one)); `FieldChecks` (`editor.fieldChecks`) makes and keeps it; two rules in `TypeValidationPlugin` read it (and `:incomplete`, Validation's word, reads what the first says: [queries](queries.md)). A note's relationship rows are checked too: a Needed row left empty is needed and a row that breaks Only one is wrong, at the row ([relationships](relationships.md#the-check-on-a-notes-rows)). Needed is amber and is an issue of Validation's third level, `.incomplete` (valid, and shown nowhere on the board: no ring, no Issues line, no table-of-contents dot, not `:invalid`, but `:incomplete`, and a run says it of a shape it did not write); wrong is red and an error; a warning (a type whose JSON refers to a type that is gone, or whose `$title` titles nothing) is an orange ring and an Issues line, no red and not `:invalid`.

- The one pure part is `FieldChecker` (one for each distinct list of types, over a `FieldBook`, no editor: a type's name and schema text are the book's, `FieldBook.name(of:)` and `schemaText(of:)`): it runs the core's validator (`SchemaValidator`, `formats: .standard`) over the list's composed schema without its `allOf` (`main`) and over each type's rule object (one validator each, so a problem on the root names the rule's type), each made checkable first, and says each violation as a problem of a class. A `$ref` to a type is served by a resolver (`Resources`): `urn:uuid:T` is T's checkable resolved schema with T's own root `$defs` and `definitions` laid in (read from the last good JSON), so a ref with a fragment and the `#/` refs inside are checked too; a type that is gone is served as nothing and recorded as gone (a type that comes back makes the checker stale). `isCurrent()` re-reads the list's resolution and each served input, and compares them: an edit that changes nothing the check reads (a root `description`, whitespace, text broken mid-edit) leaves the checker and every answer it made standing.
- `FieldSchemas.checkable` (public, pure) rewrites a schema on a copy, at every place that holds a schema: T1 drops a `pattern` or a `patternProperties` entry ICU cannot read (the type's fault, not the note's); T2 drops `additionalProperties`, `propertyNames`, `patternProperties`, `unevaluatedProperties` and `maxProperties` from an object with `properties` (a value no field names is never wrong, at any depth; `minProperties` stays, as an unnamed member can only help meet it), and T2b an `additionalProperties: false` from anywhere (a pairs field's schema `additionalProperties` and its counts are its own rules); T3 turns `dependentSchemas` into an `if/then` (the core ignores it); T4 rewrites an `if` that has `properties` into `{if: presence, then: {if: X, then: T, else: E}}`, so a condition asks only once the fields it tests have values (an empty Order is not asked for a gift note, and its `else` is not applied to a note that never said; an `if` with only `required` is the author's); T5 turns an `anyOf` or `oneOf` of required-only branches into "if none holds, ask for every field of them" (and for a `oneOf`, the author's `oneOf` only when one holds, so two is the validator's own violation at the node). Limits: an `if` through `anyOf`, `not` or `$ref`, and an applicator's branch under an object that has no `properties` of its own, keep the validator's reading.
- Classes. `required` and `dependentRequired` are needed, at the missing member, dropped when no field is there (a name no type has, a path under an `Other`) and for a yes or no (an unticked box is a value); inside an absent group nothing is asked. `type`, `enum`, `const`, the bounds, lengths, `pattern`, `format`, the list limits, `minProperties`, `propertyNames`, `not`, `anyOf`, `oneOf` and `false` are wrong, at the value's path (the list's, the object's, the member's); a path no row has is clamped to the deepest row above, and dropped when even its first step names no field. A needed list is needed while empty (never `minItems`: an empty list is no value); one filled short of `minItems` is wrong. A violation at the root is a rule across fields: wrong, keyword `rule`, no row. `$ref` (a gone type, a loop) is dropped. Problems are deduped (a field asked for by the root and by a rule is one; two wrongs at one path are two), capped at 100 keeping at least one of each class (so `isWrong` and `isIncomplete` never change), and sorted by row, then path (a key by its text, an index by its number, the root last).
- Words (`FieldCheckWords`): a wrong problem is its place and the validator's own tail (`line items › Plate › quantity is 0, and should be at least 1`: one wording across the app; the place is the rows' names, an item named by its first plain value cut to 24 characters, else `item 2`, a pairs key as written), a needed one `<place> is needed`, a root one `Order rule: the note <tail>`, a refused pair name `labels › Size 2 isn’t a name labels allows`. `SchemaViolation.detail` is the tail.
- `FieldChecks` (`@Observable`, `revision` is what a view tracks; an `EditorService` in an extension with its own `boardDidChange`): `check(of:)` is the answer with `typing`'s path left out, `fullCheck(of:)` the whole (queries); both `.clean` for no `is`, a type's own note, an opaque shape, and a list none of whose types is a type. It keeps one `FieldChecker` for each distinct list (at most 1,024, then all dropped) and one answer for each shape asked. An answer is never invalidated; it is trusted while the record's own list and values are the ones it was made from (two compares that end at the storage's identity) and its checker is current (`FieldBook.revision` against the checker's stamp, rebased when a re-read finds it equal). A note with no values takes the checker's one check of no values, kept with the rename count it was made at: one that holds a root problem (a rule across fields that fails on an empty note) says its rule's type by name, so a rename makes it again. `documentDidChange` reads no record and checks nothing: it forgets what left the file, moves `revision` if a changed shape has an answer (a move changes no answer), and clears everything for a document put in place.
- `typing` (`(shape, path)?`): set by whoever has the caret in a field when it gets it and cleared by them when it leaves, one at a time. The field's path and below are left out of `check(of:)`, so a half-typed value is never red and never rings. Setting it moves `revision`, has Validation check the old and the new shape again (`ValidationEngine.recheck`) and redraws. It is also cleared when its shape goes from the file, when that shape's editing ends (the ring is Validation's, drawn once editing ends) and on a board switch or a file put in place; a board switch tells Validation too (`recheck`), which files the shape under the board it keeps, so the answer stashed with that board, made with the path left out, is made again when the board is taken back.
- A type's change reaches shapes through `TypeIndex.onChange`, told once the index has the commit: if only names changed (`chainRevision` stands) only the answers that name a type (a root problem) are checked again, so a rename costs what its chips do; otherwise `TypeReferences` (the core's `ReverseIndex`: the types each type's text refers to, both ways, a gone target kept so its return reaches the types waiting for it) is updated, and the reached set is the changed types, their instances, the types that refer to them, and so on to a fixpoint; it is what the rule over values answers Validation's `affected` with once every service has the commit (`FieldChecks.affected(by:)`, kept by the commit's revision; the arrows whose sentence quotes a renamed note too), but not an instance whose answer's checker is still current once asked (the edit changed nothing the check reads of it: a description, whitespace, a field it doesn't have) and whose answer names no type: each reached list is made current inside the commit and the instance keeps its answer, so an edit that reads nothing costs the types alone (2.7 ms over 10,000 instances, against 57 to 73 when all were named). Which shapes are reached matters only to Validation: the answers are found stale by themselves. `goneReferences(of:)` is a type's refers-to-a-gone-type list.
- The check of a run (`FieldChecks.check(of: record, reading:)`, public; `FieldClass.word` says a class to an agent): a shape's values against its types as a `LayoutReading` has the file, which for a transaction's `shapes.reading` is the file as the transaction leaves it (draw's script, a paste, a dry run), where the committed answers would be of the file before. The committed answer (`fullCheck`) when the reading is the editor's, and when neither the shape nor any type it reads is `reading.isChanged` (the list, what each builds on and the types their JSON refers to, over and over, from the committed index and `TypeReferences`, kept per list until the chain moves; a type the file never had and the reading lacks too differs in nothing): a restated note costs a lookup. The kept checker when only the shape's values are new, checking them and keeping no answer (`typing` is not left out: it is the full check). Else a `FieldBook` over the reading (`init(reading:record:shared:)`: it resolves each list over `ReadingTypes` for its own life, reads a type's name from Titles and its text from the shape, shares the editor's book's levels, whose `level(readFrom:of:)` is `RowBook`'s read of a type mid-transaction, lifted) and a `FieldChecker` over it, kept in `scratch` by list with what it read (each type's text, title and `is` list, and the types its `$ref`s met) and found again while the reading still has those the same, at most 16, let go by the next commit or board change; `scratchMade` counts them in debug builds. A type the reading deleted is a gone type's: nothing. For an arrow of a relating kind the answer also has the relation part over the run's own graph (`RelationshipIndex.graph(in:)`, [relationships](relationships.md#the-service-fieldsrelationshipindexswift)): a misfit, an end that cannot be read, a free end, for what the run wrote (the committed part is left out of whichever answer it comes from).
- An arrow of a relationship type carries one more part, apart from the validator's: what its ends say ([relationships](relationships.md#the-check-on-an-arrow-checksrelationcheckswift-checksrelationwordsswift-fieldchecks)). A `FieldProblem` has a `locus` (a field, an end of the arrow or a row), and `placeName` and `pointer` are the one mapping Validation, draw and the queries say it by.
- The rules (`TypeValidationPlugin`): the one over values asks `FieldChecks.affected(by:)` for its `affected:` (above); a wrong value is an error and a warning (`FieldClass.warning`: an end that cannot be read, or is not there) is orange, each with the problem's message, the row's key as `field` and its JSON Pointer as `path` (both nil for the root); a needed one is an issue of the third level, `ValidationIssue.incomplete` with the same message, field and path (a needed field with no value, a Needed relationship row left empty; the caret's field left out of a wrong one, as nothing red should turn up under the caret, but not of a needed one: what a shape still needs shows nowhere, so `:incomplete` lists a note while its empty needed field has the caret); a type whose JSON refers to a gone type carries a warning, `Refers to “Address”, which is gone.`, never the instances, and so does one whose `$title` titles nothing ([A title from a field](#a-title-from-a-field); it asks the `FieldBook`, so draw, which has none, says nothing of it). Where the context has no editor (draw's check, which `registry.validationIssues(of:in:)` runs over the transaction's shapes) the value rule asks the service through `context.reading` and `check(of:reading:)`, so a wrong value is in draw's warnings, against a type on the board or drawn in the same script.
- Known limit: the Issues tab refreshing when a caret leaves a row (the ring and the dot do, immediately; the tab on the next time the panel is made).

- **What a run's writes reach.** The rule over values has a `touches:` ([validation](validation.md)): `FieldChecks.touched(by:reading:)` (`Checks/RunTouches.swift`) names, for writes that have not committed, the shapes whose values' or relationships' issues they may change without writing them, so `canvas draw` warns of what it breaks on a shape it did not write ([command line](command-line.md#what-a-run-brings-to-shapes-it-did-not-write)). For values: a type the run restates (its text, title, own list, mark, or its coming or going) reaches what `typesChanged` reaches after a commit (`FieldChecks.reached(byTypes:)`: its instances, and the types whose JSON refers to it, over and over), near and far, and the shapes whose answer says a type's name; an instance the run does not restate whose held value the restated fields refuse (an `enum` narrowed) is then an error of that note, said on the type's line, and a field made needed is what each instance still needs (the third level, `severity` `incomplete`, said once on the type's line for each instance the run does not restate, near and far; past twenty, counted). The check of such a note is `check(of:reading:)`, which reads the type as the run leaves it (a scratch checker over the reading). A type's own warnings (a `$ref` to a type that is gone, a `$title` that titles nothing) are said over a reading by `typeWarnings(of:reading:)`, which the third rule asks where it has no editor: the types gone are those the reading no longer has as types, named as the committed index knows them. The relationships' part is in [relationships](relationships.md#what-a-runs-writes-reach). The compiling of relationship types the run redid is walked only when the writes asked of include a type or an arrow at one, so asked of each statement's writes alone (to find a warning's line) a statement that writes neither names nothing through it. ## A title from a field

A type's `"$title": {"x-canvas-from": "name"}`, a member of its root `properties`, says which field titles a note that is one. The most specific type's wins whole (an Employee gets Person's; one that writes its own replaces it, even naming a field only Person defines). The note's real title is a copy of that field's value as its row shows it, never read live from the field.

- `SchemaDirectives` (`JSON/SchemaDirectives.swift`: the readings under the chain use it too): `isField(key)` (no `$` first) and `name(key)` (`title` for `$title`, nil for a field and a bare `$`); `Chain` uses it, and so does everything that counts or lists fields as the slices go on, so a `$` key is never a field.
- `TitleSource.of(resolved)`: the `title` directive's `x-canvas-from` field, found through the chain (`resolved.field(named:)`), when its kind can title: `titles(_:)` is text, a number, a date, a date-time or a choice (`PlainKind`), never a yes or no or anything that is not plain (a list, a group, another type's fields, pairs, an either even of two plain kinds, what no control suits). Nil for a `$title` that is no object, names nothing, names no field (a name only `allOf` or `patternProperties` gives is none) or names one that can't title: nothing is then titled and no pill hides, never a crash or a partial title. `problem(_:writtenBy:)` says why for the type that wrote the winning directive, and nil for a child that inherits it. A `$title` inside a group's `properties` is a member of that group, never a directive. A field a more specific type restates as something that can't title makes the source nil for its notes, silently.
- `FieldBook.titleSource(types:)` and `titleSource(of:)`: `TitleSource.of` the list's resolution, kept beside `kept` and emptied with it. `titleSource(of:)` is nil for a type's own note, **before** it reads the `is` list: Employee's type note holds `[Person]`, and without the guard it would be titled by Person's `$title`.
- `$title` is never a field. The chain, `RowList`, `FieldsPanel` and the schema the check validates read `Resolved.fields`, which has none. At the root, `SchemaDocument.fields` filters the resolver's (so "N fields" and TypeSync's two `fields.isEmpty` tests don't count it: a type holding only `$title` and words was never answered), `SchemaReadings` leaves `$` keys out of the names a root `required` may use (`“$title” is required, but isn’t a property.`), the `required` completions (`TypeCompletions.requirable`) skip them at the root, and `SchemaTarget.locate` answers nil for a pointer into `/properties/<a $ key>` so a highlight of it is dropped (the net under the prompt telling the model to link nothing there). In a group's `properties` a `$` key is an ordinary member, and a `"$title"` member of `fieldValues` is a value no field names (dimmed, never wrong).
- `FieldTitling` (`FieldTitling.swift`, pure, the locale passed): `shown` (the value as its row shows it, folded to one line; nil for none and for a list or an object under the key), `fits` (80 code points, as Validation counts), `retitle(held:old:new:_:)` and `firstTitle`. `retitle` in order: the field's own value did not change, leave (a write to another field never titles); no title held, write the new value if it fits; the title held is the old value as shown, write the new one if it fits and else take the title off (a title never outlives the value it was copied from); anything else is a title set by hand, leave. A title typed by hand that equals the value when the field is next written is the same as a copy, and follows from then on.
- The write: `FieldWriter.apply`'s one `update` reads the old values from the record inside the closure (never `values(of:)` before it), changes them, then asks `titling`, which answers nil (leave) unless the kind holds a title (`registry.properties(for:)` has `TitleProperty.key`: a set on another kind would be dropped silently, and an arrow is typeable and holds none) and `fieldBook.titleSource(of:)` is there (never a type's own note); then `FieldTitling.retitle(held:old:new:_:)` with the stored title (`TitleProperty.folded(record[TitleProperty.self])`: a title a kind derives is not a title held). A row, the fold's card, the panel's Fields and the form all come through `apply`, so all of them title with no code of their own, in the field's own step (`Change Name`, or the card's held step): undo puts back the value and the title together. A read-only shape is refused before the transaction, title included. Draw never calls the writer and titles by statement; paste and duplicate copy a title with its values (both then carry Titles' duplicate warning); a script or test that sets `FieldValuesProperty` titles nothing; a type's `$title` or its field changing re-titles nobody (a title is a copy).
- `/is-a`, Is a ▸ and `newType` all end in `addTypeLink`, which in its one `update` titles a note once: when its title is empty, it is no type's own note, its kind holds a title and the new list's source (`fieldBook.titleSource(types:)`, from the committed index, which has the type added) gives a value that fits (`firstTitle`). The same step (`Add Type`), on any board, once for each shape of a selection. A note with a title is left alone, whatever it is; a type added to a titled note changes no title. `removeTypeLink` writes `is` alone: the title stays, and with no type to source it, it reads as set by hand.
- A `$title` that titles nothing is a warning on the type that wrote it (`TitleSource.problem(_:writtenBy:)`: the winning directive's `source` is this type, so an Employee inheriting Person's bad one says nothing and Person's note does), `field: "schema"`, joining the third rule of `TypeValidationPlugin` beside the gone-reference warning (so no new line in the registry report). It is asked of `fieldBook.fields(types: [type])`, the type with what it builds on, so a field Person defines counts for Employee's own `$title`; a change to a type it builds on asks again through `FieldChecks.typesChanged` (a child is an instance of its parent). Words (`FieldCheckWords.title`): `“$title” names no field.`, `“$title” names “nme”, which isn’t a field.`, `“$title” names “address”, which isn’t text, a number, a date or a choice.` Without an editor (draw's check) there is no `FieldBook` and it says nothing; an instance shows nothing.
- The Schema tab completes the directive at the root (`TypeCompletions`, over `JSONCompleter`): a key in the root's `properties` (`path == [properties]`) offers `$title` when there is none, written `"$title": {"x-canvas-from": ""}` with the caret in the quotes and the list asked again; in `properties/$title` only `x-canvas-from` belongs (`belongs` drops the keywords of a property's schema the meta-schema would offer), written `"x-canvas-from": ""`, asking again; its value (`offers(at:)` beside `required` and `$ref`: the schema lists no values there, and the engine asks the client where it offers nothing) is the root's fields that can title (`TitleSource.titles` of `FieldKindReader.kind`, a `#` reference read through one step), each with its type (`SchemaFieldType.label`), none from a group's `properties`. A field inherited from another type is not offered (the tab sees this type's text); typed by hand it works through the chain. The seam is the engine's one generic hook for a key the schema doesn't list, `JSONCompleter(keys:)` returning `JSONCompletionKeyOffer`s (key, detail, the value it starts as and where the caret lands in it, `reopens`), appended after the schema's keys, those the object holds left out, written as `keyItem` writes one (the key alone where its colon is there).
- No second pill. A note that is one shows no title pill for a title copied from its field: the type's chip above it and the name in its row. `FieldBook.fieldTitled(record, title:)` is the test: `titleSource(of:)` is there and its title is that field's value as shown (a lookup by the list, one `FieldTitling.shown`, a compare; nil for a title set by hand and for a type's own note). Types registers a second pill look, on the kinds that hold `is` (`forShapes(holding: IsAProperty.self) { $0.titlePillLook { … } }`, the three-argument form, [titles](titles.md)) that hides the pill when the record is no type's note, has an `is` list, its kind draws rows (`RowBook.kinds`, the kinds with `typeRows`: a property of the kind alone, so **a folded name's pill stays hidden**: the fold is a place rows show, and a type gaining a ninth row must not bring the pill back on every instance) and `fieldTitled` answers. A kind that draws no rows keeps its pill: nothing else says the name. The guards that cost nothing come first, since Find's build asks the look of every titled shape. A cover is asked with no services, so zoomed out the cover still says the name. A title by hand shows its pill as any title does; typing the name into the field to match hides it again and the title follows from then on. Find leaves out a hidden pill's title text (the row's hit, `FieldFindPlugin`, is the same text, marked on the fold where the name is folded); a type edit that drops `$title` or changes its field names the instances (`FieldFindPlugin.affected`), so the title's text comes back with no commit on the note.
- The label is kept by shape and made again only for a shape a commit added or changed other than by moving; a type's change that names no instance (its `$title`, or the kind of the field it names) leaves labels stale, so `RowBook.typesChanged`, where it has the instances the chain's move reached (`index.affected(by:)`), calls `editor.adornmentsDidChange(for:)` on them: it forgets by id and reads no record, and the labels in view are made again at the next draw. Nothing per frame.

## Agents

What draw, queries and `--ask` read and write of Types, through Types' own `TypeNamesPlugin` and `FieldValuesPlugin` (`TypeNames.swift`, `FieldValuesDraw.swift`), which fill Draw's points, and `TypeQueriesPlugin` (`TypeQueries.swift`), which fills Queries' ([command line](command-line.md), [queries](queries.md)): each a plugin value of its own, so a list without the draw engine or the query engine leaves its words out, and neither engine knows Types.

- In draw and queries (`TypeNamesPlugin`, `TypeQueriesPlugin`): an edge's `is` tells two arrows of one pair apart (`registrar.drawEdgeIdentity(IsAProperty.key)`, Draw's `DrawEdgeIdentities`: Types names it, not Connections, which makes the edges); `is: "Employee card"` on a shape or an edge is a `drawTextFormat` for `TypeLink.mediaType`: the title names a type as draw finds titles (`context.shape(called:admitting:)`, file-wide, or drawn in the same run, `writing` carrying the keys the statement writes, so a note given `schema:` there is a type), and what it admits is `TypeIndex.canBeNamed(_:registry:writing:)`, the one pure function over a record and the registry that says which shapes a script may name in `is` (a type that is no pill), anything else refused "which isn't a type or a shape"; it writes the id (the entry's coded form), and refuses the shape itself (`DrawTextContext.owner`, the shape whose attribute it is: "can't be its own type") and a shape already built on the owner (a cycle through shapes of the file, by the index's `graph.reaches`, read through `DrawTextContext.service`: "is already built on this shape, which would make a cycle"; a cycle between shapes a single script draws isn't checked, `reaches` is cycle-safe). A quoted value for a list of text is a list of one (Draw's `KindVocabulary.value(of:)`), several are JSON. A string in `fieldValues: {…}` is only text: `"@[Ann Lee]"` is ten characters, with no lookup and no warning ([command line](command-line.md)). Queries: `:is-a(S)` is `queryRelation(… reads: .file, transitive: true, namesTypes: true)` over `TypeFacts`, a `QueryFileFact` of `TypeIndex.graph`, a `TypeGraph` (`links` and `names`, copy on write, kept by the index beside its own tables, so a query takes it up in O(1)); `related` is `TypeGraph.parents(of:)` (a shape's types, later first), and `TypeIndex.lineage(of:)` is the same graph's `lineage(of:)`, the order rows are read in, where `:is-a` asks only reachability (`graph.parents`, transitive: no order). `:edge-to(S, is-a)` stays direct. A `queryTextFormat` for `TypeLink.mediaType` reads an entry as its type's title (`TypeGraph.names`; a gone type stays its id), so `[is~="Person"]` and a row's `is` read titles; `.Person` is refused toward `:is-a("Person")`.
- `FieldChecks.check(of:reading:)` ([checks](#checks)) is what a run's shapes are checked by. `FieldChecks.drawn(_:written:reading:)` adds what a statement wrote: `DrawnFields` is the `check`, the `unnamed` members and the types' titles in the list's order (`Untitled` for one with none). `FieldBook.unnamed(in:types:)` (public) walks a written object beside `fields(types:)`, `members(of:)` and `item(of:)`: a key no field names is an `UnnamedMember` (its `FieldPath` and the row it sits under, nil at the top; a list's item names its list's row), never looked into, so a member under it is not listed twice; it looks into a group, another type's fields and a list's items and no further (pairs, `Other`, an either, a plain value, a `$ref` to a gone type or to a part of one); a member written `null` is never one. `drawn` leaves `unnamed` empty while any type of the shape's chain is gone, however far up (`Resolved.gone`: its values are kept and not checked) and for a shape with no types; `FieldValuesPlugin` says that one itself.
- `FieldValuesPlugin`'s one `drawReports` closure declares the members `is`, `endOf`, `fits` and `relates` and adds them (`RelationshipDrawReport`, `Engines/RelationshipDraw.swift`) to the row of an arrow a run draws, restates or deletes, and of a relationship type, with the warnings of an arrow that says nothing: what they say, and when, is in [relationships](relationships.md#what-agents-are-told). A misfit is drawn and its `problems` come from `FieldChecks.drawn` over the run's own graph.
- `FieldClass.word` is how a problem's class is said (`needed`, `wrong`, `warning`). A `FieldProblem` becomes a Queries `ShapeProblem` in the word plugins, `FieldValuesPlugin` and `TypeQueriesPlugin` (`field` the path's first key, `path` its JSON Pointer, both nil for a rule across fields). `:incomplete` (Validation's word, `ValidationQueriesPlugin`) lists a shape's issues of the third level as its rows' `problems`, `class` `needed`: the ones Types' rule over values handed Validation from this check.
- `FileFields` (`editor.fileFields`, `FieldBook.fileFields`, kept until `chainRevision` moves) is what every type of the file says of the members a note's values can hold, by key at each depth: the union of each type's own fields (`fields(types: [t])` with `source` `t`; a `$`-key is a directive, never one), a group's members, another type's fields' and a list's items'; a step past a plain value leads to nothing, past pairs, `Other`, an either, a `$ref` to a type that is gone or to a part of one, or beyond six levels, to anything. `unknown(_:)` is what `TypeQueriesPlugin` answers `queryPathCheck(attribute: fieldValues)` with: the first member of a path nothing of the file can hold and the members that could be there, nil while any note lists a type that is gone (`TypeIndex.listsAGoneType`: its fields are unknown, and its notes' values are kept), so a typo in `[fieldValues.stauts]` is the query's `unknown_field` warning.
- `FileFields.text(for: question)` is what `--ask` is told, as `<fields>` (`TypeQueriesPlugin`, `queryAskFact(tag: "fields")`): a line for each titled type (made once, kept with `FileFields`): `Title: key "name" (kind), …`, `Title builds on Parent: …`, `Title: no fields`; a relationship type's line says `relates A to B` after its name (and `builds on`), A and B the titles of the types at its from ends and to ends joined by ` or ` (the ends its arrows are judged by, `sides(of:)`: one with none of its own says those it takes, while its `; rows:` clause is its own ends' alone, `ownSides(of:)`), `anything` for a side no end names, `an untitled type` for an untitled end type, and ends with `; rows: Person "jobs" (only one), Company "staff" (needed)`: for each row its ends give that has a name typed on an end's arrow or a flag, the end type, the name in quotes and the flags in parentheses (a derived name is not sent; no clause when no row has either), the end types' titles and the typed names among the line's words for ranking; an untitled relationship type is told as `#` and its id, an untitled plain type not at all; the quoted name only where the field's own `title` is another than the key's words (`FieldName.words`); the kinds in `FileFields`' words; at most 60 fields a type (`, +N more`) and 8,000 characters, types ranked by a word the question shares with the title, a field's key or its name, then by how many notes are one, however far (`TypeIndex.allInstances(of:)`: never a type), then by title, whole types dropped from the end and the rest named on `and N more types: A, B` while that fits, then `…`. Never a value, and of an arrow only what is typed on a relationship type's end and its flags. Made again when `TypeIndex.stamp` moves (a type's schema, name or own types) or the relationship index's `revision` does (an end drawn, taken away or re-aimed, its words or rule edited, a mark flipped): `FieldBook.fileFieldsKept` holds `(stamp, revision)`.
- `FileFields.instancesText(for: question)` is what `--ask` is told of which shapes are of which type, as `<instances>` (`TypeQueriesPlugin`, `queryAskFact(tag: "instances")`, after `<fields>`), since without it the model took every note for a type's (it called "coaches" unanswerable for want of a coach type, and put plain notes in a Student type that fit them): a line for each titled plain type with a shape of it, however far (`allInstances`, as `:is-a` finds them), `Pet: "Bubbles", "Finn", and 4 more` (titles in quotes as the language writes one, by title, 20 a line), in `<fields>`' order of types; then `Of no type:` and the titled notes of the kinds that can be of a type that are of none and are no type, on every board the app can show, those sharing a word with the question first, then in the boards' order, 20 and `and N more`; 4,000 characters at most, whole lines; empty with no titled plain type. Read afresh at each question (one walk of the file's shapes on the main actor: a note given a type, or renamed, moves no stamp). Titles only, which `<titles>` sends already: never a value. QueryPromptTests hold it on the story, and the eval's school board (`QuerySchoolBoard`) what the model does with it ([queries](queries.md)).
- `FileFields.connectionsText(for: question)` is what `--ask` (and every translation: a reference's words, `/query`) is told of how arrows join the file's shapes, as `<connections>` (`TypeQueriesPlugin`, `queryAskFact(tag: "connections")`, after `<instances>`), since without it the model couldn't follow a phrase across an arrow ("the member's district" went from 0 of 4 answers to 4 of 4 in the chips' live check): a line for each pair of ends of the file's connections (Topology's resolved snapshot, an arrow through a pill read as into what it stands for), `Person → Company: 2 Employment arrows`, each end its first type's title (`an untitled type` for one with none), else its kind, then how many and the arrows' own type when they have one; ends that are types themselves left out; most first, then by name, 30 lines at most; empty with no connection. TypesTests' `FileFieldsTests` and QueryPromptTests hold it; the prompt's bullet for it is `Shipped/ask-prompt.txt`'s, evaluated in `scripts/query-prompts` round `connections-1`.
- `canvas measure` and `canvas draw`'s own handling of a note's values are [command line](command-line.md)'s: draw joins a statement's `fieldValues` with what the note holds, member by member, and its result and warnings say what each shape's values still need and what is wrong (`FieldChecks.drawn`, above); `canvas measure` reads the types of the board in front, so a tool can size a typed note with its rows before it draws it (`namesBeyond:`, laid out over that editor's own context, with a warning where an `is:` names nothing), and reads the script's own edges too, so the types, the ends and the notes of one script measure as a run of it draws them.

## The model

Types asks the model for three things, each from the person's text: the schema from the words and the words from the schema, which words say which part of the schema (the highlights), and a note's values from its words (the fill).

- The sync: `TypeSync<Schema>` (`editor.typeSync` for a type's, `TypeSync<TypeSchemaProperty>`) keeps a shape's words and a `SyncedSchema` property (one whose value is a `SchemaText`: the property it writes, the prompts it sends, `SchemaSyncPrompts`, its update step's name, and whether the shape's name is the schema's, `namesTheShape`) in step through `editor.languageModel`: only the person's edits sync (not its own write, a board read again, a move, a lock, a rename, an agent's write, `ChangeSet.byAgent`, which is taken as in step), on a commit (editing the words ended however it ended, `editingDidEnd`, an edit still waiting committed the next turn; ⌘↩ or the keyboard leaving in the panel's JSON) or 3 s after the last (`TypeSync.idleDelay`, `editor.scheduler`), the wait held while the JSON's completion list is open (`completing`); words that changed rewrite the schema (whole: past 34 KB never sent, "Schema too long to rewrite", `TypeSyncFailure.tooLongMessage`), else a schema changed in meaning (its root `"title"` left out of the comparison) rewrites the words; never for text that isn't a JSON object, a read-only shape (`editor.isReadOnly(inFile:)`, asked as the sync starts and as an answer lands), or a shape with no fields and no words; with no model at all (a plugin list without LanguageModels) a side to write fails, saying `No language model` (`TypeSyncFailure.noModelMessage`, no Retry). A shape seen for the first time is in step, but words over a schema with no fields, never answered, are ahead; a shape the person makes a type (`/is-a type`) waits as an edit does and is committed on the scheduler's next turn (an undo or a draw in the make's turn lands first, and a shape that is no type then asks nothing): the read starts at once, with no 3 s wait. A rows' spot reads the sync through `onSpotChange` (told a shape's id for each status change and each flip of the wait), `isWaiting` and `hasAnswered` (its words at the last sync are not empty: a read has answered). The name: the type's title goes in as the root `"title"` of each schema sent (`TypeNaming.titled`, unless the schema has one the person typed), the model's comes out of what it writes back (`TypeNaming.untitled`), and an untitled shape is named by it in the same step. While a call is out the side it writes is read-only (the JSON, `detailUpdating`; the note's editor, read-only and faint, showing what lands); its answer is dropped if that side changed. The file closing (the editor going, and the sync with it: the `isolated deinit` of its `SyncShapes`, a class apart and not generic, since Swift's optimizer crashes on an isolated deinit of a generic class built for macOS 14) cancels every call out and every edit's wait, so none runs on spending tokens on an answer nothing lands. Its write is a step of its own ("Update Schema", "Update Text"), on the shape's board shown or not (`transact(on:)`), the words through the kind's `TypeWords.write`; undo and redo start nothing (`restoredStep`) and the pair last synced goes back with the steps (`UndoParticipant`). Its status (`TypeSyncStatus`, `TypeSide` `.words`/`.schema`, `TypeSyncFailure` and its remedy) shows under the JSON ("Updating schema…", "Updating text…"). What it asks is `SchemaPrompts` (the file name, constant names and prompt literals byte for byte, since `scripts/schema-prompts` parses them; they still say "prompt" for the words): Haiku 5.5 at `medium`. A system prompt is `SchemaPrompts.system(_:schemas:)`: the direction's base, then what it says of titles (`toSchemaTitle` and `toSchemaNewTitle`; `toPromptTitle`), on every request, then what it says of other schemas (`toSchemaReferences`, `toPromptReferences`), only in a request that names some, each a blank line apart; highlights get no titles. A rename keeps the field's key and puts the new name in the field's `"title"` (the words are written by it); a word the prompt gives as a part of the thing ("its @Address") or as a bare `@` word is a `$ref` to the type's word, and a phrase that relates the thing to it ("is in a @Layer", "links to", "points to", "refers to") writes no property and stays in the words through every rewrite (no literal the model reads names a pointer, a link or a `contentMediaType`: `pointer-like` fails any answer that writes one); a `$title` (`{"x-canvas-from": key}`, last in `properties`) is written on request, and for a new type whose first field is plainly its name, and the words say nothing of one the person never said; an `if` names its field under its own `properties`. Tests pin the text without a model (`promptTests`: each JSON example in it parses, the `$ref` the prompt shows reads back as the type's id, and no references or title literal names a pointer). The model never sees an id: each type the texts name is `@` and a word (`SchemaReferenceNames(words:schemas:name:isType:)`), a mention of a type its word and a `$ref` that word as its URI; a mention of a shape that isn't a type reads as its label, words. Words that are a JSON object never go to the model as a prompt (at every edit and when a note first becomes a type): when words that changed parse as one (`TypeWordsSchema.schema(fromWords:)`: no mention in them) they are the schema as written, in one "Update Schema" step, none if it already says so (`adopt`), and the words are then written from it, as when a note becomes a type, the pair said to have been these words over `{}`; that request has no `<prompt>` (the model writes the words from nothing), and its answer's preamble is judged against no words. JSON still being typed (`{`, then `"` or `}`, or only `{`) asks nothing; words in braces that are no JSON (`{name, age} for each guest`) are a prompt. With no key the schema is the JSON and the rows show, the status asks for a key, and one saved asks for the words. The words are answered inside `<answer>` and `</answer>` (`SchemaPrompts.answerTag`; the schema is answered bare, as ever), and only what is inside them is the words, since Haiku sometimes opens with a remark and asking it not to made that worse (`parse`, `tagged`): the last complete pair, trimmed; after an opening tag never closed, what follows it; with no tags the whole answer, as before; `NO_CHANGE` judged outside the tags (first word or last line) and inside them; tags with nothing in them a failure. `<answer>` and not `<prompt>`, the request's own block, which a remark names ("There is no <prompt>, so I write one"). A rewrite of the words that reads like a preamble, a fence or `NO_CHANGE` inside text is unusable, unless the words it replaces read so themselves (`SchemaPrompts.parse(_:_:words:)`): a note that begins "Here are the fields" is a note.
- The to-schema system prompt is its base, the two titles literals, and, only for the words of a relationship type (`record[RelationshipProperty.self] == true`, passed by `TypeSync` as `relationship:`), `toSchemaRelationshipType`, then, only for a request that names other schemas, `toSchemaReferences` (`SchemaPrompts.system(_:schemas:relationship:)`). The lite eval of the relationship phrases failed `s_reltype_vague` 0 of 3 (for the one word "employment" the model wrote an `employer` field) and no wording of the references could say that a type is a relationship, so this is the ladder's last rung: it says the type describes a relationship between two things, that who or what it relates is drawn as arrows and is never a property, and that the properties are only what the relationship itself holds. The to-prompt direction is told nothing of it. `evals.py` sends it for the cases that say they are a relationship type's (`relationship` in `cases.csv`, set in `gen_cases.py`: the `s_reltype_*` cases) and its `--check` holds that it is sent after the titles, before the references, to those and no other; `promptTests` pin the text and the order, and `syncTests` that the sync sends it.
- A pill in a type's words that names another type offers `Relate to <Type>` on a click (IsA `RelateOffer.swift`, a face asked before Mentions'): one click, one writer (`newRelationshipType`), no model asked, neither the words nor the schema of either type changed; [relationships](relationships.md#relating-from-the-words-the-offer) holds when it is offered and what it does. The sync, the highlights and the `$ref` completion ask `TypeIndex.canBeEmbedded` of a mention, so a relationship type is never a mention-as-type.
- Highlights: a prose map of the words into the schema (`SchemaTarget`, its guide `SchemaPrompts.highlightsGuide`, `highlightsReferences` its context when the words name types), asked once the shape is drawn in full and in step, read through `ProseHighlights` (generic over a target whose addresses are JSON Pointers, keyed by shape and target: the texts as the model reads them, made once per text, and the map moved onto the drawn text, `MentionReading.drawnRange`; `forget(_:)`, `forgetEverything()` in debug builds). A kind's render calls `context.fillTypeHighlights(of:in:)` with the placement it draws its words in, between its fill and its text (a note's), which costs one added-value lookup on a shape that isn't a type: a field's pale blue, a rule's pale amber (`TypesStyle`). Resting the pointer on them shows that part of the schema, or of the type a `$ref` names, in the core's popover: on the canvas through Selection's `shapePopovers` (`TypeHighlights.popover(at:on:in:)`), in a note's editor through `EditContext.hoverPopover` (the editor asks `editor.types`).
- The fill (`FieldFill`, Sync `FieldFill.swift`, `editor.fieldFill`, an `EditorService` and `UndoParticipant` registered after `FieldWriter`; the Sync part uses Fields for it): a shape of a type has its fields filled from its words by the model, one way, where it autofills (`TypeIndex.autofills`), the owner's ask of 2026-10-21 ("the feature card should use a prompt like the json schema gen one to fill out this object"). What starts one is the person's edit of the shape itself, never an agent's write (`byAgent`), undo or redo, a board opened or read again, or a change to its type (which would ask once for each of its shapes): its words changed (filled once editing ends, `editingDidEnd`, else `idleDelay`, 3 s, after the last change), a type given to it (`is` gains one) or its own say turned on, both read on the scheduler's next turn from the words whole, and editing it ending with words that were never read over fields none of which holds a value (a note written before its type had fields). Nothing for a type (its words are its schema's), a read-only shape, empty words, or types that give no fields; with no model at all it fails saying so. The model is told the fields (`fields(_:book:)`: a JSON Schema object of the fields in row order, another type's fields written out in place of a `urn:uuid:` reference, two deep), what they hold, today (`FillPrompts.day(of:)`, the weekday and the day in the person's calendar; `FieldFill.now` is the clock a test sets) and the words, mentions as their names, with the words the values were last filled from (`filled`, by shape; a shape seen first is taken as filled from the words it had when the person first edited them; a fill from the words whole sends none, and takes nothing away): what changed between the two is what it is asked for. Its answer is the changes (`FillPrompts.parse`: a JSON object, unwrapped, `NO_CHANGE` none; a refusal is "Claude declined" with no Retry, anything else unusable), written field by field (`FieldValuesMerge.merged`, moved from Engines to Fields for it: a group member by member, a list whole, `null` taking a value away, empties pruned) to the shape's fields alone (a key no field names passed over) and only where the person left the value as it was sent (theirs stands), through the one writer (`FieldWriter.fill`: one step, `Fill Fields`, on the shape's board shown or not, `transact(on:)`, never joined to another, a title from a field written in the same update, nothing shaken). A shape locked, made a type or turned off while the call was out takes nothing. Words typed while a call is out wait and fill once it lands, against the words it asked about. The words each shape was filled from are kept as services hear the write (`writing`), so the step holds the words before as it begins and these as it ends, and go back and forth with undo; undoing a fill leaves the words ahead of the values, and nothing reads them until the person edits them. Status (`FieldFillStatus`: idle, filling, failed with a `FieldFillFailure` and its `TypeSyncFailure.Remedy`) is observed a shape at a time (`status(of:)`); a sign-in in Settings retries the shapes that failed for want of one, and the file closing cancels every call and wait (`FillShapes`' isolated deinit). While a call is out the note's empty field rows draw a bar of the reading bars' gray where their `add …` words would be (`RowsValuesDrawing`, `isFilling`, an unobserved read; a status change redraws, nothing moves). What it asks is `FillPrompts` (Sync `FillPrompts.swift`: the system prompt a raw literal byte for byte, `fill_evals.py` reading it): Haiku 5.5 at `medium`, 4,096 tokens, no temperature. FillTests' `fillTests` and `fillPromptTests` hold it over memos with a scripted model.
- `scripts/schema-prompts` (`evals.py`, `highlight_evals.py`, `fill_evals.py`, their cases, `evalkit.py`, what the three share (the key, a literal and a setting read out of the Swift, the call, unwrapping), and a README that says how to run them and what each round found) are the lite evals of the prompts: read the Swift, so there is one copy, and never run by a test or without the lead (no key is printed: `--check`, which `evals.py` runs with a self-test of every check, returns before one is loaded). A prompt's change reruns every case; a new check is shown able to fail in `selftest()`; `evals.py`'s `parse` copies `SchemaPrompts.parse` (the words read from their `<answer>` tags, the tag's name out of the Swift), and `selftest()` reads the answers `promptTests` reads, so a change to how an answer is read changes both; the new cases go in `gen_cases.py` (then `python3 gen_cases.py`) and `highlight_cases.py`, never in `cases.csv`.
