# The detail panel

The detail panel: sections, tabs and status lines, headers, bindings, read-only shapes, text boxes, list rows (a row, a group's header, a search field), the code editor with its completions, and its takeovers (what it shows in place of a shape's details, from other plugins).

Related: [fuzzy matching](fuzzy-matching.md), [table of contents](table-of-contents.md), [find](find.md), [text editing](text-editing.md), [titles](titles.md), [validation](validation.md), [mentions](mentions.md), [neighbors](neighbors.md), [boards](boards.md), [types](types.md), [locking](locking.md) ([every topic](../../CLAUDE.md#map)).

In the guide, `docs/plugin-system.md`: [The detail panel](../../docs/plugin-system.md#the-detail-panel).

## Architecture

- The detail panel (DetailPanel, platform, a horizontal in the window) lays out a header, tabs, sections and a footer; plugins fill them with `x.details { DetailSection("…", in: SomeTab.self) { $x in … } }` and `scope.detailStatus` (on the kinds a scope reaches: [plugins and kinds](plugins-and-kinds.md)): status lines, the shape's state rather than its settings, in the footer, or with `in: SomeTab.self` in a tab that shows only while the shape has lines there (Validation's Issues tab, `ValidationDetailsPlugin`), and whose title in the strip follows its worst line's symbol while that warns or errs (`layout.alert(in:)`). A tab is a type (`DetailTab`) any plugin may declare, and its `order` (an `Order`, [plugins and kinds](plugins-and-kinds.md)) says where it goes among a shape's tabs: by default where it first appears (a kind's own contributions first, then the rest in plugin order: Types' Types tab, then Neighbors' Connections, on every shape, always: [neighbors](neighbors.md)); a kind's own tabs are `.first` (a reference's Reference, a subboard's Description), so a feature's tab that its opt-in brings from the kind's own `register`, saying nothing, still goes after them; `StyleTab` is `.last`, as a shared tab trails in Keynote; Types' Schema is `.after(NeighborsTab.self)`, so a type's panel opens on what is connected to it ([types](types.md)); Validation's Issues, a tab of the shape's state that comes and goes, is `.after(StyleTab.self)`, so the others keep their places. The report names the tabs a contribution's sections fill, each with its order if it says one (`ShapeDetails in Schema after Connections → every shape holding schema`, `DetailStatuses in Issues after Style → every shape`), and the registry refuses tabs placed beside each other in a circle. A section with no title, one row whose own label says what it is, names its undo steps with `.named("Color")` ("Change Color"; without it a headerless section's steps are named for the kind): Colors' row in the Style tab, which so costs the panel one line. A section whose showing is the shape's state says when with `.shownWhile { shape, context in … }` (Types' Fields: while the shape's types give it a field or it holds a value; a reference's Matches: while its query has found shapes), asked on every commit beside the status lines, whatever the commit changed, so it reads a service's cached state, O(1), and only the sections that have one are asked (found once per kind); a tab shows only while it has a section shown or status lines, and the shape's panel draws again only when its record, whether it can change, its status lines or the sections `shownWhile` hides change (`panelsDrawn`, debug builds, counts it drawn). A titled section few people need is collapsed until the person opens it (`.collapsedUntilOpened()`, as Finder's Get Info keeps Sharing & Permissions folded: Types' Advanced, the box that turns off filling a note's fields from its words), then is as they leave it: `DetailPanelState` remembers what the person collapsed and opened by key (`isCollapsed`, `setCollapsed`), a section they haven't touched as it starts, and a headerless one never collapses (DetailPanelTests' `stateTests`). `scope.details { DetailSection<AnyShapeRecord> … }` puts sections on every shape of the kinds a scope reaches, over the whole record (`ShapeBinding<AnyShapeRecord>.record`): `registrar.forShapes(holding: ColorProperty.self) { $0.details { … } }`, Colors' row on the kinds that have colors, and `forEveryShape`, Neighbors' Connections; a section that shows only in some state says so with `shownWhile` (both `ShapeDetails` and `DetailStatuses` take scopes, so a section or a line for every kind with none is refused). Beside it is `detailsForEveryBox`, on the kinds whose shapes are boxes (`.adopting(.box)`). `DetailCheckbox(_:isOn:)` (a label-hidden `Toggle` and a `Text` that toggles on click, `smallSpacing` apart, the text hidden from assistive apps, which read the title as the toggle's) is a `LabeledContent` row's checkbox that reads "Label [] Title" on macOS 14 and 15; `DetailShapeRow(symbol:tint:name:detail:lines:dimmed:)` is a list row for a shape on `DetailStyle`'s own internal metrics (status icon column, 11 pt name, 10 pt secondary detail and up to two lines, `dimmed` dims the whole row); `DetailGroupHeader(_:count:tint:isCollapsed:isRow:toggle:)` is the head of a list of rows inside a section, which folds it (one borderless button, or with `isRow` a plain line, a row of a `DetailRowList`, at a section header's height: a disclosure triangle, a dot in `tint` when the list has a color of its own, the title and, if given, the count; the caller keeps whether it's folded and leaves the rows out while it is); `DetailSearchField(_:text:list:)` is a field that searches such a list (a magnifying glass, the text typed as keyed, nothing corrected, and once there's text a button that clears it, a list row's height; ↓ goes into the list its `DetailListHandle` names, and Escape clears the text first); all of them, and `DetailRowList`, in `ListRows.swift` (DetailPanelTests' ListRowTests). A horizontal's feature is opt-in sugar on `ShapeRegistrar` that contributes to its own point and may call `details`, so opting in can bring a tab (Types' Types tab, over `is`; `x.opensBoard(…)` brings none). An added property's sections are over its value: `x.details(of: P.self) { DetailSection(…) { $value in … } }`, or on `PluginRegistrar` for every shape that has it (scoped `.holding(P)`: the report's `ShapeDetails → every shape holding schema`, Types' Schema tab); a kind's own sections and header read added values through `$x.shape`, the whole record (`$note.shape[TitleProperty.self]`), and bind one, nil while the shape has none, with `$x[P.self]` (a `Binding<P.Value?>`, as `TitleHeader($x)` does; the bindings one `$x` makes of its added properties read back the last write through any of them; on a kind that doesn't opt in, it reads nil and drops writes, and `$x.holds(P.self)` says which, so `TitleHeader($x)` on a kind that doesn't opt into titles stops a debug build). `DetailCommandButton(command:title:systemImage:)` runs a declared command (`editor.perform`, on the selection, which is what the panel shows), dimmed while `editor.canPerform` says no, titled as the command is (`registry.title(of:)`) unless `title` says otherwise, its tooltip the title and the command's key (Keyboard's `registry.help(_:forCommand:)`, which DetailPanel imports Keyboard for; none for a command with no key, whose label says it all); the header's back button is Selection's `.goBack` the same way, called and keyed as the command is and shown while its rule says there's a step back (`canPerform(_:on:)`, the rule alone, so a press on the canvas doesn't take it away): the panel's counterpart of a menu item (it reads the editor from the panel's environment, `\.detailEditor`). A number being typed is committed in `boardWillChange` (`DraftTracker.commit`), so it lands on its shape before another board is shown. Sections write only through `$x.field` or `$x.update` (one transaction each, on the live shape, which the binding reads back until the panel redraws; a field's writes, one after another, are one undo step, and `$x.update(\.field) { … }` runs with them for a field written through a method), bind the narrowest key path, and use `LabeledContent` and DetailPanel's primitives, never their own layout or fonts (the one exception is Types' `FieldForm`, which a section places whole on a line of its own under a field's name, the section's full width: one AppKit view in `DetailStyle.textFont` with a layout, a text editor and a JSON box of its own, since a form of nested rows needs keys no SwiftUI control gives: [types](types.md)): among them `DetailTextArea` (its words' spelling checked as the app's is, `Spelling.app`; with `placeholder:`, and `onCommit:`, which makes Return commit, ⇧↩ a new line, and the keyboard leaving commit too, a turn later if the view left the window with it, so a commit captures its service weakly), `DetailCodeEditor` (code typed as keyed: monospaced, every substitution, automatic completion, prediction and Writing Tools off; lines never wrap; as tall as its lines, from `DetailStyle.codeEditorHeight`, a text area's 76, to what the panel shows; ⌘↩ and the keyboard leaving commit; `coloring:` names its runs, `DetailCodeSpan`, and the panel colors them, red only for `.invalid`; `completions:`, a `DetailCodeCompleting`, says what can be typed at the caret, UTF-16 in, a `DetailCodeCompletions` out: the `range` a pick replaces, which holds the caret, the `query` typed so far, and every candidate as offered, unfiltered, each a `DetailCodeCompletion`, its `label`, `detail`, `insert`, the `selection` within it a pick leaves, `reopens` and `isRelevant`; ⌃Space, and AppKit's `complete:` (⌥⎋, F5), opens it in the app's one caret list (TextEditing's) while the text can be written and something's offered, else nothing, no beep: under what's typed, each label in the code's font, matched in its semibold, its detail after it, VoiceOver saying both, ranked with `FuzzyMatcher` (FuzzyMatching's, as every list ranks) (nothing typed: as offered; else by tier, then `isRelevant`, then as offered; what doesn't match left out); while it's open ↑ ↓ ⌃P ⌃N and Page Up and Down move, Return, Enter and Tab pick, Escape, ⌘., ⌃Space, ⌥⎋ and F5 close it, ⌘↩ closes it and commits, and the rest is the text's; it asks again after every keystroke and move of the caret, and closes once nothing's listed, the caret leaves the place it opened for (the range starts elsewhere), the text is shown from outside or can't be written, the keyboard or the window goes, the window resigns key, or what's typed scrolls out of view; a pick is `insertText` over the range between two `breakUndoCoalescing()`, one step of the text's undo that the box writes as typing, and one that `reopens` opens it again; `onCompleting:` hears it open and close (a pick that opens it again, not at all), for what waits on the person's choosing (a type's sync); without `completions`, F5 does nothing and ⌥⎋ leaves the panel: code gets no dictionary words), `.detailUpdating(_:)` (read-only and dimmed while something else writes the box; the words go on a status line where the person is, never over the text), and `DetailStatusLine` (a tone's symbol or, `isBusy`, a spinner, one width so the text doesn't move, then the text, which takes no tone and can be selected and copied (a line whose selected text has the keyboard as it goes hands it back to the canvas), with `DetailStatusAction` buttons under it). Text that isn't a string edits in a `DetailTextBox(value, height:placeholder:onCommit:make:)`, a box as a text area's (the panel's own, `DetailCodeEditor` and `DetailTextArea`, with `onCommit:` or without, are boxes over `PlainTextView`, on TextKit 1 as they always were: a text area's words checked as the app checks spelling, a code editor's code never), over the view `make` returns: a subclass of `EditingTextView` (TextEditing's: [text editing](text-editing.md)) that adopts `DetailTextContent` (`value`, which the box writes to the binding on every change, and `show(_:)`, which the box calls only when the binding changed from outside, an undo or another plugin's write: the storage alone, never `didChangeText`, since the board may be applying a step, and then the box drops typing's undo), set in `DetailStyle.textFont` (the panel's text, 11 pt), as Mentions' `MentionTextArea` is. A box's first text baseline is its first line's (`DetailStyle.textBaseline`, `codeBaseline` for a code editor: the box's padding and the font's baseline in its line, `detailTextBox(height:baseline:)`'s alignment guide, since an AppKit view tells SwiftUI none, and SwiftUI then lines a row up on its top edge), so a `LabeledContent` row's label sits on the text's line; a box in another font than the panel's text passes its own `baseline`. The panel's root hears Escape before the view does, and lets it through only to a view holding keys, and drops a draft first (a number being typed, a search field's text: `DraftTracker.pending`; the first Escape reverts it and the field stays, the next leaves), and ⌥⎋ to a view that `offersCompletions` (a code editor with completions it can write), list or not; ⌘. with no list open goes on up and leaves the panel from its root (`onExitCommand`). Disabled (a read-only shape's section, `detailUpdating`), a box is read-only and dimmed by its view (`showEnabled(false)`), never by SwiftUI, which would take every click over it: its text is still selected and copied, and its clicks land (a pill followed), but it never commits (`onCommit`): nothing was written. A section reaches a service of its plugin's with `$x.service(S.self)` (a type's `TypeSync`), never the editor, and `$x.shapeID` is the shape a binding writes, whatever its value. A read-only shape's panel (a locked one, one in a locked frame, or one on a locked subboard's board) shows everything with its sections' controls and its header disabled (`editor.isReadOnly(id)`, asked on every commit where the panel's root reads the shape's record, O(the shape's depth), and handed to the shape's panel, which never reads the document and draws again only when it changes: a container locked or unlocked, the shape joining or leaving one, a lock landing above its board in a commit on another), but a section's that changes nothing of the shape (`DetailSection(…).enabledWhileReadOnly()`: References' Go to Original, Neighbors' Connections lists); Back, the tabs and collapsing still work. The header is a view made from the shape's binding too, `x.details(header: { $x in DetailHeader("…") }) { … }`: a `DetailHeader`, or a view of the kind's own built on `DetailHeaderLabel` (with `DetailHeaderField` for a title renamed in place, an AppKit field, `HeaderTitleField`, drawn pixel for pixel as SwiftUI's plain one was, whose field editor is an `EditingTextView` of its own (`HeaderField.editor`, which its cell hands AppKit, keeping nothing itself, since AppKit may copy a cell bit for bit) that checks the title's spelling as the app does, since SwiftUI's fields can't be told to, and tests still find it as an `NSTextField`; `isEditable: false` shows it as text at full strength, for a title that can't be written just now), so every header looks alike; the panel hands it the kind's name and symbol through the environment, and tests read it back with `layout.header` or `layout.header(as:)`. Keep the sugar's closure types unannotated, and never name `Transaction` in a file that imports SwiftUI. The window places it: `DetailPanelPlugin` gives `DetailPanelView(editor:onExit:)` as the window's inspector (`registrar.hostInspector`, the host's slot for one: [host slots](host-slots.md)), which CanvasUI's `BoardChrome` puts in an `.inspector`, with `onExit` set to `CanvasFocus.take`, which gives the keyboard back to the canvas. It says the inspector has something to show while shapes are selected (`shownWhile: { !$0.selectedShapeIDs.isEmpty }`, so with nothing selected the window hides the panel, whatever the person chose), that it is wanted while a takeover is active (`registry.activeDetailTakeover(in:)`), and that dismissing it runs that takeover's `onEscape`, so the window shows the inspector for a takeover whatever the person chose and whatever is selected, leaves it as they chose (and as there is something to show) once the takeover ends, and escapes the takeover when the person hides the inspector meanwhile, knowing nothing of what took the panel over ([host slots](host-slots.md) holds the rule).
- What the panel shows in place of everything is a point of its own, in `Sources/DetailPanel/DetailTakeovers.swift`, which `DetailPanelView(editor:onExit:)` reads from the editor's registry itself (it takes nothing else), making each view as it's made (in its `init`, handed its editor and `onExit`), so it never learns what any is. **Takeovers** (`DetailTakeovers`, a list in plugin order, the first that's active showing; the report's `detail takeover`): `registrar.detailTakeover(isActive:onEscape:) { editor, onExit in … }` (`isActive: (CanvasEditor) -> Bool`, `onEscape: (CanvasEditor) -> Void`, and the view): while `isActive` holds the panel shows the view in place of everything else, every selection branch included, with the panel's environment, frame and width as ever. `isActive` is read in the body, so it reads observable state and the panel redraws only when it flips. Escape and ⌘. in the panel run the showing takeover's `onEscape`, then `onExit` (a text view holding keys hears Escape before either, as ever). Find gives its panel (`FindPanelPlugin`: `FindPanel`, while `editor.find?.isActive`; `onEscape` closes Find: [find](find.md)). The point's owner is the panel's plugin, so a contributor is a plugin value of its own after `DetailPanelPlugin` (a face), left out of a list without the panel and refused in one with it and no panel (`ownerNotInstalled`). With no takeover active the panel shows by the selection: several shapes, their header alone (`SelectionSummary(header:)`: the header and a divider, with no list under it and no "Select one shape to see its details" line); one shape, its panel; and none, "No Selection", which only a panel hosted alone shows, since the window hides the inspector then (`shownWhile`, above). A takeover turning active over a shape's panel removes that panel as it stands, so a number field's uncommitted draft is dropped: one that can start while a field is being typed in should end the edit first.

- View › Focus Inspector (⌥⌘I, the window's, [host slots](host-slots.md)) gives the keyboard to a view in this panel with no help from it: with a shape selected its header's title field (`HeaderField`, an `NSTextField`; the back button above it also accepts first responder and is passed over), else the first control in the panel. It is dimmed with nothing selected, when the inspector has nothing to show, and never gives the keyboard to the sidebar's list (View › Focus Sidebar does: [table of contents](table-of-contents.md#the-keyboard)). The panel says this itself: `DetailPanelState.keyboardEntry`, set as the panel appears, is the header's field (`WindowBox.entry()`, over the panel's own controls: the first editable text field or text view, else the first table), and `registrar.hostInspector(keyboardEntry:)` hands it to the window; the window's sniffing is the fallback. F6 and ⇧F6 (View › Focus Next Pane, Previous Pane) go to the same entry ([host slots](host-slots.md)). Escape in any of them is the panel's own (`onKeyPress(.escape)`, and `.onExitCommand` for ⌘., which comes up the responder chain as `cancelOperation:`), which gives the keyboard back to the canvas with the selection as it was; CompositionTests' `inspectorFocusShippedTests` hold both, pressing ⌥⌘I through the app's menus (`pressFocusInspector`, a `KeyDriver` with View's items sent to the window's controller).

- **The panel by keys** (P1, P3, P5, P8, P9 of [keyboard navigation](keyboard-navigation.md)). Four things, each in the panel with no help from the plugins that fill it:
  - **Tabs.** View › Next Inspector Tab (⌥⌘]) and Previous Inspector Tab (⌥⌘[) are commands the panel declares (`.nextInspectorTab`, `.previousInspectorTab`, with Keyboard's `command(… shortcut:)`, so DetailPanel needs Keyboard installed; enabled while one shape is selected, its panel has two tabs or more and shows them: not under a takeover (Find), which has no tab strip). They call `DetailPanelState.stepTab(by:)`, which `choose`s the tab `delta` away among `layout.tabs`, round the ends, as a click on the strip does (so it is remembered for the kind), and then gives the keyboard to the first control the new tab shows: `focusesFirstControl`, which the view sets as it appears (weakly over the editor: the state is the editor's), reads the panel's controls off the window by place (`WindowBox.controls()`: the views of the window that can take the keyboard by Tab and sit inside the panel's reader view; an `.inspector` is the window's content beside the canvas, not a subtree), skips those the tab just left and those above the strip (`ContentReader`, a view that fills the scroll area), and tries again every 20 ms up to 12 times while SwiftUI makes its views. A tab with no control leaves the keyboard where it was. The strip takes ← → through `onMoveCommand` (the same step) and is an accessibility tab bar; a section's fold says "expanded" or "collapsed".
  - **A ring.** Tab off the panel's last control goes round to its first, and ⇧Tab off the first to the last (decision d-panelwrap): `WindowBox.closeRing()` sets each control's `nextKeyView` to the next in reading order (rows within 8 points of one another are a row, left to right), the last to the first, whenever the panel's content may have changed (a tab or fold changes what it shows, a status line goes) and as the window's first responder moves into the panel (a KVO on `NSWindow.firstResponder`, stopped when the reader that started it leaves its window: an `.inspector` moves a new reader in before the old one goes out, so only the box's current reader stops it). A control is the panel's by its place, or by its outermost scroll view's (`WindowBox.isPlaced`): a control scrolled out of the panel's view, and a table taller than the panel, are in the ring, which AppKit scrolls into view as Tab reaches it. The ring is redone only while the keyboard is in the panel (`hasKeyboard`), so a selection stepping on the canvas costs no walk of the window. Escape, ⌘. and the canvas's own keys are how the keyboard leaves it.
  - **`DetailRowList`** (`ListRows.swift`; P1, P3, P4, P9): a list of rows that is one Tab stop, a `List(selection:)`. `DetailRowItem` is a line (`id`, `kind` `.row` or `.head(isFolded:)`, the head it is under, its height, what Return does); ↑ ↓ Page Up and Down are the table's, Home and End and ← → come by `performKeyEquivalent` on a view in the list's background (the table takes them itself, so SwiftUI never hears them), Return and Space by `onKeyPress` (the table leaves them): Return or Space (with Caps Lock on too: the modifiers are read through `KeyMeaning.Modifiers`) runs the highlighted line, → unfolds a head and ← folds it (← on a row goes up to its head); a click is a borderless, unfocusable button's, as before. Entering the list highlights its first line; `onHighlight` hears the highlighted id while the list has the keyboard and nil as it leaves, so a list lights what a row stands for as the pointer does (Neighbors calls `hover` and `endHover`). The list is as tall as its rows and no taller than the panel less 96 points, and scrolls inside that. `detailRowAccessibility(label:value:activate:)` makes a row one element (a shape's name, board and caption; a head's title, count and "expanded" or "collapsed"), and `label:` names the list. `DetailListHandle` is what ↓ in the search field above it goes through. Types' rows and the color grid may adopt it later.
  - **Names and ⌘↩.** `DetailTextArea`, `DetailCodeEditor` and `DetailTextBox` take `label:` (else the placeholder), which the host sets as the text view's accessibility label; `DetailStatusLine` reads "<Tone>: <text>" (a neutral line its text; the symbol is hidden); the header's field is "Name". ⌘↩ in a box with no `onCommit` leaves the panel (`onExit`, through the box's `onKeyDown`), keeping what was typed, which is written as it is typed; with one it commits, as ever. A number field's Escape reverts its draft (and the field shows what it was) and a second Escape leaves.
  - Keys the panel's text answers to are key hints under Editing Text (⇥ or ⇧⇥ next or previous control, ⌥⇥ types a tab, ⎋ back to the canvas, ⌘↩ commit, ⌃Space complete). View › Focus Inspector now takes the topmost list, table or text field (a selected shape's title above its Connections list, not the list).

## Testing

- For the detail panel, check layouts and writes headlessly (`editor.detailPanel?.layout(for:)`, a tab's status lines with `layout.statuses(in:)`, the footer's with `statuses(for:)`, `editor.shapeBinding`, `editor.propertyBinding(id, P.self)`). For its views, host `DetailPanelView` in an `NSHostingView` in an unshown `NSWindow`, beside the canvas when focus matters (DetailPanelTests; `Tests/CompositionTests/InspectorTests.swift` uses the app's own wiring), with the real `TaskScheduler`; `panelsDrawn` (debug builds) counts a shape's panel drawn, so a test holds a commit to drawing it again only when what it shows changes (ReadOnlyTests: a shape in a frozen corral, two corrals down, and one on a frozen pocket's board, the lock landing above in a commit on the root). CanvasTesting drives the window: `window.spin { condition }` runs the main run loop until the state a test expects arrives (and fails the test after 2 s), `window.settle()` runs it a few turns where there's nothing to wait for, and `focus`, `type`, `press`, `pressCommandPeriod` (the menus' turn, then the window's, which sends `cancelOperation:` up the responder chain) and `mouse` send input. Call `canvas.mouseDown` directly (`sendEvent` mouse-downs don't reach views in an unshown window). A click in the panel goes where the window's hit test from its top view lands (`PanelWindow.hit(_:in:)` and `clickThroughWindow(at:in:)`, MentionsTests' `MentionPanel` likewise): SwiftUI takes the clicks over what it disables, which a view's own `mouseDown` called directly never meets. Once there's a step back, the header has a back button, an `NSButton` in the panel's top 44 points, and a kind renamed in its header has a text field there, so tests that count the panel's buttons, checkboxes or fields leave the header out (`isInPanelHeader`). WindowTests hold the header field to SwiftUI's plain field (set alike, and drawn alike pixel for pixel, with a title, one too long for it and its placeholder) and its editor, its own, to the app's spelling; TextViewTests a text area's, with a commit or without, to it, and a code editor's to none. Render the panel standalone: in the app's `.inspector` it doesn't draw offscreen. DetailPanelTests hold a plugin's text box with a fixture view (`KeyedTextView`, an `EditingTextView` whose value wraps a string, with a settable `holdsKeys`, logging its commands, `didChangeText`s and `show`s; a view that doesn't hold keys never hears Escape in the panel, whose root takes it first), and `shownWhile` with a service of their own (`Watchers`); `PanelWindow(inBoardWindow:)` hosts the panel in a `BoardWindow` for Edit › Undo. A code editor's completions (Code completions) are the `CodeBoxesPlugin`'s code editor completing fruit (`fruitCompletions`: a basket that reopens, a cherry whose count a pick selects, a word after an x whose range is elsewhere; `codeCompleting` logs what `onCompleting` hears) and `BareCodePlugin`'s with none, read through the text view's `caretListWindow`, with ⌃Space pressed as `press(" ", code: 49, modifiers: .control)`, ⌥⎋ and F5 likewise; TypesTests' `panelCompletionTests` hold a type's, and CompositionTests' `boardWindowTests` the app's menus leaving ⌃Space to the text, ⌘↩ with the list open committing, and a mouse pick joining the typing's step (the list made the board window's child first, as showing it would).
- TextViewTests holds a row's label to its box's first line (`LabeledBoxesPlugin`: a mark that says its baseline and a text label in rows of a box of words, one typed as keyed and a code editor, empty and with text; SwiftUI's global space starts at the window's top, its title bar included, which the test takes off).
- Takeovers are tested in `Tests/DetailPanelTests/TakeoverTests.swift` (suite "Panel takeovers"), given as a plugin gives them, never handed to the view: `TakeoverPlugin` (active while its `TakeoverModel` says, a field named for it) goes in the editor's plugins (`gadgetEditor([TakeoverPlugin(model: model)], …)`), and `PanelWindow(editor)` hosts the panel as ever. They hold: Next and Previous Inspector Tab stepping no tab under a takeover (`canStepTab`); a takeover in place of the selection's panel and with nothing selected, and with it inactive and nothing selected the panel showing no field; the report's summary, and a list without the panel refusing a takeover; of two takeovers the first active showing, Escape its own; Escape and ⌘. in its field running `onEscape`, then `onExit`; and the inspector's `shownWhile` (false with nothing selected, true with one shape selected and with several) beside its `isRequested` and `onDismiss`.
