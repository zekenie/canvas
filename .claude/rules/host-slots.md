# Host slots

What a board's window puts around its canvas, and what the app takes from the plugins besides, said by plugins and placed by the host: views over the canvas, the sidebar, the inspector, the window's subtitle, settings panes, tool tips, the Keyboard Shortcuts window's rows, the menu bar's commands and the keys menus show, what Copy as Image draws and the commands the app's server runs; CanvasUI's `BoardChrome` (the panes as sheets over a canvas that fills the window), the inspector's rule, the panes, the Settings window, the Keyboard Shortcuts window and the menu bar; and the window's look every surface around the board shares (Chrome's `ChromeStyle`).

Related: [build](build.md), [command line](command-line.md), [detail panel](detail-panel.md), [boards](boards.md), [jump to](jump-to.md), [keyboard](keyboard.md), [language models](language-models.md), [decision models](decision-models.md), [find](find.md), [editor](editor.md), [plugins and kinds](plugins-and-kinds.md) ([every topic](../../CLAUDE.md#map)).

In the guide, `docs/plugin-system.md`: [Host slots](../../docs/plugin-system.md#host-slots).

## Architecture

- **Why it exists.** A plugin can't import the window (CanvasUI), and the app and CanvasUI may import only the core, so neither can hand the other a view. A module that imported every plugin, Composition, used to join them (`boardChrome`, `detailPanel`, `boardPath`, `coveredTop`, `boardName`, `settings`, `help` and `commands(in:)`); it's gone, and nothing joins them now. The core (`Sources/Canvas/Plugins/HostSlots.swift`, which imports SwiftUI, since its slots hold views, and the wire, whose types the commands are said in) declares what passes between them: eleven extension points, each the core's (`owner` is `.core`, so always installed), each conformed in an extension with a `summary`. A plugin contributes from its own `register`, with no plugin value of its own for it (nothing can be missing), and a list without the plugin is a window without its piece. It knows no plugin and no window.
- **The eleven slots**, what a plugin says and what the host reads (all `Registry` extensions, public):
  - Views over the canvas (`HostOverlays`, a list in plugin order, a later one over an earlier; the report's `host overlay`): `registrar.hostOverlay(alignment:coveredTop:) { editor, onExit in … }` (`alignment` `.center` and `coveredTop` zero by default). Read as `registry.hostOverlays` (`HostOverlay`: `alignment`, `coveredTop`, `view(for:onExit:)`) and `registry.hostCoveredTop(on:)`, the most any says for a board. Not a `CanvasOverlay`, which the canvas draws into itself in the world. Shipped: Boards' path (`.top`, covering `BoardPath.coveredHeight(on:)`), then Jump To's palette, over it.
  - The sidebar (`HostSidebars`, keyed, at most one, a second `RegistryError.conflict`; `host sidebar`): `registrar.hostSidebar(width:keyboardEntry:) { editor, onExit in … }`: the view, how wide the window makes it (`PaneWidth`: `min`, `ideal`, `max`, the width it starts at and the narrowest and widest a drag of its edge takes it to, `clamped(_:)` a width the person left kept within them; `.standard`, 200, 260 and 400, by default), and the view in it that takes the keyboard when the window gives the sidebar the keyboard (none named by default: the window then looks for one). Read as `registry.hostSidebar` (`HostSidebar`: `width`, `keyboardEntry`, `view(for:onExit:)`). Shipped: the table of contents' list (`TableOfContentsPlugin`, from its own `register`, its outline the entry: [table of contents](table-of-contents.md)).
  - The inspector (`HostInspectors`, keyed, at most one, a second `RegistryError.conflict`; `host inspector`): `registrar.hostInspector(width:shownWhile:isRequested:onDismiss:keyboardEntry:) { editor, onExit in … }` (`width` as the sidebar's; `shownWhile`, whether it has something to show now, read in a view's body, always by default; never requested, nothing on dismissal and no entry named, by default). Read as `registry.hostInspector` (`HostInspector`: `width`, `shownWhile`, `isRequested`, `onDismiss`, `keyboardEntry`, `view(for:onExit:)`). Shipped: the detail panel (`DetailPanelPlugin`), with something to show while shapes are selected, requested while a takeover or a band is active (`registry.activeDetailTakeover(in:)`, `activeDetailBand(in:)`), dismissal running that takeover's `onEscape`, then the band's `onCancel`.
  - The window's subtitle (`WindowSubtitles`, keyed, at most one; `window subtitle`): `registrar.windowSubtitle { editor in … }`, nil for none. Read as `registry.windowSubtitle(for:)`. Shipped: Boards, the board shown's crumb's title, none on the root.
  - Settings panes (`SettingsPanes`, keyed by title, in plugin order; `settings pane Claude`): `registrar.settingsPane(title) { … }`, the view made each time the window opens, handed no editor. Read as `registry.settingsPanes` (`SettingsPane`: `title`, `view()`). Shipped: the sign-in's, Claude, which `LanguageModelsPlugin(model:)` adds only when its model is a `SignedInModel`, over that model's account, so the pane and the model read one way, one key and one Claude Code, and a registry built with a test's `ScriptedLanguageModel` has no pane and never points at the app's files ([language models](language-models.md)); then the TypeSafe key's, which `DecisionModelsPlugin(model:)` adds only when its model is a `JevModel` ([decision models](decision-models.md)). The window tells each pane whether it's the first (`EnvironmentValues.settingsPaneTakesFocus`, the core's, true by default), and only the first takes the keyboard as it opens.
  - Tool tips (`ToolTips`, keyed, at most one; `tool tips`): `registrar.toolTips { tool, registry in … }`. Read as `registry.toolTip(for:)`, the tool's label without one. Shipped: Keyboard, `registry.help(for:)` ("Note (N)").
  - The Keyboard Shortcuts window's rows (`KeyboardHelp`, keyed, at most one; `keyboard help`): `registrar.keyboardHelp { registry in … }`, `[KeyboardHelpRow]` (`keys` as they're written, `title`, `place`: `.tool`, `.command(c)`, `.canvas`, `.pointer`, `.text`), read off the built registry. And key hints (`KeyHints`, a list in plugin order; `key hint ⌥ Drag Drag a Copy`): `registrar.keyHint(keys, title, while: .pointer | .text)`, a key no shortcut has, said beside the code that reads it. Read together as `registry.keyboardHelp`, the list's rows then the hints, each row once; none with neither. Shipped: Keyboard's rows (`keyboardHelpRows`) and every plugin's hints ([keyboard](keyboard.md)).
  - The menu bar's commands, and the keys menus show (`MenuKeys`, keyed, at most one; `menu keys`): a command a plugin declares in a section (`registrar.command(… in:placed:…)`, the core's Commands point: [editor](editor.md)) is in the menu bar, read as `registry.menuBarCommands` (`MenuBarCommand`: `command`, `title`, `section`, the `MenuBarSection` it's in, `placement`, its place among the section's items, `menu`, the section's menu as a `MenuPath`, and `key`, a `MenuKey`, in plugin order), which the window arranges with its own items, section by section ([keyboard](keyboard.md), the menu bar); the keys an item has for a command are the plugin's that keeps the keys, `registrar.menuKeys { command, registry in … }` (`[MenuKey]`, each the key equivalent as a menu item takes it and the `Modifiers`, `modifierFlags` for the item, the one the item shows first), read as `registry.menuKey(of:)`, the first, nil for none, which a context menu's items read too, and `registry.menuKeys(of:)`, all of them, whose others held with ⌘ or ⌃ the menu bar answers through hidden items ([keyboard](keyboard.md)). Shipped: Keyboard, every key registered to run the command, in the order registered (`KeyChord.menuKey`).
  - What Copy as Image draws (`ImageSources`, keyed, at most one, a second `RegistryError.conflict`; `image source`): `registrar.imageSource(isAvailable:_:)`: `isAvailable: (CanvasEditor) -> Bool`, asked as the menu item is validated, so cheap, and the shapes, `(CanvasEditor) -> [ShapeID]`, those of the board shown to draw, back to front, none for nothing. Read as `registry.imageSource` (`ImageSource`: `isAvailable`, `shapes(for:)`), nil where no plugin says. Shipped: Clipboard, over `editor.canCopyAsImage` and `editor.imageShapes()`: the shapes a copy of the selection would take, or every copyable shape of the board shown with nothing selected ([clipboard](clipboard.md)).
  - The commands (`HostCommands`, keyed, at most one; `host commands`): `registrar.hostCommands(specs:run:)`, what the command line shows of each command and how to run one by name, both handed the frozen registry (the commands are every plugin's, known only once it's built). Read as `registry.hostCommands`, `(specs, run)`: no specs, and every name `unknown_command`, where no plugin gives any. The one slot said in the wire's types (`CommandSpec`, `JSON`, `RPCError`). Shipped: Automation, over `automationCommands` and `runAutomationCommand` ([command line](command-line.md)).
- **A view a plugin gives** is made for a window's canvas: handed that board's editor and `onExit`, which gives the keyboard back to the canvas (`CanvasFocus.take`), and it follows the editor by itself. A view over the canvas is over the whole of it and takes clicks only where it draws.
- **The window's side** (CanvasUI, which knows no plugin):
  - `BoardChrome(editor:showsSidebar:showsInspector:sidebarWidth:inspectorWidth:onExit:)` (`Sources/CanvasUI/BoardChrome.swift`), a public view modifier the app applies to its `CanvasRepresentable` after its frame and toolbar, in a window whose content runs under its title bar (`BoardWindowController`: `.fullSizeContentView`, the title bar transparent): the canvas fills the window, its title bar's row too (`ignoresSafeArea`), so that nothing on the board moves as anything around it comes or goes (the owner's ask, 2026-11-01: a pane toggled shifted the board when the sidebar was a split view's column and the inspector SwiftUI's `.inspector`). Over it: the sidebar's view (`registry.hostSidebar`) along its leading edge while `showsSidebar`, and the inspector's (`registry.hostInspector`) along its trailing edge by the inspector's rule (below), each in a `PaneSheet`, a sheet of the chrome's surface flush to the window's side, top and bottom (its top the title bar's row, under the window's own buttons, so its content starts under it; a hairline along its inner edge with the shade the board takes beside it; that edge a grip a drag resizes it by, within the slot's `PaneWidth`, the pointer the column-resize one, and to VoiceOver an adjustable element, "Sidebar width" or "Inspector width", whose increment and decrement widen and narrow it 20 points (`BoardChromeStyle.edgeStep`): there is no key for it, as the split view's divider had none; while a pane is resized the board, the minimap and the zoom follow its edge at once, and glide only as a pane shows or hides, `CoveredEdges.hasSameSides`), as wide as the person left it (`sidebarWidth`, `inspectorWidth`, nil for the slot's `ideal`, which the app binds to `BoardViewState`'s, remembered per file: [boards on disk](boards-on-disk.md)), coming in from its edge over the board and going back out (`ChromeStyle.paneIn`, 200 ms; with Reduce Motion fading in place, `paneFade`), the inspector over the sidebar in a window too narrow for both. Each view the plugins put over the canvas (`registry.hostOverlays`, in plugin order, a later one over an earlier, each where its alignment puts it) is in the part the title bar and the sheets leave clear, so the path's pill and Jump To center there. The editor is told what the chrome covers (`editor.coveredEdges`: the title bar's height along the top, from the safe area, read by a `GeometryReader` that keeps to it, since one that ignores the safe area reads none, which once left the sheets' headers under the window's buttons and every fit under the title bar; and each sheet's width while it shows, host API, [editor](editor.md)), so fits and reveals land in the clear part and the canvas lays out the minimap in its corner ([drawing](drawing.md)). The toolbar's buttons: Sidebar at its leading end (`.navigation`, over the sidebar while it shows and where it stays while it doesn't: `sidebar.leading`, its tooltip "Show Sidebar" or "Hide Sidebar") and Inspector at its trailing end (`.primaryAction`: `sidebar.trailing`, "Show Inspector" or "Hide Inspector", disabled while `!canToggle`; `BoardChromeStyle`). No sidebar registered, no sidebar and no button; no inspector, likewise. A pane hidden is taken out of the window; a view in it that has the keyboard as it goes gives it back to the canvas itself (the table of contents' list, `onLeave`, as it leaves its window with the keyboard in its outline; the detail panel's reader, `onHide`). Each sheet has a `PaneMarker` behind it (a view that draws nothing and takes no clicks, `PaneMarkerView`, `.leading` or `.trailing`), which is how the window tells its panes apart now that they lie over the canvas (Panes, below). `BoardViewState.showsSidebar` (true by default) is remembered per file with `showsDetails`, `showsMinimap` and the widths (`BoardViewMemory.View.showsSidebar`; an entry from before reads as true: [boards on disk](boards-on-disk.md)), and the app's `ContentView` binds them (`showsSidebar: $view.showsSidebar`). The window's title shows only while no sidebar does, whose header names the board shown (`BoardWindowController.showTitle`, following `showsSidebar`; the title is still set, for the Window menu and Mission Control).
  - The inspector's rule, public API on `HostInspector` (CanvasUI's `BoardChrome.swift`), shared by the toolbar button and the View menu: `isShown(in:chosen:)` is `isRequested || (chosen && shownWhile)`; `canToggle(in:chosen:)` is `isShown || shownWhile`; `toggle(in:chosen:onExit:)` hides what shows (`chosen` becomes false, and if something wanted it, `onDismiss` then `onExit`) and shows what doesn't (`chosen` becomes true). `chosen` is the window's `showsDetails`, the person's choice. A request (Find's takeover) shows the inspector by the rule without changing the person's choice, and when it ends the inspector is as they chose (and as there is something to show). It comes in over the canvas's trailing edge as a shape is selected; the core keeps what was just revealed or selected in view meanwhile, panning it clear of the sheet with the sheet's slide ([editor](editor.md), a view kept while the view settles). The body reads the rule, so the window follows the selection and a takeover. Generic over whatever requests it: the window has never heard of Find, whose ⌘F shows a hidden inspector through the detail panel's takeover ([find](find.md), [detail panel](detail-panel.md)).
  - What the canvas covers: `BoardDocument` sets each board's `editor.coveredTop` to `registry.hostCoveredTop(on:)` (host API, [editor](editor.md)), so a fit leaves the path's pill clear, measured from under the title bar's row; `BoardChrome` sets `editor.coveredEdges` as the panes come and go. A headless editor is told nothing and covers nothing, which is why this stays the window's to say and isn't a core point the editor reads.
  - The subtitle: `BoardWindowController` reads `registry.windowSubtitle(for:)` observed, so it follows a switch of board or a rename; with no plugin saying one it reads nothing, and is never read again.
  - `SettingsWindow` (moved here from Composition): `SettingsWindow(registry:)` hosts `registry.settingsPanes` one under another (`SettingsPanesView`), unheaded, since a pane says what it is itself; `init(autosaveName:content:)` hosts any view. Made afresh each time it opens, sized before it goes in. It and the Keyboard Shortcuts window are a `SecondaryWindow` (`SecondaryWindow.swift`, an `NSWindow` whose `cancelOperation:` is Close), so Escape and ⌘. close them when nothing inside took the key (P5, P6). A key's rows (LanguageModels' `KeyRows`: the Claude pane's on the API key's way, and every `KeySettingsView`'s, TypeSafe's pane) put the keyboard in the field as the pane appears when it's the window's first (`@FocusState`, `settingsPaneTakesFocus`), make Save the default action while the field has the keyboard (Return saves the key typed there), say each status as it changes (`KeySettings.announce`, an announcement; tests set it) and give the status line `statusHelp` as its accessibility hint; Tab goes down from one pane's field to the next.
  - `ToolPicker(editor:)` reads `registry.toolTip(for:)`; it takes no `help:` closure. It shows the tool lock on the selected segment ([keyboard](keyboard.md)).
  - The menu bar: `BoardMenus.make(appName:registry:editor:)` (the app's `BoardMenus.install(appName:registry:)`), the window's own items and the commands placed in it, each item carrying its command (`BoardMenus.command(of:)`), followed by a hidden item for each other key of its command held with ⌘ or ⌃ (`BoardMenus.addOtherKeys`: Go › Back's ⌃-), and sending `BoardWindowController.runCommand:`, the controller's one action for every command, whose one validation dims an item while `editor.canPerform` says no and while text has the keyboard for a `.whenIdle` command, and retitles it by `editor.menuTitle(of:)`. Edit's Undo, Redo, Cut, Copy, Paste, Delete and Select All are AppKit's own, run on the board as the commands that answer them (`editor.perform(answering:)`): the window's `undo:` and `redo:`, the controller's `cut:`, `copy:` and `paste:`, the canvas's `delete:` and `selectAll:` ([keyboard](keyboard.md)).
  - Panes (P1, P3 of [keyboard navigation](keyboard-navigation.md)). `HostSidebar.keyboardEntry` and `HostInspector.keyboardEntry` (optional, `registrar.hostSidebar(keyboardEntry:)` and `registrar.hostInspector(keyboardEntry:)`) are the views each says takes the keyboard now (the table of contents declares its outline, the detail panel its header's field). View › Focus Sidebar (⌥⌘S, `focusSidebar:`, enabled with a `hostSidebar`) shows a hidden sidebar and gives the keyboard to `sidebarKeyView()`: the declared entry, else by kind among the views on the sidebar's sheet; with nothing there to take it (an empty board's list) it leaves the keyboard where it is. View › Focus Inspector (⌥⌘I, `focusInspector:`) is enabled while `canToggle`, shows the inspector by the person's choice if the rule doesn't show it, and gives the keyboard to `inspectorKeyView()`: the declared entry, else by kind among the views on the inspector's sheet, never the sidebar's list, and when the panel has none at all (after the retries a just-shown inspector needs) to the window's hosting view, so Tab enters the loop: it never stops silently. By kind is `keyView(in:)`: of the window content's views that are shown, accept first responder, aren't scroll or clip views and are in that pane (`pane(of:markers:)`: a view whose middle is on a sheet's marker, the sheets lying over the canvas, is that sheet's; what the window puts over the canvas elsewhere, the path's crumbs, is the canvas's), a table (`NSTableView`), text field or text view before any other control, the topmost then the leftmost of its kind; both try again each 16 ms for up to 30 turns while a pane just shown has no views yet (`InspectorFocusStyle`, `editor.scheduler`). View › Focus Next Pane (F6) and Focus Previous Pane (⇧F6) are `BoardWindowController`'s (`focusNextPane:`, `focusPreviousPane:`, placed after the inspector tabs) and move the keyboard round the panes shown (`Pane`: `.canvas`, `.inspector`, `.toolbar`, `.sidebar`; `shownPanes()`: the canvas; the inspector while the rule shows it; the toolbar while it is visible and one of its items' views, or a view in one, takes the keyboard; the sidebar while it is shown and a host sidebar exists), so F6 goes canvas, inspector, toolbar, sidebar and round, and ⇧F6 is the exact inverse. `currentPane()` reads where the keyboard is by the first responder: the toolbar's views are the toolbar's pane; of the window's content, a view is in the pane its middle is in (`pane(of:markers:)`): on the sidebar's sheet the sidebar's and on the inspector's the inspector's (each sheet's `PaneMarkerView`, whose frame the window reads, since the canvas fills the window under them), else the canvas's (so a view the window puts over the canvas, the board path or Jump To's field, is the canvas's pane); the content (hosting) view itself, where Focus Inspector's last resort leaves it, the inspector's; nowhere, on the window itself: F6 then goes to the second pane, ⇧F6 to the last. A shape being edited ends first, and the way back to the canvas is `CanvasFocus.take`. Escape in the toolbar, which takes no text, or on the bare hosting view, gives the keyboard back to the canvas (`BoardWindow.escapeToCanvas`, the selection kept), with Caps Lock on too (`Modifiers(event.modifierFlags)`, the four that change a key). Ctrl-F6 is the system's floating-window key and F6 may need fn on a laptop, which is why the items name F6 in the window. The toolbar's item views exist only once the window is on screen, so CompositionTests' `inspectorFocusShippedTests` put a segmented control into the unshown toolbar's item view.
  - `BoardWindowController`'s own items, with no command: Edit › Copy as Image (⇧⌘C, `copyAsImage:`) is dimmed unless `registry.imageSource?.isAvailable` and otherwise draws `source.shapes(for:)` from the board's own file with `BoardPicture(file: editor.file, registry:, board: editor.board, showing:)` (the whole file in the picture's editor, as opening it reads it, so the file's own colors, the types notes are of and what mentions name are there to read, wherever they are; only those shapes drawn, the core's `editor.drawnShapes`, and fitted; a big file costs what opening it does; `picture.inset = ImageCopyStyle.inset`, 32 points of margin in all; `pngData(within: ImageCopyStyle.limit, scale: ImageCopyStyle.scale, appearance:)`: at most 2048 × 2048 points, 2 pixels to a point, in the window's `effectiveAppearance`, the PNG's resolution saying so) and writes the PNG to `BoardConfiguration.imagePasteboard` (`.general` in the app, so `BoardConfiguration(registry:imagePasteboard:content:)`; a test passes its own, as CompositionTests' `configureBoards(pasteboard:)` does, and no test reads or writes the general one); View › Show Sidebar (⌃⌘S, `toggleSidebarShown:`, titled Show or Hide Sidebar, enabled with a `hostSidebar`) and Focus Sidebar (⌥⌘S); View › Show Inspector (⌃⌘I, `toggleDetails:`), the rule's `toggle`, titled by `isShown` and enabled while `canToggle` (so dimmed with nothing selected and nothing wanting the inspector), and Focus Inspector (⌥⌘I), enabled likewise: how each gives the keyboard is the panes' above. Escape from the sidebar or the inspector gives the keyboard back to the canvas through the view's `onExit` ([detail panel](detail-panel.md), [table of contents](table-of-contents.md)).
  - `KeyboardShortcutsWindow(registry:menu:autosaveName:)`: Help › Keyboard Shortcuts (⌥⌘/), `registry.keyboardHelp` and the menu bar filed into sections (`KeyboardShortcutSection.sections(menu:rows:)`, [keyboard](keyboard.md)), made afresh each time it opens, as Settings is: its rows read again and its list a new view under an identity per opening (`KeyboardShortcutsView.opening`; a hosting controller keeps its root's state when the root is replaced), so nothing typed last time stays, and the search is given the keyboard again (AppKit does it only as a window is first shown). It opens with a search field focused (`KeyboardShortcutSection.filtered(_:by:)`: `FuzzyMatcher` over a row's title and each of its keys, best first, a section with no match gone); a row is one accessibility element reading "Title, keys" (`Row.spoken`) and a section title is a header; the rows are still generated, never written by hand (P6, P9).
  - `BoardConfiguration(registry:content:)` has no `coveredTop` or `boardName`: both come with the registry.
  - `CommandServer.Commands(registry:)` is `registry.hostCommands`, what the server runs beyond its own.
- **The app** (`Sources/InfiniteCanvas/InfiniteCanvasApp.swift`) imports Canvas and CanvasUI, and no plugin: it builds the registry from the product's list, each plugin made by its name (`Registry(plugins: ShippedList.plugins())`, [build](build.md), What ships), hands it to `BoardConfiguration`, `SettingsWindow(registry:)`, `KeyboardShortcutsWindow(registry:)`, `BoardMenus.install(appName:registry:)` and `CommandServer.Commands(registry:)`, opens a new, empty board when it's launched with nothing to open, and its `ContentView` is the canvas, the toolbar's `ToolPicker`, and `BoardChrome`.
- **Adding a slot**: a point in `HostSlots.swift` owned by `.core`, its sugar on `PluginRegistrar`, a public reader on `Registry`, and the window's use of it in CanvasUI. Never a module that imports both sides, and never a plugin's type in CanvasUI or the app.

## The window's look

- **Chrome** (`Sources/Chrome/Chrome.swift`, a core library on Palette, which knows no canvas, registry or plugin) is the look every surface around the board shares, so the two panes and what floats over the board read as one kind of thing (the owner's ask, 2026-11-01: the sidebar had been the system's floating glass and the inspector its flush sheet, rounded every which way). `ChromeStyle` (`@MainActor`, public): the panes' and the window's floats' surface (`surface`, one tone the board never wears: white 18.4% dark, 92.2% light), the `hairline` where a sheet meets the board and round what floats, the `shade` a sheet lays on the board along its inner edge (`shadeWidth`, 12), the float's shadow (`floatShadow`, `floatShadowY`, `floatShadowBlur`), the fill of the chosen item and of an item under the pointer (`selectedFill`, the system's unemphasized selection; `hoverFill`); one rule for every corner: an item (a row, a tab, a field, a button, a crumb) `itemCorner`, 6; what floats holds its items that far in, so `floatCorner`, 12, or a capsule where it's shorter than twice that (`floatCorner(height:)`); a sheet square, its one curve the window's own; a pane's header, the same in both panes (`headerHeight` 44, `headerInset` 16, `headerSymbolWidth` 20, `headerSymbol` 15 points, `headerTitle` and `headerTitleFont` 13 semibold, `headerSpacing` 6), a row's highlight's inset from a pane's sides (`rowHighlightInset`, 8), how a pane comes and goes (`paneIn`, 0.2 s; `paneFade`, 0.15 s with Reduce Motion) and the grip a drag of its edge resizes it by (`resizeGrip`, 6). `floatingSurface(in:)`, a `View` extension, sets a view on that surface as the window's floats are: filled within a shape, a hairline round it, the float's shadow.
- Who reads it, each through its own style enum (which keeps only what's its own): CanvasUI (`PaneSheet`, the minimap's frame and the zoom, a card's and a popover's corners and shadow, which keep their own fill), TableOfContents (the list's pinned header and its rows' highlight), DetailPanel (the header, tabs and fields), Boards (the path's pill and a crumb's menu, through `floatingSurface`), JumpTo (its field, likewise), Find (its rows' corners) and TextEditing (the caret list's corners: its window keeps the menu's material). A surface added around the board reads it too, rather than numbers of its own.
