# Adornments

Adornments, the small things shown on a shape (Titles' pill, Locking's chip): placing one, its places, layout, drawing, pointing and cost, and how to test them.

Related: [titles](titles.md), [locking](locking.md), [selection](selection.md), [drawing](drawing.md), [validation](validation.md) ([every topic](../../CLAUDE.md#map)).

In the guide, `docs/plugin-system.md`: [Adornments](../../docs/plugin-system.md#adornments).

## Architecture

- Adornments (platform, a horizontal in the window, on Selection) lays out and draws the small things shown on a shape, just outside it or on its corner, from what the shape knows: Titles' pill (`TitlePill`) and Locking's lock (`LockChip`) are adornments. An adornment is a type (`Adornment`: its `placement` and its `order`, in the tabs' bands: 0 a kind's own, 50 a horizontal's feature, 300 a state that comes and goes), never a record. Any plugin places one, on kinds it defines or not: `x.adornment(A.self) { shape in … }` for a kind's own (a horizontal's opt-in sugar may call it, so opting in brings the adornment), `registrar.adornment(A.self, on: Kind.self) { … }` for another plugin's kind, and `scope.adornment(A.self) { shape, context in … }` on the kinds a scope reaches (`registrar.forShapes(holding: LockedProperty.self) { lock in lock.adornment(LockChip.self) { … } }`: Locking's lock goes only on kinds that can be locked; `forShapes(adopting: .titlePill)`, Titles' pill on the kinds whose title shows one; [plugins and kinds](plugins-and-kinds.md)), which the registry resolves once a kind (`ShapeAdornments` takes scopes: one for every kind with none is refused). A kind's own takes the place of a scoped one of the same type, and one adornment twice on a kind is a conflict (`ShapeAdornments` is keyed). A label is an `AdornmentLabel` (one line of text, after a small SF Symbol if it names one, `AdornmentLabel(title, systemImage:)`, in a place as wide as the text is tall, a little softer than the text unless the label says `brightSymbol()` (as white as the text: Publishing's globe, a symbol that is itself the news); or an SF Symbol alone in a disc; `tone: .success` is green; `counted(_:)`, a number after its text, and `line(_:ink:)`, plain text with no capsule: **Plain text and a number** below; `opacity(_:)`, `compact(_:)`: a narrower face the label takes at the row's right end when the full one would leave the left end's text, a title's pill, cut short or out and the narrower one gives it more room, below), or nil for none on that shape, and every plugin's looks alike. It reads only its shape, the registry, and services that call `editor.adornmentsDidChange(for:)` when what they keep changes (`ChipFade`'s fading chips). Places (`AdornmentPlacement`; a fourth, a label's lead, is **A label's lead** below): the row 9 points above the top edge, 18 tall, from its left end (`aboveTopLeft`: text gives way to what's at the other end, ending in "…", and needs 30 points, and its symbol's place more) and from its right end (`aboveTopRight`: whole); the top-right corner (`onTopRightCorner`: 16 tall, centered on the top edge, from 6 points past the right edge). `ChipFade` (a public service of `AdornmentsPlugin`, per editor) is the linger-and-fade state every chip that shows a moment shares (Locking's open lock; Publishing's "Private"): a plugin `show`s a chip on a shape (`fade.show(A.self, on: id)`: in full for 1500 ms, re-looked at every 500 ms while the pointer's on it, then 300 ms of fading in 16 ms frames on `editor.scheduler`, each frame `adornmentsDidChange`), reads `opacity(of:on:)` from its label (nil when not showing), and `hide`s it (returns whether it was showing) or asks `isShowing`; keyed by chip (its type's identity, so asking makes no `AdornmentID`) and shape, so two chips on one shape fade apart, every ask a dictionary hit and nothing while no chip shows (a commit asks about each shape it deletes, a frame about each in view; Locking and Publishing find the service once), `show` on a showing chip starts its linger over, a deleted shape's chips go (told once) and another board shown stops all; the caller tells Adornments for what `show` and `hide` do, once for all it changes (`AdornmentStyle` holds the times). The compact face is the layout's (`AdornmentLayout.Item.compact`, pure): after the row is laid out in full, if some left-end text isn't whole (cut short or out), the right end's items that have a compact face take it, nearest the edge first: the fewest that give the left-end texts as much width in all as every one of them does (so one already whole goes no farther), and every one when even that shows no text; if the full faces show every text whole, or the row has no text at its left end, none is. So as a shape widens, however narrow it starts, the faces change once: compact, until the full faces and the whole text fit together, then full (a public note's title at 50% zoom: compact from the narrowest, no title below 92 points, "Payments ro…" beside the globe and the count from 92, whole from 179, the full "Public · 3" from 221), where the earlier rules flipped the chip full, compact and full again; only `aboveTopRight` items take one, an item without one costs nothing, the compact face is made with the label (`LaidOutAdornments.compactFaces`, counted by `adornmentFacesMade`), is the label `adornmentFrames(of:)` reports where taken, and fades with the label (`opacity(_:)` sets both). Each place is a run, 4 points apart, so on one shape none overlap, by construction (`AdornmentLayout`, pure); the row's right end and the corner reach no more than 200 points (what would is left out), and its left end stays within the shape's width, which bounds culling and hit testing; sizes are view points, constant down to 25%. Below 35% a shape drawn as a skeleton (`editor.drawsSkeleton(of:)`) shows no adornments: none is made, laid out, drawn or pointed at for it (what a title says is said on the shape instead, Titles' cover); the shape being edited, drawn in full, keeps its own, in full, text and all. Drawing, hit testing and `adornmentFrames(of:)` all go by `drawsSkeleton(of:)` (the overlay reads the shape being edited once a frame, and returns at once with nothing to draw), so what's hit is what's drawn (a lock can't be clicked on a skeleton, and the edited shape's pill is hit in full). `AdornmentState` keeps each shape's labels with their faces (text set and measured, symbols' images), made together for what's drawn or pointed at, and drops them for what each commit's `ChangeSet` names but for the shapes that only moved (labels are laid out against the shape's bounds when asked, never kept at a place, so a drag of thousands makes none again; CacheTests), a group's "+N" faces made once for each text and place (the same on every shape); read as services hear a commit, it has heard it (looking it up has it hear the commit first: [editor](editor.md)); what `adornmentsDidChange` is told has its leads looked at once every service has settled the change being delivered, if one is (`editor.onceSettled`), and when a pill's room moved the select tool then looks again under the pointer; on `drawsSkeletonsDidChange` it lets everything kept go as skeletons start (the edited shape's are made again as it's drawn), and nothing more: Selection hears it too and looks again under a still pointer, buttons first, for a button come or gone. `AdornmentOverlay` draws them in one pass over `editor.paintOrder(in:)` (the view grown by `context.worldDrawingReach` too, so a shape shaken into view shows them), above every shape, where the shape is drawn (a shake moves them), never in the minimap; once labels are kept, a frame allocates nothing per adorned shape (one slots buffer a frame, `AdornmentLayout.slots(of:width:into:)`, and the hovered and pressed buttons read once and compared field by field, never boxed). A `ClickableAdornment` (the lock) is a canvas button, whose `click(_:in:)` gets an `AdornmentClick` (the shape, the label's `AdornmentTag`, the chip's rect in world units, `isOverflow`), and its shape's only for the context menu; any other adornment is its shape for the select tool, hover and a double-click, and every adornment, the lock too, for the context menu (Selection's `DecorationHits`). `editor.adornmentFrames(of:)` is where each of a shape's is, in world units. `editor.adornmentExtent(of:)` is how far above its top edge a shape reaches with the adornments it would show at 100% zoom (`AdornmentLayout.extent(of:in:)`, pure: 27 view points for the row, 8 for the corner, 0 for none, text left out for want of room counting for nothing), asked of any record, on a board or not, and keeping nothing for it (`AdornmentState.laidOut(_:keeping:)`): `canvas measure`'s `above` ([command line](command-line.md)). Debug builds count the shapes whose labels are kept (`adornmentsKept`, those with none too), those that show something, their faces made (`adornmentFacesKept`), and the faces made (`adornmentFacesMade`).

**Plain text and a number.** `AdornmentLabel.line(_:ink:)` is plain text with no capsule (`lineInk`, `isLine`), in the 11-point system font, in `.primary` (the canvas's label color) or `.secondary` (the softer one), set once in each appearance (`AdornmentStyle.Face.line(light:dark:size:ascent:width:)`, two Core Text lines chosen by `NSAppearance.currentDrawing()` when drawn, `CTLineDraw` at the baseline a string drawn at the frame's top sets, a whole `ascent` below it: pixel for pixel what string drawing drew, which typeset the text again each draw), laid out in the row as a chip of its width is and drawn with no look for hover or press: Types' `from` and `to` above an end's arrow ([types](types.md)). `AdornmentLabel.counted(_:)` (`count`) puts a number after a chip's text, `AdornmentStyle.countGap` (4) past it, in the text's ink at `countStrength` (60%): the chip is wider by the gap and the number (`AdornmentStyle.countRoom(of:in:)`), and the row's layout counts that room with the text's lead (`ShownAdornment.lead`), so where the text gives way it is cut and the number stays whole at the capsule's right end. It is part of what makes two labels equal, none for a symbol alone or a negative number, and its copies keep it (`wrong()`, `tinted(_:)`, `tagged(_:)`, `opacity(_:)`). Types' relationship chip on an arrow says with it how many fields the arrow's relationship type gives it ([types](types.md)). Nothing here knows a relationship.

**A label's lead.** `AdornmentPlacement.inLabelLead` is the pill at the start of an open shape's label (an arrow's, for a relationship: Types' `RelationshipPill`), in the line's cut, with the label's words after it. It is the one place not laid out against the shape's anchor: the shape's geometry keeps room for it (`ShapeGeometry.labelLead`, a rect in world units within `label`, the core's, which knows nothing of what is drawn in it) and the layout puts the slot there (`AdornmentState.placeLeads`: a `Slot` with `lead` set, its frame that rect's size at the origin, and `unit` 1: one world unit to a point, so it follows the zoom as the words do, where every other place keeps its size on screen down to 25%; `AdornmentOverlay` draws it from the rect, a shake included; `hit` reads it in world units; `adornmentFrames(of:)` says its rect), and nothing where the geometry kept none. The geometry asks how wide the pill is through a layout input Adornments provides (`AdornmentLead.faces`, `registrar.layoutInput` in `AdornmentsPlugin`, answered by `AdornmentState.leadFaces(of:recording:)` from the first of the kind's contributions at `inLabelLead` whose label shows something): the size of each face the label may take, widest first, and its tint (`AdornmentLead.Faces`); the faces are the label (`AdornmentStyle.face(of:in:)`, its width cut at `leadMaxWidth`, 240) and `AdornmentLabel.narrowing(to:)` (the narrower faces: the name without its note, a dot), measured once for each label (512 kept). The geometry takes the first that fits (Arrows': [arrows](arrows.md)) and gives the rect that size; Adornments draws the face whose size the rect is (`Slot.lead.face`, `LaidOutAdornments.look(of:in:)`). What the pill says changing with no commit to the shape (`adornmentsDidChange(for:)`, which a rename, a check and a change of a type's fields already call) compares the answer the geometry last read (kept per shape, `leadAnswered`, only for a kind that has a lead and only as the editor's own reading: a transaction's is not what is on the board; one from before a commit that changed the shape is void) and lays the shapes out again (`editor.shapesDidChangeLayout`) where it moved, never when it did not; no commit, nothing stored. A mark is a label with no text (`AdornmentLabel.mark(.dot)`: a disc `dotSize` (10) across, in the tint's color at 70% (85% hovered, solid pressed) with the light line, `.ghost`: a dashed capsule, `ghostWidth` (30) by a row, in the canvas's label colors, as a missing chip's dashes), and a note (`noted(_:)`) is a word after the text in the number's ink, after a `·` (`noteMark`), kept whole where the text gives way, as a number is. Nothing here knows a relationship or an arrow. A pill is a canvas button (`ClickableAdornment`): the lead is hovered and pressed as any chip. Zoomed out nothing is drawn by Adornments (a skeleton shows none): the shape's own skeleton draws a capsule where the rect is, from `AdornmentLead.faces`' tint (Arrows').

**Slanted open shapes.** (On a point anchor the row is centred first, **Anchors**, and the slide starts from there: the limits below are the same, the row moves only as far as it must from where centring put it, and the row is continuous in the slope, centring being constant in it.) The row above an open shape's anchor never sits across the shape's own body: it slides along the anchor, toward the side the body leaves clear, only as far as it must (`AdornmentLayout`, pure; `Slant.slope` is the chord's dy / dx, y down, read where `AdornmentState.anchored(of:in:zoom:)` reads the anchor, for an open shape only: a closed shape's is nil and nothing of it is read). With `h` half the anchor's height and `margin` (4) the clear space, a slot grown by `margin` on every side is not crossed by the body's centre line through the anchor's middle. The row (bottom `h + rowGap` above the middle) moves whole, its right end and the corner with its left end, so each keeps its own packing: where the body falls to the right (slope > 0) its leftmost point is no farther left than `-(h + rowGap - margin) / slope + margin` from the middle, where it rises (slope < 0) its rightmost no farther right than `(h + rowGap - margin) / |slope| - margin` (an upright chord counts as falling); the slide is at most `maxSlide` (200 world units, or what a 120-point anchor needs if that is more: `slideLimit(atScale:)`), which bounds the reach of an anchor as wide as an arrow's label. The tally on the corner rides along and is not held clear. A level chord (slope 0) slides nothing: its row is laid out as centring puts it on a point anchor, and as it was on a labelled or closed shape. A turn of the arrow moves each slot continuously. The layout slides the slots against the anchor `AdornmentState.anchored(of:in:zoom:)` gives (the middle of the outline, widened), which does not move, so the layout stays pure; `editor.adornmentAnchor(of:)` is that anchor moved along by the row's centring and slide (`AdornmentState.rowAnchor(of:)`: the layout run, its shift in world units; the plain anchor when nothing is shown, or the shape is closed), so what hangs from it (an arrow's card, the list at an arrow, the name card) hangs from where the row is: at the chip's left edge, on a level arrow with one chip too (the card, the list and the name card open at that edge, which is now half a chip left of the arrow's middle, not 60 points); a click's rect and `adornmentFrames(of:)` are the slid slots. **What holds, and what does not**: the slide reasons from the chord, the line from the outline's first point to its last, which is parallel to the tangent of a bent arrow's arc at its middle, so the slots are clear of the tangent line through the anchor on any arrow; a straight arrow's body is that line, so a straight arrow's chip is never crossed. A bent arrow's arc leaves the tangent across the anchor's 120 points, so on a bent arrow (a second arrow between two notes is stored bent apart from the first) a chip may still be crossed by the arc, more as the bend grows; a level bent arrow always was. Not fixed: the slide would need the arc itself, not the chord. Measured against the arc itself (the drawn body flattened; `Geometry.outline` is only start, middle and end and cannot see a bow), with no words, Employment's chip, at 150, 250 and 400 long and level or 10, 20 and 35 degrees either way: at a -34 bend the chip is clear everywhere, and at +34 the arc touches it at 150 long rising 10 degrees and comes nearer than the margin at a few others (the pinned cells are in `centreBentArrowTests`). Nothing is kept: the slope is read each frame from the geometry the editor keeps, so a bound note's move that turns the arrow lays the slots out again as it already did. A turn of the arrow through the vertical moves the row from one side of the body to the other (some 126 to 130 points for a chip), the one jump, which no layout that keeps the row clear on both sides can avoid. Culling's area grows left by `max(2 * maxRun, maxSlide + cornerOverhang)` and right by `max(2 * maxRun, maxSlide + maxRun)` (view points scaled, the `maxSlide` terms in world units).

**The wrong look.** `AdornmentLabel.wrong()` (`isWrong`) is a chip's look for something that is wrong: a light tint in the system red (`AdornmentLabel.wrongColor`: red at 14%, 22% hovered and 30% pressed, text and symbol red, following the appearance, as `tinted(_:)` does) without the 1-point line inside the edge, and under the text a wavy line, `waveWidth` (1) wide, a corner every `waveStep` (2) up and down `waveHeight` (1) from the text's baseline, stroked in the overlay with the chip (`AdornmentStyle.paintWave`: nothing is made for it). It wins over a tint, whichever is set first (`tinted(_:)` leaves a wrong label's red), a missing label stays missing (no fill, no wave), and a symbol alone is a red disc without the wave. Equal only to a wrong label, so a label whose wrongness changes lays the row out again. It is the look and nothing more: which chip is wrong is for the plugin that places it.

**Groups.** Types' `TypeChips` (order 100, after the title's pill) is the shipped group: one chip per type a shape is, light in the type's color, each tagged by its type's id, `.missing()` for one that is gone, clickable (a menu below the chip's rect, `editor.popUpMenu`), on an arrow above its label ([types](types.md)). One contribution may show several labels (`x.adornments(A.self) { shape in [...] }`, `registrar.adornments(A.self, on:)`, `scope.adornments(A.self)`; the single-label sugar is a group of one that isn't marked one). `ShapeAdornments.check` refuses a group anywhere but `aboveTopLeft` (`RegistryError.invalid`, naming the adornment and the plugin): at the right end a run is cut at `maxRun` with everything after it (the lock), and the corner is a tally's. `AdornmentState` flattens each contribution's labels into one `ShownAdornment` each (blank labels left out first), carrying its `AdornmentTag` (`label.tagged(_:)`), its group (the contribution's index) and, for a group, its "+N" label and face (`overflowFaces`, made and counted with the label's; "+N" is `AdornmentStyle.overflowPrefix` and the count of labels from this one on). In `AdornmentLayout` (pure) an `Item` has `group` and `overflowWidth`: in the row's left end, a group's item is whole or folded, but for the group's last, which may be cut short like any text (30 points at least, else it folds into "+1"); the first that isn't whole, and the rest of its group, are one slot (`isOverflow`, at that item's index, the rest nil), which always shows: when the "+N" doesn't fit whole after the chips shown before it, they fold into it, the later first, until it does (the "+N" then counts more), and when not even the group's first chip's "+N" fits none of the group is shown and the run ends (a chip shown with some left out and no "+N" would hide them silently). Text before a group at the left end (a title's pill) leaves the first group item's `overflowWidth` and a gap (`reservation`), but never under the 30 points it needs: so a long title can't hide every chip, "+N" included (pinned in GroupTests). A group's chips are not title text: the compact-face rule counts only items with no group. A button is `AdornmentButton(A.self, on:, tag:)`, the "+N" `AdornmentButton.overflow(of:on:)` (tag nil, `isOverflow`), and hover and pressed compare tag and overflow, so only the chip under the pointer lights. `AdornmentFrame` has `tag` and `isOverflow` (its `label` is the "+N" label for the fold). `AdornmentLabel.tinted(_:)` (an `AdornmentTint`: a color and an ink, either of which may follow the appearance (`NSColor(swatch.solid)`), `.solid` or `.light`; the ink is white for a solid tint and the color itself for a light one when not given) draws the capsule in a color in place of the tone's: solid is the color (hover 15% toward white, pressed 20% toward black) with its text and symbol in the ink (Types' pill: the color's `onSolid`, black on yellow and orange; a placeholder's at 55%); light is the color at 14% (22%, 30%) with a 1-point line of it at 35%, the text and symbol its ink (a placeholder's at 60%); a tinted label's symbol is made again for the appearance drawn; the fills are made once a tint and appearance (`AdornmentStyle.fills(of:)`, a dictionary keyed by the tint and whether the appearance drawn is dark, its color resolved to what it is in that appearance, so a tint whose color follows the appearance has a fill for each and nothing is made per frame for a label that did not change; the appearance's darkness is asked once an appearance, not a label; 1,024 are kept, then the lot is let go), and a dynamic color is equal to itself only, so a caller that makes tints again and again for one color keeps the tint it made (Types' `TypeColors` keeps one `TypeLook` a swatch) or the dictionary grows; a tint wins over the tone, a missing label ignores it, `opacity(_:)`, `tagged(_:)` and `brightSymbol()` keep it, a compact face and the group's "+N" are tinted only if whoever made them did (the "+N" never), and the tint is part of `==`, so a changed tint lays the row out again. `AdornmentLabel.missing()` is the look of a name that is gone: no fill, a dashed stroke (`AdornmentStyle.missingStroke`, `missingDash`, as Mentions' missing pills), text in italic (`missingText`) in the canvas's dynamic label colors, no symbol before the text (white), a dashed ring for a symbol alone, a faint fill hovered or pressed.

**Anchors.** `editor.adornmentAnchor(of:)` (public) is where a shape's adornment row is anchored, in world units at the zoom now, nil for a shape that is not there or has no outline: what something that hangs from the same place goes by (a menu under an arrow's label, a card at a shape's pill), so it hangs where the row does, moved along with it where the row slid (**Slanted open shapes**) (AdornmentsTests' `anchorTests`: a note's top-left, above an arrow's label or the middle of its outline, widened, at 50%; `laidOutAdornmentTests`: a shape laid out smaller than it stores). A shape's adornments are laid out from `AdornmentState.anchor(of:in:zoom:)`: a closed shape's bounds (its geometry's, so a box laid out larger or smaller than it stores, a `LaidOutBox`, has its chip row at its laid-out right edge, and it follows a layout change with no commit, since nothing is kept); an open shape's (`geometry.isClosed == false`) `geometry.label` if it has one, else a zero-size rect at its outline's middle point (`outline[count / 2]`); either widened about its center to `AdornmentLayout.minAnchorWidth` (120 view points, so `120 * scale` world units; it lives on the pure layout, which can't read the main-actor style enum). So an arrow's lock, title pill and chips sit above its label, or its middle. **A point anchor's row is centred**: for an open shape with no label (`Anchored.isPoint`, given to the layout as `Slant.centersRow`) the row is not packed from the widened rect's left edge (it has no left edge of its own: that 60 points left of the middle put an arrow's chip off its middle, and an end's `from` over the note a short arrow starts from) but moved whole, in `slideRow`, so the middle of its extent (the left end's run and the right end's items together, the corner riding along, as the slide's extent is read) is the anchor's middle; a chip of one is centred above the middle, a chip and a lock, or text and a flag, fill the 120 points and stay, and a chip wider than the anchor reaches past it either side. A labelled shape keeps its row from the label's left edge and a closed shape's reads nothing of it (no `Slant`, or one with `centersRow` false). It is the layout's, pure, with nothing kept. The area still holds every slot: a centred run is at most 200 points wide (the right end reaches `maxRun` from the right edge, the left end stays within the width), so it lies within 100 points of the middle, from -40 to 160 of a 120-point anchor, inside the 400 the area grows by either side for a slid row, and the corner moves with it (AdornmentsTests' CentreTests hold the area over every row at three scales). `AdornmentLayout.area(showing:scale:)` grows left by `cornerOverhang + minAnchorWidth / 2` (66, not the bare 6) for the row: an anchor reaches 60 points past a label's center, and the corner 6 more, so a line just left of the view whose chips reach in is found by `paintOrder(in:)`, drawn and hit. A slanted shape's slid row widens both sides (**Slanted open shapes**): left by 400 and right by 400 in view points (a centred run and a slide of up to 264), with a world-unit term for an anchor as wide as a label. The click's rect is the slot's world rect against the anchor. **What a frame makes and draws is less than the area.** The area is the widest reach any shape has, and 400 points either way is a few columns of notes more than a view holds: a note's first frame at 35% made the labels of 87 notes more than it shows, and drew a symbol (some 170 µs a draw, the cost of an adornment with a symbol) for each whose chip was out of the view. So `AdornmentState.mayShow` (the overlay's and `hit`'s) lets a closed shape through only if its own reach, `AdornmentLayout.reach(ofClosed:scale:)` (the row's right end and the corner up to `maxRun` leftward of the right edge, the left end in the width, the corner 6 past; held to the layout's property check in LayoutTests), meets the view, and an open shape stays as the area has it; and the overlay draws a slot only if its frame, grown by `slotMargin` (4 points: a dash, a descender, a wave), meets the view. Nothing is drawn that was visible, and a gallery of the states is byte for byte what it was (`./build.sh bench`: a pan's frame of 10,000 shapes 8.7 ms to 6.6, its first frame at 35% 43.8 to 30.8).

**In content.** A label is also drawn inside a shape, as a chip in a row of fields (Types' rows): `label.contentSize` (the row's 18 points tall, its face's width) and `label.draw(at:in:)` (top-left at the point, in the context's units, so in a shape's render in world units, scaling with the zoom as the shape's text does), `AdornmentContent.swift`. It is the same face and the same drawing as the row above a shape (`AdornmentStyle.face(of:in: .aboveTopLeft)`, `draw` at scale 1), the face kept by label (512, then the lot is let go; debug builds count them, `AdornmentContentCounts.facesMade`), and always drawn as in the light appearance: a shape's body is the same in both, so a tint's colors (which follow the canvas) and a missing chip's dashes and italic (the canvas's label color, readable on a dark canvas and not on a note's paper) are resolved light. Adornments learns nothing of the caller: Types builds the label with its own chips.

## Testing

- LeadTests (`leadTests`, over the tether, an open shape of the target's own that does what an arrow does: its label is the lead's room): the slot is the rect the geometry kept at every zoom and is that rect in world units; the faces (widest first, the tint, a dot 10 across); the face the room is the size of (the whole, the name alone, a dot, each tagged alike); nothing for a geometry that kept none or a kind that has no lead; a canvas button (a click in the rect, not in the padding or off it, at 100% and 50%, hovered); drawn in its rect and nowhere else, at 100% and 200%; a dot and a ghost drawn; what it says changing with no commit laying the shape out again once and only if its size moved; the label's `noted`, `narrowing(to:)` and marks.

- ChipFadeTests hold the fade on fixture chips (`Ghost`, `Echo`: the timings, hover, restart, hide, two chips apart, a deleted shape, another board); CompactTests the compact face, pure on fixture items (`AdornmentLayout.frames`: kept in full with room, taken to give a title the most width they can (whole, or cut short where it would have vanished or been cut shorter) with the fewest that do, all of them where no title shows even then, nearest the edge first, only the right end, one change of face across a sweep of widths from the narrowest, a seeded property check) and through a board (frames, a title the full chip pushes out drawn cut short beside the disc at 100% and at 50%, pixels, opacity).
- For adornments, `AdornmentLayout.frames(of:width:)` is pure (AdornmentsTests' layout tests and their seeded property check), as `AdornmentLayout.extent(of:in:)` is, and `editor.adornmentFrames(of:)` gives a shape's headlessly, in world units, where it is (not where a shake draws it). Pixels go through `renderBitmap` and `changedPixels`; a clickable one's identity, as `editor.hoveredButton` holds it, is `AdornmentButton(A.self, on: id)`.
