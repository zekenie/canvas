# References between shapes

Values that bind other shapes (`ShapeReferencing`) or name them (`ShapeNaming`), and what deleting, copying, cutting and drawing do to each.

Related: [clipboard](clipboard.md), [mentions](mentions.md), [arrows](arrows.md), [topology](topology.md), [command line](command-line.md), [properties and schemas](properties-and-schemas.md) ([every topic](../../CLAUDE.md#map)).

In the guide, `docs/plugin-system.md`: [Values that name shapes](../../docs/plugin-system.md#values-that-name-shapes).

## Architecture

- References between shapes have two forms behind one entry, `record.remappingReferences(_:in:)` (`docs/plugin-system.md`, "Values that name shapes"). Bindings (`ShapeReferencing`, a kind's definition: an arrow's ends) must be on the board: a delete lets them go (or, for a shape of a kind that takes bound shapes, `TakesBoundShapes`, deletes them but a read-only one, which lets go), a copy takes along what is bound only to shapes copied when one of those takes bound shapes, a copy renames those copied along and lets the rest go, moving shapes onto the board below lets go of those across the edge, both ways (`transaction.move(_:toBoardOwnedBy:)`: [boards in a file](boards-in-a-file.md)), and `canvas draw` refuses one that isn't there (`record.boundShapes(in:)`); `registry.referencingKinds` (public) are the kinds that have them, the only ones a plugin walks. Names (`ShapeNaming`, a value: a mention) may be of a shape on any board of the file (ids are unique across it: `editor.file.record(id)`), and may outlive their shapes: a copy renames those of shapes copied along and keeps the rest (nil keeps a name; across a copied tree of boards, `remappingReferences(_:naming:in:)` passes names through the whole tree's map and bindings through their board's, as Clipboard's paste does), a cut and its paste move the shapes under their ids, so a name of one from outside what moved names it again (a binding to one was let go at the cut), a delete never looks for them (it asks only `referencingKinds`, so deleting a mentioned shape writes nothing), and `draw` keeps a name of a shape that's in no board of the file (or one the script deletes), or on a board the app can't show, and warns ("<shape>: cite names shapes that aren't in the file: …; they show as missing", "… names shapes on boards this app can't show: …"), and refuses a binding to one on another board as to one in none (`invalid_value`: "… is bound to a shape on another board of the file"). The core finds names through property lists: a kind's props by their list (a property whose member's type is `ShapeNaming`, found once by a runtime check, so a plain property carries nothing), a struct in them or in an added value adopts `ShapeNaming` in an extension with nothing to write (`extension MemoNote: ShapeNaming {}`), and the registry refuses one that holds names without adopting it; optionals, arrays, sets and dictionaries keyed by strings (an object with `additionalProperties` in a schema) of names are names, and a leaf coded by hand implements `forEachNamedShape` and `renamingShapes` (pure): mention text, a type's `SchemaText` (its `$ref`s), Types' `TypeLink`, one entry of a shape's `is`, a wrapper of one `ShapeID` coded as the bare uuid string (`ShapeID` itself must not be `ShapeNaming`: an arrow's bindings are ids, and a name is not a binding). `registry.namingKinds` are the kinds whose props or opted-in added values can hold names, and `record.forEachNamingValue(in:)` walks a shape's, with the property each is in (none kept aside, which a copy doesn't rename either); `value.forEachNamingLeaf` goes into each for the leaves it holds, however deep (how Mentions finds its text in a struct in a list). A kind without names costs a set lookup where names are walked, and nothing on a delete. Core tests use CanvasTesting's `NamingPlugin`: `Citation` (a leaf that counts its visits), `Memo` (names in a struct in a list, an optional, a dictionary, and `CitationsProperty`) and `CitingTag` (binds and names).
- **A binding to a part of a shape** (a row: [line rows](line-rows.md), the kit's columns). A kind that binds parts (arrows, from K3) says which in two defaulted `ShapeReferencing` requirements, `boundParts(of:)` (the `PartAddress`es it's bound to) and `movingParts(of:_:)` (its props with each passed through a map; nil when nothing changed); the record forms are `record.boundParts(in:)` and `record.movingParts(in:_:)`. A part goes by a write to its shape, which no delete handler hears, so the one writer of a kind's parts tells the transaction: `removeParts(_:)` (parts just taken out on this board: what's bound to one is deleted, counted in `deletedAlong`, when the part's shape takes bound shapes, else bound to that shape as a whole; a read-only one stays as it was, still naming the part, drawn where it was; returns what it deleted) and `moveParts(_:)` (parts moved to another place on the board, a part of another shape or a shape as a whole: every binding to one follows it, a read-only one left, then every `registrar.partsMoved` handler hears the moves, the core point `PartsMoved`, so what names a part as a value renames it in the same step). A key of `moveParts` may be a shape as a whole (part nil): a shape that was all its one row, gone as the row went into another shape (a taken-out endpoint put back, LineRows' `landRows`), so what names that shape as a whole (a mention, a pill for it) names the row where it went; no binding is to such a key, as `boundParts` lists parts alone. Each is one pass over the board's binding kinds, nothing for a list of none; a part that moves within its shape keeps its address and needs neither. LineRows' default `apply` calls `removeParts` for every row a commit takes out, and `canvas draw`'s `delete "orders".x` takes a row out through the kind's `PartedShape.removing(_:from:)` (`registry.removing`, defaulted nil) and then calls it. Arrows bind parts (an end at a row, `ArrowBinding.part`: [arrows](arrows.md)), so a table's column deleted deletes the keys at it ("Delete Column and 1 Arrow"), a table deleted its keys ("Delete Table and 3 Arrows"), and a copy of tables keeps the keys among them with their rows (part ids are the shape's own, so the part rides the renamed binding). Both reach a binding through a pill (K10): a key to a column on another board is bound to a pill for its table with the column on its binding, so each asks the one stand-in lookup a plugin gives the core (`registrar.standInLookup`, Topology's `standIns(naming:)`, the `CorePoints.StandInLookups` point, read through `FileAccess.standIns`; and its `names`, what one stand-in's record names (Topology's `standIns.directTarget(of:)`), so `Transaction.standIns(naming:)` also finds a pill the same transaction put in earlier, which the index doesn't hold yet (a ⌘L pick's or a script's pill, then a row taken out or moved in that step: K14, CanvasTests' `partsThroughPillsTests`); `transaction.standIns(naming:)` gives each with its board as the transaction leaves the file, the lookup itself as the file was before it) for the pills of each part's shape, and treats a binding to (pill, part) on the pill's board as one to the part, in the same step: `removeParts` deletes it (counted along, `delete(_:on:)`) when the part's shape takes bound shapes, else binds it to the pill as a whole; `moveParts` rebinds it to the part where it went through the same pill when it stays in that shape, straight to its shape when that is on the pill's board, through a pill there that stands for it, else to the pill as a whole (the core makes no pill). Without a lookup nothing is reached through a pill. CanvasTests' `partsThroughPillsTests` (a muster on the root, boxes on a pocket's board standing for it through a lookup the test writes, straps bound through them). CanvasTests' `partLifecycleTests` hold both over CanvasTesting's `StrapsPlugin` (`Muster`, a roster that takes bound shapes, and `Strap`, a box bound at each end to a shape or a row, with a `PartsMovedLog`): a strap at a muster's row deleted with the row and counted, one at a roster's row bound to the roster, a locked one left, straps rebound to where rows moved and the handler heard once, an undo of each. A step on a board that is not shown (`transact(on:)`) leaves the bindings on the board shown alone (`Transaction.mayWrite(on:)`, like `mayReachBoardShown`): a write there would drop the whole commit, so a key on the board shown through a pill stays bound to the pill (the geometry ignores a part it does not list), as do `partsMoved` handlers (References') that write on pills' boards.
- **Names of parts.** A value that names shapes may name one of a shape's parts (a mention of a table's row: `@[…](shape:…/row:…)`, [mentions](mentions.md)): `ShapeNaming.renamingParts(_:)` (defaulted to the value itself; collections, listed structs and added values go into what they hold, `AnyShapeRecord.renamingNamedParts(_:in:)` a record's, nil when nothing changed) renames those parts, and a name of a shape as a whole that the rename gives an address (a shape moved as a whole, above). `Transaction.moveParts` uses it: after rebinding what's bound to the moved parts, it renames the parts named in the values of the shapes that name the moved parts' shapes (`Transaction.shapesNaming(_:)`, the editor's lookup, Topology's: the carriers of the edges a property holds into a shape, `registrar.standInLookup(_:names:namers:)`), on any board the step may write, a read-only shape left as it was, then runs the `partsMoved` handlers. `NamedPartsTests` hold it over a leaf of the test's own (`PartCite`; a shape moved as a whole named as its row, here and on another board, Undo and Redo) in a kind's list, set, dictionary, optional and struct and in an added value, on this board and another, a read-only shape left as it was, Undo giving the names back with the move, and `editor.editingPart` (a shape's edited row, nil unless a shape is edited). A copy renames shapes and keeps parts.
