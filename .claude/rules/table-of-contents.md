# The table of contents

The table of contents: rows and marks, nesting, what's listed (front to back), hover, rows read from more than their shape, another board's outline, and dragging rows.

Related: [detail panel](detail-panel.md), [selection](selection.md), [neighbors](neighbors.md), [validation](validation.md), [locking](locking.md), [titles](titles.md), [mentions](mentions.md), [boards](boards.md), [containers](containers.md), [moving](moving.md), [boards in a file](boards-in-a-file.md) ([every topic](../../CLAUDE.md#map)).

In the guide, `docs/plugin-system.md`: [The table of contents](../../docs/plugin-system.md#the-table-of-contents), [Dragging rows](../../docs/plugin-system.md#dragging-rows), [Another board's outline](../../docs/plugin-system.md#another-boards-outline).

## Architecture

- The table of contents (TableOfContents, a surface) is a native `List` in the right-hand panel, with data-canvas's TOC contract: `x.contentsRow(when:nestedUnder:) { ContentsLabel(title, placeholder:, systemImage:, color:, badge:, standsIn:, path:) }`, and `registrar.contentsRow(P.self)` for another plugin's kind. The list owns the row's chrome; a row reads only its own shape (unless it names what else it reads, below; `contentsRow { shape, context in … }` reads services besides, with nothing `affected`, and keeps its row until the shape changes), and its symbol's color is the shape's, `context.color(of: shape)` (`editor.tint(of:)`: the color it has of its own where a plugin gives it one, Colors', else its kind's tint; every shipped kind's row passes it, an arrow's nil without one of its own). A change to the shape remakes the row; the file's colors changing, which makes no commit, remakes every row (`shapeTintsDidChange`: the rows of the board shown, those kept for boards left and the outlines of other boards, as the next read needs them), and uses `ContentsLabel` so rows from different plugins look alike (its text its tooltip, `label.help`, up to 200 characters, cut between words with "…", none for a placeholder). `standsIn: true` is a row that stands in for another shape and wears its name, symbol and color (a reference's: [references](references.md)), so it would read as that shape: `StandInArrow` cuts a round hole out of the symbol's bottom-leading corner and puts `arrow.turn.up.right` in it (6.5 pt black, in the title's color, secondary with a placeholder, the hole 10 pt, 1 pt out and 2 pt down, at the sidebar's medium rows and scaled with `sidebarRowSize` as its symbols are; the mask reaches 4 pt past the symbol's frame so no ink past it is cut: `ContentsStyle.standIn…`), a Finder alias's arrow, with no fill behind it, so it reads on any symbol and on a selected row; VoiceOver says the row's value is "Alias" (`standInSpoken`), since its name alone is its shape's; the list knows nothing of what it stands for, and the crumbs' outline draws it the same (it draws the row's `ContentsLabel`). `path:` is where what the row names is, for a row that names a shape somewhere else (a reference's original: [references](references.md)): the names of the shapes it's inside, outermost first (each a `ContentsPathName`, `text` and `isPlaceholder`; a string literal is one that's a title), shown before the title, dimmed, with "›" between (`ContentsStyle.pathSeparator`), "Alpha › Beta › Checkout" (`ContentsPathTitle`), a name that is only what its kind is called (`isPlaceholder`: an untitled frame's "Frame") fainter than a title (tertiary, the rest secondary), as Jump To's rows have it, so a frame nobody named doesn't read as one named "Frame"; the tooltip has it whole (`help`: the path, then the text, a placeholder's too when it has a path), VoiceOver says "Checkout, in Alpha › Beta" (`pathSpoken`), and `text` stays the title. The path gives way before the title: with the room, all of it; else the names between the first and the last folded into "…" (`ContentsLabel.folded(_:)`, nil for two names or fewer; `ViewThatFits`, so the fold is by the row's width, never a count: the owner's pick, the first and the nearest always said, where the path over the canvas folds past four crumbs); narrower than that, each name is cut short on its own, the longest first, none to less than `ContentsStyle.pathNameFloor` (34 pt at the sidebar's medium rows, scaled with them: a few letters and "…") or its own width, every "›" and the fold's "…" kept; and only then the title (`ContentsPathLine`, a `Layout` over texts tagged `ContentsPathPart` name, title or fixed: a stack with the title given priority pushed the names down to slivers between their "›"s). The list knows nothing of what a path is of. `registrar.contentsMarkForEveryShape(where:) { shape, context in ContentsMark(symbol:color:help:) }` puts a mark at a row's trailing end from any plugin, a dot or, with `symbol`, an SF Symbol, on the kinds `where` picks (a `ContentsKind`, asked once per kind, as an adornment's `where`: `$0.holds(LockedProperty.self)`) (Validation's `ValidationContentsPlugin`: a red or orange dot for a shape with issues, its tooltip the issues; Locking: a lock on a locked shape's row). A mark is part of the row, made with it, so one read from more than its shape names, with `affected:`, the shapes each commit may change the marks of (Validation's `dependents(of:)`): their marks are read again, and their rows replaced only if the marks changed. A row's view reads only its `ShapeRow`, never the document: the list makes every row's view on each redraw. A kind without a row gets its label and symbol (`registry.label(of:)`: what the kind declares, `x.label(_:systemImage:)`, one per kind, as the arrow does, since no tool places one; else its placing tool's; else its id; the label a placeholder, dimmed until a shape is named, where another plugin names the kind's rows with `contentsTitle`), and `x.contentsRow(when:nestedUnder:)` with no builder keeps that row. `ContentsState.name(of: record)` is what the list calls a shape, wherever it is in the file (its row's text, named as `ContentsTitles` names it, a placeholder for a shape with nothing to say; nil for a row that is no `ContentsLabel`), made for the call with no row kept or counted: what a plugin that lists shapes elsewhere calls them by (Neighbors' lists: [neighbors](neighbors.md)). `x.contentsTitle { … }` (`ContentsTitles`) names a kind's rows from another plugin without replacing them: a name that isn't blank takes the place of a `ContentsLabel`'s title, keeping its symbol and badge (Titles names rows this way). Rows run front to back at every level, as a layers list's (the owner's choice when rows became draggable; data-canvas's went back to front): `entries` keeps document order, as the core keeps shapes, and the list shows each level the other way round (`ListedRows`; `BoardRows` a board's rows open under its owner's, then the rows nested under the owner, each front to back; `OutlineRows.lines` a crumb's menu), so a container's rows near the bottom of a long list open only once they're in view after a replacement (SwiftUI applies a row's disclosure as it makes the row); a row nests under the shape its kind names (`nestedUnder`), else under the container its shape is in (a frame: the core's `registry.container(of:in:)`, read from its record), only while that shape is listed, so rows nest as deep as containers do. Arrows nest under the shape they start from (under the one shape a stand-in stands for, where one starts at a stand-in: [connections](connections.md)). Each row with rows under it has a disclosure triangle of its own (`ContentsTree`, a `DisclosureGroup` a row, bound to `ContentsState.isExpanded(_:)`, observed per row): a container's row starts expanded (`entry.startsExpanded`: what it holds is on the board shown), every other collapsed (a note's arrows, a subboard's board), and a row keeps what the person turns it to (`setExpanded(_:_:)`), by shape, whatever's listed: Show All, its rows replaced, another board shown and back. A shape that owns a board (a `BoardOwning` kind's, not kept aside: `entry.nestedBoard`, nothing to opt into) opens onto that board's rows (`outline(of:)`, no marks), before those nested under it, with no disclosure triangle while the board has no shapes; read only once it's expanded (`BoardRows`, behind `ListedRows`, a collection with a `@MainActor` conformance: SwiftUI's outline asks every row for its nested rows' collection on every update, but for what's in it only once expanded), so a collapsed subboard costs its row; selecting one goes there (`editor.goTo`, its board shown, one step back; several, the board of the one listed first, front to back, those on it selected in document order; not when a selection adds rows of the board shown too, a ⇧-click's range), its primary action `goInto`, and the pointer on one hovers nothing; a crumb's menu's rows open the same way. `ContentsState` keeps each shape's row and where it's listed (`ContentsListing`, per scope), and redoes only those of the shapes each commit's `ChangeSet` names but those it only moved (`ChangeSet.moved`), whose rows stay, the same objects (a row, `when`, `nestedUnder` and a name say what a shape is, never where it is), and whose marks the next read reads again (the commit only notes which moved, one set's upkeep a drag event, whatever it carries): a read after a drag makes no row, a frame dragged with all it holds neither; joining or leaving a container changes the shape's record, so its row is made again and placed under its new container. Selecting on the canvas lists just the selection; selecting rows selects and zooms to their shapes (Selection's `editor.reveal`) without changing what's listed (`editor.selectionRevision` tells the two apart). It lists the board shown, and keeps each board's rows and listing for the boards shown last (a `BoardStash`, taken back while the board's revision is unchanged, so going back makes no row: `rowsMade`, debug builds, counts rows made); a switch drops a pinned scope. The header is "Board" (`ContentsStyle.heading`), or whatever a plugin calls the board listed (the `ContentsHeadings` point, a `KeyedExtensionPoint`, at most one: a second is `RegistryError.conflict`; the report's `contents heading`): `registrar.contentsHeading { editor in … }` gives a `ContentsHeading` (title, symbol, and an `up` command offered as a button while `canPerform`, an arrow turning up, since a chevron in a list reads as collapsing, called `upTitle` with `upHelp` its tooltip), or nil for the list's own; the header reads it in its body through `ContentsState.heading` (public, the plugin's or "Board"), so it reads observable state and the header alone redraws as that changes, and the list never learns what a board is called. `TableOfContentsView(editor:onExit:)` takes no heading. Boards gives the board shown's crumb (`BoardContentsPlugin`, `Sources/Boards/BoardHeadings.swift`: [boards](boards.md)). A double-click or Return on one row opens its shape as Return on the canvas does, through Selection's `editor.open` (`ContentsState.open(_:)`, `.contextMenu(forSelectionType:primaryAction:)`); in the app's panel, picking one row shows its shape's panel in the list's place and gives the canvas the keyboard, so Return opens it there and a double-click doesn't reach the list. A change of what's listed replaces the rows, and so does a commit that brings rows in at more than one place (`ContentsListing.isScattered`, counted in `ContentsState.replacements`; the list's `.id(ContentsListID(board:scope:replacements:))`, so another board's rows too), bringing the new ones in as one run: rows coming in at several places at once make AppKit, whose table sizes a `List`'s rows, log "a reentrant operation in its NSTableView delegate" (a future assert), in a list of any length (replaced rows keep their disclosure, a container's open), as it still does when a list first shows 200 rows or more. A row that goes (replaced, say) lets go only of a hover the pointer on it set (`hover(_:)`), never one the canvas set; a switch of board lets go of it. Hover is shared: the pointer on a row sets Selection's `editor.hovered`, which the canvas outlines faintly, and a shape hovered on the canvas tints its row (`isHovered`), in the selection highlight's place; a selected shape shows neither. A row of a board nested under its owner's is of a shape the canvas doesn't show, so the pointer on it sets no hover and leaves a hover as it is (one the canvas set, or another row's), and the shape is Selection's `editor.hoveredElsewhere` instead (set by `hover(_:)` for a shape that's in a board of the file and not on the board shown, with `rowHoverElsewhere` noting that this row's pointer set it; cleared by `endHover(_:)` for that shape if it did, which the row's exit and its going away both call, never a hover another plugin set, and by a switch of board; another row's late exit leaves it; Selection outlines the shape on the board shown that holds it faintly, and lets go of it when tools change, another board is shown or the shape leaves the file: [selection](selection.md)): what a plugin that shows where that shape is reads (Neighbors lights what it's connected to: [neighbors](neighbors.md)). A row's tint observes only its own shape's hover (`editor.isHovered`) and, while that shape is hovered, the selection, so a hover redraws the row it leaves and the row it reaches: rows that read `editor.hovered` would all be redrawn on every hover change. It's the detail panel's overview, which the panel shows with nothing selected, and under the header of several: `ContentsOverviewPlugin` (id `contents-overview`, `Sources/TableOfContents/ContentsOverview.swift`) gives `TableOfContentsView` to the panel's `DetailOverviews` point (`registrar.detailOverview`: [detail panel](detail-panel.md)), so TableOfContents imports DetailPanel, a surface on a surface, and the panel never learns what its overview is. It's a plugin value of its own, after `DetailPanelPlugin` and `TableOfContentsPlugin` in the shipped list: a list without the panel leaves it out and keeps the list's state, which the path's crumbs' menus read, and one with it and no panel is refused (`ownerNotInstalled`). Another board's rows (`docs/plugin-system.md`, "Another board's outline") are `ContentsState.outline(of: board)`: those `entries` would list there with nothing selected, less their marks (the board shown's), whichever board is shown, made on the first read and kept while the board's revision is unchanged and it's on the path to the board shown, the rows of kinds with `affected` made again on a read after a commit that did more than move shapes (their answers are the board shown's; `ChangeSet.onlyMoves`, internal, names nothing anew) and kept unless their labels changed; `ContentsOutline(editor:board:highlighted:maxHeight:onPick:)` shows them in a small panel (the path's crumb menus): 24-point rows as their kinds draw them, lazily, nested ones behind a disclosure triangle, a container's open from the start (the panel's own, each time it shows), `highlighted` tinted as a hovered row is, a click `onPick`, opening at the top (scrolling a lazy stack to a far row lays out the rows before it).

## Dragging rows

- Rows drag, and take drops (`ContentsDragging.swift`; the guide's "Dragging rows"). A row is a drag source of its own (`.itemProvider` on `ContentsRow`: the list's table asks it for each dragged row, `outlineView(_:pasteboardWriterForItem:)`, which begins the drag, `dragItem`, `beginDrag(from:)`), carrying a private type (`UTType.contentsRows`, `com.example.InfiniteCanvas.contents-rows`, conforming to `public.data`, declared in the app's Info.plist by build.sh, `.ownProcess`); its drag is its shape's, or, when it's selected, every selected shape that can move and whose row shows (`showsRow(of:)`: listed, every row it's under open, as the table drags only the selected rows that show), in document order (`dragged(from:)`: a box, not kept aside, not read-only, on a board the editor can show; a selection's worked out once, `selectionDrag`, while the revision, the selection and the rows' disclosure, `turns`, are as they were, since the table asks every dragged row), and a press that drags doesn't select (the table selects on a click's release). The drag under way is kept per process (`ContentsDrag.current`, the editor whose list began it), since AppKit doesn't tell the list how a drag ended: a list takes a drop only of its own (`dragShapes`). Each row is a drop target of its own (`.onDrop(of:delegate:)`, `ContentsRowDrop`: SwiftUI puts a target view in the row's cell, registered for every type, to which AppKit hands a drag over the row; the height from `onGeometryChange`), never SwiftUI's `onMove`/`onInsert`, which turn every drop on a row of an outline into an insertion after it. Where on a row a drag is (`target(onRow:at:)`, `ContentsDropZone` above, on, below): a frame's or a subboard's row (one whose shape `isContainer` or `ownsShowableBoard`) by quarters, its middle on it (`ContentsStyle.dropEdge`), any other by halves; below an open row with rows of its own, on it for one that takes shapes, nowhere for one that doesn't. `place(for:)` (`ContentsPlace`: a board, a container there or nil, `front`, `inFront(of:)` or `behind`): on a row, into its frame or onto its subboard's board, in front; above or below, beside its shape where it is (its board, `registry.container(of:in:)`); nil beside a shape with no box, or a row nested under something other than its container (`kindNestingParent`, a kind's `nestedUnder`). `admits(_:)`: either all already in the place (a restack; not `front`, where they are, nor beside one of them) or `ContentsMoves`' contributor takes them (refusing what goes with them: a frame among them or in one, beside one, a board one of them owns); kept for the drag (`ContentsDrag.serial`), the target and the editor's revision (`admitted`), as it's asked on every move of the pointer. `drop(on:)`: a restack in one step, Reorder (`ContentsPlace.restack(_:in:containingKinds:)`, public, in `ContentsPlace.swift` apart from SwiftUI's `Transaction`: in front of everything there, of a neighbor and all it holds, or just behind it, keeping their order, on any board through the core's `move(_:above:on:)`), else the contributor's move; the drag is over either way. `ContentsMoves` (a `KeyedExtensionPoint`, one contribution: `registrar.contentsMoves(admits:move:)`) is Moving's ([moving](moving.md)); without one, a drop only restacks. Pointing (`dragMoved(over:at:)`, `dragLeft(_:)`, `drop(onRow:at:)`, which the delegate calls) shows a line above or below the row from a ring at its start (`ContentsDropLine`, an overlay offset half its ring past the edge) or a ring around the row (`ContentsDropHighlight`, in the row background's place, `ContentsRowBackground`), observed per row (`dropZone(for:)`, Observation key paths per shape, as disclosure is), so a drag over the list redraws the rows it leaves and reaches; resting on a closed row that opens onto rows opens it after `ContentsStyle.springDelay` (700 ms, `springing`, on `editor.scheduler`). A switch of board lets go of what's pointed at.

## Rows read from more than their shape

- A row may nest through a service: `contentsRow(nestedUnder: { shape, context in … })` (the closure also gets the `ContentsContext`; `nestedUnder` is required in that form, `affected:` may follow), and a service whose answer arrives outside a commit tells the list with `editor.contentsDidChange(for: ids)` (as `adornmentsDidChange(for:)`): those rows are forgotten and made again on the next read, nesting included, `entries` reads an observed counter so a list observing it reads again, and no other row is made (ServiceNestingTests).

- A table-of-contents row read from more than its shape (an untitled note's text with the live names of the shapes it mentions, and an arrow's: its label's first line that says something, each mention its shape's name now, else "Arrow", nested under the shape it starts from through the form that takes both `nestedUnder:` and `affected: MentionRows.affected`; ArrowsTests' `labelRowTests` hold it, a rename remaking the row): `x.contentsRow(when:nestedUnder:affected:) { shape, context in ContentsLabel(…) }` (`registrar.contentsRow(P.self, affected:)` for another plugin's kind), whose builder gets the shape and a `ContentsContext` (services) and returns a `ContentsLabel`, and whose `affected: { changes, context in … }` names the shapes each commit may change the rows of without changing them. `ContentsState` makes those rows again on the next read and replaces a row (so the list redraws it) only if its label changed (title, placeholder, symbol, color, badge, `standsIn` and path), as marks' `affected` does; a read before it hears a commit makes every kept row of such a kind again. `affected` runs on every commit, listed or not, so it must cost O(changed): a service keeps who reads what, per shape (AffectedRowTests' `RefIndex`). A row may read a shape on any board (`editor.file.record`): `affected` looks at `changes.elsewhere` too (what a commit did beyond its board: a subboard's deletion, `transact(on:)`), and going back to a board as it was left hands it what changed on the others meanwhile, as `elsewhere`, so those rows are made again and the rest taken back (AffectedBoardsTests). `when`, `nestedUnder` and `contentsTitle` work as for any row.

## Testing

- Dragging rows: TableOfContentsTests' `DragTests` drive it headlessly (`dragged(from:)`, `beginDrag(from:)`, `target(onRow:at:)`, `admits(_:)`, `drop(on:)`, `dragMoved(over:at:)`, `dragLeft(_:)`, `dropZone(for:)`, observed per row), with `RecordingMoves` (a `ContentsMoves` that notes the places it's asked to move to) standing in for Moving, pockets for subboards and the `ManualScheduler` for spring-loading; `DragWindowTests` drive the list itself in an unshown window: the table's writer for a row (`ContentsWindow.writer(ofRow:)`), and a row's own drop target (the view in its cell registered for `public.item`, `drag(rows:over:at:)`) sent `draggingEntered`, `draggingUpdated`, `performDragOperation` with a `RowDrag` (an `NSDraggingInfo` over a private pasteboard), and snapshots of the line and the ring. CompositionTests' `contentsDragShippedTests` drop notes' rows as shipped (into a frame, onto a subboard's board, restacked, a locked note and an arrow not dragging). A synthesized press on a row doesn't reach the table in an unshown window (`window.sendEvent`); `outline.mouseDown(with:)` with the drag and release posted first does, and is how a press-and-drag was seen not to select (the scratch spike, not a kept test).
- For the table of contents, read `editor.tableOfContents?.entries` headlessly (each row's text, symbol and badge through `entry.label`, its marks through `entry.marks`, the board under it through `entry.nestedBoard` and `outline(of:)`, whether it starts expanded through `entry.startsExpanded`), and drive `select(_:)`, `showAll()`, `hover(_:)`, `endHover(_:)` (read back, for a row of another board, with `editor.hoveredElsewhere`) and `setExpanded(_:_:)` (read back with `isExpanded(_:)`); shapes nest in CanvasTesting's corrals (`editor.put(_:in:)`; ContainerTests, with a seeded run of joins, leaves, drags, deletions, undo and redo held to listing afresh). `rowsRedrawn` (debug builds) counts how often the list has asked a row for its view, so a test holds a change to redrawing the rows it changes (HoverTests), and `rowsMade` how many rows it has made (BoardTests: going back to a board makes none). Headless, `open(_:)` is a row's primary action; in a window, `pressReturn()` with the list focused runs it (a synthesized double-click doesn't reach the list). SwiftUI's hover doesn't answer synthesized events, so check a row's tint by rendering the list with `snapshot(view)` (CanvasTesting; highlights draw, a source list's vibrant text may not). In a window, its list is an `NSOutlineView` whose first row is the header, a group row (TableOfContentsTests; `ContentsWindow.isExpandable(_:)`, `isExpanded(_:)`, `expand(_:)` and `collapse(_:)` read and turn a row's disclosure triangle, and `selectRowIndexes` reaches the list's selection as a click does, NestedBoardTests). AppKit's warnings go only to standard error: `standardError(during:)` (CanvasTesting) returns what was logged, so a test can check a list's update logged no `tableReentrancyWarning`. A shape's panel can hold lists too, so CompositionTests tell them apart with `isTableOfContents` (`Tests/CompositionTests/ContentsPanelTests.swift`), and InspectorTests' `tables` leave it out.
- Rows' symbol colors: TableOfContentsTests' `tintRowTests` (a kind `Chip` whose row asks `context.color(of:)`, CanvasTesting's `Frozen` flag and a contributor's `shapeTints` standing in for a color a shape holds: its own over its kind's, one row made for a commit, and a change to what tints are reaching the rows of the board shown, of a board left and of another board's outline); each shipped kind's row has one such test in its own tests.
- A label's path: ContentsTests hold `folded(_:)`, the tooltip, and, drawn at widths between the label's own (`fixedSize` and `fittingSize`: all of it, folded, the bare title), all of it with the room, the fold where it doesn't fit, the names cut short with the title whole and both ends still said, then the title cut with both ends still said, a path of two names cut the same way (two labels that differ by a letter drawn alike or not, `changedPixels`), and a placeholder name drawn fainter than the same words as a title.
- TableOfContentsTests' `nameTests` hold `name(of:)` (a row's title, another plugin's name, a placeholder, a kind with no row, nil for a row that is a view, no row made).
- The heading is given as a plugin gives it (BoardTests' `headingPlugin(_:)`, a contributor calling `registrar.contentsHeading`, in the editor's plugins; `ContentsWindow(editor)` takes none) and read headlessly as `editor.contents.heading`: BoardTests hold a plugin's heading on the root and inside a pocket, "Board" with no plugin and for nil, a second a conflict, and one without the table of contents refused; `boardWindowTests` its button and pixels. `overviewTests` (`OverviewTests.swift`) hold `ContentsOverviewPlugin`: the list in a `DetailPanelView` hosted alone, with nothing selected and under several, gone for one shape, no list without the plugin, and its refusal without the panel.
