# The table of contents

The table of contents: rows and marks, nesting, what's listed (every shape of the board shown, front to back, the selection on its rows), hover, rows read from more than their shape, another board's outline, the window's sidebar it is in, the keyboard (Focus Sidebar, walking the list, what VoiceOver hears, the outline's keys), and dragging rows.

Related: [host slots](host-slots.md), [detail panel](detail-panel.md), [keyboard navigation](keyboard-navigation.md), [selection](selection.md), [neighbors](neighbors.md), [validation](validation.md), [locking](locking.md), [titles](titles.md), [mentions](mentions.md), [boards](boards.md), [containers](containers.md), [moving](moving.md), [boards in a file](boards-in-a-file.md) ([every topic](../../CLAUDE.md#map)).

In the guide, `docs/plugin-system.md`: [The table of contents](../../docs/plugin-system.md#the-table-of-contents), [Dragging rows](../../docs/plugin-system.md#dragging-rows), [Another board's outline](../../docs/plugin-system.md#another-boards-outline).

## Architecture

- The table of contents (TableOfContents, platform, a horizontal in the window) is AppKit's outline in the window's sidebar (`ContentsList`, below), with data-canvas's TOC contract: `x.contentsRow(when:nestedUnder:) { ContentsLabel(title, placeholder:, systemImage:, color:, badge:, standsIn:, path:) }`, and `registrar.contentsRow(P.self)` for another plugin's kind. The list owns the row's chrome; a row reads only its own shape (unless it names what else it reads, below; `contentsRow { shape, context in … }` reads services besides, with nothing `affected`, and keeps its row until the shape changes), and its symbol's color is the shape's, `context.color(of: shape)` (`editor.tint(of:)`: the color it has of its own where a plugin gives it one, Colors', else its kind's tint; every shipped kind's row passes it, an arrow's nil without one of its own). A change to the shape remakes the row; the file's colors changing, which makes no commit, remakes every row (`shapeTintsDidChange`: the rows of the board shown, those kept for boards left and the outlines of other boards, as the next read needs them), and uses `ContentsLabel` so rows from different plugins look alike (its text its tooltip, `label.help`, up to 200 characters, cut between words with "…", none for a placeholder). `standsIn: true` is a row that stands in for another shape and wears its name, symbol and color (a reference's: [references](references.md)), so it would read as that shape: `StandInArrow` cuts a round hole out of the symbol's bottom-leading corner and puts `arrow.turn.up.right` in it (6.5 pt black, in the title's color, secondary with a placeholder, the hole 10 pt, 1 pt out and 2 pt down, at the sidebar's medium rows and scaled with `sidebarRowSize` as its symbols are; the mask reaches 4 pt past the symbol's frame so no ink past it is cut: `ContentsStyle.standIn…`), a Finder alias's arrow, with no fill behind it, so it reads on any symbol and on a selected row; VoiceOver says the row's value is "Alias" (`standInSpoken`), since its name alone is its shape's; the list knows nothing of what it stands for, and the crumbs' outline draws it the same (it draws the row's `ContentsLabel`). `path:` is where what the row names is, for a row that names a shape somewhere else (a reference's original: [references](references.md)): the names of the shapes it's inside, outermost first (each a `ContentsPathName`, `text` and `isPlaceholder`; a string literal is one that's a title), shown before the title, dimmed, with "›" between (`ContentsStyle.pathSeparator`), "Alpha › Beta › Checkout" (`ContentsPathTitle`), a name that is only what its kind is called (`isPlaceholder`: an untitled frame's "Frame") fainter than a title (tertiary, the rest secondary), as Jump To's rows have it, so a frame nobody named doesn't read as one named "Frame"; the tooltip has it whole (`help`: the path, then the text, a placeholder's too when it has a path), VoiceOver says "Checkout, in Alpha › Beta" (`pathSpoken`), and `text` stays the title. The path gives way before the title: with the room, all of it; else the names between the first and the last folded into "…" (`ContentsLabel.folded(_:)`, nil for two names or fewer; `ViewThatFits`, so the fold is by the row's width, never a count: the owner's pick, the first and the nearest always said, where the path over the canvas folds past four crumbs); narrower than that, each name is cut short on its own, the longest first, none to less than `ContentsStyle.pathNameFloor` (34 pt at the sidebar's medium rows, scaled with them: a few letters and "…") or its own width, every "›" and the fold's "…" kept; and only then the title (`ContentsPathLine`, a `Layout` over texts tagged `ContentsPathPart` name, title or fixed: a stack with the title given priority pushed the names down to slivers between their "›"s). The list knows nothing of what a path is of. `scope.contentsMark { shape, context in ContentsMark(symbol:color:help:) }` puts a mark at a row's trailing end from any plugin, a dot or, with `symbol`, an SF Symbol, on the kinds a scope reaches (`registrar.forShapes(holding: LockedProperty.self) { lock in lock.contentsMark { … } }`, as an adornment's: [plugins and kinds](plugins-and-kinds.md); `ContentsMarks` takes scopes) (Validation's `ValidationContentsPlugin`, `forEveryShape`: a red or orange dot for a shape with issues, its tooltip the issues; Locking: a lock on a locked shape's row; Publishing: a globe on a public one's). A mark is part of the row, made with it, so one read from more than its shape names, with `affected:`, the shapes each commit may change the marks of (Validation's `dependents(of:)`): their marks are read again, and their rows replaced only if the marks changed. A row's view reads only its `ShapeRow`, never the document: the list makes it again whenever the row comes into view. A kind without a row gets its label and symbol (`registry.label(of:)`: what the kind declares, `x.label(_:systemImage:)`, one per kind, as the arrow does, since no tool places one; else its placing tool's; else its id; the label a placeholder, dimmed until a shape is named, where another plugin names the kind's rows with `contentsTitle`), and `x.contentsRow(when:nestedUnder:)` with no builder keeps that row. `ContentsState.name(of: record)` is what the list calls a shape, wherever it is in the file (its row's text, named as `ContentsTitles` names it, a placeholder for a shape with nothing to say; nil for a row that is no `ContentsLabel`), made for the call with no row kept or counted: what a plugin that lists shapes elsewhere calls them by (Neighbors' lists: [neighbors](neighbors.md)). `x.contentsTitle { … }` (`ContentsTitles`) names a kind's rows from another plugin without replacing them: a name that isn't blank takes the place of a `ContentsLabel`'s title, keeping its symbol and badge (Titles names rows this way). Rows run front to back at every level, as a layers list's (the owner's choice when rows became draggable; data-canvas's went back to front): `entries` keeps document order, as the core keeps shapes, and the list shows each level the other way round (`ContentsListController`'s `top` and each row's `children`: a board's rows open under its owner's first, then the rows nested under the owner, each front to back; `OutlineRows.lines` a crumb's menu); a row nests under the shape its kind names (`nestedUnder`), else under the container its shape is in (a frame: the core's `registry.container(of:in:)`, read from its record), only while that shape is listed, so rows nest as deep as containers do. Arrows nest under the shape they start from (under the one shape a stand-in stands for, where one starts at a stand-in: [connections](connections.md)). Each row with rows under it has a disclosure triangle of its own (the outline's, open as `ContentsState.isExpanded(_:)` says: a row as it comes in, and the rows under one as it opens; the person's turns go to `setExpanded(_:_:)`, and a turn made anywhere else, a drag springing a row open or a headless one, reaches each list that shows the row, `watchTurns`, `ContentsTurnWatcher`): a container's row starts expanded (`entry.startsExpanded`: what it holds is on the board shown), every other collapsed (a note's arrows, a subboard's board), and a row keeps what the person turns it to (`setExpanded(_:_:)`), by shape, whatever's listed: its rows replaced, another board shown and back. A shape that owns a board (a `BoardOwning` kind's, not kept aside: `entry.nestedBoard`, nothing to opt into) opens onto that board's rows (`outline(of:)`, no marks), before those nested under it, with no disclosure triangle while the board has no shapes; read only once it's expanded (the outline asks for a row's nested rows only once it's open: `ContentsListController.children(of:)`), so a collapsed subboard costs its row; open, its rows follow the board, though it isn't shown (`outlineRows(of:)`, whose `version` is new each time they're made again, so each update reads them again by shape only when it changed, and a closed owner's triangle comes and goes with its board's first shape and last); selecting one goes there (`editor.goTo`, its board shown, one step back; several, the board of the one listed first, front to back, those on it selected in document order; not when a selection adds rows of the board shown too, a ⇧-click's range), its primary action `goInto`, and the pointer on one hovers nothing; a crumb's menu's rows open the same way. `ContentsState` keeps each shape's row and where it's listed (`ContentsListing`), and redoes only those of the shapes each commit's `ChangeSet` names but those it only moved (`ChangeSet.moved`), whose rows stay, the same objects (a row, `when`, `nestedUnder` and a name say what a shape is, never where it is), and whose marks the next read reads again (the commit only notes which moved, one set's upkeep a drag event, whatever it carries): a read after a drag makes no row, a frame dragged with all it holds neither; joining or leaving a container changes the shape's record, so its row is made again and placed under its new container. It lists every shape of the board shown that its kind lists, whatever is selected: there is no scope, and a selection, wherever it is made, changes no row and no listing (`rowsMade` holds it), and shows on the rows (below); selecting rows selects and zooms to their shapes (Selection's `editor.reveal`), as one step back, however near they are (`editor.navigate`: a row's pick is going somewhere, as an outline's is in VS Code's history; a walk by keys is one step, back to where it began, `navigate(from:)` of the start the walk's first key took, `select(_:byKey:)`), and `editor.selectionRevision` tells the list's own selection from another's. It lists the board shown, and keeps each board's rows and listing for the boards shown last (a `BoardStash`, taken back while the board's revision is unchanged, so going back makes no row: `rowsMade`, debug builds, counts rows made). The header is "Board" (`ContentsStyle.heading`), or whatever a plugin calls the board listed (the `ContentsHeadings` point, a `KeyedExtensionPoint`, at most one: a second is `RegistryError.conflict`; the report's `contents heading`): `registrar.contentsHeading { editor in … }` gives a `ContentsHeading` (title, symbol, and an `up` command offered as a button while `canPerform`, an arrow turning up, since a chevron in a list reads as collapsing, called as the command is, `registry.title(of:)`, with `upHelp` its tooltip), or nil for the list's own; the header reads it in its body through `ContentsState.heading` (public, the plugin's or "Board"), so it reads observable state and the header alone redraws as that changes, and the list never learns what a board is called. `TableOfContentsView(editor:onExit:)` takes no heading. Boards gives the board shown's crumb (`BoardContentsPlugin`, `Sources/Boards/BoardHeadings.swift`: [boards](boards.md)). A double-click or Return on one row opens its shape as Return on the canvas does, through Selection's `editor.open` (`ContentsState.open(_:)`: the outline's `doubleAction`, which reaches the list since the list stays on screen, and Return as the list hears it). A click on one row selects and reveals its shape (the outline's own selection changing: `selectionChanged`, `select(_:)`) and the canvas gets the keyboard a turn on (`giveCanvasTheKeyboardSoon`, on `editor.scheduler`: `onExit`, and only if the outline still has the keyboard), so the canvas's keys act on the shape at once and Return there opens it (the owner's call, kept when the list moved to the sidebar: a click gives the canvas the keyboard). What tells a click's selection from a key's is whether a key reached the outline that turn (`key(_:)` sets the controller's private `keyArrived` until the next turn), so ↑ ↓ Tab ⇧Tab and ⇧ with them keep the keyboard in the list, where Return is the list's ([the keyboard](#the-keyboard)); a click on a row already selected changes no selection, and the outline's `action` (`rowClicked`, sent once the click is let go: never for a press that drags, nor for the header's row) gives the canvas the keyboard for it. The list (`ContentsList`, `Sources/TableOfContents/ContentsList.swift`, its views in `ContentsListViews.swift`) is an `NSOutlineView` in source-list style (`ContentsTable`, in its scroll view, `ContentsListView`, an `NSViewRepresentable`'s) whose data source and delegate, `ContentsListController`, reads the state's rows as the outline asks for them: one `ContentsItem` a row it has, by shape (a row that moves, or comes back, is a new one), the header its first row, a group row (`ContentsHeaderItem`; `ContentsHeaderCell` hosts `ContentsHeader`). Its rows are all of one height, the system's sidebar row size (`rowSizeStyle` `.default`), so AppKit measures none, whatever comes in where, and never logs "a reentrant operation in its NSTableView delegate", and it makes views for the rows in view alone: a row's cell (`ContentsCell`) hosts its `ContentsRowView` in SwiftUI (`ContentsCellContent`: its symbol in a column as a source list's, `ContentsRowLabelStyle`, the text at the sidebar's size, `ContentsStyle.rowFontSize`, white on a selected row of a list with the keyboard, the cell's `backgroundStyle` as `backgroundProminence`), reused as rows scroll in. So a board of 100,000 shapes shows, and its rows are replaced, at the cost of a pass over its top-level rows and the rows in view. `TableOfContentsView`'s body reads `entries` and hands the list what it shows (`ContentsShown`: the board, which listing, `ContentsListing.serial`, how often it changed, `version`, the editor's revision and the selection), so SwiftUI updates the list on each change: a listing it doesn't show (another board, one listed afresh) it shows whole (`reloadData`, then each top-level row that's open opened); the one it shows it follows row by row as the listing's updates changed it (`ContentsListingChanges`: the rows that came, went or moved, the rows whose nested rows changed and the rows made again in place, gathered over updates until the list takes them, `takeListedChanges(by:)`, so a read by anyone else in between loses nothing, and a second list taking them makes the first show the listing whole): the rows that left their place out (`removeItems`), those that came in where they are now (`insertItems`, found by stacking key, open as they were), the triangles of rows whose nested rows came or went (`reloadItem`) and the rows made again where they are redrawn where they have views (`enumerateAvailableRowViews`); then the selection (`selectRowIndexes`, the rows of a few shapes found one by one, `ContentsStyle.selectedRowLookups`, 64, more found in one pass down the rows). A selection the list did not make (on the canvas, in Jump To or Find, by an undo) is brought into view too: told from the list's own by `listSelection`, the editor's `selectionRevision` as the list's own selection left it, and forgotten whenever a listing is shown whole (`reload()`: another board, a listing made afresh), so after a row of a nested board takes the window to that board the selection there is brought into view; the first selected row is scrolled to (`scrollRowToVisible`) and, only when exactly one shape is selected, the rows its row is nested under are opened first (`openRows(above:)`, the outermost first, each turned open as the person would, `setExpanded`, so it stays open), since several selected open nothing (⌘A would open every row). A selection the list made scrolls and opens nothing. A row that goes (replaced, say) lets go only of a hover the pointer on it set (`hover(_:)`), never one the canvas set; a switch of board lets go of it. Hover is shared: the pointer on a row (the outline's tracking area, `pointerMoved(to:)`) sets Selection's `editor.hovered`, which the canvas outlines faintly, and a shape hovered on the canvas tints its row (`isHovered`), in the selection highlight's place; a selected shape shows neither. A row of a board nested under its owner's is of a shape the canvas doesn't show, so the pointer on it sets no hover and leaves a hover as it is (one the canvas set, or another row's), and the shape is Selection's `editor.hoveredElsewhere` instead (set by `hover(_:)` for a shape that's in a board of the file and not on the board shown, with `rowHoverElsewhere` noting that this row's pointer set it; cleared by `endHover(_:)` for that shape if it did, which the row's exit and its going away both call, never a hover another plugin set, and by a switch of board; another row's late exit leaves it; Selection outlines the shape on the board shown that holds it faintly, and lets go of it when tools change, another board is shown or the shape leaves the file: [selection](selection.md)): what a plugin that shows where that shape is reads (Neighbors lights what it's connected to: [neighbors](neighbors.md)). A row's tint, drawn by its row view (`ContentsTableRow`, in the selection highlight's place), observes only its own shape's hover (`editor.isHovered`) and, while that shape is hovered, the selection (each row view in view reads `isHovered` and `dropZone(for:)` for its shape in `withObservationTracking`, again after each change), so a hover redraws the row it leaves and the row it reaches: rows that read `editor.hovered` would all be redrawn on every hover change. `TableOfContentsPlugin` gives the list as the window's sidebar from its own `register` (`registrar.hostSidebar(keyboardEntry: { $0.tableOfContents?.keyboardEntry })` with `TableOfContentsView(editor:onExit:)` and `.navigationSplitViewColumnWidth(min:ideal:max:)` from `ContentsStyle.sidebarMinWidth`, `sidebarWidth` and `sidebarMaxWidth`: 180, 240 and 400 points; [host slots](host-slots.md)), so its report line is `table-of-contents    service ContentsState · host sidebar`. TableOfContents imports Canvas, Selection and TextEditing and not DetailPanel: the window, not the panel, places the list, and the panel never learns it exists. A host slot's contributor needs no owner installed, so the list needs no plugin value of its own, and the shipped list names `TableOfContentsPlugin` alone. `ContentsState.keyboardEntry` (weak) is the last list's outline, which the window gives the keyboard (View › Focus Sidebar, F6: [the keyboard](#the-keyboard)). Another board's rows (`docs/plugin-system.md`, "Another board's outline") are `ContentsState.outline(of: board)`: those `entries` would list there, less their marks (the board shown's), whichever board is shown, made on the first read and kept while the board's revision is unchanged and it's on the path to the board shown, the rows of kinds with `affected` made again on a read after a commit that did more than move shapes (their answers are the board shown's; `ChangeSet.onlyMoves`, the core's, names nothing anew) and kept unless their labels changed; `ContentsOutline(editor:board:highlighted:maxHeight:onPick:)` shows them in a small panel (the path's crumb menus): 24-point rows as their kinds draw them, lazily, nested ones behind a disclosure triangle, a container's open from the start (the panel's own, each time it shows), `highlighted` tinted as a hovered row is, a click `onPick`, opening at the top (scrolling a lazy stack to a far row lays out the rows before it).

## The keyboard

- The list's keys (`TableOfContentsView`; P1, P3, P4 of [keyboard navigation](keyboard-navigation.md)): View › Focus Sidebar (⌥⌘S, which shows a hidden sidebar; F6 coming round the panes does too: [host slots](host-slots.md)) gives the list the keyboard (`ContentsState.keyboardEntry`, the window's `sidebarKeyView()`), and a click on a row gives the canvas the keyboard instead; with the keyboard in the list, ↑ and ↓ walk the rows (⇧ extends), each selecting and revealing its shape as a click does (`select(_:)`); Tab and ⇧Tab (with Caps Lock on too: `KeyMeaning.Reading(event:)` drops it) step them as ↓ and ↑ do and stay in the list (the owner's call: the list is one stop, its rows its own; the outline gets an arrow in their place, ⇧Tab heard as the back-tab character, `ContentsStyle.backTab`); → and ← open and close a row's disclosure triangle (the table's own); Return opens the row's shape (`open(_:)`); Delete deletes; Escape gives the canvas the keyboard (`onExit`) with the selection as it was, and so do ⌘↑ (out to the list's container, when Go › Enclosing Board doesn't take it first, on a board inside another), ⌘↩ (finish) and ⌘. (`cancelOperation:`); ⌥Tab and ⌥⇧Tab, into drawn parts a row has none of, do nothing (P6: same key, same meaning; `keyMeaningsShippedTests` press every key of the table on the list, entering by ⌥⌘S). The outline hands every key to the list before it handles it (`ContentsTable.keyDown`: `ContentsListController.key(_:)` answers Tab, ⇧Tab, Return, Delete and the keys out, and leaves the rest to the outline, `ContentsKey`). Edit › Delete (`delete:`) in the list runs the `.delete` command as Delete does. The list keeps the keyboard through the selections its keys make: the inspector shows the shape picked, or the header of several, and takes none, so the next ↓ walks on rather than nudging that shape; the keyboard leaves it by Escape and the keys out, by Return opening the shape (a note's text has it then) and by a click on a row, even one already selected. Hiding the sidebar while the list has the keyboard gives the canvas the keyboard too (the list's view says when it is hidden, `onHide`, which SwiftUI doesn't; `reclaimSoon`).
- Showing and hiding the sidebar are the window's, with no command of the plugin's: View › Show Sidebar (⌃⌘S, `toggleSidebarShown:`) and the toolbar's Sidebar button, and View › Focus Sidebar (⌥⌘S, `focusSidebar:`), which leaves the keyboard where it is when there is no list to take it, an empty board's (P1, P10: nothing pointer-only; [host slots](host-slots.md)).
- What VoiceOver hears: a row's value is its kind (`entry.kindName`, `registry.label(of:)`, handed to `ContentsLabel` through the environment as its symbol is: "Checkout, Note"), "Alias" for a stand-in (`ContentsLabel.spokenValue(kind:)`); each disclosure triangle, which the outline adds to the row and names nothing, is named by its row (`ContentsTableRow.nameTriangle()`: the row view finds the triangle among its subviews, `NSOutlineView.disclosureButtonIdentifier`, as the outline adds the row, as a button is added to it, as it's laid out and as it's reused, and sets its label, and its cell's, to `entry.spokenName`, the row's text else its kind's name); the header's heading is a header (`.isHeader`).
- The outline (`ContentsOutline`, the path's crumb menus: [boards](boards.md#the-path-by-keys)) takes the keyboard from its host: `keyboard:`, an `OutlineKeyboard` the host feeds keys (`press`: ↑ ↓, a row above, below, stopping at the ends; → opens a row's disclosure, then goes on to its first nested row; ← closes it, then goes to the row it is nested under) and reads back (`row`, `rowName`), the row with it outlined and scrolled into view, first the highlighted row (the way you came), else the first; `take()` asks for it as the outline shows, `reset()` gives it up; Return, Space and Escape are the host's. A row's disclosure triangle is no stop of its own (the arrows turn it), and assistive apps get its action ("Checkout, expand"); every row is named as the list names it (`ContentsEntry.spokenName`: its text, else its kind's name; the words are `ContentsStyle`'s: `outlineCurrentPath`, `outlineDisclosureSpoken`), and the highlighted one reads "current path". Without a `keyboard` the rows never take one (`focusable(false)`).

## Dragging rows

- Rows drag, and take drops (`ContentsDragging.swift`, the outline's part in `ContentsList.swift`; the guide's "Dragging rows"). The outline drags rows: it asks for what each dragged row carries (`outlineView(_:pasteboardWriterForItem:)`, `pasteboardWriter(from:)`, which begins the drag, `beginDrag(from:)`), an `NSPasteboardItem` of a private type (`UTType.contentsRows`, `NSPasteboard.PasteboardType.contentsRows`, `com.example.InfiniteCanvas.contents-rows`, conforming to `public.data`, declared in the app's Info.plist by build.sh), dragged within the app alone (`setDraggingSourceOperationMask([], forLocal: false)`); its drag is its shape's, or, when it's selected, every selected shape that can move and whose row shows (`showsRow(of:)`: listed, every row it's under open, as the table drags only the selected rows that show), in document order (`dragged(from:)`: a box, not kept aside, not read-only, on a board the editor can show; a selection's worked out once, `selectionDrag`, while the revision, the selection and the rows' disclosure, `turns`, are as they were, since the outline asks every dragged row), and a press that drags doesn't select (the outline selects on a click's release). The drag under way is kept per process (`ContentsDrag.current`, the editor whose list began it) until a drop or the next drag (the outline hears a drag end, `draggingSession(_:endedAt:operation:)`, and lets go of only what's pointed at): a list takes a drop only of its own (`dragShapes`). The outline takes drops (registered for the private type) as a whole: `validateDrop` puts the drop on the outline itself (`setDropItem(nil, dropChildIndex: NSOutlineViewDropOnItemIndex)`, so the outline neither targets a row's children nor springs a row open itself) with its own highlight off (`draggingDestinationFeedbackStyle = .none`), and hands the row under the pointer and how far down it is (`rowUnder`) to `dragMoved(over:at:)`; `acceptDrop` to `drop(onRow:at:)`; a drag leaving the outline or ending, `point(nil)`. Never the outline's own drop targeting (on an item, or between its children), which turns a drop on a row into an insertion beside it. Where on a row a drag is (`target(onRow:at:)`, `ContentsDropZone` above, on, below): a frame's or a subboard's row (one whose shape `isContainer` or `ownsShowableBoard`) by quarters, its middle on it (`ContentsStyle.dropEdge`), any other by halves; below an open row with rows of its own, on it for one that takes shapes, nowhere for one that doesn't. `place(for:)` (`ContentsPlace`: a board, a container there or nil, `front`, `inFront(of:)` or `behind`): on a row, into its frame or onto its subboard's board, in front; above or below, beside its shape where it is (its board, `registry.container(of:in:)`); nil beside a shape with no box, or a row nested under something other than its container (`kindNestingParent`, a kind's `nestedUnder`). `admits(_:)`: either all already in the place (a restack; not `front`, where they are, nor beside one of them) or `ContentsMoves`' contributor takes them (refusing what goes with them: a frame among them or in one, beside one, a board one of them owns); kept for the drag (`ContentsDrag.serial`), the target and the editor's revision (`admitted`), as it's asked on every move of the pointer. `drop(on:)`: a restack in one step, Reorder (`ContentsPlace.restack(_:in:containingKinds:)`, public, in `ContentsPlace.swift` apart from SwiftUI's `Transaction`: in front of everything there, of a neighbor and all it holds, or just behind it, keeping their order, on any board through the core's `move(_:above:on:)`), else the contributor's move; the drag is over either way. `ContentsMoves` (a `KeyedExtensionPoint`, one contribution: `registrar.contentsMoves(admits:move:)`) is Moving's ([moving](moving.md)); without one, a drop only restacks. Pointing (`dragMoved(over:at:)`, `dragLeft(_:)`, `drop(onRow:at:)`, which the delegate calls) shows a line above or below the row from a ring at its start (`ContentsDropLineView`, a subview of the row's view along its top or bottom edge, from where its cell starts, so it shows how deep the drop is) or a ring around the row (drawn by the row's view, `ContentsTableRow`, in the selection highlight's place), observed per row (`dropZone(for:)`, Observation key paths per shape, as disclosure is), so a drag over the list redraws the rows it leaves and reaches; resting on a closed row that opens onto rows opens it after `ContentsStyle.springDelay` (700 ms, `springing`, on `editor.scheduler`; the outline opens it as `setExpanded` tells it). A switch of board lets go of what's pointed at.

## Rows read from more than their shape

- A row may nest through a service: `contentsRow(nestedUnder: { shape, context in … })` (the closure also gets the `ContentsContext`; `nestedUnder` is required in that form, `affected:` may follow), and a service whose answer arrives outside a commit tells the list with `editor.contentsDidChange(for: ids)` (as `adornmentsDidChange(for:)`): those rows are forgotten and made again on the next read, nesting included, `entries` reads an observed counter so a list observing it reads again, and no other row is made (ServiceNestingTests).

- A table-of-contents row read from more than its shape (an untitled note's text with the live names of the shapes it mentions, and an arrow's: its label's first line that says something, each mention its shape's name now, else "Arrow", nested under the shape it starts from through the form that takes both `nestedUnder:` and `affected: MentionRows.affected`; ArrowsTests' `labelRowTests` hold it, a rename remaking the row): `x.contentsRow(when:nestedUnder:affected:) { shape, context in ContentsLabel(…) }` (`registrar.contentsRow(P.self, affected:)` for another plugin's kind), whose builder gets the shape and a `ContentsContext` (services) and returns a `ContentsLabel`, and whose `affected: { changes, context in … }` names the shapes each commit may change the rows of without changing them. `ContentsState` makes those rows again on the next read and replaces a row (so the list redraws it) only if its label changed (title, placeholder, symbol, color, badge, `standsIn` and path), as marks' `affected` does; the rows' and marks' `affected` are asked once every service has the commit (`documentDidSettle`, [editor](editor.md)), or first by a read of the list after it took the commit in. `affected` runs on every commit, listed or not, so it must cost O(changed): a service keeps who reads what, per shape (AffectedRowTests' `RefIndex`). A row may read a shape on any board (`editor.file.record`): `affected` looks at `changes.elsewhere` too (what a commit did beyond its board: a subboard's deletion, `transact(on:)`), and going back to a board as it was left hands it what changed on the others meanwhile, as `elsewhere`, so those rows are made again and the rest taken back (AffectedBoardsTests). `when`, `nestedUnder` and `contentsTitle` work as for any row.

## Testing

- Dragging rows: TableOfContentsTests' `DragTests` drive it headlessly (`dragged(from:)`, `beginDrag(from:)`, `target(onRow:at:)`, `admits(_:)`, `drop(on:)`, `dragMoved(over:at:)`, `dragLeft(_:)`, `dropZone(for:)`, observed per row), with `RecordingMoves` (a `ContentsMoves` that notes the places it's asked to move to) standing in for Moving, pockets for subboards and the `ManualScheduler` for spring-loading; `DragWindowTests` drive the list itself in an unshown window: the outline's writer for a row (`ContentsWindow.writer(ofRow:)`), and the outline as the drop target (registered for what a row carries; `drag(rows:over:at:)`, at a point of a row) sent `draggingEntered`, `draggingUpdated`, `performDragOperation` with a `RowDrag` (an `NSDraggingInfo` over a private pasteboard), and snapshots of the line and the ring. CompositionTests' `contentsDragShippedTests` drop notes' rows as shipped (into a frame, onto a subboard's board, restacked, a locked note and an arrow not dragging). A synthesized press on a row doesn't reach the table in an unshown window (`window.sendEvent`); `outline.mouseDown(with:)` with the drag and release posted first does, and is how a press-and-drag was seen not to select (the scratch spike, not a kept test).
- For the table of contents, read `editor.tableOfContents?.entries` headlessly (each row's text, symbol and badge through `entry.label`, its marks through `entry.marks`, the board under it through `entry.nestedBoard` and `outline(of:)`, whether it starts expanded through `entry.startsExpanded`), and drive `select(_:)`, `hover(_:)`, `endHover(_:)` (read back, for a row of another board, with `editor.hoveredElsewhere`) and `setExpanded(_:_:)` (read back with `isExpanded(_:)`); shapes nest in CanvasTesting's corrals (`editor.put(_:in:)`; ContainerTests, with a seeded run of joins, leaves, drags, deletions, undo and redo held to listing afresh). `rowsRedrawn` (debug builds) counts how often the list has drawn a row anew (a row's cell given its row as the row comes into view, a row made again redrawn in view, a row's tint, drop or triangle changing), so a test holds a change to redrawing the rows it changes (HoverTests); a row out of view has no view and is drawn as it comes into view, so a test counts rows in view. `rowsMade` counts how many rows the state has made (BoardTests: going back to a board makes none). Headless, `open(_:)` is a row's primary action; in a window, `pressReturn()` with the list focused runs it (a synthesized double-click doesn't reach the list), and `ContentsWindow.click(row:)` (CompositionTests' `SampleBoardWindow.click(row:)` too) picks a row as a click's selection does, since a synthesized click doesn't reach a table in an unshown window: the outline made first responder, then `selectRowIndexes`, with no key that turn. AppKit's warnings go only to standard error: `standardError(during:)` (CanvasTesting) returns what was logged, so a test can check a list's update logged no `tableReentrancyWarning`.
