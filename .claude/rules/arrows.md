# Arrows

Arrows, which mirror tldraw's arc arrows: the intended deviations, labels, moving them, what an end may bind to, a port's drag, and the proof-of-concept parity tests.

Related: [connections](connections.md), [drawing](drawing.md), [editing](editing.md), [selection](selection.md) ([every topic](../../CLAUDE.md#map)).

In the guide, `docs/plugin-system.md`: [The connector: arrows](../../docs/plugin-system.md#the-connector-arrows), [A shape without a frame](../../docs/plugin-system.md#a-shape-without-a-frame).

## Architecture

- Arrows see other shapes only as `ConnectableShape` (a closed shape's core geometry). Keep specific shape kinds out of arrow code.
- `ArrowFindPlugin` (Connections', after Find in the shipped list: [find](find.md)) gives Find an arrow's label (field `label`, washed: it's over the canvas), its rects from the label's own layout, string drawing's centered in its frame less its padding (`ArrowLabels.layout(of:width:)`, TextEditing's `StringDrawingLayout`, kept by text and width, the last 256), from the arrow's bounds; ArrowsTests' `arrowFindTests` hold a hit to its word's ink.

## Arrows follow tldraw

- Arrow behavior and geometry deliberately mirror tldraw's arc arrows: binding precision and timers, clipping, arrowhead gaps, handles and hint overlays. The constants come from tldraw's source. Two intended deviations. The first is about outlines, at every zoom: every closed shape's outline, the shape an arrow's end is aimed at (the hint) included, stands off its edge 4 view points (`CanvasEditor.outlineStandoff`) where tldraw's lies on it, so every kind is outlined alike; an arrow's own outline, open, is on its line as tldraw's is. The second, below 35% only, from tldraw (whose arrows only trade a drawn style for a solid one, below 25%) and from the render: zoomed out an arrow is its skeleton (`ArrowSkeleton.swift`), which reads only its kept geometry's three points (start, the body's middle, end), never working `ArrowGeometry` out again: the arc through them, straight when the middle is under a quarter of a view point off the chord, stroked in its color at its own width but never under a view point, with butt caps, and each head a filled triangle sized and swung as the render's chevrons, a label left off; its outline there (`CustomSkeletonOutline`) is the same arc, so a selected or hovered arrow's outline follows it. The transcript and every render at 35% and above are untouched.
- Check tldraw (`packages/tldraw/src/lib/shapes/arrow/`, `bindings/arrow/`, `overlays/`) before changing arrow behavior, and call out any intentional deviation.
- Labels are tldraw's (`arrowLabel.ts`, `PointingArrowLabel`): `Arrow.text` (plain text for now, stored as a string, so it becomes mention text with no migration; notes' mentions and a prose map's highlights, as schema cards have, are to come; `canvas draw` writes both on an edge, `edge "A" -> "B" text: "calls"`) and `labelPosition` (0 to 1 along the visible body, 0.5 by default; `ArrowBody`, the body as a segment or a trimmed arc). `ArrowLabels` measures (no wider than the body less 64 on a body wider than it's tall, else 16 ems, kept by text and width) and places it (`ArrowLabelLayout.frame`, pure: centered at its position, clamped clear of an end with a head or a binding by `labelClearance`, 23), and the render cuts the body, heads and all, where it is (a rounded rect, 3.5), drawing the text centered; its bounds, hit test (anywhere on the label), marquee and outline (the body less the cut, and the cut's outline) hold it. A double-click on the arrow (or on the bend handle), Return, or a click on the only selected arrow's label edits it (`ArrowLabelEditor`, an `EditingTextView` over the label, its spelling checked as the app's is: Return a new line, Escape and ⌘↩ end it; all its text selected unless begun on the label; an empty one shows halfway, an "i" wide, and its first text puts it there, so an edit that types nothing writes nothing); a drag from the label slides it to the body's nearest point, snapping to halfway within 10 view points ("Move Label", Escape puts it back). Its row is its first line, else "Arrow". The label is part of its kept geometry (`ShapeGeometry.label`, the core's, tldraw's `isLabel` geometry), which the hit test, marquee, outline and Selection's `labelFrame` read; zoomed out its skeleton leaves the label off and draws the arc whole (its text would be a few pixels, and a cut there, with its greeked bars as dark as the canvas, reads as a gap in the arrow), the outline the same. Deviations: the system font at 15 points on 18-point lines (tldraw's draw font at 20, as notes here are 16 to its 22), no text outline (the cut leaves the text clear), its padding 4 (tldraw's 4.25) and its frame on whole points, since the editor's TextKit 2 sets text on whole view points where string drawing doesn't (`LabelTests` hold the editor's text to the render's), and a squeezed label as wide as its widest line or the limit (tldraw measures it again at the limit).
- Moving arrows is `ArrowDefinition: CustomTranslation`. Alone (or with unrelated shapes) it is the POC's translation exactly; moved with a shape it's bound to, it keeps every binding, as tldraw. Its ends probe at the full delta (tldraw: half), a known deviation the transcript pins.
- What an end may bind to is tldraw's canBind, from any plugin: `ArrowBindingRules` (`registrar.arrowBindingRule { query in … }`, over `ArrowBindingQuery`: the arrow as it is, the end, a shape under it, the registry). An end binds only to a shape every rule allows, looking through the rest to the shapes behind, wherever an end binds: `ArrowTargeting.bindTerminal` (a port's drag, re-aims, the precise timer, a modifier's change) and a lone arrow's translation probe (`beginTranslation`); rules are asked only of the shapes the point hits, and with none nothing is filtered. The arrow code knows no channel: `ArrowPortsPlugin`'s rule does, and lets an arrow without one bind to anything, as arrows always did (the transcript holds them). A shape that stands for others (a reference) is also judged by what it stands for now: `ArrowTargeting.filter` adds `PortState.allows` for a re-aimed end, since the rule is per kind and a stand-in's kind has ports on every channel ([connections](connections.md)). `shapes.canBindArrow(to:)` is whether an arrow can bind to a shape in a `ShapeContext` (there, closed, not an arrow; `editor.canBindArrow(to:)` in the editor's), `registry.arrowMayBind(arrow, end, to:)` asks the rules for an arrow made without a pointer (`ArrowPortsPlugin`'s `make`), and `editor.beginArrow(from:at:canBind:complete:)` (internal: only `ArrowPortsPlugin` calls it) draws one for a port's drag: it inserts an arrow, its start bound to the shape at its center (imprecise) and its end free at `origin`, and returns an `ArrowHandleDragSession` dragging the end (`connecting`), which binds by tldraw's rules (fast to the center, slow or after a 320 ms dwell precise, ⌥ exact, ⌘ or ⌃ nothing, ⇧ snaps the angle) only to shapes `canBind` and the rules allow, seeing through the rest; released bound, `complete(&record, target)` finishes it in one transaction. `BindingHintsOverlay` draws for the arrow being dragged (the session's `arrowID`), else the selection, so re-aims and a port's arrow hint alike. A port's drag deviates from tldraw on purpose, as data-canvas's does: released free (or on Escape) the arrow is deleted, where tldraw keeps a free end; it scrolls the canvas at its edges (`scrollsAtEdges`), where tldraw's handle drags don't, so a shape off screen can be linked to without zooming out; its start is the port's shape's center, with none of tldraw's arrow tool's hover and press timers; and the selection and the tool are left alone (no selecting the new arrow, no step of the selection's history).
- CompositionTests holds the app to the proof of concept (commit 804db97): an arrow transcript compared byte for byte, and renders (outlines, binding hints, handles) compared pixel for pixel, drawn with skeletons off (`editor.skeletonZoom = 0`: the POC drew every shape in full at every zoom, so "re-aimed-zoomed-out", at 15%, would differ in the shapes, which the app draws as skeletons there on purpose; the goldens stay as recorded) and outlines on the edge (`editor.outlineStandoff = 0`: the POC outlined a shape on its own edge, B as the arrow's target in "re-aiming-into-b" hugging it, where the app stands every closed shape's outline off its edge 4 view points on purpose; both set in ParityTests beside each other, never in `Replay.swift`, which the POC compiles). `Tests/CompositionTests/Replay.swift` drives both the POC recording and the test. The fixtures come only from the POC, via `scripts/record-poc-fixtures.sh` (re-running it must change nothing). Never regenerate them from the app to make a change pass; an intended change to arrow behavior needs an explicit note. A scenario that ends differently on purpose gets an entry in `intendedDeviations` (TranscriptTests): the golden line, the app's, and why. The one so far: a press doesn't bring a shape to the front (the POC's did; tldraw's doesn't). Scenarios are only ever appended, recorded from the POC; the one exception: retiring the arrow tool (A; arrows are drawn from ports alone now) took the scenarios and renders that drew with it, and re-aims of an arrow's end the same ways took their place, appended. The POC's timers run on real time, so keep an event 50 ms or more from when one fires, or it records either way. The "pan, then re-aim" scenario presses H and holds still 100 ms before `.up` (both no-ops in the POC), and the welcome renders use the POC's note text, `pocWelcomeTexts`.
