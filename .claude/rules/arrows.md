# Arrows

Arrows, which mirror tldraw's arc arrows: the intended deviations, labels, moving them, what an end may bind to, a port's drag, and the proof-of-concept parity tests.

Related: [connections](connections.md), [mentions](mentions.md), [slash commands](slash-commands.md), [find](find.md), [drawing](drawing.md), [editing](editing.md), [selection](selection.md), [palette](palette.md) ([every topic](../../CLAUDE.md#map)).

In the guide, `docs/plugin-system.md`: [The connector: arrows](../../docs/plugin-system.md#the-connector-arrows), [A shape without a frame](../../docs/plugin-system.md#a-shape-without-a-frame).

## Architecture

- Arrows see other shapes only as `ConnectableShape` (a closed shape's core geometry). Keep specific shape kinds out of arrow code.
- An arrow's label is mention text, so Find searches it as it does a note's, and arrows have no Find plugin of their own (`ArrowFindPlugin` is gone): Mentions' `MentionFindPlugin` reads the arrow's one placement (`ArrowLabels.placement(of:in:)`, registered with `arrow.mentionText { arrow, shapes in … }`, from its kept geometry's `label`, so an arrow with no label has none), washed, since the placement is `overCanvas` ([find](find.md), [mentions](mentions.md)); ArrowsTests' `arrowFindTests` hold a hit to its word's ink. Arrows depends on Mentions (a product on the platform), so a list with arrows installs `MentionsPlugin()` after Titles and the table of contents, and what it stands on: Topology, ContextMenus, SlashCommands and Adornments (Titles') (`arrowsPlugins`, ArrowsTests' Support; the registry refuses the arrow's `mentionText` without Mentions, `ownerNotInstalled`).
- `ArrowDefinition` is a `StrokedShape` (a core capability: a kind drawn as a line says how wide, in world units): `ArrowStyle.strokeWidth`, 3.5, whatever the arrow's props, which `render` strokes its body and heads with. The core covers a tinted outline of an arrow with it (`Outlines.tinted`: Neighbors' lights): in full the outline, its `indicatorPath` (the body, its heads and, with a label, the cut's outline), is stroked `max(2, 3.5 × zoom + 1)` view points wide, round, so the arrow's own line and heads are under it entirely rather than a thin stripe along the middle of a thick line at 200%; zoomed out, where the skeleton is a point-wide line, the plain 2 points. The selection's and a hover's outlines stay as thin as they are, and tldraw's arrows light nothing of this kind (the light is this app's). ArrowsTests: the arrow says 3.5 (`registry.lineWidth(of:in:)`, nil for a box) and a tinted arrow is as wide as its line and a point more at 50% and 200%, with none of it showing; the core's cover is CanvasTests' ([drawing](drawing.md)).

## Arrows follow tldraw

- Arrow behavior and geometry deliberately mirror tldraw's arc arrows: binding precision and timers, clipping, arrowhead gaps, handles and hint overlays. The constants come from tldraw's source. Two intended deviations. The first is about outlines, at every zoom: every closed shape's outline, the shape an arrow's end is aimed at (the hint) included, stands off its edge 4 view points (`CanvasEditor.outlineStandoff`) where tldraw's lies on it, so every kind is outlined alike; an arrow's own outline, open, is on its line as tldraw's is. The second, below 35% only, from tldraw (whose arrows only trade a drawn style for a solid one, below 25%) and from the render: zoomed out an arrow is its skeleton (`ArrowSkeleton.swift`), which reads only its kept geometry's three points (start, the body's middle, end), never working `ArrowGeometry` out again: the arc through them, straight when the middle is under a quarter of a view point off the chord, stroked in its color at its own width but never under a view point, with butt caps, and each head a filled triangle sized and swung as the render's chevrons, a label left off; its outline there (`CustomSkeletonOutline`) is the same arc and the heads it draws, each the closed triangle `addHead` fills (`ArrowSkeleton.outline(startHead:endHead:strokeWidth:)`, the heads the arrow has), so any outline of a skeleton arrow, selected, hovered or lit by a tint, shows which way it points. Skeletons are this app's own: tldraw has none to match, so what one draws and outlines is for the app to say, and the heads in its outline deviate from nothing of tldraw's. The transcript and every render at 35% and above are untouched.
- An arrow has a color (`arrow.colorable()`, no default, [colors](palette.md)): with none it is `ArrowStyle.color` as it always was, and the parity tests and fixtures never see one. With one (`context.colors(of: shape)`, one lookup a render; a name the file lacks counts as none) its body and heads are the color's `solid`, its label's text, pills and the editor's text and caret the color's `ink` (`ArrowStyle.labelStyle(ink:)`, `ArrowLabels.placement(of:in:ink:)`; the editor reads the swatch's dynamic `NSColor`, so it follows the appearance), and zoomed out its skeleton the `solid`, at the cost it had. Chrome stays `ArrowStyle.color`'s and `bindingHintColor`'s: the binding hints, handles, the port dots, a channel's color, and Neighbors' lights (a lit arrow is covered in its light's color); the table of contents' row symbol is the row's own. tldraw's arrow (v5.5.2, `ArrowShapeUtil.tsx`, `TLArrowShape.ts`) has `color` (the body and heads, `getColorValue(colors, color, 'solid')` for the theme's mode) and a separate `labelColor` (the text, `'solid'` too), and a `fill` (the heads' fill) that the arrow here has no prop for. Deviations: one color, the label's ink following it (no `labelColor`); ink, not the solid, for the text (the solid is a line's, the ink reads on the canvas); a color is a name or a hex value in the file's list, not tldraw's fixed theme names; no head fill. ArrowsTests' `arrowColorTests` draw an arrow in both appearances (a named color, a pale and a dark hex value), as a skeleton, with `setColor(nil)` and undo, and a name the file lacks.
- Check tldraw (`packages/tldraw/src/lib/shapes/arrow/`, `bindings/arrow/`, `overlays/`) before changing arrow behavior, and call out any intentional deviation.
- A move to another board takes along an arrow bound only to shapes that move (`Transaction.move(_:to:)`; a read-only one stays and lets go), a deviation from tldraw, checked at v5.5.2: `Editor.moveShapesToPage` (`packages/editor/src/lib/editor/Editor.ts:7213-7265`) moves only the shapes it is given and their descendants (`getContentFromCurrentPage`, `:9580`, keeps a binding only when both of its shapes are among them), so an arrow that is not among them stays on its page and loses its bindings. Here a pair of shapes moved together keeps its arrow, however the move was asked for (a drop on a subboard's row, `/move-to`, a frame turned into a subboard). An arrow bound at one end and free at the other rides too, and its free end goes as far as its note does where the move is put down: `Transaction.rodeAlong` names what rode, and the caller translates those with the shapes it moved (Moving's commit does; ArrowsTests' `arrowTranslateTests` hold it with the real arrow, CompositionTests' `movingShippedTests` through `/move-to`). What rides is judged against what was moving before any arrow joined.
- A second arrow made between a pair, with imprecise ends, is stored bent apart from the first (a deviation from tldraw, checked at v5.5.2: its arrows made between the same two shapes coincide, and a pair that runs opposite ways reads as one line with a head at each end; a person separates them by bending one or binding precisely at different spots, and nothing does it for them). Two arrows aimed at the shapes' centers read as one arrow with a head at each end, which a type related to itself (two arrows) must never be. So a new arrow bound at both ends to the centers, with no bend, takes `ArrowConnections.pairBend(index:from:to:)` (`ArrowPairs.swift`): the offsets 0, +1, -1, +2, -2 (and on) times `ArrowStyle.pairBend` (34 world units) by the first offset none of the arrows that already join the pair holds, either way (`ArrowConnections.parallels(of:_:except:in:)` lists them, one read of Topology's literal arrow edges of one shape, at a gesture, never in a frame; `freeSlot` reads each one's stored bend as the slot it holds, so after a delete the next arrow takes the freed offset and never one a surviving arrow bows through; an arrow bent some other way holds none), along the chord from the lower id to the higher and negated for an arrow that runs the other way, so two arrows of a pair bow apart whichever way each runs. The bend is tldraw's own property, so geometry stays a pure function of the arrow: a person who straightens one puts the pair back on one line, and nothing re-separates it. It is applied where an arrow is made and nowhere it is changed: a port's drag at `complete` (the list excludes the arrow itself, bound already), and `ArrowConnections.arrow(for:existing:in:)` for a new arrow, whose `ConnectionRequest.beside` (`[ShapeID]`; nil: read from Topology, which a context lends: `ShapeContext.service`) a `canvas draw` run sets to its own and the board's arrows of the pair (`EdgeRequest.beside`, Draw's `Drawing.pairArrows(of:)`: the arrows `apply` commits and the arrows `provisionalConnections()` lays out are made beside the same list, the laid-out ones over a document that holds the arrows beside), since the run's arrows are not in Topology yet. An arrow with a side named (a precise end), one already bent, one `existing` made again, and a re-aim are left as they are. ArrowPortsTests' `pairTests` hold it (the formula for the second to the fifth arrow, the offset freed by a delete, an arrow the other way, a drag and a request alike, a precise or re-made arrow untouched, one step and an exact undo); PortEdgesTests and AutomationTests' `drawPairTests` hold the arrows a run passes, and CompositionTests' `drawConnectionShippedTests` the run's second arrow bent and the same script `unchanged`, also when one script draws the notes and both edges and when it adds an edge to a pair the board joins (the laid-out arrow agrees with the drawn one).
- Deleting a relationship type deletes its end arrows, in the same step, but a locked one (a deviation from tldraw, checked at v5.5.2: its arrow's binding util moves a terminal bound to a deleted shape to its drawn point and keeps the arrow, `ArrowBindingUtil.ts:111-125`, and overrides no hook that deletes it; here an arrow bound to a deleted shape still lets go of it where it is drawn, and stays, everywhere but this). The arrows are those between the type and the types it relates or nothing (its ends: the relationship index's, `RelationshipIndex.endArrows(of:)`, and the arrows on its own board bound to it, read from the transaction's records), on whichever boards they are, through pills as well, but not on the board shown when the step is on another board (a commit that reached it would be dropped); arrows between it and a note that is no type are plain arrows and stay. The handler is Types' `willDelete` (`RelationshipDeletes.swift`), which runs inside the delete's cascade, where the core's refusal checks are off, so it leaves a locked arrow (a lock on it, on a frame that holds it or on its board) for itself: that arrow lets go of the type where it was drawn, stays, and, with a name or a rule on it and one end at a type's note, is a loose end the check warns of (`This arrow isn’t attached at both ends.`, [relationships](relationships.md#deleting-a-relationship-type-and-what-is-left-behind)). The delete of the type is never refused. One Undo brings back the type and its end arrows exactly, on every board they were on. A type at an end deleted, a pill deleted, an arrow deleted by hand and Remove Type on a relationship type are tldraw's, and take nothing along. ArrowsTests hold no part of it (the arrow knows no relationship): TypesTests' `relationshipDeleteTests` and CompositionTests' `relationshipSelfDrawTests` do.
- Labels are tldraw's (`arrowLabel.ts`, `PointingArrowLabel`): `Arrow.text` (`MentionText`, as a note's: pills for shapes, stored as one string with `@[label](shape:ID)` tokens, so the bytes are a string still, and a migration, `PropsMigration("text may mention shapes")` (`MentionTextFormat.escapeLookalikesV1`, as notes'), escapes text from before that reads as a mention, so it stays the text it was; `Fixtures/kinds/arrow.v2.json` sets every property, a mention and a look-alike in its text; a prose map's highlights, as type notes have, are to come; `canvas draw` writes both on an edge, `edge "A" -> "B" text: "calls @[Cache]"`, the title between the brackets naming the shape as in a note's text) and `labelPosition` (0 to 1 along the visible body, 0.5 by default; `ArrowBody`, the body as a segment or a trimmed arc). `ArrowLabels` measures (no wider than the body less 64 on a body wider than it's tall, else 16 ems, kept by the text as stored and width, each mention measured as a live pill by the name it shows now, `MentionTextStyle.measuredString`: a pure function of the arrow and its layout input, as its geometry must be) and places it (`ArrowLabelLayout.frame`, pure: centered at its position, clamped clear of an end with a head or a binding by `labelClearance`, 23), and the render cuts the body, heads and all, where it is (a rounded rect, 3.5), drawing the text through Mentions (`context.drawMentionText`, `ArrowStyle.labelText`: a `MentionTextStyle` at 15 on 18, centered (its `alignment`), the pills in the arrow's own color, over the canvas); its bounds, hit test (anywhere on the label), marquee and outline (the body less the cut, and the cut's outline) hold it. A double-click on the arrow (or on the bend handle), Return, or a click on the only selected arrow's label edits it (`ArrowLabelEditor`, a `MentionTextView` over the label with the arrow as its owner, its spelling checked as the app's is: `@` lists the shapes to mention, each a pill, and a `/` on an empty line lists the commands the arrow offers (`/lock` among them, Locking's, which is on every shape, and `/is-a`, Types': an arrow is `typeable()`, so a type picked there makes it an instance of that type; `/title` isn't, an arrow has no title), Return a new line unless the line is a command, which it runs and takes out of the label, Escape and ⌘↩ end it; all its text selected unless begun on the label; an empty one shows halfway, an "i" wide, and its first text puts it there, so an edit that types nothing writes nothing); a drag from the label slides it to the body's nearest point, snapping to halfway within 10 view points ("Move Label", Escape puts it back). Its row is its first line, each mention its shape's name as it is now (`MentionRows.affected`: a rename remakes the rows of the arrows that mention the shape), else "Arrow". The label is part of its kept geometry (`ShapeGeometry.label`, the core's, tldraw's `isLabel` geometry), which the hit test, marquee, outline and Selection's `labelFrame` read; zoomed out its skeleton leaves the label off and draws the arc whole (its text would be a few pixels, and a cut there, with its greeked bars as dark as the canvas, reads as a gap in the arrow), the outline the same. Deviations: tldraw's label is plain rich text, where this one holds mention pills and slash commands (Return on a command line runs the command, where tldraw's Return is always a new line: a line that is `/` and a command's name, and what it takes, is only ever typed to be run) and chips above it, one for each type the arrow is (Types' `TypeChips`, a plain type's, and a relationship type's second on an arrow, which says how many fields it gives the arrow and is red with a wave while an end is a misfit, its click the arrow's card; the arrow's first relationship type is not a chip but the pill that leads its label, below; and `from` or `to` above an unnamed end's, all Adornments' and Types', none the arrow's geometry, bounds or hit test; in the row Adornments lays out against the label, [adornments](adornments.md); the system font at 15 points on 18-point lines (tldraw's draw font at 20, as notes here are 16 to its 22), no text outline (the cut leaves the text clear), its padding 4 (tldraw's 4.25) and its frame on whole points, since the editor's TextKit 2 sets text on whole view points where string drawing doesn't (`LabelTests` hold the editor's text to the render's), and a squeezed label as wide as its widest line or the limit (tldraw measures it again at the limit). A rename lays the label out again: its frame is measured by the names its mentions show now, not those its text holds (a geometry can't read another board, and a rename writes nothing to the text), through a layout input: the arrow registers `arrow.mentionLayout { $0.props.text }`, Mentions sets `MentionLayout.names` on each arrow whose pills show a name other than the one the text holds (when a rename, on any board, changes a name, when the text changes, and as a file opens), the geometry reads it (`context.mentionNames(of:)`, passed to `ArrowLabels.frame(of:on:names:editing:)`, which keeps its measure by the text, the width and the names) and the editor lets go of the kept geometry (`editor.setLayoutInput`, [editor](editor.md)). Nothing is stored: no step, nothing edited, the text keeps the name it was written with, and undoing a rename lays it out as it was. A pill drawn missing is still measured by the name it holds. ArrowsTests' mention tests (`LabelMentionTests`, with `labelPlugins`: Mentions and what it stands on, a titled and mentionable `Box`, and a `head` command) hold a label with pills (measured with them, laid out again by a rename on the board and on another, longer and shorter, and by its undo, with no step of its own; each pill inside it, the arrow among `mentioners(of:)` of the shape it mentions; the editor's lines and pills on the render's, pills within 0.01 point and the pixels but anti-aliasing at 1× and 2×; what's typed written as mention text, a look-alike staying text, in one step; a mention-only label having a label and a row of its own); `labelSlashTests` the commands and the picker in an unshown window (`EditedLabel`: a slash mid-line is a slash, on a new line it lists `head`, Return runs it and takes the line out, a line that isn't a command is a new line, `@gro` picks a pill and the label grows); `labelTypeTests` `/is-a` in a label (the picker lists the types, not other shapes; Return picks one and a second runs the line: the arrow is an instance in one step, Add Type, the line gone, and the chip above the label, renamed with its type; over `Gloss`, a fixture box that `canBeType()`); CompositionTests' `slashCommandsShippedTests` `/lock` in a label, `typesShippedTests` `/is-a` in a label in the app's canvas view, `drawMentionsTests` an edge's `@[Title]`, and `persistenceTests` and `SubboardFileTests` the migration and the files. The parity goldens are plain labels', and unchanged.
- **An arrow that is an end of a relationship type is half of one line through the type's pill** (a deviation from tldraw, only for such an arrow; plain arrows, and so every parity golden, are as they were: `ShapeGeometry.detail` nil, `ArrowGeometry` called as ever, with `minBend` at its default). Types says which arrows these are (`EndArrow.input`, a layout input it provides: the type, the side, and, when the type has exactly one end on each side, the partner half; [types](types.md#what-a-shape-is)); `ArrowEndLine.swift` draws them, in both halves alike, as an `EndLine` kept in the geometry's `detail`, worked out when the geometry is, from the shapes it looks up (so a move of a far type, a re-aim of the partner or a move of the pill works it out again with no announcement from Types: the index of shapes follows what a geometry read; Types announces only when the ends themselves change). Three things: **no head at the pill** (the stored head flags are left as they are, and `canvas draw`'s end arrows read the same); **no words** (no label, no cut, no lead, no editing: the words are kept and name the row, which the type's card edits; a double-click or Return on such an arrow opens that card at the end's row name through `ShapeOpener.opens`, a claim Arrows makes for them alone, every other arrow being edited as ever; the bend handle is hidden on an arc half); and **one arc**: when the type has one end on each side and their far ends are bound to two different shapes, each half is its part of the circle through the far anchor of one half, the pill's middle (the hub end made imprecise) and the far anchor of the other, as `ArrowThrough` (pure: `bend(from:to:awayFrom:)`, the bend that makes the half lie on that circle on the side of the chord away from the third point, in `Arrow.bend`'s terms; `isStraight`, the pill within `straightness`, 0.25 world units, of the line between the two far anchors, a world constant where the spec's quarter of a view point would tie the geometry to the camera) which `ArrowGeometry` then clips at both shapes and draws as any bent arrow's, with `minBend` 0 (a person's bend under 8 draws straight; this one is worked out from the pill's place), so the two halves meet at the pill with the same tangent, and drag of the pill (a move of the type's shape) bends the line; a straight line gets bend 0 whatever the arrow stores. Otherwise (a side with several ends, a type related to itself, an end free at its far end, an end whose partner has no far shape) each half keeps the bend it stores, with the two rules above. Zoomed out the skeleton reads the kept three points, so it follows the arc, and drops its head at the pill by the same `detail` (`ArrowDefinition.heads(of:in:)`). ArrowsTests' `endLineTests` hold the pure arc (three points, either way round, collinear, nearly collinear), the heads, the words, the circle through the three middles and no corner at the pill after each move of a far type or the pill, the cases with no arc, the plain arrow, the double-click and the skeleton (bitmaps); the parity goldens pass unchanged.
- **A pill leads the label of an arrow of a relationship type** (a deviation from tldraw, only for an arrow that has one; plain arrows, and so every parity golden, are as they were, `labelLead` nil and `ArrowLabels.frame(of:on:names:editing:)` the same function of the arrow). Types' `RelationshipPill` ([types](types.md#what-a-shape-is)) is an Adornments adornment at `inLabelLead` ([adornments](adornments.md), **A label's lead**); the arrow's geometry keeps its room. `ArrowLabels.laid(of:on:names:editing:lead:)` (pure, internal; `laid(of:on:in:editing:)` reads the layout inputs from a `ShapeContext`) is the label: given the sizes the pill may take (`AdornmentLead.faces`, read as `context.layoutInput(AdornmentLead.faces, of: shape)`, which Adornments provides) it has a label even with no words (`hasWords || lead`), whose frame is a padding (`labelPadding`, 4), the pill and then the words as they are measured (their own padding the gap; with no words the pill and a padding after it), as tall as the taller of the words and the pill and two paddings (26 for a pill 18 high), centred at `labelPosition` as a label is (clamped clear of an end with a head or a binding by the same range, the frame whole points, the pill's origin a point too), the words squeezed with the pill's room taken off (`size(of:bodyBounds:names:reserving:)`). The pill's face is the widest of the sizes whose label is no wider than the body less `labelSqueezeMargin` (64: a label's own room), else the last, which is a dot: "drop the value, then a dot", never wider than the line, still the pill (a click opens the card as ever). `Laid` says the frame, the lead's rect within it and the words' frame (`text`: the frame from the pill's right edge). The cut is the whole frame (the body, heads and all, is cut where the label is: `render` clips around `laid.frame`; `indicatorPath` and the outline cut and outline it, so a red ring of a misfit goes round the pill and its padding); `geometry` holds `labelLead` beside `label` (and the bounds hold the label), so Adornments finds it (and `ShapeGeometry.labelLead` is all it reads of an arrow); the words are drawn in `text` (`drawMentionText`, Mentions' placement and Find's: `ArrowLabels.textFrame(of:)`), the pill by Adornments over the canvas, where it is pressed and hovered. `render` takes the label from the kept geometry (`ArrowLabels.laid(from:)`), not from Adornments' layout input, which a frame would ask afresh for every arrow (a pan of 450 relationship arrows cost a third more); Adornments keeps the answer for a shape until a commit that changed it (not one that only moved it) or `adornmentsDidChange` names it, as it keeps its labels, so a bound note's drag asks no label. `labelFrame(of:)` no longer asks for words (the geometry has a label only if there are words, an edit or a pill), so a press on the pill is on the arrow and the label's drag slides the label (`labelPosition`, which a label with no words but a pill honours); the editor goes over the words alone (`editingFrame` is `text`; an empty label is "i" wide after the pill), so the first character typed grows the label about its middle and the pill moves left by half of it, as a label's words do. The bend handle is left off where its point is within `leadHandleClearance` (4 view points) of the pill (`handles(of:)`): it would sit on the name, and a press there is the pill's. Zoomed out the skeleton ([above](#arrows-follow-tldraw)) draws what Adornments does not, a capsule of the pill's size where it is (`ArrowStyle.skeletonCapsuleFill` 40%, `skeletonCapsuleLine` 80%, in the tint of `AdornmentLead.faces`, none for the dashed pill, which has none), no letters, and the arc runs whole through it as it does through a label's place. When what the pill says moves its room with no commit to the arrow (a rename, a field added before the first filled one, the check's verdict) Adornments says the layout changed, and the arrow's frame follows. ArrowsTests' `arrowPillTests` hold it (no pill with no relationship type or with a plain type, the label's frame and its middle, the words after it, the body cut around it, every slope, a short line's name and dot, the first value dropping first, a rename and a value reaching the room with the arrow unwritten, the editor, the words' frame, the skeleton's capsule and nothing beyond it, the bend handle), and the parity goldens pass unchanged. Arrows imports Adornments for this (`AdornmentLead`; [build](build.md)).
- Moving arrows is `ArrowDefinition: CustomTranslation`. Alone (or with unrelated shapes) it is the POC's translation exactly; moved with a shape it's bound to, it keeps every binding, as tldraw. Its ends probe at the full delta (tldraw: half), a known deviation the transcript pins.
- What an end may bind to is tldraw's canBind, from any plugin: `ArrowBindingRules` (`registrar.arrowBindingRule { query in … }`, over `ArrowBindingQuery`: the arrow as it is, the end, a shape under it, the registry). An end binds only to a shape every rule allows, looking through the rest to the shapes behind, wherever an end binds: `ArrowTargeting.bindTerminal` (a port's drag, re-aims, the precise timer, a modifier's change) and a lone arrow's translation probe (`beginTranslation`); rules are asked only of the shapes the point hits, and with none nothing is filtered. The arrow code knows no channel: `ArrowPortsPlugin`'s rule does, and lets an arrow without one bind to anything, as arrows always did (the transcript holds them). A shape that stands for others (a reference) is also judged by what it stands for now: `ArrowTargeting.filter` adds Connections' `editor.portsAllow(_:as:at:)` for a re-aimed end (the start a source, the end a target), since the rule is per kind and a stand-in's kind has ports on every channel ([connections](connections.md)). `shapes.canBindArrow(to:)` is whether an arrow can bind to a shape in a `ShapeContext` (there, closed, not an arrow; `editor.canBindArrow(to:)` in the editor's), `registry.arrowMayBind(arrow, end, to:)` asks the rules for an arrow made without a pointer (`ArrowPortsPlugin`'s `make`), and `editor.beginArrow(from:at:canBind:complete:finished:)` (internal: only `ArrowPortsPlugin` calls it) draws one for a port's drag: it inserts an arrow, its start bound to the shape at its center (imprecise) and its end free at `origin`, and returns an `ArrowHandleDragSession` dragging the end (`connecting`), which says its arrow is what it touches (`Session.touching` = `[arrowID]`, for a port's drag and for a re-aim alike, from the press to the release, `end` and `cancel` and their commits included, since `editor.session` is cleared only when the press ends: a service that counts arrows leaves an arrow in flight out until then; ArrowPortsTests' `arrowTouchingTests`), which binds by tldraw's rules (fast to the center, slow or after a 320 ms dwell precise, ⌥ exact, ⌘ or ⌃ nothing, ⇧ snaps the angle) only to shapes `canBind` and the rules allow, seeing through the rest; released bound, `complete(&record, target)` finishes it in one transaction and `finished(arrowID)` (`ArrowConnecting.finished`: `ArrowPortsPlugin` passes `{ editor.connectionDrawn($0) }`, Types') hears of it, still in the press and before its step closes, so what it does is the press's step; every way a drag session ends (`end` on a re-aim, `finish` on a bound release, a free release's delete, `cancel`) then tells the relationships (`editor.connectionReleased(arrowID)`, once: `connectionDrawn` does it itself), which count the arrow, still named by `touching`, as it is now. `BindingHintsOverlay` draws for the arrow being dragged (the session's `arrowID`), else the selection, so re-aims and a port's arrow hint alike. A port's drag deviates from tldraw on purpose, as data-canvas's does: released free (or on Escape) the arrow is deleted, where tldraw keeps a free end; it scrolls the canvas at its edges (`scrollsAtEdges`), where tldraw's handle drags don't, so a shape off screen can be linked to without zooming out; its start is the port's shape's center, with none of tldraw's arrow tool's hover and press timers; and the selection and the tool are left alone (no selecting the new arrow, no step of the selection's history) but for the release's three deviations from tldraw, all Types' (`connectionDrawn`, [types](types.md#the-release-of-a-ports-drag)): a port's release may give the arrow a relationship type, in the press's own step (the step is still `Add Arrow`); it may open a list of types at the arrow, and selects the arrow while the list is open (selecting what a tool makes is tldraw's own, so this retires part of this deviation); and between two types the release replaces the arrow drawn with a relationship type and an arrow to it from each type, one step, and selects the type (a port's drag otherwise leaves the selection alone), with the card that names it shown in the same step (`CanvasCard.adoptsPress`); and a pass of a port's drag, or a re-aim, over a relationship type's note counts as no end until the release (`Session.touching`, [relationships](relationships.md#the-service-fieldsrelationshipindexswift)): nothing of tldraw's is read differently.
- A row's or a card's pick makes an arrow with no drag (a deviation from tldraw, which makes an arrow only by dragging one): `editor.relate` makes it through the connector's own maker (`registry.drawConnection`, as `canvas draw` makes an edge), to a note or, for a note on another board, to a pill that stands for it, bound at both ends, one head at the end, and gives it the row's relationship type; its direction is set by the types (the row's side), not by which note the person was on. A row's delete (`editor.unrelate`) removes the arrow, its values and the pill it empties, on whichever board they are, in one step; tldraw's delete of an arrow leaves what it was bound to, and so does Selection's Delete here. Neither touches the arrow's geometry, bounds or hit test, and the parity goldens do not move ([relationships](relationships.md#relating-from-a-row)).
