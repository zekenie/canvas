# Arrows

Arrows, which mirror tldraw's arc arrows: the intended deviations, labels, moving them, what an end may bind to, a port's drag, and the proof-of-concept parity tests.

Related: [connections](connections.md), [mentions](mentions.md), [slash commands](slash-commands.md), [find](find.md), [drawing](drawing.md), [editing](editing.md), [selection](selection.md) ([every topic](../../CLAUDE.md#map)).

In the guide, `docs/plugin-system.md`: [The connector: arrows](../../docs/plugin-system.md#the-connector-arrows), [A shape without a frame](../../docs/plugin-system.md#a-shape-without-a-frame).

## Architecture

- Arrows see other shapes only as `ConnectableShape` (a closed shape's core geometry). Keep specific shape kinds out of arrow code.
- An arrow's label is mention text, so Find searches it as it does a note's, and arrows have no Find plugin of their own (`ArrowFindPlugin` is gone): Mentions' `MentionFindPlugin` reads the arrow's one placement (`ArrowLabels.placement(of:in:)`, registered with `arrow.mentionText { arrow, shapes in … }`, from its kept geometry's `label`, so an arrow with no label has none), washed, since the placement is `overCanvas` ([find](find.md), [mentions](mentions.md)); ArrowsTests' `arrowFindTests` hold a hit to its word's ink. Connections depends on Mentions (concern to concern), so a list with arrows installs `MentionsPlugin()` after Titles and the table of contents, and what it stands on: Topology, ContextMenus, SlashCommands and Adornments (Titles') (`arrowsPlugins`, ArrowsTests' Support; the registry refuses the arrow's `mentionText` without Mentions, `ownerNotInstalled`).
- `ArrowDefinition` is a `StrokedShape` (a core capability: a kind drawn as a line says how wide, in world units): `ArrowStyle.strokeWidth`, 3.5, whatever the arrow's props, which `render` strokes its body and heads with. The core covers a tinted outline of an arrow with it (`Outlines.tinted`: Neighbors' lights): in full the outline, its `indicatorPath` (the body, its heads and, with a label, the cut's outline), is stroked `max(2, 3.5 × zoom + 1)` view points wide, round, so the arrow's own line and heads are under it entirely rather than a thin stripe along the middle of a thick line at 200%; zoomed out, where the skeleton is a point-wide line, the plain 2 points. The selection's and a hover's outlines stay as thin as they are, and tldraw's arrows light nothing of this kind (the light is this app's). ArrowsTests: the arrow says 3.5 (`registry.lineWidth(of:in:)`, nil for a box) and a tinted arrow is as wide as its line and a point more at 50% and 200%, with none of it showing; the core's cover is CanvasTests' ([drawing](drawing.md)).

## Arrows follow tldraw

- Arrow behavior and geometry deliberately mirror tldraw's arc arrows: binding precision and timers, clipping, arrowhead gaps, handles and hint overlays. The constants come from tldraw's source. Two intended deviations. The first is about outlines, at every zoom: every closed shape's outline, the shape an arrow's end is aimed at (the hint) included, stands off its edge 4 view points (`CanvasEditor.outlineStandoff`) where tldraw's lies on it, so every kind is outlined alike; an arrow's own outline, open, is on its line as tldraw's is. The second, below 35% only, from tldraw (whose arrows only trade a drawn style for a solid one, below 25%) and from the render: zoomed out an arrow is its skeleton (`ArrowSkeleton.swift`), which reads only its kept geometry's three points (start, the body's middle, end), never working `ArrowGeometry` out again: the arc through them, straight when the middle is under a quarter of a view point off the chord, stroked in its color at its own width but never under a view point, with butt caps, and each head a filled triangle sized and swung as the render's chevrons, a label left off; its outline there (`CustomSkeletonOutline`) is the same arc and the heads it draws, each the closed triangle `addHead` fills (`ArrowSkeleton.outline(startHead:endHead:strokeWidth:)`, the heads the arrow has), so any outline of a skeleton arrow, selected, hovered or lit by a tint, shows which way it points. Skeletons are this app's own: tldraw has none to match, so what one draws and outlines is for the app to say, and the heads in its outline deviate from nothing of tldraw's. The transcript and every render at 35% and above are untouched.
- Check tldraw (`packages/tldraw/src/lib/shapes/arrow/`, `bindings/arrow/`, `overlays/`) before changing arrow behavior, and call out any intentional deviation.
- Labels are tldraw's (`arrowLabel.ts`, `PointingArrowLabel`): `Arrow.text` (`MentionText`, as a note's: pills for shapes, stored as one string with `@[label](shape:ID)` tokens, so the bytes are a string still, and a migration, `PropsMigration("text may mention shapes")` (`MentionTextFormat.escapeLookalikesV1`, as notes'), escapes text from before that reads as a mention, so it stays the text it was; `Fixtures/kinds/arrow.v2.json` sets every property, a mention and a look-alike in its text; a prose map's highlights, as type notes have, are to come; `canvas draw` writes both on an edge, `edge "A" -> "B" text: "calls @[Cache]"`, the title between the brackets naming the shape as in a note's text) and `labelPosition` (0 to 1 along the visible body, 0.5 by default; `ArrowBody`, the body as a segment or a trimmed arc). `ArrowLabels` measures (no wider than the body less 64 on a body wider than it's tall, else 16 ems, kept by the text as stored and width, each mention measured as a live pill by the name it holds, `MentionTextStyle.measuredString`: a pure function of the arrow, as its geometry must be) and places it (`ArrowLabelLayout.frame`, pure: centered at its position, clamped clear of an end with a head or a binding by `labelClearance`, 23), and the render cuts the body, heads and all, where it is (a rounded rect, 3.5), drawing the text through Mentions (`context.drawMentionText`, `ArrowStyle.labelText`: a `MentionTextStyle` at 15 on 18, centered (its `alignment`), the pills in the arrow's own color, over the canvas); its bounds, hit test (anywhere on the label), marquee and outline (the body less the cut, and the cut's outline) hold it. A double-click on the arrow (or on the bend handle), Return, or a click on the only selected arrow's label edits it (`ArrowLabelEditor`, a `MentionTextView` over the label with the arrow as its owner, its spelling checked as the app's is: `@` lists the shapes to mention, each a pill, and a `/` on an empty line lists the commands the arrow offers (`/lock` among them, Locking's, which is on every shape; `/title` isn't, an arrow has no title), Return a new line unless the line is a command, which it runs and takes out of the label, Escape and ⌘↩ end it; all its text selected unless begun on the label; an empty one shows halfway, an "i" wide, and its first text puts it there, so an edit that types nothing writes nothing); a drag from the label slides it to the body's nearest point, snapping to halfway within 10 view points ("Move Label", Escape puts it back). Its row is its first line, each mention its shape's name as it is now (`MentionRows.affected`: a rename remakes the rows of the arrows that mention the shape), else "Arrow". The label is part of its kept geometry (`ShapeGeometry.label`, the core's, tldraw's `isLabel` geometry), which the hit test, marquee, outline and Selection's `labelFrame` read; zoomed out its skeleton leaves the label off and draws the arc whole (its text would be a few pixels, and a cut there, with its greeked bars as dark as the canvas, reads as a gap in the arrow), the outline the same. Deviations: tldraw's label is plain rich text, where this one holds mention pills and slash commands (Return on a command line runs the command, where tldraw's Return is always a new line: a line that is `/` and a command's name, and what it takes, is only ever typed to be run); the system font at 15 points on 18-point lines (tldraw's draw font at 20, as notes here are 16 to its 22), no text outline (the cut leaves the text clear), its padding 4 (tldraw's 4.25) and its frame on whole points, since the editor's TextKit 2 sets text on whole view points where string drawing doesn't (`LabelTests` hold the editor's text to the render's), and a squeezed label as wide as its widest line or the limit (tldraw measures it again at the limit). Known gap: the label's frame is worked out from the names its mentions hold, since a geometry can't read what a mentioned shape is called now (nothing but a render reads another board), and the pills draw with the names the shapes have now, so a rename leaves the frame as it was: a shorter name fits with room to spare, a longer one may wrap a pill to a line past the label's last, where it isn't shown, until the label is edited (the editor writes each name as it is). `LabelMentionTests` hold it, on the same board and another; the way out is a hook that tells the editor a rename changes the arrows' layout, driven by Mentions' `rowsAffected`. ArrowsTests' mention tests (`LabelMentionTests`, with `labelPlugins`: Mentions and what it stands on, a titled and mentionable `Box`, and a `head` command) hold a label with pills (measured with them, each pill inside it, a Mentioned In row for the arrow; the editor's lines and pills on the render's, pills within 0.01 point and the pixels but anti-aliasing at 1× and 2×; what's typed written as mention text, a look-alike staying text, in one step; a mention-only label having a label and a row of its own); `labelSlashTests` the commands and the picker in an unshown window (`EditedLabel`: a slash mid-line is a slash, on a new line it lists `head`, Return runs it and takes the line out, a line that isn't a command is a new line, `@gro` picks a pill and the label grows); CompositionTests' `slashCommandsShippedTests` `/lock` in a label, `drawMentionsTests` an edge's `@[Title]`, and `persistenceTests` and `SubboardFileTests` the migration and the files. The parity goldens are plain labels', and unchanged.
- Moving arrows is `ArrowDefinition: CustomTranslation`. Alone (or with unrelated shapes) it is the POC's translation exactly; moved with a shape it's bound to, it keeps every binding, as tldraw. Its ends probe at the full delta (tldraw: half), a known deviation the transcript pins.
- What an end may bind to is tldraw's canBind, from any plugin: `ArrowBindingRules` (`registrar.arrowBindingRule { query in … }`, over `ArrowBindingQuery`: the arrow as it is, the end, a shape under it, the registry). An end binds only to a shape every rule allows, looking through the rest to the shapes behind, wherever an end binds: `ArrowTargeting.bindTerminal` (a port's drag, re-aims, the precise timer, a modifier's change) and a lone arrow's translation probe (`beginTranslation`); rules are asked only of the shapes the point hits, and with none nothing is filtered. The arrow code knows no channel: `ArrowPortsPlugin`'s rule does, and lets an arrow without one bind to anything, as arrows always did (the transcript holds them). A shape that stands for others (a reference) is also judged by what it stands for now: `ArrowTargeting.filter` adds `PortState.allows` for a re-aimed end, since the rule is per kind and a stand-in's kind has ports on every channel ([connections](connections.md)). `shapes.canBindArrow(to:)` is whether an arrow can bind to a shape in a `ShapeContext` (there, closed, not an arrow; `editor.canBindArrow(to:)` in the editor's), `registry.arrowMayBind(arrow, end, to:)` asks the rules for an arrow made without a pointer (`ArrowPortsPlugin`'s `make`), and `editor.beginArrow(from:at:canBind:complete:)` (internal: only `ArrowPortsPlugin` calls it) draws one for a port's drag: it inserts an arrow, its start bound to the shape at its center (imprecise) and its end free at `origin`, and returns an `ArrowHandleDragSession` dragging the end (`connecting`), which binds by tldraw's rules (fast to the center, slow or after a 320 ms dwell precise, ⌥ exact, ⌘ or ⌃ nothing, ⇧ snaps the angle) only to shapes `canBind` and the rules allow, seeing through the rest; released bound, `complete(&record, target)` finishes it in one transaction. `BindingHintsOverlay` draws for the arrow being dragged (the session's `arrowID`), else the selection, so re-aims and a port's arrow hint alike. A port's drag deviates from tldraw on purpose, as data-canvas's does: released free (or on Escape) the arrow is deleted, where tldraw keeps a free end; it scrolls the canvas at its edges (`scrollsAtEdges`), where tldraw's handle drags don't, so a shape off screen can be linked to without zooming out; its start is the port's shape's center, with none of tldraw's arrow tool's hover and press timers; and the selection and the tool are left alone (no selecting the new arrow, no step of the selection's history).
- CompositionTests holds the app to the proof of concept (commit 804db97): an arrow transcript compared byte for byte, and renders (outlines, binding hints, handles) compared pixel for pixel, drawn with skeletons off (`editor.skeletonZoom = 0`: the POC drew every shape in full at every zoom, so "re-aimed-zoomed-out", at 15%, would differ in the shapes, which the app draws as skeletons there on purpose; the goldens stay as recorded) and outlines on the edge (`editor.outlineStandoff = 0`: the POC outlined a shape on its own edge, B as the arrow's target in "re-aiming-into-b" hugging it, where the app stands every closed shape's outline off its edge 4 view points on purpose; both set in ParityTests beside each other, never in `Replay.swift`, which the POC compiles). `Tests/CompositionTests/Replay.swift` drives both the POC recording and the test. The fixtures come only from the POC, via `scripts/record-poc-fixtures.sh` (re-running it must change nothing). Never regenerate them from the app to make a change pass; an intended change to arrow behavior needs an explicit note. A scenario that ends differently on purpose gets an entry in `intendedDeviations` (TranscriptTests): the golden line, the app's, and why. The one so far: a press doesn't bring a shape to the front (the POC's did; tldraw's doesn't). Scenarios are only ever appended, recorded from the POC; the one exception: retiring the arrow tool (A; arrows are drawn from ports alone now) took the scenarios and renders that drew with it, and re-aims of an arrow's end the same ways took their place, appended. The POC's timers run on real time, so keep an event 50 ms or more from when one fires, or it records either way. The "pan, then re-aim" scenario presses H and holds still 100 ms before `.up` (both no-ops in the POC), and the welcome renders use the POC's note text, `pocWelcomeTexts`.
