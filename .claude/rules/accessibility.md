# Accessibility

What VoiceOver hears of the canvas: the core's description of a shape (`ShapeAccessibility`, `editor.accessibleDescription(of:)`), the order and parts one plugin may claim, announcements, the selection as the focus, and CanvasUI's elements (`ShapeElement`), the only NSAccessibility code for the canvas.

Related: [keyboard navigation](keyboard-navigation.md) (P9, named for VoiceOver), [selection](selection.md), [titles](titles.md), [editing](editing.md), [drawing](drawing.md), [host slots](host-slots.md), [benchmarks](benchmarks.md), [testing](testing.md) ([every topic](../../CLAUDE.md#map)).

In the guide, `docs/plugin-system.md`: [Describing a shape for VoiceOver](../../docs/plugin-system.md#describing-a-shape-for-voiceover).

## What a shape says

- `CorePoints.ShapeAccessibility` (core, `Sources/Canvas/Editor/Accessibility.swift`) is a scoped list point: a feature says what it adds of the shapes it reaches, `registrar.forShapes(holding:/adopting:) { $0.accessibility { record, editor in AccessibleTraits(…) } }`, or a kind of its own shapes, `shape.accessibility { record, editor in … }` (typed); the registry's report names each, `accessibility → every shape with a title`. `AccessibleTraits` has a `name` (what the shape is called: the first plugin's that says one, a kind's own before a scope's), `labelParts` (what else it is, said after the kind: a type), `value` (what it holds: the first), `states` ("locked", "1 error": every plugin's, said last) and `help` (the first). A nil or blank field says nothing.
- `editor.accessibleDescription(of:)` (an id on the board shown or elsewhere in the file, or a record) joins them, worked out each time it's asked, never kept: `name` (else the kind's label), `kind` (`registry.label(of:)`, always said), `label` ("Checkout, Note, Person, locked": the name, the kind, the parts, the states, joined with ", "), `value`, `help`. With no contribution a shape is its kind's label alone (`registry.label(of:)`), and so is a shape kept aside (`isOpaque`).
- Who says what, each scoped by its opt-in: Titles the title as the name, on every kind with a title (`forShapes(adopting: .titled)`), so a titled note reads "Checkout, Note" and an untitled one "Note"; StickyNotes a note's words as its value, each mention read as its shape's name now with no "@" (`noteAccessibility()`, `NoteAccessibility.swift`, through `MentionState.labels`); Locking "locked" as a state, on a shape locked itself (`forShapes(holding: LockedProperty.self)`, `LockingStyle.accessibleState`); Validation "1 error", "2 warnings" as states, the issues that show, of a shape on the board shown (`forEveryShape`, since every shape is checked; `ValidationStyle.errors`, `.warnings`); Frames "Empty", "1 shape", "N shapes" as a frame's value, what it holds directly (`FrameStyle.accessibleContents`); Subboards the same of its board, as its card's caption says it (`SubboardStyle.caption(of:)`). States are said in plugin order, so Validation's come before Locking's: "Checkout, Note, 1 error, locked". Arrows says where an arrow runs, as a label part after its kind, by the names of the shapes its ends are bound to (their `accessibleDescription` names), and its label's words as its value (mentions as their live names): "Arrow, from Checkout to Payment", "Arrow, to Payment" with a free start, "Arrow" with neither bound ([arrows](arrows.md#an-arrow-by-the-keys)).
- `editor.accessibleBoardName`: the root's name (`rootName`), else the name of the shape that owns the board; nil for a root the host hasn't named, which CanvasUI calls `CanvasUIStyle.unnamedBoard` ("Canvas").

## The selection, the order and the parts

- The selection is VoiceOver's focus while the canvas has the keyboard (`editor.hasKeyboard`): while a list, a field or the editing view has it, no shape's element is focused and nothing is posted of the selection, so a list that selects shapes as it's walked (the table of contents, Find's results) keeps VoiceOver on its row. The core knows it only as what commands act on (`registrar.commandShapes`, `editor.accessibleSelection`, on the board shown); the claim also says how to select (`commandShapes(_:select:)`, `editor.selectForAccessibility(_:)`, host SPI), which a press on a shape's element calls: Selection selects and brings the shapes into view if they're off it (`revealIfOffscreen`), as Tab does. The core has no selection of its own, so Selection says when it changes: it calls `editor.accessibilityFocusDidChange()` as it tells its observers (`SelectionState.tellObservers`), once the change is heard.
- `editor.accessibilityFocusDidChange()` tells the host (`editorAccessibilityFocusDidChange`, a defaulted delegate hook); a plugin whose focused part changes calls it too.
- Two claims, each filled by at most one plugin, Selection's on the shipped list ([selection](selection.md), `Sources/Selection/Accessibility.swift`): `registrar.accessibleOrder { editor in [ShapeID] }`, the Tab stops where the selection is, in reading order (Tab's own, kept between commits); and `registrar.accessibleParts(_:focused:)`, a shape's drawn parts (`AccessiblePart`: an id, a title, a `Role`, a rect in world units and a press), ⌥Tab's `ShapeParts`, and the one focused (`AccessiblePartFocus`), ⌥Tab's. `editor.accessibleParts(of:)` and `accessibleFocusedPart` read them. Selection's part press selects the shape alone, focuses the part and presses it, as ⌥Tab and Return would.
- `editor.accessibleShapes(limit:)`: the claimed order, then the shapes in view (the paint list's kept extents that meet the view: `forEachShape(in:)`, the one rule `paintOrder(in:)` goes by too), then the selection; with no claim, the selection, then the shapes in view. Past `limit`, those nearest any selected shape (up to `Style.accessibleNearestSelected`, 32, selected; past that the middle of them; with none, the middle of the view) are kept, the last selected first, in the list's order. The selection is always among them, out of view too.

## Announcements

- `editor.announce(_:priority:)` (`AnnouncementPriority`: low, medium, high) says something to whoever uses VoiceOver, through `editorAnnounce`; debug builds keep every one in `editor.announcements`, which tests read. The zoom's label announces where the zoom settled through it (`ZoomIndicatorStyle.announcement`, "Zoom 50%"), the canvas "N selected" (`CanvasUIStyle.selected`) as several shapes become selected, Selection's `RefusalShake` "Locked" or "N shapes locked" when a try is refused on read-only shapes, once while tries of them keep coming (a held key: [selection](selection.md)), and the command bar the step a command run from it makes ("Change Color", "Change Color, on 3 of 5 shapes" where it reached fewer than the bar was opened on: `SlashBarStyle.acted`, [slash commands](slash-commands.md)). The bar is a card, so a popover to VoiceOver, named "Command for Groceries" (one shape as VoiceOver calls it, else "5 shapes"), its field "Command".

## CanvasUI's elements

- `ShapeElements.swift` holds the only NSAccessibility code for the canvas. The canvas is a layout area (`.layoutArea`) named `accessibleBoardName`; its children are `ShapeElement`s (`NSAccessibilityElement`, role `.layoutItem`) for `accessibleShapes(limit: CanvasUIStyle.accessibleShapesLimit)` (500), then the views it hosts (the minimap, the zoom while it shows, a card, a popover; the editing view is under its shape's element). Its selected children are the selected among its children, in the selection's order (never an element for each of a selection of thousands); `accessibilityFocusedUIElement` is the focused part's element while a part of the last selected shape has the keyboard (⌥Tab), else that shape's element, else the canvas (a shape's element isn't focused while its part is); a hit test gives a hosted view, else the shape under the point (`editor.shape(at:)`), else the canvas.
- A `ShapeElement` is kept by shape id (`ShapeElements.elements`) until a commit removes the shape or another board is shown, so it is the same element across pans and commits (past four times the limit, those not shown go). Its label, value, help, frame, selected and focused state are asked of the editor each time AppKit asks, never stored: its frame is the shape's bounds through the camera now (`NSAccessibility.screenRect(fromView:rect:)`), so a pan, a glide or `revealIfOffscreen`'s easing leaves nothing stale. Its press is `selectForAccessibility([id])`; its Show Menu (VoiceOver's VO-Shift-M) selects it unless it's among the selection and pops the selection's menu below the selection (`editor.menuForSelection(omitting: [])` through `editor.popUpMenu`, host API, so the canvas view pops it as it pops any). Its children are its parts' `PartElement`s (`editor.accessibleParts(of:)`, in ⌥Tab's order; while it's edited, what VoiceOver sees of the editing view instead: `ShapeElements.children(of:)`, asked of the editor each time), each kept by the part's id while the shape lists it (`ShapeElements.parts(of:)`): a role from the part's (`AccessiblePart.Role.accessibilityRole`: button, menu button, link, checkbox, handle, and a connection as a link), its title as its label, its rect through the camera as its frame, and a press that is the part's. AppKit's accessibility methods on `NSAccessibilityElement`, and NSView's `accessibilityFocusedUIElement` and `accessibilityHitTest`, are nonisolated: they read the editor in `MainActor.assumeIsolated`.
- The children are worked out again only when the board, `revision`, `layoutRevision`, the camera, the view's size or the selection changed (`ShapeElements.Key`); debug builds count each (`canvas.accessibleChildrenBuilds`, host SPI). A shape's parts are listed once for the same and the shape being edited (`listedParts(of:)`), so every part's role, label, frame and focus read one listing; a part's press lists them afresh.
- Nothing runs until an assistive app asks: AppKit calls these only for one, nothing is computed in `draw`, and the canvas tells an app of changes only once it has asked something (`ShapeElements.isListening`, set by the children, the focus, the selected children and a hit test). Then, while the canvas has the keyboard: `.focusedUIElementChanged` on the focused element when the selection changes, and `.selectedChildrenChanged` with "N selected" when several are, on the next turn of the run loop, once however many changes came (a marquee's drag); `.layoutChanged` at most once a turn, after a settled commit that added a shape in view or removed one VoiceOver was given (`editorShapesDidComeOrGo`, a defaulted delegate hook the core calls after a commit's delivery, never for one that only changed shapes), and when another board is shown. Announcements are posted at once, listening or not. Debug builds keep what was posted (`canvas.accessibilityPosts`, host SPI).
- Editing: as editing begins (and when an app first asks during an edit), the editing view's first responder is labelled with the shape's description when it has no label of its own. While the shape is edited, its element's children are the editing view as AppKit shows any view (`NSAccessibility.unignoredChildren`: the view if it's an element, else every control in it, a typed note's words and its rows' group alike, a text field by its cell), and the editing view's accessibility parent is the element, so every control's way up is the shape; as editing ends the canvas lets go of it (`editingEnded`), and the element's children are its parts again.
- The minimap's value is how much of the board the canvas shows, counted as asked (`MinimapStyle.accessibleValue`: "Showing 12 of 40 shapes").

## Testing

- `CanvasUITests/CanvasAccessibilityTests.swift`, on a probe kind with a label and a description of its own (`Spot`), a selection the test keeps (`commandShapes(_:select:)`), and a second plugin's states: the role and the board's name (the root's, Canvas, a pocket's owner); the description joined; the children in view and the selection first, then hosted views; the cap, and the selected children of a selection past it; one listing of a shape's parts for every question of every part; an element's identity and frame across a pan, the builds counted; the focus following the selection and what's posted (nothing before an app asks, nor while another view has the keyboard); announcements; `.layoutChanged` once per turn; the editing view named, also when first asked mid-edit; every control of an editing view of two fields (`Duo`) under the shape's element, and its parts again once editing ends. `ZoomIndicatorTests` checks the zoom's announcement in `editor.announcements` and the post.
- `CompositionTests/CanvasAccessibilityShippedTests.swift`: Tab walks three titled notes with `KeyWalk`, `expectShows` holding at each stop with the role `AXLayoutItem` and "Checkout, Note"; the board named for its owner, a press selecting, "2 selected"; the children in Tab's reading order (a note put down last, above, first); ⌥Tab moving VoiceOver's focus to the lock chip (`AXButton` "Locked", `expectShows`), then the arrow it is joined by, and Escape back; a note's part elements its chip and its arrow (a link), the chip's press unlocking the note; Show Menu selecting and popping the menu; the descriptions ("Checkout, Note, 1 error, locked" with its words and a mention by name as the value, a frame's and a subboard's counts); Delete held on a locked note announcing "Locked" once. `SelectionTests/RefusalShakeTests.swift` holds the announcement's counts and its quiet. The canvas no longer needs the `pending` VoiceOver gap in walks (SteppingShippedTests dropped it).
- `BenchmarkTests/AccessibilityBenchmarks.swift`: `Budget.accessibleChildren` and `accessibleChildrenAfterCommit` ([benchmarks](benchmarks.md)).
