# Connections: ports, channels and the connector

Connections: ports and their dots, channels, the one connector, connections made by name, and the port table every question about ports reads.

Related: [relationships](relationships.md), [arrows](arrows.md), [mentions](mentions.md), [topology](topology.md), [references](references.md), [selection](selection.md), [neighbors](neighbors.md), [command line](command-line.md) ([every topic](../../CLAUDE.md#map)).

In the guide, `docs/plugin-system.md`: [Ports](../../docs/plugin-system.md#ports).

## Architecture

- Connections (platform, a horizontal and a shape trait, on Draw, Mentions, Selection, TextEditing and Topology: `canvas draw`'s edges, Mentions' catalog for ⌘L's picker, the dots as Selection's canvas buttons, the channels' edge types) holds ports, channels and the connector's interface, in three plugin values (`PortsPlugin`, `PortEdgesPlugin` and `ConnectPickerPlugin`, below), and names no arrow. The connector is Arrows (product, a vertical: [arrows](arrows.md)), the shape a connection is drawn as, on Connections, Colors, Mentions, Neighbors, Palette, Publishing, Selection, TableOfContents, TextEditing, Topology and Types (so an arrow can be an instance of a type, and a relating kind: `arrow.relating()` beside `typeable()` adds `endRule`, and the arrows between a type and a relationship type are its ends: [relationships](relationships.md)), in three plugin values of its own: `ArrowsPlugin` (the kind; an arrow's label is mention text, so Mentions draws, edits and finds it, and a list with arrows installs `MentionsPlugin()` and what it stands on), `ArrowPortsPlugin` (arrows as what a port's drag draws, below, the only code that knows both arrows and channels) and `ArrowLightsPlugin`, id `arrow-lights`, after Neighbors and `ArrowPortsPlugin` in the shipped list, gives Neighbors arrows' two lights, titled Points To and Pointed To By (`ArrowStyle.pointsTo`, `pointedToBy`), what a shape points to in green on a plain hover and what points to it in purple with ⌥ held (its `keys`, said as the key hint "⌥ Hover"), `ArrowStyle.pointsToLight` and `pointedToByLight`, each with the arrows between as its `between` (outlined, never listed), their queries written from `ArrowEdge.id`, which `ArrowPortsPlugin` declares (without it the engine refuses both and nothing lights), and Topology's `StandingEdge.id`; ArrowPortsTests' `arrowLightTests` hold the two queries, their keys and the hint, and its refusal without Neighbors: [neighbors](neighbors.md), so a list can have arrows without ports (ArrowsTests) or ports with a connector of its own (PortsTests). The arrow is tldraw's arc arrow ([arrows](arrows.md)), a `StrokedShape`, saying its line is 3.5 wide so a tinted outline covers it, and its skeleton's outline holds its heads. Ports (data-canvas's) are dots just outside a shape's sides, from which a drag draws a connection (an arrow) that may end only on a shape that takes its channel. A channel is a kind of connection, a type (`Channel`: `static let id: ChannelID`, `static var color` for its dots, computed, gray by default, and `static var summary`, nil by default, its edge type's), declared by the plugin that owns what it means (`registrar.channel(NoteLink.self)`, the `Channels` point; one declaration per id), which declares Topology's edge type of the same name too, under the connector's family (a root without a connector). `ChannelID` is what a connection keeps, so it never changes once shipped, and the id audit holds its literal to the module that declares it. A kind has ports on a channel with `x.ports(C.self, sides:role:)` (`registrar.ports(K.self, on: C.self, sides:role:)` for another plugin's kind; the `ShapePorts` point, one per kind and channel, and a kind may have ports on several), or on every channel there is with `x.portsOnEveryChannel(sides:role:)` (`registrar.portsOnEveryChannel(K.self, …)`: a contribution with no channel, which `KindPorts.init(of:in:)` expands to each channel the registry declares, in plugin order, where it stands in the list, so the kind names none and imports none of the plugins that own them; a channel the kind has ports on earlier keeps those): a dot at the middle of each of `sides` (`PortSide`, all four by default), and a `PortRole`, `.both` (the default), `.source` (dots; nothing ends on it) or `.target` (connections end on it; no dots). `ShapePorts.check` refuses ports on a channel no installed plugin declares, or on the connector's own kind. One plugin draws connections, the connector (`registrar.connects(K.self, edgeType: F.self, canConnect:make:ends:begin:)`, the `Connectors` point, at most one): `F` is the family of edges connections are (an `EdgeType` it declares; with `ends`, a connection bound at both ends is one edge, of its channel's type, else `F`'s, and `F`'s too for a channel that names a declared type that isn't a channel's; a free end, none), `K` opts into `ChannelProperty` (key `channel`, nil by default, so only a connection drawn on a channel has one; one `canvas draw` makes with `channel: none` has none), `canConnect` says which shapes one may start from and end on in a `ShapeContext` (the editor's for the dots, a transaction's for a connection made by name: `registry.canConnect(_:in:)`), `begin` gets the `Connection` (`shape`, `side`, the dot's `channels` in the port table's order, `origin`, where it was pressed, world units) and returns the session the press hands the drag to (nil: nothing), and the optional `make` and `ends` connect shapes without a drag: `registry.connection(_:existing:in:)` makes a `ConnectionRequest` (ends, optional sides, channel, both heads, and `beside`: how many connections already join the pair, nil to count, which bends a new arrow aimed at both centers apart from them) into a connection, not inserted, or changes `existing` (only named sides and the heads; `existing` itself when nothing would change, so `==` tells unchanged), and `registry.connectionEnds(of:)` is which shapes one joins. Without a connector no shape shows ports. Every question about ports reads one port table per kind (`KindPorts`, PortTable.swift: plugin order, a kind's first contribution on a channel its only one there), the registry's lookups too (`connectorKind`, `makesConnections`, `dotChannels(of:side:)`, `takes(_:_:as:side:)`, `connectionChannel(from:fromSide:to:toSide:)`, …), so a drag and a connection made by name pick channels alike (an edge `canvas draw` draws with no channel written is on `connectionChannel`'s, as a drag's is on `editor.channel(of:endingAt:)`'s). Each lookup that asks about a kind takes `standingFor:` too (the kinds of what a stand-in of it stands for, none by default; `hasPorts`, `portChannels(of:)`, `dotChannels`, `takes`, `portSides(of:standingFor:on:as:)`, `connectionChannel(from:fromSide:fromStandingFor:to:toSide:toStandingFor:)`, and `portSides(of:standingFor:)`, the sides it takes connections on at all): it answers as `PortState.ports(of:)` does for the stand-in (`KindPorts.standing(for:)`: its kind's ports as far as every kind stood for takes them, on its own sides), for a connection made by name to a stand-in `canvas draw` is about to put down ([command line](command-line.md)), which no editor holds yet. `editor.channel(of:endingAt:)` is the first of a connection's channels a shape takes as a target, by `PortState.ports(of:)` (its kind's, but for a stand-in, below; the shape it starts from asked as any other, below; nil for one kept aside, and one that holds it or that it holds: `mayConnect`, below); `registry.accepts(shape, on:as:)` and `registry.declares(_:)` are what rules for connections already drawn go by. The dots are Selection's canvas buttons, dragged (`PortDot`, from `PortState.dot(atViewPoint:)`), shown for one shape, the host: the one whose dot the pointer is on (any shape's, so a dot reached straight from empty canvas shows its shape's, as data-canvas's halo does), else the shape with dots under the pointer, kept while the pointer stays within reach of its dots (its view bounds grown by 24 points, but deeper inside them than 24 only where the shape is under the pointer: `PortState.isNear`, so a hollow shape's, a frame's that holds shapes, show near its edge and go as the pointer goes in among what it holds) over empty canvas or a shape without ports; none while a kind's own handle is under the pointer (it takes the hover; a resize handle leaves them, since a side is one along its whole length and the pointer crosses it on the way out to a dot), and none once the tool changes or the shape is deleted. A shape has dots when its kind has a side that isn't `.target`, it isn't kept aside, and `canConnect` allows it. They show in the select tool only, on a board that takes shapes (`PortState.isShowing` reads `editor.isBoardReadOnly`: a connection is a shape, so a locked subboard's board, and every board under it, has no dots, none is hovered or pressed, and a press by where one would be is the canvas's or the shape's; one the pointer was on as a lock landed above goes with the pointer's next move, and `beginArrow` returns nil there, for a drag from a dot that began just before the lock landed, since the arrow's insert would be dropped), at 25% zoom and up (`PortStyle.minZoom`), while the pointer's over the canvas (`editor.pointerOverCanvas`) and, where it is now (`editor.pointerWorldLocation`), within reach of them, and nothing is under way but a dot's press: each 12 view points out from the middle of its side of the shape's bounds, a disc 8 points across in its first channel's color at 60% in a white 1.5-point ring, and hovered or pressed 10 points across at full color in a halo 24 points across at 24% (`PortStyle`'s radii: 4, 5, 12); constant on screen, following the host's drawing offset (a locked shape's shake). A dot shows, and is hit, only while no selectable shape painted above its own covers its center (an arrow leaving the shape there, a neighbor on top; `PortState.liveDots(of:)`), so a shape lying on a bigger one shows its dots over it. The nearest showing dot within 12 view points is pressed (none where the select tool hasn't seen the pointer move, just after a tool change or a first click into the window, since none shows there), but where the point is on a container (the core's `registry.isContainer`: a frame's edge, or its tab) only on its disc (within `PortStyle.litRadius`), so a frame's edge moves and resizes it right up to its own dots and those of what it holds near it; the nearest dot within 12 still keeps its shape's dots showing there, so they stay as the pointer crosses the edge to one (frames' first follow-up, built with frame ports); past the drag threshold the connector draws, and while it does each shape in view the connection could end on is outlined faintly, as a hover (along the core's `editor.outlinePath(of:)`, so on the line a hover's is, stood off the edge; the one it would end on now gets the hint). `PortsPlugin` comes after `AdornmentsPlugin` in the shipped list, so a dot is found before a lock chip (a clickable adornment) and draws above it. Locked shapes keep their ports: connecting doesn't change them. **Ports at a stand-in** are a deliberate departure from ports being per kind. A stand-in kind (Topology's `standInKinds`: a reference) has ports on every channel statically (`portsOnEveryChannel(sides: [.left, .right])`, so `registry.accepts` is true of it on every channel, an arrow translated onto one keeps its binding, and `ArrowDefinition`'s translate filter, which keeps only a binding that already exists, needs nothing more), but every question about one shape (its dots, a drag from one, `editor.channel(of:endingAt:)`, the faint outlines while a connection is drawn) goes through `PortState.ports(of: record)`, which for a stand-in answers from the kinds it stands for (`topology.standingKinds(of:)`, each once, the first shape's first, however many shapes it stands for): one kind stood for, that kind's channels and roles, in its order, on the stand-in's own sides; several, the channels every kind takes with the roles all allow, in the order of the first (`KindPorts.standing(for:)`: a pill for a subboard has the subboard's channels, `subboard-link` first, so its dots are indigo and a drag from it to a subboard is a `subboard-link`, where its own order, the declaration's, would put `note-link` first; a `.source` and a `.target` role share none); none (empty, pending, missing, private, unavailable), nil: no dots, and no port drag ends there (an arrow on no channel still binds, as it binds anywhere). It is kept for each stand-in until `topology.revision(of:)` changes, and dropped with the shape (`forgetStandingPorts`: a commit's removed shapes, and what it names on boards it took or changed, `elsewhere`, a kept one of which is worked out again when next asked; every one for a board or file put in place; nothing for a move, and nothing at all while none is kept), so a pointer move costs a dictionary hit and a session that deletes stand-ins keeps none of theirs (`editor.standingPortsKept`, debug builds). A re-aimed arrow end is judged the same way: `ArrowTargeting.filter` adds `editor.portsAllow(_:as:at:)` (Connections' public face of `PortState.allows`, which takes the end's role, the start a source and the end a target, so the port side names no arrow) to the plugins' rules and `also` (a stand-in only; an arrow on no channel, or one nobody declares, binds anywhere; the arrow's channel must be taken now in that end's role), since the static rule would let any channel bind. Arrows keep their bindings and channels when a stand-in is re-pointed (tldraw's never let go), so an attached arrow's channel may stop being taken: that is References' to show (`ReferenceState.channelMismatch(of:)`, from the literal edges at the pill and `registry.accepts`; the pill's trailing warning and a panel line, `“Order” doesn’t take note links.`, with `ChannelID.pluralName`), never a Validation rule (Connections has no Validation, and a rule may not read a dynamic reference's provided answers). `ArrowPortsPlugin` makes arrows the connector (its family `ArrowEdge`, `arrow`, its id made from `Arrow.kind`; `canConnect` is `shapes.canBindArrow(to:)`, `begin` is `editor.beginArrow(from:side:at:canBind:complete:)`, internal to Arrows, `canBind` a shape that takes one of the port's channels (`editor.channel(of:endingAt:)`), and `complete` sets the arrow's `ChannelProperty` and bends a second arrow of a pair apart from the first when both its ends are imprecise ([arrows](arrows.md): `ArrowPairs.swift`, `pairBend`, `parallels`); `make` binds both ends, an unnamed side at the center (imprecise) and a named one at its middle (precise, not exact), its channel set before `registry.arrowMayBind` asks the rules), and its `arrowBindingRule` keeps every arrow's ends off a container and what it holds (`mayConnect`, over the query's `shapes`, whatever its channel), and an arrow with a channel some installed plugin declares to shapes that take it at that end (the start as `.source`, the end as `.target`), re-aimed or moved; one without (`channel: none`'s), or with an undeclared channel, binds wherever an arrow can but there. Shipped: notes have ports on `NoteLink` (`note-link`, a brown amber) on all four sides (`NoteLinks.swift`; the top dot is in the row above the shape where a titled shape's pill is, so a title long enough to reach the middle of the top edge runs under it: the dot draws above the pill and is pressed before it, as before a lock chip), and subboards on `SubboardLink` (`subboard-link`, the card's indigo) and then on every channel there is, on all four sides (`SubboardLinks.swift`: `ports(SubboardLink.self)`, then `portsOnEveryChannel()`; its own channel is first, so its dots are indigo and a drag from one is drawn on the first channel the shape it ends on takes: `subboard-link` to a subboard, `note-link` to a note), each channel declared by its kind's plugin (Subboards' after its shape block), so a note links to a note or a subboard, and a subboard to either. An arrow keeps its channel, so a `note-link` between a note and a subboard is re-aimed onto notes and subboards only, and a `subboard-link` onto subboards only. Frames have ports on `FrameLink` (`frame-link`, the frame's gray), declared by Frames after its shape block, on their right, bottom and left (`FrameLinks.swift`: the top edge is the title's tab, which grabs the frame; the arrow leaves by the side that faces where it goes whatever dot it was drawn from, since it starts at the frame's middle), so a frame links to a frame or a subboard, and a subboard to a frame on `frame-link`; a note and a frame share no channel (in `canvas draw`, an edge between them says `channel: none`). **A connection never joins a container and what it holds**, at any depth, either way round (`ShapeContext.mayConnect`, Connections', over the core's `nests`: a frame and a note in it, or a frame inside it): drawn, it would run from inside the one (an arrow aims its end at a shape that holds its other end at a point inside it, tldraw's nested-shape geometry), and the containment says already what it would. A port's drag ends on neither and outlines neither (`channel(of:endingAt:)`), `ArrowPortsPlugin`'s rule keeps a re-aimed or moved end off it and refuses a connection made by name (Connect shakes both; `canvas draw` says `cannot_connect`, `"Sprint 3" can't be connected to "Fix the login", which it holds`, checked before the channel, so `channel: none` too: `EdgeChannels.connection`). An arrow that comes to join them by a move (a note dropped into the frame it's connected to) stays, drawn as tldraw draws it, until it's moved alone, when both its ends let go where it's drawn (the rule, asked of each end as it moves).
- Connect (`Connect.swift`, ⌘L, `CanvasCommand.connect`, titled "Connect", declared by `PortsPlugin` with its key, `runs: .betweenPresses`, in `ConnectSection`, Insert's section after the shapes': the menu bar item is how the key reaches a note being edited, where text has the keyboard and only the menu bar's keys are answered): for two shapes, a connection from the one selected first to the other, at once; for one, the picker below when `ConnectPickerPlugin` is installed, in a note's words ending editing first (its words kept), else the loop at once (the drag from its dot back onto it, by keys: arrows draw a loop on its right side), and then, as before the picker, never while a shape is edited. Everything is made by one writer, `editor.connect(_ from: ConnectEnd, to: ConnectEnd, plan: ConnectPlan? = nil, fulfil: Bool = false)` (`editor.connect(_ ids:)` is the two-or-one shapes' entry to it), with `registry.drawConnection(from:to:in:)`, the `DrawEdges` maker `canvas draw` makes an edge with (its channel, a second of a pair bent apart), inserted in one `transactWhole(plan.step)` ("Connect" for none) inside an undo group, the selection kept. A stand-in end (a reference pill) is read as what it stands for, as a drag reads its ports (`PortState.standingKinds(of:)` into `drawConnection`'s `standingFor:`: a pill for a subboard joined to a subboard is a `subboard-link`), and one that stands for nothing now (empty, pending, missing, private) is no end. **A target on another board** (not in the document shown) is reached as a script's edge reaches one and as a relationship row does: `FarVerdicts` (Draw) judges it, and `StandInRoute.placePill(standingFor:label:beside:edgeIs:sharing: .any, verdicts:in:)` finds the pill of the kind that stands for it in the source's container or makes one, seated and landed beside the source where a draw run lands it, claimed (`PlacedPill.claim`: the far shape made what the kind needs, asked for its admission again as the step leaves it) and put (`PlacedPill.put`) in the same transaction, the arrow's end the pill (read as what it stands for). A far shape that must be made public first (`FarVerdict.fulfils`) is connected only with `fulfil: true`, which a person's pick passes and nothing a script runs does. A pair the maker refuses (no channel both take, a note and a frame; one holding the other, a frame and a shape in it; a glossy fixture tile), such a pill, a far shape that cannot be reached, `plan.prepare` throwing, or an insert the core refuses (a board that takes nothing, a locked subboard's) changes nothing: no step, and the shapes shake (a pill before any transaction, with `refuseChanges`; the rest given up whole, `shaking:`). A locked shape is an end like any other, as its ports are (locking changes nothing it is bound to). It knows no arrow and no kind. `ConnectMenuPlugin` (id `connect-menu`, shipped after `PortsPlugin`, so after `ContextMenusPlugin`) adds Connect to a right-click on two shapes (`MenuItem(command: .connect, when:)`) and "Connect To…" on one (the picker's item, shown while the picker is installed and the shape can be connected to something), each with ⌘L; a plugin value of its own so a list with ports and no context menus (PortsTests, PortEdgesTests) keeps the key and has no item. Connections imports ContextMenus and Keyboard for these. Once the connection is committed, inside the same step (a group around the commit and the hooks), `editor.connect` tells every `ConnectionMade` hook (an extension point of Connections', owner `.ports`, `registrar.connectionMade { connection, editor in … }`, naming no kind) with the connection, as a port's release does: Arrows' `ArrowPortsPlugin` fills it with `connectionDrawn`, so a relationship type that fits is taken, or the type list pops at the arrow (selected while it is open, first row highlighted; Escape leaves a plain arrow), and the keyboard and the pointer give one document. Computed after the commit, as the drag's menu needs the arrow's rect.
- **⌘L's picker** (`ConnectPicker.swift`, `ConnectPickerHost.swift`, `ConnectPlan.swift`; `ConnectPickerPlugin`, id `connect-picker`, after `PortsPlugin` and `MentionsPlugin` in the shipped list, a plugin value of its own so a list without it keeps ⌘L's loop and PortsTests and PortEdgesTests do not change): one shape selected and ⌘L opens `editor.connectPicker` (`ConnectPickerHost`, a service), a core card about no shape's content (`showsZoomedOut: true`, as the command bar is) hung under the shape out to its outline (brought into view first; following the shape when a commit moves it), holding a one-line `EditingTextView` (`ConnectPickerTextView`, "Connect to", with a faint `→` lead) and a `CaretListWindow` under it, as Jump To and References show theirs. The model, `ConnectPicker`, is headless like `RelationPicker`: each keystroke asks `mentions.candidates(matching:in: AnyMentionable.self, for: source, kinds:, listingUnnamed: true, admitting:)`, so the ranking, recency, nearness and places are the `@` picker's, over **the kinds the port table can join the source to** (`ConnectPicker.reach(of:in:)`: `registry.connectionChannel(from:fromStandingFor:to:)` for each kind but the connector's and the stand-ins', or a plain connection where neither has ports; the source must be one the connector can connect, `registry.canConnect`; a pill source is read as what it stands for), and `admitting` leaves out the source itself, a pick its kind does not admit (`ConnectPicks`), a shape it holds or is held by (`mayConnect`) and a far shape that cannot be reached (`FarVerdict.cannot`); the verdicts and the rule are asked once a commit (`editor.revision`). Lines are `MentionChoice.items` (the name, and where it is: "Note · Plans" when two are called alike, nothing for the board shown), a far shape a pick makes public with `· will be made public` after its place and in what VoiceOver says; the first is highlighted, **Itself** is always last while anything is listed or nothing is typed (the connector can join the kind to itself and the kind admits it), and picking it is the loop; a typed word that finds nothing leaves the list empty (`No matches`), so a Return there makes no loop. Return and Tab pick, ↑ ↓ ⌃P ⌃N and the page keys move the highlight (↑ from the first wraps to Itself), ⇧Tab moves it up, Escape and ⌘. close it and the canvas has the keyboard with the selection as it was, a click elsewhere, another card, an undo, another board and the selection changing close it (the card's reasons); keys it has no use for type or change nothing. A pick closes it first (the canvas has the keyboard, so the release a connection offers is the canvas's), then asks `ConnectPicks` what it means and makes it through `editor.connect(plan.from, to: plan.to, plan:, fulfil: true)`, and VoiceOver hears the plan's step; a kind whose plan is nil for it refuses the pick (the source shakes, no step). **`ConnectPicks`** (a `KeyedExtensionPoint`, owner `.ports`, one per source kind: `x.connectPicks(admitting:_:)` on a kind's own registrar, `registrar.connectPicks(K.self, admitting:_:)` for another plugin's) is what a kind means by a pick: `admitting { ConnectPick, editor in Bool }` says which picks are listed and `plan { ConnectPick, editor in ConnectPlan? }` what one is, a `ConnectPlan` (`step`, "Connect" by default; `from` and `to`, `ConnectEnd`s, a shape for now; and an optional `prepare` that writes first, inside the step, and returns the ends the arrow then joins, throwing `Transaction.Refused` to refuse the pick). No shipped kind contributes one. The test fixture is CompositionTests' `ConnectPickerShippedTests`.
- **A port's drag may end where it starts**: `channel(of:endingAt:)` asks the shape it starts from as any other (a kind whose ports there only start connections, `.source`, takes none, there as anywhere), so the faint outlines show it as the drag begins and the hint lights it as the pointer comes back over it. Let go there, the drag joins the shape to itself, which the connector draws as it says: Arrows, a loop on the side of the dot pulled ([arrows](arrows.md), `ArrowLoops.swift`), shown as soon as the end is back on the shape; and the release says what it is (`ArrowConnecting.finished`): Types makes a type's loop a relationship type from it to itself ([types](types.md#the-release-of-a-ports-drag)), and a note's takes what fits as a drag between two notes does. Letting go there no longer abandons a drag: Escape does, and so does a release off every shape. Connect (⌘L) on one shape and `canvas draw`'s `edge "A" -> "A"` (on the channel it dots and takes, `EdgeChannels`, as any pair) make the same through the connector's maker (on one side: a script's `from:` and `to:` naming two is `invalid_value`). PortsTests' `eligibilityTests` (where it starts, on its channel, outlined; a `.source` box taking none) and ArrowPortsTests' `arrowLoopTests` (the loop on release, shown mid-drag and let go of on the way to another shape, the same however it arrives, below from a bottom dot, nesting and the slot a delete frees, Escape and a free release, a disc on its own channel) hold it, and CompositionTests' `loopWindowTests` the same on plain notes through the app's canvas view, with the mouse events its window hands on (from each dot out and back, straight in slowly or held past the precise timer, a selected note with a title and several lines, and a release back on the dot, off the note, which draws nothing).
- `PortEdgesPlugin` (`PortEdges.swift`, Connections' own, filling Draw's `DrawEdges`; it names no arrow, only the connector's kind, `registry.connectorKind`) makes `canvas draw`'s edges the connector's connections, on the channel both ends take (`EdgeChannels`, built on the port table and `canConnect`), and a list without Draw leaves it out ([command line](command-line.md)). It names no identity key: what tells two arrows of one pair apart is the arrow's `is`, which Types names (`registrar.drawEdgeIdentity(IsAProperty.key)` in `TypeNamesPlugin`, Draw's `DrawEdgeIdentities`), so two arrows of one pair are two edges to `canvas draw`, told apart by the type each is of, `edge "A" -> "B"` over two is `ambiguous_edge`, and Connections imports no Types.
- An arrow's row in the table of contents (Arrows' `ArrowRow.swift`) is under the shape it starts from, and, where it starts at a stand-in (Topology's `standsIn`: a reference) that stands for exactly one shape its record names on the stand-in's board (`forwarding(of:) == .record([a])`, both `end`s live on one board), under that shape, where the arrow reads as starting from; else under the stand-in. A stand-in's re-pointing, or what it stands for going private or deleted, moves such a row with no change to the arrow, so `ArrowRowNesting` (an `ArrowsPlugin` service; it follows `topology.follow`, and works out nothing on a switch of board) tells the list which arrows' rows to make again (`editor.contentsDidChange(for:)`): the arrows that start, as drawn (the literal view), at a stand-in the change touched. It knows no reference, only stand-ins; CompositionTests' `referencesShippedTests` hold it.
- The drag that draws or re-aims an arrow (Arrows' `ArrowHandleDragSession`, a `Session`; [arrows](arrows.md), [editor](editor.md)) answers `Session.touching` with its arrow, from the press to the release: a service that reads arrows (the relationship index, Types) leaves it out while it is still bound and unbound at each hover, and reads it once the drag ends.
