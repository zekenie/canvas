# Connections: ports, channels and the connector

Connections: ports and their dots, channels, the one connector, connections made by name, and the port table every question about ports reads.

Related: [relationships](relationships.md), [arrows](arrows.md), [mentions](mentions.md), [topology](topology.md), [references](references.md), [selection](selection.md), [neighbors](neighbors.md), [command line](command-line.md) ([every topic](../../CLAUDE.md#map)).

In the guide, `docs/plugin-system.md`: [Ports](../../docs/plugin-system.md#ports).

## Architecture

- Connections (a concern, on Draw, Mentions, Neighbors, Publishing, Selection, TableOfContents, TextEditing, Topology and Types, so an arrow can be an instance of a type, and a relating kind: `arrow.relating()` beside `typeable()` adds `endRule`, and the arrows between a type and a relationship type are its ends: [relationships](relationships.md)) holds ports, channels and the arrow, in three plugin values (`PortsPlugin`, `ArrowsPlugin` and `ArrowPortsPlugin`; an arrow's label is mention text, so Mentions draws, edits and finds it: [arrows](arrows.md), and a list with arrows installs `MentionsPlugin()` and what it stands on), and a fourth, `ArrowLightsPlugin`, id `arrow-lights`, after Neighbors and `ArrowPortsPlugin` in the shipped list, gives Neighbors arrows' two lights, titled Points To and Pointed To By (`ArrowStyle.pointsTo`, `pointedToBy`), what a shape points to in green and what points to it in purple, `ArrowStyle.pointsToLight` and `pointedToByLight`, each with the arrows between as its `between` (outlined, never listed), their queries written from `ArrowEdge.id`, which `ArrowPortsPlugin` declares (without it the engine refuses both and nothing lights), and Topology's `StandingEdge.id`; ArrowPortsTests' `arrowLightTests` hold the two queries and its refusal without Neighbors: [neighbors](neighbors.md), so a list can have arrows without ports (ArrowsTests) or ports with a connector of its own (PortsTests); it owns the arrow, the shape a connection is drawn as (tldraw's arc arrows: [arrows](arrows.md); a `StrokedShape`, saying its line is 3.5 wide so a tinted outline covers it, and its skeleton's outline holds its heads). Ports (data-canvas's) are dots just outside a shape's sides, from which a drag draws a connection (an arrow) that may end only on a shape that takes its channel. A channel is a kind of connection, a type (`Channel`: `static let id: ChannelID`, `static var color` for its dots, computed, gray by default, and `static var summary`, nil by default, its edge type's), declared by the plugin that owns what it means (`registrar.channel(NoteLink.self)`, the `Channels` point; one declaration per id), which declares Topology's edge type of the same name too, under the connector's family (a root without a connector). `ChannelID` is what a connection keeps, so it never changes once shipped, and the id audit holds its literal to the module that declares it. A kind has ports on a channel with `x.ports(C.self, sides:role:)` (`registrar.ports(K.self, on: C.self, sides:role:)` for another plugin's kind; the `ShapePorts` point, one per kind and channel, and a kind may have ports on several), or on every channel there is with `x.portsOnEveryChannel(sides:role:)` (`registrar.portsOnEveryChannel(K.self, …)`: a contribution with no channel, which `KindPorts.init(of:in:)` expands to each channel the registry declares, in plugin order, where it stands in the list, so the kind names none and imports none of the plugins that own them; a channel the kind has ports on earlier keeps those): a dot at the middle of each of `sides` (`PortSide`, all four by default), and a `PortRole`, `.both` (the default), `.source` (dots; nothing ends on it) or `.target` (connections end on it; no dots). `ShapePorts.check` refuses ports on a channel no installed plugin declares, or on the connector's own kind. One plugin draws connections, the connector (`registrar.connects(K.self, edgeType: F.self, canConnect:make:ends:begin:)`, the `Connectors` point, at most one): `F` is the family of edges connections are (an `EdgeType` it declares; with `ends`, a connection bound at both ends is one edge, of its channel's type, else `F`'s, and `F`'s too for a channel that names a declared type that isn't a channel's; a free end, none), `K` opts into `ChannelProperty` (key `channel`, nil by default, so only a connection drawn on a channel has one; one `canvas draw` makes with `channel: none` has none), `canConnect` says which shapes one may start from and end on in a `ShapeContext` (the editor's for the dots, a transaction's for a connection made by name: `registry.canConnect(_:in:)`), `begin` gets the `Connection` (`shape`, `side`, the dot's `channels` in the port table's order, `origin`, where it was pressed, world units) and returns the session the press hands the drag to (nil: nothing), and the optional `make` and `ends` connect shapes without a drag: `registry.connection(_:existing:in:)` makes a `ConnectionRequest` (ends, optional sides, channel, both heads, and `beside`: how many connections already join the pair, nil to count, which bends a new arrow aimed at both centers apart from them) into a connection, not inserted, or changes `existing` (only named sides and the heads; `existing` itself when nothing would change, so `==` tells unchanged), and `registry.connectionEnds(of:)` is which shapes one joins. Without a connector no shape shows ports. Every question about ports reads one port table per kind (`KindPorts`, PortTable.swift: plugin order, a kind's first contribution on a channel its only one there), the registry's lookups too (`connectorKind`, `makesConnections`, `dotChannels(of:side:)`, `takes(_:_:as:side:)`, `connectionChannel(from:fromSide:to:toSide:)`, …), so a drag and a connection made by name pick channels alike (an edge `canvas draw` draws with no channel written is on `connectionChannel`'s, as a drag's is on `editor.channel(of:endingAt:)`'s). Each lookup that asks about a kind takes `standingFor:` too (the kinds of what a stand-in of it stands for, none by default; `hasPorts`, `portChannels(of:)`, `dotChannels`, `takes`, `portSides(of:standingFor:on:as:)`, `connectionChannel(from:fromSide:fromStandingFor:to:toSide:toStandingFor:)`, and `portSides(of:standingFor:)`, the sides it takes connections on at all): it answers as `PortState.ports(of:)` does for the stand-in (`KindPorts.standing(for:)`: its kind's ports as far as every kind stood for takes them, on its own sides), for a connection made by name to a stand-in `canvas draw` is about to put down ([command line](command-line.md)), which no editor holds yet. `editor.channel(of:endingAt:)` is the first of a connection's channels a shape takes as a target, by `PortState.ports(of:)` (its kind's, but for a stand-in, below; nil for the shape it starts from, or one kept aside); `registry.accepts(shape, on:as:)` and `registry.declares(_:)` are what rules for connections already drawn go by. The dots are Selection's canvas buttons, dragged (`PortDot`, from `PortState.dot(atViewPoint:)`), shown for one shape, the host: the one whose dot the pointer is on (any shape's, so a dot reached straight from empty canvas shows its shape's, as data-canvas's halo does), else the shape with dots under the pointer, kept while the pointer stays within reach of its dots (its view bounds grown by 24 points) over empty canvas or a shape without ports; none while a handle is under the pointer (it takes the hover), and none once the tool changes or the shape is deleted. A shape has dots when its kind has a side that isn't `.target`, it isn't kept aside, and `canConnect` allows it. They show in the select tool only, on a board that takes shapes (`PortState.isShowing` reads `editor.isBoardReadOnly`: a connection is a shape, so a locked subboard's board, and every board under it, has no dots, none is hovered or pressed, and a press by where one would be is the canvas's or the shape's; one the pointer was on as a lock landed above goes with the pointer's next move, and `beginArrow` returns nil there, for a drag from a dot that began just before the lock landed, since the arrow's insert would be dropped), at 25% zoom and up (`PortStyle.minZoom`), while the pointer's over the canvas (`editor.pointerOverCanvas`) and, where it is now (`editor.pointerWorldLocation`), within reach of them, and nothing is under way but a dot's press: each 12 view points out from the middle of its side of the shape's bounds, a disc 8 points across in its first channel's color at 60% in a white 1.5-point ring, and hovered or pressed 10 points across at full color in a halo 24 points across at 24% (`PortStyle`'s radii: 4, 5, 12); constant on screen, following the host's drawing offset (a locked shape's shake). A dot shows, and is hit, only while no selectable shape painted above its own covers its center (an arrow leaving the shape there, a neighbor on top; `PortState.liveDots(of:)`), so a shape lying on a bigger one shows its dots over it. The nearest showing dot within 12 view points is pressed (none where the select tool hasn't seen the pointer move, just after a tool change or a first click into the window, since none shows there); past the drag threshold the connector draws, and while it does each shape in view the connection could end on is outlined faintly, as a hover (along the core's `editor.outlinePath(of:)`, so on the line a hover's is, stood off the edge; the one it would end on now gets the hint). `PortsPlugin` comes after `AdornmentsPlugin` in the shipped list, so a dot is found before a lock chip (a clickable adornment) and draws above it. Locked shapes keep their ports: connecting doesn't change them. **Ports at a stand-in** are a deliberate departure from ports being per kind. A stand-in kind (Topology's `standInKinds`: a reference) has ports on every channel statically (`portsOnEveryChannel(sides: [.left, .right])`, so `registry.accepts` is true of it on every channel, an arrow translated onto one keeps its binding, and `ArrowDefinition`'s translate filter, which keeps only a binding that already exists, needs nothing more), but every question about one shape (its dots, a drag from one, `editor.channel(of:endingAt:)`, the faint outlines while a connection is drawn) goes through `PortState.ports(of: record)`, which for a stand-in answers from `topology.forwards(of:)`: one kind stood for, that kind's channels and roles, in its order, on the stand-in's own sides; several, the channels every kind takes with the roles all allow, in the order of the first (`KindPorts.standing(for:)`: a pill for a subboard has the subboard's channels, `subboard-link` first, so its dots are indigo and a drag from it to a subboard is a `subboard-link`, where its own order, the declaration's, would put `note-link` first; a `.source` and a `.target` role share none); none (empty, pending, missing, private, unavailable), nil: no dots, and no port drag ends there (an arrow on no channel still binds, as it binds anywhere). It is kept for each stand-in until `topology.revision(of:)` changes, and dropped with the shape (`forgetStandingPorts`: a commit's removed shapes, and what it names on boards it took or changed, `elsewhere`, a kept one of which is worked out again when next asked; every one for a board or file put in place; nothing for a move, and nothing at all while none is kept), so a pointer move costs a dictionary hit and a session that deletes stand-ins keeps none of theirs (`editor.standingPortsKept`, debug builds). A re-aimed arrow end is judged the same way: `ArrowTargeting.filter` adds `PortState.allows(_:of:at:)` to the plugins' rules and `also` (a stand-in only; an arrow on no channel, or one nobody declares, binds anywhere; the arrow's channel must be taken now in that end's role), since the static rule would let any channel bind. Arrows keep their bindings and channels when a stand-in is re-pointed (tldraw's never let go), so an attached arrow's channel may stop being taken: that is References' to show (`ReferenceState.channelMismatch(of:)`, from the literal edges at the pill and `registry.accepts`; the pill's trailing warning and a panel line, `“Order” doesn’t take note links.`, with `ChannelID.pluralName`), never a Validation rule (Connections has no Validation, and a rule may not read a dynamic reference's provided answers). `ArrowPortsPlugin` makes arrows the connector (its family `ArrowEdge`, `arrow`, its id made from `Arrow.kind`; `canConnect` is `shapes.canBindArrow(to:)`, `begin` is `editor.beginArrow(from:at:canBind:complete:)`, internal to Connections, `canBind` a shape that takes one of the port's channels (`editor.channel(of:endingAt:)`), and `complete` sets the arrow's `ChannelProperty` and bends a second arrow of a pair apart from the first when both its ends are imprecise ([arrows](arrows.md): `ArrowPairs.swift`, `pairBend`, `parallels`); `make` binds both ends, an unnamed side at the center (imprecise) and a named one at its middle (precise, not exact), its channel set before `registry.arrowMayBind` asks the rules), and its `arrowBindingRule` keeps an arrow with a channel some installed plugin declares to shapes that take it at that end (the start as `.source`, the end as `.target`), re-aimed or moved; one without (`channel: none`'s), or with an undeclared channel, binds wherever an arrow can. Shipped: notes have ports on `NoteLink` (`note-link`, a brown amber) on all four sides (`NoteLinks.swift`; the top dot is in the row above the shape where a titled shape's pill is, so a title long enough to reach the middle of the top edge runs under it: the dot draws above the pill and is pressed before it, as before a lock chip), and subboards on `SubboardLink` (`subboard-link`, the card's indigo) and then on every channel there is, on all four sides (`SubboardLinks.swift`: `ports(SubboardLink.self)`, then `portsOnEveryChannel()`; its own channel is first, so its dots are indigo and a drag from one is drawn on the first channel the shape it ends on takes: `subboard-link` to a subboard, `note-link` to a note), each channel declared by its kind's plugin (Subboards' after its shape block), so a note links to a note or a subboard, and a subboard to either. An arrow keeps its channel, so a `note-link` between a note and a subboard is re-aimed onto notes and subboards only, and a `subboard-link` onto subboards only. Frames have no ports (in `canvas draw`, an edge between a frame and a note or a subboard says `channel: none`).
- `PortEdgesPlugin` (`PortEdges.swift`, Connections' own, filling Draw's `DrawEdges`) makes `canvas draw`'s edges the connector's connections, on the channel both ends take (`EdgeChannels`, built on the port table and `canConnect`), and a list without Draw leaves it out ([command line](command-line.md)). It names `is` (`IsAProperty`) as the arrow's identity key (`DrawEdging.identity`), so two arrows of one pair are two edges to `canvas draw`, told apart by the type each is of, and `edge "A" -> "B"` over two is `ambiguous_edge`.
- An arrow's row in the table of contents (`ArrowRow.swift`) is under the shape it starts from, and, where it starts at a stand-in (Topology's `standsIn`: a reference) that stands for exactly one shape its record names on the stand-in's board (`forwarding(of:) == .record([a])`, both `end`s live on one board), under that shape, where the arrow reads as starting from; else under the stand-in. A stand-in's re-pointing, or what it stands for going private or deleted, moves such a row with no change to the arrow, so `ArrowRowNesting` (an `ArrowsPlugin` service; it follows `topology.follow`, and works out nothing on a switch of board) tells the list which arrows' rows to make again (`editor.contentsDidChange(for:)`): the arrows that start, as drawn (the literal view), at a stand-in the change touched. It knows no reference, only stand-ins; CompositionTests' `referencesShippedTests` hold it.
- The drag that draws or re-aims an arrow (`ArrowHandleDragSession`, a `Session`; [arrows](arrows.md), [editor](editor.md)) answers `Session.touching` with its arrow, from the press to the release: a service that reads arrows (the relationship index, Types) leaves it out while it is still bound and unbound at each hover, and reads it once the drag ends. ArrowPortsTests' `arrowTouchingTests` hold it (a port's drag, a re-aim and a bend, the release's commits, a free release and Escape, each hearing the session name the arrow). Every way the session ends tells Types the arrow is no longer in flight (`editor.connectionReleased(arrowID)`: `end` on a re-aim, a free release's delete, `cancel`); a bound release calls `ArrowConnecting.finished` instead, after the transaction that completed the arrow and before the press ends, which `ArrowPortsPlugin` fills with `{ editor.connectionDrawn($0) }`: Types reads what the arrow is (an end of a relationship type, a relationship between two notes, a relationship type to be made between two types) and gives it its type in the press's step, then counts it. The arrow code learns no kind and no type; ArrowPortsTests' `releaseHookTests` hold it with real arrows and a real press (`Add Arrow` for the one type that fits, `New Relationship Type` between two types, the arrow named by `touching` held through a pass, an Escape and a free release).
- A relationship's row in the table of contents is titled by its relationship type, in the type's color (`ArrowRow.swift`; the label stays on the arrow): the first marked type in its `is` list, by its live title, else the arrow's own row. `ArrowTypeRows` (an `ArrowPortsPlugin` service, registered after Types, whose `onChange` it hears; it works out nothing on a switch of board) tells the list which rows to make again (`editor.contentsDidChange(for:)`): the arrows `RelationshipChange.arrows` names, and the instances of a relationship type that was renamed; the title is read when a row is next made, never in a follower. ArrowPortsTests hold it (given at the release, renamed, untitled, taken back).

## Testing

- For ports, PortsTests use fixtures only, and no arrow: `portsHarness` installs Keyboard, Selection, Topology, the fixtures, Ports, `channelsPlugin` (boxes have ports on its `Wire` channel, `sides:` and `role:` as a test asks, discs on `Pipe`) and a recording connector over CanvasTesting's `Tag` (`connectorPlugin(ConnectionLog())`: `begun` lists each `Connection`, its session records each drag in `drags`, and `canConnect` and `draws` refuse; its family is `Hookup`, and `edgeTests` hold connections as edges). A 200 × 100 box at (100, 100) (`boxFrame`) has its dots at `topDot`, `rightDot`, `bottomDot` and `leftDot` at zoom 1. Move onto a dot before pressing, as a user does (`h.hoverDot` comes from the shape's middle): dots draw only while `editor.pointerOverCanvas` is set. PortsTests' `standInPortTests` hold `portsOnEveryChannel` and the instance ports over `relayHarness` (a `Relay` fixture that stands for the shapes it cites and has ports on every channel on its left and right, a `Hub` whose wire only starts connections, and a `Junction`, from `junctionsPlugin()`, with ports on the pipe and then on every channel, as a subboard's: one kind, several, none, re-pointing, a deleted target and its undo, a connection from a box ending on a relay for a box and not on one for a disc, a relay for a junction having `[pipe, wire]`, the pipe's gray dots and a drag ending on another junction on the pipe, several kinds in the first's order, the faint outlines, and the registry's lookups by `standingFor:`: one kind's channels on the relay's own sides, a hub's wire that only starts, several kinds' shared channels, `connectionChannel` with a stand-in at either end and a side named); ArrowPortsTests' `standInArrowTests` the arrows (`PortBoard(more: [relaysPlugin()])`: a port drag ending on a relay or not, an end re-aimed onto one that takes its channel, onto an empty, non-taking or source-only one, an arrow on no channel binding anywhere, bindings kept when re-pointed, a moved arrow keeping both ends); PortsTests' `standInPortTests` also hold the kept ports' upkeep (`standingPortsKept`: let go of with the shape deleted here, with a board taken that isn't shown, and with a file put in place on the board shown or another; a move and leaving a board keep them); ReferencesTests' `portTests` and CompositionTests' `referencesShippedTests` the shipped Reference (drag from a note ending on a pill for a note and not on one for a frame, a pill for a subboard drawn with the subboard's dots and its links both ways, re-aiming onto empty, missing, private and non-taking pills, the mismatch glyph and panel line, an arrow drawn into a pill reading as into the original in Topology, a query and the table of contents). ArrowPortsTests draw real arrows between fixtures on a `PortBoard` (boxes A and B where the POC's first two welcome notes are, taking `Wire`, and a disc C below B taking `Pipe`; `board.connect(to:speed:release:)` hovers A and drags from its right dot, `aRightDot`, at `fast` or `slow`), and read `board.arrows`, their bindings and their `ChannelProperty` (`arrowEdgeTests`, their edges). PortsTests' `portsBoardTests` hold a locked subboard's board (`portsHarness(…, more: [PocketsPlugin(), FreezingPlugin()], file:)`): no dot hovered or drawn there and no connection begun by a press where one would be, the dots back once thawed, and a lock landing above while the pointer is on a dot (nothing drawn though the pointer hasn't moved, none hovered once it does); ArrowPortsTests' `connectionDragTests` the drag that began as the lock landed (no session, no arrow, the board as it was). CompositionTests' `portsShippedTests` hold the shipped ports on real notes and subboards: their dots (all four sides, each in its channel's color), a drag between each pair (a note to a note or a subboard, a subboard to a note or a subboard, each on the channel the shape it ends on takes; a subboard to a frame draws nothing), the shapes that take a link outlined while one is drawn, and an arrow's end re-aimed (a `note-link` onto a note or a subboard and not a frame, a `subboard-link` onto a subboard and not a note).
