# Quick Look

A board file's Quick Look preview (space in the Finder) and its thumbnail (its icon): `BoardPicture`, the app's Quick Look extension, how it's built, signed and registered, and how to check it.

Related: [drawing](drawing.md), [boards on disk](boards-on-disk.md), [build](build.md) ([every topic](../../CLAUDE.md#map)).

In the guide, `docs/plugin-system.md`: [Boards on disk](../../docs/plugin-system.md#boards-on-disk).

## Architecture

- `BoardPicture` (the core's, `Sources/Canvas/Rendering`, since it draws with the editor's host API and a plugin's command takes one too: `canvas capture`, [command line](command-line.md#capture); the window's Copy as Image and the extension take theirs) is a picture of a file's root board (or, `init(file:registry:board:showing:)`, of another board of it, or of only some of its shapes: Copy as Image's, drawn from the live file through the core's `editor.drawnShapes`, which `paintOrder(in:)` goes by, so what isn't shown is still there to read, and fitted to the shapes shown), drawn by the editor's own drawing (`editor.draw`, host API) with nothing selected, so a kind needs nothing for it, and a shape is laid out in it as on the canvas (the picture has an editor: a `LaidOutBox` shape takes room by its laid-out frame, which a board preview, a plain context, does not): it reads the file as the app opens one (`BoardCodec`, then `replaceFile`), fits the whole board into the picture, centered, with `inset` of margin in all (a first visit's, `CanvasEditor.firstVisitInset`, host API), no closer than `maxZoom` (1) and as far out as it takes (past the canvas's 10%, down to 1%, `Camera(fitting:in:inset:minZoom:maxZoom:)`), and builds every board preview its cards ask for in the one picture: it draws, and if any was asked for, draws again, which builds them first (`PictureStyle.previewBudget`, 5 s for all). Under 35% its shapes are skeletons, as on the canvas. `size(within:minimum:)` is the size that shows the board at `maxZoom`, or as close as fits the limit; `draw(in:size:appearance:)` draws into a context y up (a bitmap's, Quick Look's); `image(size:scale:appearance:)` is an sRGB image, and `pngData(within:scale:appearance:)` that image as PNG data at the size `size(within:)` gives, its resolution saying `scale` pixels to a point (nil for a board with nothing to show): what Edit › Copy as Image puts on the pasteboard ([host slots](host-slots.md)). CanvasTests' Board picture holds it too (a PNG of the size, the scale in its resolution). A picture of part of a board says what it's about (`focus`: the shapes the camera is fitted to, everything around them drawn, where `showing` leaves the rest out; one with no bounds is about what it shows) and, at a zoom, which (`zoom`: centered on what it's about, 1% to 800%, not held to `maxZoom`); `framing(in:)` is how a picture of a size sees the board (`Framing`: `zoom`, `region` in world units, `about`, and `drawsSkeletons`, the canvas's rule), `pngData(size:scale:appearance:)` draws one of a size, and `size(within:)` is a fit's size of what it's about.
- The extension is CanvasQuickLook (`// layer: app of InfiniteCanvas`, importing the core and no CanvasUI): one executable, in two extensions (`Contents/PlugIns`), with the shipped plugins (`BoardPictures.registry`: the product's list, in its executable as in the app's, each plugin made by its name, [build](build.md); nothing it draws asks the model, which in its sandbox could read no key and reach no network). `PreviewProvider` (com.apple.quicklook.preview, data-based) replies with a PNG at the deepest screen's pixels to a point, saying its size in points by its pixels to an inch: Quick Look makes a reply's own context a pixel to a point. The preview is the board at no more than 100%, at least 480 × 320 and at most 1600 × 1200, in the system's appearance. `ThumbnailProvider` (com.apple.quicklook.thumbnail) fills the context Quick Look gives it, whatever its scale: the board as close as fits the request's `maximumSize` (to 800%), the picture no smaller than its `minimumSize` either way (a reply's context may not be), a margin of an eighth of its shorter side, always in the light appearance, since Quick Look keeps a thumbnail across a switch of the system's; an empty board has none, so it shows the document icon, and so does a size under 32 points (`QLThumbnailMinimumDimension`). Quick Look asks for a thumbnail off the main thread, and `QLThumbnailProvider` has no async form to override, so it waits on the main thread for the picture; it asks for a preview async, which hops there.

## Building, signing and registering

- `build.sh` (app mode) links the executable the app carries to start in Foundation's `NSExtensionMain` (`-e _NSExtensionMain`, as Xcode links an extension), and puts it in `QuickLookPreview.appex` and `QuickLookThumbnail.appex`, each with an Info.plist it writes (its identifier, which starts with its app's as an extension's must, so `com.example.InfiniteCanvas.QuickLookPreview` in the app and `com.example.InfiniteCanvas.worktree.<name>.QuickLookPreview` in a worktree's copy, [build](build.md); the point, the principal class, `QLSupportedContentTypes`: the app's exported type, `com.example.InfiniteCanvas.board`, in every copy) and signed ad hoc with the sandbox entitlement, which Quick Look requires of an extension; the app isn't signed, and needn't be. Each is signed as a copy, then what changed is installed: the same files sign ad hoc to the same bytes, so a build that changed nothing changes no file in the bundle. The entry point is only in `build.sh`'s link (the one-line manifest can't carry a linker flag), so SwiftPM can't link the extension.
- Opening the app registers its extensions (`pluginkit -m -v -i com.example.InfiniteCanvas.QuickLookThumbnail` lists one). Without opening it, `pluginkit -a` on each `.appex` registers them; `lsregister -f` alone (`/System/Library/Frameworks/CoreServices.framework/Frameworks/LaunchServices.framework/Support/lsregister`) registers the app but has dropped its extensions from PlugInKit, so follow it with `pluginkit -a`. Registering an extension makes its sandbox's container, `~/Library/Containers/<its identifier>`, which stays when its copy is unregistered and which a script can't remove (macOS refuses; the Finder can): two for the app, and two more for every worktree whose copy was registered.
- Quick Look's thumbnail agent (`com.apple.quicklook.ThumbnailsAgent`) settles which extension makes a type's thumbnails the first time it's asked for one, and keeps to it until it restarts, extensions installed since or not: a Mac that showed board files as JSON before the app carried its extensions goes on doing so after it's opened, until the user logs out and back in. The agent ignores SIGTERM, and under SIP `launchctl kickstart` can't restart it; `pkill -KILL -f com.apple.quicklook.ThumbnailsAgent` does (launchd starts it again on the next request). `qlmanage -r` resets quicklookd, not the agent.
- Every checkout's build carries the extensions, registered once it's opened, each claiming board files: the app's, a worktree's copy's under identifiers of its own ([build](build.md), which app a build is), and those of a worktree's build from before it was a copy, under the app's. LaunchServices hands Quick Look one build's extension, by no rule that was found (traced among builds under one identifier, it has been the newest registered, the oldest and neither, not the one `pluginkit -m` lists, and the preview's and the thumbnail's needn't be the same build's): a board file is drawn by whichever checkout's build that is, with that checkout's code. A copy's own identifiers don't make it the app's: traced with the app's bundle and a copy's in a namespace of their own (a type the agent had settled nothing for, each time), the copy's extension drew the thumbnail nine times in ten, whichever was registered first, whether the copy imported or exported the type, and whatever its identifier or its folder sorted as; the app's drew it once, registered last and its files the newer. What they change is that it can be told: the agent's log (`Launching process with config: bundleID: …`) and `pluginkit -m` name the copy. Removing a worktree unregisters nothing, and when the copy LaunchServices picks is one whose folder is gone, Quick Look gets no extension for a board file at all, however many copies still there have theirs registered, and shows the file as JSON, preview and thumbnail both (`pkd` logs LaunchServices' `could not resolve URL while initializing a bundle record` at each lookup; `pluginkit -m` goes on listing the extension all the while, so it proves nothing). Not every copy that's gone is picked (one registered after the others wasn't), so none is left. `build.sh`, in a build that changed the app's bundle and in a checkout's first (`build/.copies` marks it), unregisters every registered copy of the app whose folder is gone (`forget_copies_gone`: `lsregister -dump Bundle`, a second, lists them, each an app's bundle under the app's identifier or one that starts with it, a copy's; this build's own bundle, when it's there under an identifier that isn't its own, is unregistered with them; and it warns if the dump, whose form is documented nowhere, has stopped reading; `lsregister -u` takes a path that's gone; a copy on a volume that isn't mounted, or under a folder the shell can't list, isn't taken for gone). When one of them had extensions, what was handed none has to ask again, and unregistering makes nothing ask: a host (the Finder) asks only when an extension is registered, so the build has the copies still there register theirs again (`pluginkit -a`, which works on a copy already registered), and it restarts the thumbnail agent, which went on with none until it was (by its path from the start: `pkill -f` with the bare name kills any process whose command line has the name in it). By hand: `lsregister -u <the worktree>/build/InfiniteCanvas.app`, before or after removing the worktree, then `pluginkit -a` on a copy's two extensions and the agent's restart if Quick Look had already gone to JSON.

## Testing

- CanvasTests' Board picture (BoardPictureTests.swift) holds `BoardPicture` with fixture kinds (a cabinet shows its board's preview, as a subboard's card does): its size at 100% with the margin, the limit and the minimum; the board centered and upright; a board too big for 10% shown whole; a card's preview built in the one picture; the appearances; an image's scale; reading a file and refusing what isn't one; and its framing: a fit's zoom, region and what it's about, a picture about one shape fitted to it with its neighbor drawn, a focus with no bounds, a zoom centered on its shape with the skeleton level said, and PNG data of a size. CompositionTests' Board pictures, shipped: the sample board's notes where they are, a subboard's card with its preview, and a fixture file in an older format read and drawn.
- The extension is checked by hand: `qlmanage -t -x -s 512 -o <dir> <file>` writes the thumbnail Quick Look makes, through its agent and in the sandbox, with a fresh copy of the file each time (a thumbnail is kept per file), once the agent has been restarted since the extensions were registered (above). `qlmanage -p` can't host a data-based preview extension on this macOS (it throws, for Apple's own too), so a preview needs a host: a `QLPreviewView` in a window placed off every screen, or the Finder.
- When a board file shows as JSON, find out whether Quick Look found the extension before reading its code: no crash report and no `CanvasQuickLook` process in the log means it was never started. A host of your own (a few lines: a `QLPreviewView` given the file, in a window off every screen, its view tree printed after a few seconds) does the lookup in its own process, and run with `OS_ACTIVITY_DT_MODE=YES` its log isn't redacted (a system process's is, and so is the host's without it): `/usr/bin/log show --info --debug --predicate 'process == "host"'` (zsh's own `log` is another command) says `No exact match found in type dictionary … for 'com.example.infinitecanvas.board'` when LaunchServices handed it no extension, and the view tree is a `QLTextView` then, an `NSRemoteView` when the extension drew it. The thumbnail agent's log says which extension it started (`Launching process with config: bundleID: …`): `com.apple.quicklook.thumbnail.TextExtension` is the JSON.
