[accessibility](accessibility.md)), and tells the core each time the selection changes (`editor.accessibilityFocusDidChange()`, from `tellObservers`: the selection is VoiceOver's focus), the arrow keys' nudge (1 world unit, 10 with ⇧, `nudgeSelection(by:)`, a `translate` in one "Move", no snapping, the selection first brought into view if it is not (`revealIfOffscreen`, as Delete does too, so a key's effect shows: P7), again and again while held, one undo step, while something's selected and nothing's under way or edited; With nothing selected the same keys pan (the core's `editor.pan(_:far:)`: a step, ⇧ a page, `canPanByKeys`, no session or editing and a view with a size), one shortcut a chord, "Nudge or Pan" (`SelectionStyle.nudgeOrPan`), `arrowKey(_:large:)` choosing; SelectionTests' `nudgeTests` hold the pan, the settling and the crossing a unit a press, a stretch's laid-out frame crossing, and a container's contents gliding with it), Zoom to Selection (⇧2, `reveal` of the selection) and a trackpad's smart zoom (`.smartMagnify`, a two-finger double-tap: `reveal` of the shape under the pointer, where the gesture was, `editor.pointerLocated(at:)` (host API), since a window that isn't key hears no moves; and the next, while the camera's still there, back), and the double-click claim (`createOnDoubleClick`, the `DoubleClickPlacement` point: what a double-click on empty canvas places, and on a shape it would neither open nor edit (`canOpen`), pressed on the shape itself, as tldraw's double-click on a shape that can't be edited: an empty frame, hit anywhere inside, takes the note in; never on what `DecorationHits` draws for one, a frame's tab; SelectionTests' `editorTests` hold it over a `Corral`). Opening a shape is Selection's too: `x.opens("Open") { shape, editor in … }` (the `ShapeOpening` point; `registrar.opens(Kind.self, …)` for another plugin's kind) says what opening a kind's shape does (a board-owning kind shows its board); a double-click on a shape, Return, Enter and ⌘↓ (`.openSelection`, enabled while `editor.canOpen` the only selected shape, or the selection is a group selected whole or a container that neither opens nor edits, which they go into instead ([below](#moving-between-shapes)), and nothing is being edited, as ⌘↓ is then the text's; Return and Enter always take the key; Boards' Open in a board-owning shape's context menu runs it on the menu's shapes, [boards](boards.md); Return and Enter (`returnKey()`, and ⌥↩, Return with ⌥, which a placement reads as what lays waiting shapes out: `editor.placeLater`) first press a focused part, then, with a placing tool picked (N, F), put its shape down at the pointer or the view's middle and edit it, or put shapes waiting to be placed down where they're drawn, `editor.placeAtKeyboard()` ([editor](editor.md)), and only then open; CompositionTests' `keyboardCreationShippedTests` hold it, ⇧↩'s note beside, and the first minute by keys against its clicks) run `editor.open(id)` (a double-click's `open(id, at:clickCount:)`, from where it's pressed): the kind's opener, else `beginEditing` for an `Editable` one (a read-only one counts as openable, so its refusal shakes; an opener runs whatever the lock). ## Refusing a change

- `RefusalShake` (a public `EditorService` of `SelectionPlugin`, `Sources/Selection/RefusalShake.swift`) answers the core's one refusal signal, `didRefuseChanges` (`editor.refuseChanges(to:)`, which a transaction, a drag and editing send for read-only shapes), whoever's rule made them read-only: it shakes each shape tried through `editor.setDrawingOffset` (`SelectionStyle.shakeAmplitude` 5 view points side to side, `shakeCycles` 3 over `shakeDuration` 360 ms, dying away, `shakeFrame` 16 ms on `editor.scheduler`; `RefusalShake.offset(at:)` is the curve, `isShaking` says whether any shape is). A try while a shape shakes doesn't start it over; every refused shape on the board shown shakes (a shape whose board holds a refused shape, which isn't deleted either, is the one tried), and a container shakes with what it holds, at any depth (`document.descendants(of:containingKinds:)`), as it moves with it. Only shapes on the board shown shake: a refusal also names the owner of a read-only board something tried to put a shape on (a locked subboard's, or one under it: an insert, a placement, a move onto it), a placement on the board shown naming its own owner, which is on the board above, so nothing shakes, while a move onto a locked subboard's board from the board its card is on names the card, which does. Another board shown stops what's under way (it isn't kept per board), and a deleted shape stops shaking. A plugin whose rule makes shapes read-only (Locking's lock, Publishing's hold) installs nothing for the shake; a plugin list with `SelectionPlugin` has it. VoiceOver hears it too: a refusal naming shapes that can't change (`editor.isReadOnly`: locked, on the shipped list) announces "Locked" or "N shapes locked" (`SelectionStyle.refused`, `editor.announce`), once while tries of the same shapes keep coming (a held arrow key's refused nudges: each try puts off `refusalQuiet`, 1 s, and only a try of other shapes, or one after the quiet, is said again); a refusal of shapes that can change (a held public shape's Make Private) says nothing, its plugin's to explain. RefusalShakeTests (SelectionTests, on CanvasTesting's `FreezingPlugin` and `Frozen`, never Locking) hold the timing and curve, that any refusal shakes (a drag, a plugin's own `refuseChanges`) but a click doesn't, not starting over, containers, the owner of a read-only board (the card shaking when a move onto its board was refused from the board it's on, nothing when a placement on its own board was), and the board change and deletion, and what's announced ("Locked", "2 shapes locked", nothing for a shape that can change, a held arrow's refused nudges said once and again after a quiet second).

## Going to shapes

- Going to shapes is Selection's, one way for every plugin: `editor.reveal(ids)` only moves the camera (centers the shapes' bounds in `unobscuredViewport`, below the path over a subboard's canvas, fitted with 100 points of margin, no closer than 120%, over 200 ms: `SelectionStyle.reveal*`, data-canvas's zoomToBounds; through the core's `animateCamera(toFit:…)`, so the fit is kept while the view settles, the inspector sliding in: [editor](editor.md)), and is what selecting table-of-contents rows does. What of a selection is in view as it changes (all of it, or the part a shape cut by the canvas's edge shows) is kept in view the same way (`SelectionState.select` → `editor.keepInViewWhileSettling`, with the selection's kept bounds; not before the view has a size), so a shape selected by the canvas's edge isn't left under the inspector; a reveal after the select replaces it. `editor.goTo(ids)` is following a link (a mention): it ends editing, selects the selectable ones (a Back step only when that changes the selection; to what's selected already, or to unselectable shapes, it only moves the camera), and reveals unless they're all in view, where the camera is headed (`cameraTarget`), at a readable zoom: `SelectionStyle.readableZoom` (70%: a note's 16-point text reads at 11.2 points there, just over `NSFont.smallSystemFontSize`), or a kind's own, closer, for text set smaller (`x.readableZoom(_:)`, `registrar.readableZoom(K.self, _:)`, Selection's `ReadableZoom` point, above zero and at most 120%, `ReadableZoom.maximum`, as close as `reveal` brings shapes, `editor.readableZoom(of:)`: 11-point text reads at 100%; no shipped kind says one; the closest of the shapes' kinds counts), or as close as fitting them would bring them (a shape too big for 70% that's all in view stays put). The shapes may be on any board of the file: it goes to the board of the first of them on a board it can show, and to those of them there, showing that board and selecting them as one Back step (`showBoard(_:selecting:)`), then revealing them. `reveal` is the board shown's alone. Nothing mid-gesture, or when none of the shapes is on a board it can show. `editor.goInto(id)` is following a link into what it names (a mention of a subboard): `goTo`, then `open` there (`canOpen`), as one Back step to where it was followed from, whatever boards it went through (`SelectionState.asOneStep`: nothing inside records a step of its own); nothing for a shape whose own board is shown. A way around that takes many calls (Find's stepping, board to board) is one Back step too: `editor.navigationStart` (a `NavigationStart`: the board, the selection and the camera where it's headed) taken as it begins, and each move inside `editor.navigate(from:_:)`, which records nothing of its own; the first call that moves the board, the selection or the camera puts the start on the history, once (`SelectionState.asOneStep(from:_:)`; a later call finds it on the stack and adds none, even after a click or a pan between; a call that moves nothing adds none).

## Faces

- `SelectionObservers` (`registrar.selectionChanged { editor, left, now in … }`) tells a plugin each time the selection changes, after it has (`SelectionState.select`, and the clearing a board switch makes), with the shapes it left and the ones it holds now: Types' `RelationshipOffers`, which shows the dashed pill on the only selected plain arrow. Keep it cheap; SelectionTests hold nothing of it beyond what the offer's tests do ([types](types.md)).
- Faces (Selection's `ShapeFaces` point, `registrar.shapeFaces(at:click:doubleClick:hovering:)`) are parts of a shape's content that answer a click without taking the shape's press: a mention's pill in a note, a link, a checkbox on a card. `at` is asked only for a plain press (no ⇧, ⌘ or ⌥, not a Control-click) on the shape the select tool would press, selected or not, at a world point on that shape itself (a point on what `DecorationHits` draws for a shape is no face's), after canvas buttons and handles, and looks at that one shape; the last plugin's first. Never on a shape drawn as a skeleton (`editor.drawsSkeleton(of:)`, zoomed out), which shows none of its content: there a click, a double-click (which edits an `Editable` one from where it's pressed, then drawn in full, and is a refused edit on a read-only one) and the pointer are the shape's, and a lone box follows the pointer at once; as the level changes, Selection's `drawsSkeletonsDidChange` looks again under a still pointer, so a face's hovered target gives way to its shape; a face pressed in full whose shape is a skeleton by the release (a pinch past 35% mid-press) isn't clicked: the release is the shape's, as a canvas button gone by the release isn't clicked (`PointingShapeSession.end` checks `drawsSkeleton(of:)` again). The press is the shape's: it selects the shape as any press does and, past the drag threshold, moves the selection from the press; on a lone box it waits for the threshold too (an intended deviation from the lone box's press, pinned in SelectionTests). Released within the threshold, the shape is selected alone and `click` runs; Escape lets go first. A double-click whose press lands on one selects the shape alone and runs `doubleClick` in place of editing it, a read-only shape's too; without `doubleClick`, a double-click there edits an `Editable` shape and is otherwise one more click. The pointer over one hovers the shape `hovering` names (`editor.hovered`, so hover keeps one path), or its own shape when it names none, or one that isn't there or can't be selected; hover asks whatever keys are held, and the cursor stays the arrow. `click` runs once the press is over, so it may `goTo` or go back, and what it selects is a Back step of its own; it isn't given the press, so a face that needs a point carries it in its value (a pill edits its note from there, `editor.beginEditing(id, at:)`), and a click that begins editing leaves the pointer over the editing view, which ends the hover. `editor.face(atViewPoint:)` is the select tool's own lookup, public for the reach census ([testing](testing.md#key-walks)): the face a plain press there clicks and the shape it is on, canvas buttons and handles not counted, as `button(atViewPoint:)` and `handle(atViewPoint:)` are theirs. CompositionTests' canvas view tests hold these pieces together with `pillsPlugin`, a pill-like face in a note: edited from the pill on a click, not hovered under its text, and gone to (`goTo`) from a locked note's double-click; Mentions' pills are faces built on them. A face whose click starts nothing on its own still gives a `doubleClick` that does the same (Types' rows: `›` and an options list), or the second press of a fast pair begins editing an `Editable` shape. A face that only selects (Types' field name on a note that is one: its `click` does nothing, so the press's selection is all a click does, also on the only selected note, where a click elsewhere on it would edit it) gives a `doubleClick` that opens the shape itself (`editor.open(_:at:clickCount:)`), which is what a double-click does without a face. Types' offer is a face that answers a click on a pill in a type's words that names a type it is not yet related to, with a menu (`Relate to Layer`) in place of editing, and so gives its own `doubleClick` (begin editing at the pill, or follow it on a type that cannot be edited: read-only, or of a kind with no editing view) and `hovering`, or Mentions' pill there would lose both: only the single click changes ([relationships](relationships.md#relating-from-the-words-the-offer)). A face that edits its shape checks `editor.isReadOnly` first (as `pillsPlugin` does): a refused edit is a try, which Selection shakes, and a click on a locked shape isn't one. A click on a face that goes somewhere (it changes the selection or moves the camera: a link's `goTo`) makes the rest of its double-click its own: the select tool ignores each press after it with a click count of 2 or more, until one of 1 (`ignoresPress`), so the camera goes on to where the click sent it and nothing is placed, edited or pressed where it has got to; a click that goes nowhere (a pill's) leaves the double-click to `doubleClick`.

## Groups selected as one

- `SelectionGroups` (Selection's, `Groups.swift`, at most one: `registrar.selectionGroups { editor in source }`, a `SelectionGroupSource`, asked for the first time the selection needs it and again while it gives none, a service made after Selection's; Grouping's `GroupIndex`, [grouping](grouping.md)) says which shapes are selected as one on the board shown: `groups(of:)` (outermost first, any `AnyHashable` that tells them apart) and `shapes(in:)` (at any depth, back to front). Selection knows no group otherwise, and does tldraw's with them: `SelectionState.focus`, the groups the selection is inside (tldraw's focused group, as a path; `editor.selectionFocus`), worked out from each non-empty selection (the groups its shapes share, less those it holds whole: `Sequence.groupsInside(holdsWhole:)`, public, which Grouping's Group goes by too, so a new group goes where the selection is drawn inside), kept by an empty one, emptied by another board shown, a press on empty canvas outside the innermost one's box (`leaveFocus(unlessInside:)`, on the press, so a marquee from there takes groups whole), and Escape with nothing selected; worked out again when the source says its groups changed (`editor.selectionGroupsDidChange()`, an empty selection keeping the groups still there), since Selection hears a commit before the source does. What a click on a shape selects (`unit(of:within:)`, public as `editor.selectionUnit(of:)`, which ContextMenus' right-click selects): the outermost group it's in past those it shares with the focus, whole, else the shape. The press selects that (⇧ adds it; it's kept, unselected on the press, when it's all selected already, with ⌘, or inside the box around several); a release without a drag on a shape of a group that's all selected selects the part one level in (`unit(inside:within:)`; with ⇧ or ⌘ that part in the group's place), as tldraw's PointingShape does, and on canvas or in the box toggles or selects the unit (`canvasRelease`); a double-click on a shape whose unit is a group selects the part one level in (or the unit, if it wasn't selected) rather than opening it, before faces' double-clicks, and once the shape is directly inside the focus a double-click opens it; Escape with something selected inside a group selects that group whole, focus a level out (`escape()`), and with none left the container its shapes are in (below). The marquee maps what it reaches to units as the focus was when it began (`BrushingSession.focus`), each once, and with ⌘ keeps a group only if it holds every shape of it. The hover on the canvas outlines what a click there would select (`unit(of:within:)`; `hoversCanvas`, set by the select tool's hover on the shape under the pointer and cleared by the public `hovered` setter, so a row's hover, and a face's that names another shape (a pill's), outline the shape alone); a selected shape has no faint outline, so nothing shows over a selected group. `GroupFocusOverlay` (world space, the select tool only) dashes the box around the innermost group the selection is inside (`focusBox`), `SelectionStyle.groupFocusStandoff` (10 view points) out, the accent at 70%, 1 view point, dashes 4 and 3. Without a source every rule is as it was (`unit` is the shape). SelectionTests' `groupSelectionTests` hold it over `GroupsFixture` (paths a test sets, telling the editor): a click and a drag, a part selected and Escape out, a lone part following the pointer, a double-click going in then opening a card, groups in groups, a click outside the group, ⇧, the marquee and ⌘, a marquee inside the box and a press outside it, the hover, `selectionUnit`, groups changing under the selection, and another board shown.

## Moving between shapes

- Moving between shapes (Selection's, `Stepping.swift`; tldraw's `selectAdjacentShape`, `_getShapesInReadingOrder`, `getNearestAdjacentShape`, `selectParentShape` and `selectFirstChildShape`, v5.5.2; the guide's [Moving between shapes](../../docs/plugin-system.md#moving-between-shapes)). `editor.selectAdjacentShape(_:adding:)` (`AdjacentDirection`: `.next`, `.previous`, `.left`, `.right`, `.up`, `.down`; `canStepSelection`: nothing under way or edited) is what Selection's shortcuts run: Tab and ⇧Tab ("Next Shape", "Previous Shape"), ⌥⌘ with an arrow ("Select Nearest Shape") and ⌥⇧⌘ with an arrow ("Add Nearest Shape to Selection"), each repeating while held and taking the key when there is nothing to step to (they pass only while a gesture runs or a shape is edited). A stop is what a click there selects (`unit(of:within:)` at the selection's focus: a group whole, else the shape) at the middle of its shapes' bounds (`shapeContext.bounds(of:)`, the selection box's), a `Stop`; a shape needs a box (`AnyShapeRecord.box`, so an arrow is never one; a group is one if any of its shapes has) and has to be selectable (a locked shape is: an intended deviation, tldraw skips locked shapes). Siblings only (`stepScope`): the shapes in the container all the selected shapes are in (`registry.container(of:in:)`), else the board's own (no container); of those, with the selection inside a group (`focus`), only the shapes whose groups start with it, so stepping goes round a group; with nothing selected, the board's own, or any shape of the group the kept focus is. Reading order (`StopOrder.reading`) is tldraw's with its numbers in `SelectionStyle` (`readingRowRange` 100 world units, `readingSwapCloseness` 0.9 of the squared distance, `readingSwapAngle` 20 degrees; a stable sort, so stops level with one another stay in board order); next and previous start from the first stop in reading order that holds a selected shape and wrap, going from nothing selected to the first (an intended deviation: tldraw needs a selection) and ⇧Tab to the last, and from a selection that holds no stop (an arrow) from where its bounds' middle reads among the stops (a stop of no shapes put in reading order with them: Tab the one after it, ⇧Tab the one before), never from an end. A direction (`StopOrder.nearest`) scores the stops in its wedge as tldraw does (`stepWedge` 2, `stepAcrossWeight` 2, `stepDiagonalWeight` 1.5, `stepAngleWeight` 0.5, the angle off the direction taken both ways round), starting from the stop selected last (tldraw's: the first in reading order of those selected), the first of equal scores winning; none that way, or nothing selected, does nothing (tldraw's re-selects the current shape alone, which would collapse a selection), and with `adding` the stops selected already are left out and the stop found is appended to the selection, so the one you started from stays first. Not in tldraw: ⇧⌥⌘ and an arrow. The way back is exact (P4): each step goes on `SelectionState.trail` (`StepTrail`: before, after, direction, adding; 50 at most), and the opposite key pressed while the selection is still where a step left it restores what it was before (`step(_:adding:)`): ⇧Tab after Tab from several shapes or an arrow selects them again, ← after → comes back where tldraw's nearest would not, and the ⇧ step's opposite takes off what it added; any other change of the selection (`select`, another board) lets the trail go, and so does a step back to shapes gone since or no longer selectable (a deletion, an undo, an agent's draw), the key then stepping as with none; tldraw's scoring is unchanged. The keys are ⌥⌘ and an arrow where tldraw's are ⌘ and an arrow, since ⌘↑ and ⌘↓ are Boards' and Open Selection's here. Each stop selected is a step back, and `editor.revealIfOffscreen(_:)` (`GoingTo.swift`: tldraw's `zoomToSelectionIfOffscreen`) brings it into view only if its bounds are not all in what the host leaves clear (`unobscuredViewport`, where the camera is headed): the camera moves over 200 ms to center them with `revealInset` points of margin at the zoom it has, farther out where they do not fit, never closer (`reveal`, which goes to 120%, would zoom in on every small shape); nothing before the view has a size. The cost is the siblings' (every Tab sorts them: about 40 ms in a debug build at 10,000 shapes on one board).
- For another plugin, `editor.shape(_:from:among:)` (`.next` or `.previous`; nil for a direction) is the same reading order over the shapes `among` takes in `id`'s container (`registry.container(of:in:)`, the board's own in none), each a stop of its own at the middle of its bounds (no group taken whole, no selection read or changed), wrapping, nil when no other is taken or `id` has no box: what Tab in a note's editor goes to (StickyNotes' `NoteHops`). `steppingTests` hold it (a shape not taken passed over, a container's own, wrapping, alone).
- Going into a group or a container is `.openSelection` (Selection's: ⌘↓, and Return and Enter through `perform`; Boards' Open, a board-owning shape's context-menu item, runs it too), generic over groups and containers and asked of the shapes it is given, the selection or a menu's (a right-click asks of the shapes it will select before it selects them): `editor.canOpenSelection(_:)` (`SelectionState.canStepInto(_:)`, or a shape alone that `canOpen`; never several that are no group) is its `isEnabled`, and `editor.openSelection(_:)` runs `shapesInside(_:)` before it opens a shape alone (named apart from `canOpen(_:)` and `open(_:at:clickCount:)`, which open one shape and never go into a container). The shapes are one group whole (`wholeGroup`: `unit(of:within:)` of the first, within the groups they are all inside as `select` works them out for a selection, `focus(of:selected:)`, is every one of them, more than one): the group's parts one level in (`unit(inside:within:)` of each of its shapes, each once) as stops, and the first in reading order is selected (a part with no box counts only when no part has one; tldraw's Enter on a group selects all the children of the groups: an intended deviation), the same part a click on a selected group selects. Or they are one container alone (`registry.isContainer`) whose kind neither opens (`ShapeOpening`) nor edits (`Editable`: `!canOpen`), which a frame is: the first stop in it, in reading order (a group in it is a stop, selected whole), nothing if it holds none (then ⌘↓ passes and Return takes the key). A container that opens or edits is opened as ever, and a double-click is unchanged. What is selected is brought into view if it is not. SelectionTests' `steppingTests` hold the order (rows, the row threshold and its chain, the swap and the steep case, wrapping, siblings in a container, groups as stops and inside a group, nested groups, no arrows or ghosts, the guards and a held key, the camera staying, moving, partly in view and zooming out), the direction score (straight ahead over a nearer diagonal, the wedge, the angle both ways round, a start from the one selected last, nothing that way) and adding, and `enteringTests` ⌘↓ and Return into groups and frames, a container that opens, the command asked of shapes that aren't the selection (a group whole, a container alone, never several), the camera, and the enclosure; CompositionTests' `steppingShippedTests` the keys as the keyboard sends them (⇧Tab types the back-tab character, which `KeyStroke(event)` makes Tab with ⇧), through the shipped group, frame and subboards, and the Go item's titles.
- Stepping out is `editor.selectionEnclosure` (`SelectionEnclosure`: `.group`, or `.container(id)`; nil with nothing selected) and `editor.selectEnclosure()` (brings what it selects into view if it is not), which [Boards](boards.md) runs for ⌘↑ before it goes up a board: with the selection inside a group (`focus` not empty, and the group has parts that can be selected), the innermost group selected whole, as `escape()` selects it (focus a level out; with nothing selected the kept focus gives no group: ⌘↑ goes up); else, with the selected shapes all in one container (`commonContainer()`), that container, if it can be selected. A group is stepped out of before the container it is in, one level at a time. tldraw's `selectParentShape` takes the only selected shape's parent; several shapes in one container step out here. Escape (`SelectionState.escape()`, the select tool's) climbs the same ladder, one level a press (P5): a focused part back to its shape, then `selectEnclosure()`, brought into view, so a frame's child selects the frame (it used to deselect), and only with no enclosure, at the board's own shapes, nothing; it never leaves a board, where ⌘↑ goes on up. SelectionTests' `groupSelectionTests` and CompositionTests' `steppingShippedTests` walk it from a note in a group in a frame, ⌘↑ beside it.

## Parts of a shape

- What a shape draws that a click acts on, as the keys reach it (Selection's `ShapeParts`, `Sources/Selection/Parts.swift`; [keyboard navigation](keyboard-navigation.md)'s principles 1 to 5; the guide's [Parts of a shape](../../docs/plugin-system.md#parts-of-a-shape)). A list point, `.scoped` (a list for every shape with no scope is refused; the report says "parts <name> → <scope>"), filled through `scope.shapeParts(order:named:) { editor, shape in [ShapePart] }`, a kind's own `shape.shapeParts`, or `registrar.shapeParts(on:)`; Adornments fills it for every clickable adornment ([adornments](adornments.md)). A `ShapePart` is an id stable across listings (an `AdornmentButton`, a handle), a title (VoiceOver's), a `PartRole` (`.button`, `.menuButton`, `.link`, `.checkbox`, `.handle`, `.connection`), its rect in world units, `press` (the click) and an optional `adjust` (`PartStep`: a unit direction, `isLarge` with ⇧, `offset` a nudge's 1 or 10, `toNearest` with ⌥⌘ and `adjacentDirection` its way as stepping names it; true when it took the step). `editor.shapeParts(of:)` orders every plugin's by `StopOrder.reading` over the rects' middles (now generic over anything with a middle; stops call it as before), a row being parts whose middles are within half the shortest part's height (`rowRange`; shapes' 100 would read a note's rows, a field apart, as one row, left to right), level ones by `order` then plugin order, one per id; none for the shape being edited. Listed only when a key asks, and for the ring when its kept rect is stale (`SelectionState.focusedPartRect`: kept against `revision`, `layoutRevision` and the zoom, since an adornment's size and place are in view points and only its plugin knows them): so while the zoom changes under a focused part (a pinch, a zoom's glide) the one focused shape's parts are listed each frame, that shape's cost alone (its rows, chips and own arrows), never the board's; a ring scaled from the last listing would stand off its chip mid-pinch.
- The focus is `SelectionState.partFocus` (`PartFocus`: shape and part id; `editor.focusedPart` lists to find it, and so does `hasFocusedPart`: a part gone with no commit, a handle or chip the zoom leaves out, has no focus, draws no ring and takes no key, and Escape lets it go and goes on out). It goes with any change of `selectedIDs` (`select`), a press of the select tool (the pointer takes over from the keys, on the selected shape too: Space, Return and Delete after it are the shape's), another tool or board, `editingDidEnd` (and an edited shape lists none, so it is gone while editing), Escape (`escape()` lets it go first, one level, the shape still selected), and, in `documentDidSettle` (once every service has the commit, so Adornments' kept labels are new), a commit after which the shape lists no part of its id (a lock chip whose shape was unlocked: the open lock fades and is no part). A press that pops a menu or opens a card commits nothing, so the focus stays and Escape out of the menu lands back on the part.
- The keys, Selection's, one shortcut a chord: ⌥Tab "Next Part" and ⌥⇧Tab "Previous Part" (`canFocusParts`: one shape selected, no session, not editing; else they pass), repeating, `focusAdjacentPart(.next / .previous)`: the first or last part with none focused, else the next or previous, wrapping; a shape with no parts takes the key and focuses none; the part is brought into view (`revealIfOffscreen(rect:)`, the ids' version now calls it). Return and Enter run `pressFocusedPart()` before `.openSelection`; Space is Keyboard's, through Selection's claim of `registrar.pressFocused` ([keyboard](keyboard.md)); the nudge arrows run `arrowKey(_:large:)` and with a selection `arrow(_:large:)`: the focused part's `adjust`, else the nudge, a held key keeping what it did (once it has adjusted a part in its step, its repeats adjust or do nothing: a handle the key shrinks the box past showing goes, and the shape stays put); ⌥⌘ and an arrow ("Select Nearest Shape") offer the focused part a `toNearest` step first, else step to the nearest shape (an arrow's focused end binds to that shape, and with nothing that way stays where it is: Arrows' `ArrowParts`, [arrows](arrows.md#an-arrow-by-the-keys)). `editor.nearestShape(to:toward:among:)` is that rule (`StopOrder.nearest`) for any plugin, from a point, among the shapes with a box `among` takes, in any container, each its own stop. Delete's command is disabled while a part has the keyboard where it is asked (`!hasFocusedPart(for:)` in its `isEnabled`: a key or the menu bar, no point, on the part's shape), so Delete there changes nothing (P8) and Edit › Delete is dimmed; a context menu's Delete, which brings its shapes and its click, is never the part's. Tab stays the shapes' (landed), and from a part goes to the next shape as from the shape.
- The ring is `PartFocusOverlay` (world space, after `HandlesOverlay`): `SelectionStyle.accent`, `partRingWidth` 2 and `partRingStandoff` 3 view points over the zoom, corners half the ring's height, drawn where its shape is (`worldDrawingOffset`), so a shake or a glide carries it with the chip.
- PartsTests (SelectionTests, a `CanvasView` in an unshown window with CanvasTesting's boxes and a fixture plugin listing three parts out of order: two chips, one popping a menu and one shown only on a box 150 wide, and a handle that takes ← → but not ↑ ↓) walk it with `KeyWalk`: the reading order and the cycle with its exact inverse, the parts shown, Return, Enter and Space pressing (Space holding no Hand then, and the Hand again with none focused), adjust and the nudge, the Escape ladder, `expectInert` at every part (Delete included) by ⌥Tab, the ring (pixels, and carried with its shape's drawing offset) and the reveal, the focus going with a selection, a commit that removes the part, a tool and a pointer press, Delete kept from the part's shape only for a key or the menu bar, and nothing with none or several selected, and the report's scope and the Keyboard Shortcuts rows. PartsShippedTests (CompositionTests) walk a locked public note's two chips and unlock it with Return.
- VoiceOver (`Sources/Selection/Accessibility.swift`, one line in `SelectionPlugin.register`; [accessibility](accessibility.md)): Selection fills the core's two claims, `registrar.accessibleOrder` with Tab's order (`readingOrder`: the stops where the selection is, `StopOrder.reading`, each stop's shapes in turn) and `registrar.accessibleParts` with ⌥Tab's parts (`ShapeParts`, as `AccessiblePart`s, the `PartRole` its role; the focused one `partFocus`). A part's press selects its shape alone, focuses the part (the ring) and presses it, as ⌥Tab and Return would; `partFocus` calls `editor.accessibilityFocusDidChange()` as it changes, so VoiceOver's focus follows ⌥Tab. The stops in reading order are kept (`keptReadingOrder`, `ReadingOrderKey`: the board, its revision and layout, the step scope and the group the selection is inside), so Tab and VoiceOver work them out once between commits, and a step to a sibling or a pan finds them kept.
