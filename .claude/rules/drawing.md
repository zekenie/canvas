# Drawing

Board previews; what a frame may cost (the dot grid, culling, skeletons, drawing in batches, what the editor keeps); the minimap; the pointer over the views the canvas hosts; and how to test each.

Related: [editor](editor.md), [benchmarks](benchmarks.md), [testing](testing.md), [plugins and kinds](plugins-and-kinds.md), [subboards](subboards.md), [titles](titles.md), [adornments](adornments.md), [validation](validation.md), [Quick Look](quick-look.md) ([every topic](../../CLAUDE.md#map)).

In the guide, `docs/plugin-system.md`: [Skeletons](../../docs/plugin-system.md#skeletons), [The core's part: board previews](../../docs/plugin-system.md#the-cores-part-board-previews), [Outlines](../../docs/plugin-system.md#outlines).

## Board previews

- Board previews (the core's `BoardPreviews`, host API `editor.boardPreviews`; `docs/plugin-system.md` "The core's part: board previews"): `context.drawBoardPreview(ofBoardOwnedBy:in:maxScale:)` draws a rough picture of the board a shape owns into a rect, fitted (no larger than `maxScale` times its size, if given: a subboard's card passes a tenth, so a board of one note isn't drawn as big as one of hundreds), and returns true, or draws nothing and returns false while it isn't built, for an empty board, and for a rect under 16 view points either way on screen. Every shape there is a silhouette of its geometry in one muted gray (`Silhouettes`, the core's, host API, which the minimap draws with too; a container's, a frame's, its outline stroked, `hollow`, so what it holds shows on it; a shape under 4 device pixels both ways is its bounds, under one its pixel: `Silhouettes.form`, which the default skeleton goes by too), no text or colors, in a mask at most 256 pixels on a side. A skeleton may ask for one (the subboard's does, as its card does); the core's default skeleton never does. A preview is keyed by board and `file.revision(of:)`, so one of a board as it was before an edit or an undo is dropped, never drawn; the 64 drawn last are kept, never evicting one the frame before drew (more owners in view than that: the rest show their count, and nothing thrashes). Boards asked for are built at the start of each frame, those asked for longest ago first, a chunk of shapes at a time within 2 ms (`frameBudget`; at least 16 shapes), and the editor asks for frames (`editor.scheduler`) while any wait; what the frame just drawn didn't ask for stops waiting (`endFrame()`, and a switch of board drops it all). Keep them that cheap: `./build.sh bench` holds ten 10,000-shape subboards in view to a frame while they fill. A picture of a whole board, which is one frame (CanvasUI's `BoardPicture`, what Quick Look shows), builds all its cards ask for before it's drawn ([Quick Look](quick-look.md)).

## What a frame costs

- A frame costs what's in view and what changed, never the whole board (`./build.sh bench` holds 10,000 shapes to 16 ms, zoomed out as far as 10% goes too, and 10,000 all out of view, selected or not, to 1 ms over an empty board; an outline standing off a closed shape adds to each outlined shape in view two reads of its path's end points and a rounded rect in place of the path, grown directly, and zoomed out, where ⌘A outlines thousands, none: the rings are on the frames as before). The dot grid under the shapes (`DotGrid`, the core's) is drawn from tiles of dots filled once, copied at whole device pixels, into a bitmap through a clip of the pixels the dots touch (a bitmap composites a copy's clear pixels as dearly as its dots) and into any other context unclipped (AppKit's context for a view's `draw(_:)` records a display list, which Core Animation renders on the GPU while the window is on screen, making a clip of a rect per dot into a region again for every copy: some 400 ms a frame), in the context's own pixel layout and space (sRGB for one no 8-bit bitmap takes, an extended range's): pixel for pixel the path of an ellipse per dot it replaced wherever a whole number of dots spans whole pixels (100%, and the POC's goldens at 60% and 15%, which hold it), and elsewhere each copy within an eighth of a pixel (a tile for each quarter-pixel step its copies need); kept while the camera doesn't move, some 1 to 1.5 ms of a frame at 1200 × 800 on Retina (the path took 3 to 7), CanvasTests' GridTests against that path (CanvasTesting's `drawGridAsEllipses`, which the benchmark reports beside it) and unclipped against clipped byte for byte (debug builds' `editor.gridClipOverride`, and `gridFramesClipped` and `gridFramesUnclipped` for which a frame did; CanvasUITests' Display suite holds a canvas the window displays to the unclipped one). Shapes more than 40 world units out of view (`Style.drawingPad`, for a shadow at the edge, grown by the farthest drawing offset, `worldDrawingReach`, for a shape shaken into view) don't draw, and outlines are asked for and stroked only for shapes whose kept extent meets that area (a plugin's rects too), so ⌘A costs what's in view in strokes, and a look at each selected shape's kept extent a frame, nothing copied or allocated (`editor.outlines` hands Selection's list over as it is; a pan works the selection's box out again, a pass over its kept bounds): 10,000 selected, all out of view, add about half a millisecond (measured). Below 35% every shape but the one being edited is its skeleton (`CustomSkeleton`, else the default): a few fills, no text, shadows or pictures of its own, reading nothing that parses or lays out text, since a frame there draws thousands (what skeletons saved, as measured: `docs/plugin-system.md`, "Skeletons"). Shipped: a note's frame in its yellow with its text greeked by the core's `SkeletonTextBars` (paragraphs' lines counted, never laid out, by any kind's text: 0.52 em a column as `SkeletonText` counts them, calibrated once against string drawing, `SkeletonTextMetrics`; runs of text and inline runs a number of columns wide, `SkeletonTextRun`, which a kind's own runs say they are, `SkeletonTextRunConvertible`: a mention as wide as its pill by its stored label, Mentions' `MentionRun`, in columns, read only as far as a pill grows; blank or all-space paragraphs blank lines, CR, LF or CR LF ending one, a paragraph read only until it fills the lines left once something in it shows, text typed in the app (bridged UTF-16) read 64 code units at a time on the stack, at most the box's lines (clamped to 100,000, so any height counts in an `Int`), each bar over its line's x-height, 6.5 to 15 of 19), a subboard's fills and preview, a schema card's edge and its description, greeked as a note's text is, an arrow's arc ([arrows follow tldraw](arrows.md)). Draw what's per shape zoomed out in batches of rects or lines, in paint order a run of one look at a time: Core Graphics fills those in one path for a fraction of each alone, while many curves in one path, or rounded rects stroked together, cost more than each alone (Validation and the core fill rings). The editor keeps each shape's geometry and extent, and the paint order (`ShapeIndex`), and redoes them for what each commit's `ChangeSet` names; it keeps the board's bounds too, and where each commit moved or reshaped shapes (damage: where the shapes it touched, and those whose geometry followed them, were and are), for pictures of the board kept from frame to frame (host API: `shapeBounds`, `damageMark`, `damage(since:)`). A render may also read the editor's services (`RenderContext.service(_:)`, as `EditContext.service(_:)` lends an editing view's; made once per frame, `lend`), and nothing observes a render, so a lending service calls `editor.setNeedsDisplay()` when what it lends changes (CanvasTests' RenderLendTests). So a definition looks at other shapes only through its `ShapeContext` (`shape(_:)`, `geometry(of:)`, `hitTest(_:shape:)`: they're tracked, all of `document` or `camera` isn't); a `hitTest` that reaches past the outline declares `hitMargin`; a paint anchor follows from its shape's props and the document's order; per-frame code (overlays) goes through `editor.paintOrder(in:)` with `context.visibleRect`; and a service keeps per-shape state and redoes the shapes a change set names, as `ValidationEngine`, `ContentsState` and `AdornmentState` do.

## The minimap

- The minimap (CanvasUI's `MinimapView`, which `CanvasView.showsMinimap` hosts in the canvas's bottom-left corner; View › Show Minimap, ⌃⌘M, remembered per board in `BoardViewState` and `BoardViewMemory`; hidden, its corner the canvas's, while the board has nothing to show, `shapeBounds` null, and faded in with the first shape, once the press that placed it under the minimap, or its editing, is done, at once on another board) works as tldraw's: the board and the viewport fitted together; a click beside the viewport eases the camera there over 320 ms, a drag moves it within tldraw's clamp, a double-click centers exactly. It draws every shape as a rough silhouette of its geometry in one color (the core's `Silhouettes`; a container, a frame, its outline, so what it holds shows), never its render (no text, no colors), into a bitmap of tiles kept from frame to frame (`MinimapPicture`): damage makes tiles stale, and each frame draws again those stale longest, within its budget (4 ms, and a tile at least), so a frame spends at most that on the picture however big the board, and whatever goes stale every frame, every tile has its turn. The selection shows in the accent color, through `editor.outlines` (host API). On another board it starts afresh, never from the old board's picture, and keeps up to 8 boards' pictures (those on the path longest, as the editor keeps their indexes), taken back while `editor.isCurrent` says their marks are. Keep it that cheap.

## The views the canvas hosts

- The views `CanvasView` hosts, the minimap and the editing view, have the pointer where they are: over one the pointer isn't over the canvas, though AppKit tells the canvas it moved (its tracking area covers them), so the tool hears it leave (`pointerExited`), once, as it moves or is released there (the press that began editing), and nothing more until it's back on the canvas. So the select tool hovers no shape under a note's text (`editor.hovered` is only what another plugin sets there), `editor.pointerOverCanvas` is nil there, and the canvas leaves the view's cursor alone. Once editing ends, where the editing view was is the canvas again. So do the views a host puts over the canvas (the path's pill and a crumb's menu): the canvas asks the window what's at the pointer (`hitTest`), and where that isn't the canvas or a view it hosts the pointer is off it, under the arrow cursor, and back on the canvas the tool's cursor is set again.

## Testing

- For the minimap, CanvasUITests put a `CanvasView` with `showsMinimap` in an unshown window, send `canvas.minimap` mouse events, and hold its picture, kept through edits, to a fresh `MinimapView`'s, pixel for pixel.
- For board previews, drive a `BoardPreviews` over a `CanvasFile` (`preview(of:in:)`, `build(in:until:)` with a deadline already past for one chunk, far off for all; `waiting`, `keptKeys`, the mask's pixels; CanvasTests' PreviewTests), or render twice with `renderBitmap`: the first frame asks, and with `editor.boardPreviews.frameBudget` lengthened, the next (after `h.wait(0)`) draws them (SubboardsTests).
- For skeletons, render under 35% against the same editor with `skeletonZoom = 0` (host SPI), which draws in full at that zoom, as the control (StickyNotesTests' Note skeleton: bars within the text's ink rows at 30%, as long as their columns, a Japanese paragraph on the render's lines, a pill's width against its label as text, typed (bridged) text drawing what native text does (`isBridged`), a million-character paragraph drawing and costing as one that fills the box, notes 1e21 tall or wide; ArrowsTests' Arrow skeletons: the arc, not the chord or the two segments, and LabelTests' drawn test: a labelled arrow's body whole and the picture the same as without the label; SchemasTests' render tests: its edge, a bar where each line of its description is, as long as its columns (a wide character two), an empty card none, the first frame after an edit and its undo, cards 1e21 tall or wide, and drawing reading no schema); a test module without Geometry can't make a `Camera`, so it draws skeletons at 100% with `skeletonZoom` just over 1, where world units are view points (SubboardsTests' The card zoomed out); a test of what's drawn in full far out sets it to 0 (MentionsTests' memo at 20%, PreviewTests' preview too small on screen, the POC's renders). CanvasTests' Skeletons hold the core (a custom skeleton against the render either side of 35%, the default's gray and forms at 1× and 2×, a shape under a pixel the one device pixel its middle is in, even on a pixel's corner, the shape being edited in full, the switch, a drawing offset, previews asked only in full, outlines culled, outlines of skeletons and rings, `CustomSkeletonOutline`, `drawsSkeletonsDidChange` once each way) and its Skeleton text `SkeletonText` (scalars, joiners, a substring and a bridged string, fuzzed), SelectionTests' Skeletons faces off on skeletons, a face pressed in full not clicked once its shape is a skeleton by the release, the hover looked at again as the level flips (from the camera and from `skeletonZoom`) and the kept selection bounds (`selectionBoundsWorkedOut`), and CompositionTests' `skeletonsShippedTests` the shipped pieces together (every shipped shape, a subboard, notes, an arrow, a locked note, a schema card, saying nothing at 30% but the titles on the shapes, with no pill and no lock, and all in full at 35%, the note being edited in full, a mention's pill no face at 30% in the app's canvas view). `renderBitmap` at a scale other than 1 reads pixels with `pixel(_:at:scale:)`; `changedPixels` reads one pixel a point, so it holds only at 1×.
- For outlines, `EditorHarness` draws them on the shapes' edges (`editor.outlineStandoff = 0`, host SPI, as the POC's goldens are drawn), so the pixel tests written before outlines stood off, which read the selection, a hover and the hint at an edge, hold for whatever kind they draw; a test of the standoff sets it to what ships (`Style.outlineStandoff`, 4), and an editor made another way (`ShippedPlugins.makeEditor` in CompositionTests) has it already. CanvasTests' Outlines hold the core: a box's outline 4 view points off each side at 100% and 200% (constant on screen, never over the shape), corners concentric (a rounded rect's grow by the gap, a plain rect's are the gap's radius: single pixels on the diagonal), a hover's faint outline and the hint where the selection's is, a diamond grown by path arithmetic, an open shape stroked on its own path, none with a standoff of 0, the skeleton's ring zoomed out unchanged by it, the box around several off the same distance, a shape drawn away from where it is, and `outlinePath(of:)` and `outlineGap` (debug builds' `outlinesGrownByArithmetic`: a rect and a rounded rect none, a diamond one, which also tells whether `RoundedBox.of` reads the paths `CGPath(roundedRect:)` makes); SubboardsTests' The card and Skeletons hold a subboard to the core's rule (4 off its border and never over the card; zoomed out, on its frame); ValidationTests the ring 7 out of a closed shape's edge at 100% and 200%, clear of the outline's line, 3 out of an open shape's, and following the standoff; CompositionTests' ShippedTests the same ring on a note, and `outlinesShippedTests` every shipped box kind in the app's own editors (4 off the edge, corners concentric with each kind's radius, none grown by arithmetic, an arrow's on its line). ParityTests set `outlineStandoff = 0` beside `skeletonZoom`.
- For the pointer over the views the canvas hosts, CanvasUITests' Pointer suite sends a `CanvasView` editing a card (CanvasTesting) moves and releases, and reads what a probe tool heard (`PointerLog`); CanvasUITests can't import Selection, so CompositionTests' canvas view tests check `editor.hovered` over a note being edited.
