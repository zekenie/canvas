# Drawing

Board previews; what a frame may cost (the dot grid, culling, skeletons, drawing in batches, what the editor keeps); the minimap and the zoom above it; the pointer over the views the canvas hosts; and how to test each.

Related: [editor](editor.md), [benchmarks](benchmarks.md), [testing](testing.md), [plugins and kinds](plugins-and-kinds.md), [subboards](subboards.md), [titles](titles.md), [adornments](adornments.md), [validation](validation.md), [Quick Look](quick-look.md) ([every topic](../../CLAUDE.md#map)).

In the guide, `docs/plugin-system.md`: [Skeletons](../../docs/plugin-system.md#skeletons), [The core's part: board previews](../../docs/plugin-system.md#the-cores-part-board-previews), [Outlines](../../docs/plugin-system.md#outlines).

## Board previews

- Board previews (the core's `BoardPreviews`, host API `editor.boardPreviews`; `docs/plugin-system.md` "The core's part: board previews"): `context.drawBoardPreview(ofBoardOwnedBy:in:maxScale:)` draws a rough picture of the board a shape owns into a rect, fitted (no larger than `maxScale` times its size, if given: a subboard's card passes a tenth, so a board of one note isn't drawn as big as one of hundreds), and returns true, or draws nothing and returns false while it isn't built, for an empty board, and for a rect under 16 view points either way on screen. Every shape there is a silhouette of its geometry (`Silhouettes`, the core's, host API, which the minimap draws with too; a container's, a frame's, its outline stroked, `hollow`, so what it holds shows on it; a shape under 4 device pixels both ways is its bounds, under one its pixel: `Silhouettes.form`, which the default skeleton goes by too) in the color it's listed in (`BoardPreviews.tint`, which the editor sets to its `tint(of:)`: a plugin's for the shape, Colors' own color's `solid` or the one it's given, a type's, else its kind's tint, a note's system yellow, a subboard's indigo, a frame's gray; asked once a shape as the preview is built, each tint made into a color once a build, in the appearance it was asked for in, a run's `NSColor` by identity, so a build costs a dictionary lookup a shape for it), quieted toward the muted gray one with none (an arrow with no color, a reference) is drawn in (`Style.boardPreview`, the default skeleton's): Palette's `RGB.muted(by:toward:)`, `BoardPreviews.muting` of the way in OKLCH, lightness and chroma alike, the hue kept (`Style.previewTintMuting`, 70%: at full strength a symbol's color shouted from a card at a tenth of its board, the owner's "colors too bright", and at 45% still did, "even more muted?"; at 80% the warm colors ran into one beige; a test sets 0 to read a tint exactly), painted in the board's order a run of shapes of one color at a time (a fill and a stroke a run, either left out where the run has nothing to fill or stroke, so what's in front is over what's behind, whatever their colors), no text or fills of their own, into a picture at most 256 pixels on a side, premultiplied sRGB, four bytes a pixel (256 KB at most, 64 kept). Shown larger than its picture (zoomed in, or on a 2× screen, where a default card at 100% already is), the shapes in view are drawn as paths instead, sharp at any zoom (`Preview.draw`: every shape its outline, whatever its size, kept with the picture in cells of a grid over its board, at most 16 a side and some 32 shapes to a cell, each cell's shapes in runs of one color in the board's order (`PathRun`, its color an index into the preview's own `colors`), so only the cells meeting the clip are drawn, a fill and a stroke a run, runs of one color one after another, a cell's last and the next one's first, in one; lines 1.5 device pixels wide, as the picture's are its pixels), as long as the frame's shapes drawn so number no more than `pathShapes` (1,000, `Style.previewPathShapes`, reset by `endFrame()`): a shape costs a path some 1 to 2 µs, so a 10,000-shape board drawn whole that way costs more than a frame, and past the number the picture is drawn, enlarged, until zooming in leaves fewer of its shapes in view. A skeleton may ask for one (the subboard's does, as its card does); the core's default skeleton never does. A preview is keyed by board and `file.revision(of:)`, so one of a board as it was before an edit or an undo is dropped, never drawn; its colors are of a `Look`, the appearance it was asked for in (`NSAppearance.currentDrawing()`'s name) and how many times tints had changed with no commit (`tintsDidChange()`, which `editor.shapeTintsDidChange()` calls: the file's colors, a type's color its shapes take), and one asked for in another look is drawn as it is and asked for again, built in this one while it's drawn (a build of the same revision already waiting goes on in the look it was asked in, and is asked for again once done), so a card never goes back to its count for a change of color or appearance; a build that will take a kept preview's place needs no room (`endFrame()`, `keep`, which keeps the place's last frame drawn). The 64 drawn last are kept, never evicting one the frame before drew (more owners in view than that: the rest show their count, and nothing thrashes). Boards asked for are built at the start of each frame, those asked for longest ago first, a chunk of shapes at a time within 2 ms (`frameBudget`; at least 16 shapes), and the editor asks for frames (`editor.scheduler`) while any wait; what the frame just drawn didn't ask for stops waiting (`endFrame()`, and a switch of board drops it all). Keep them that cheap: `./build.sh bench` holds ten 10,000-shape subboards in view to a frame while they fill. A picture of a whole board, which is one frame (the core's `BoardPicture`: what Quick Look shows, Edit › Copy as Image copies and `canvas capture` writes), builds all its cards ask for before it's drawn ([Quick Look](quick-look.md)).

## What a frame costs

- The kept geometry is let go of by a commit's `ChangeSet` and by `editor.shapesDidChangeLayout` (a layout input changed, [editor](editor.md)): `ShapeIndex.layoutDidChange` forgets the entries of the shapes named that have one, and what looked them up, and notes the damage as a commit does, so a rename lays out the arrows that mention it and a frame still costs what changed.
- Finding shapes by where they are (the core's `SpatialGrid`, kept with the paint list in `ShapeIndex`): `editor.paintOrder(in:)` (what drawing and every overlay go by), `editor.shape(at:where:)` and `ShapeContext.shapes(mayHit:)` (the shapes a point may hit, front-most first in the document's order, which Arrows' `Connectables` asks) cost the cells a rect or a point reaches and the shapes in them, never the board's shapes: a view's rect or a point takes some 3 µs of the thread's CPU at 1,000, 10,000 and 100,000 shapes alike (`./build.sh bench`, "Spatial index benchmarks": held to 3 times the 1,000-shape board's and 10 µs over, since a pass over every extent costs ten times as much at ten times the shapes), where a hover's several searches took 2 to 5 ms of input at 100,000, and a drag from a note's dot 21. Each shape is filed under its paint position in every cell its extent covers (a cell is one and a half times the middle of a sample of the board's longer sides, at least 32 world units; the cells are in a table keyed by their coordinates, so the board has no edge and a shape dragged far away costs a cell). A search asks each shape it finds the same `CGRect.touches` and `ShapeIndex.mayHit` a pass over every extent asks of every one, so the grid only narrows which, and what it finds is what that pass finds, in paint order, each once (CanvasTests' SpatialIndexTests, over seeded runs of adds, moves, resizes, deletes, restacks, undos, redos and zooms, against the same search through every extent, debug builds' `editor.scansEveryShape`, and against an editor made afresh from the same shapes; an edge or a corner of a rect on an extent counts, as `touches` has it, whatever the rect's size or sign). A point is searched out to the greatest hit margin any kind of the registry declares (its view points divided by the zoom) and a cell more all round, against rounding. Many found are put in order by marking them in a bitmap of the paint list and reading its words, a few by sorting. A shape whose extent can't be filed by cell is on a short list every search reads: one with no bounds to cull it by (infinite, or not a number: a shape with no outline), one past a billion world units, and one that covers more cells than a shape is filed in (`SpatialGrid.mostCells`, 64: a frame round a neighborhood); a null extent (a shape kept aside) is in no cell. A rect with no bounds, or that covers more cells than a quarter of the board's shapes number, or finds more shapes than that (zoomed out as far as it goes, on a board of thousands), is a pass over every extent, as it was, and so is any search of a list of under 64 shapes, or of one with an eighth of its shapes on the short list. The grid is made by the first search after the paint list is built (some 9 to 12 ms at 100,000 shapes), filed again as a commit's stale extents are worked out (a shape that stays in the cells it was in costs two comparisons), let go when a commit changes the extents of more than an eighth of the board's shapes (a drag of all of it: made again by the next search) and when the paint list is built again, and made again when far more shapes are wide than when it was made. A commit that only adds shapes at the top of the stack doesn't build the paint list again (`ShapeIndex.appendAdded`): its plain shapes are put at the end of it, and an anchored shape (an arrow) above the last plain one, which is last too, and their extents are worked out and filed by the next search (some 0.03 ms at 100,000, where building the list took 40 ms, and the grid 9 more). Shapes that come anywhere else in the stack, any that go or move in it, an undo or a redo (anything may refer to a shape it brings back: an arrow bound to it is painted above it then), a shape from another board, an anchored shape above a plain shape that isn't the last, anything painted on top (an anchored shape with nothing to be above), and a plain shape added while some shape is on top, build it again.
- A `LaidOutBox` shape costs a frame one lookup in a dictionary by id when its geometry is worked out again (a kept size, nothing erased), and its kind's arithmetic once, the first time it is asked (a board first shown asks every shape of such kinds once; `./build.sh bench` holds what 10,000 stretches by a hundred gauges add to their board's first frame, and a gauge they all name changed, its commit and the frame after, which works out every geometry again as any commit that changes them all does: [benchmarks](benchmarks.md)). A drag asks no kind and no provider: the kept size holds across a move. A skeleton asks no provider: its paper is the kept frame (`context.shapes.frame(of:)`). A board preview is as stored (a plain context lays nothing out): a shape laid out smaller than it stores is drawn in its card's preview at the box it stores, and arrows in the preview stop at that edge, as one laid out larger is (the preview asks no provider, 2 ms a frame); the minimap's silhouettes, the board's bounds (Zoom to Fit, a first visit, the minimap's fit) and a Quick Look picture's size follow the laid-out frame through the kept extent: [editor](editor.md).
- A frame costs what's in view and what changed, never the whole board (`./build.sh bench` holds 10,000 shapes to 16 ms, zoomed out as far as 10% goes too, and 10,000 all out of view, selected or not, to 1 ms over an empty board; an outline standing off a closed shape adds to each outlined shape in view two reads of its path's end points and a rounded rect in place of the path, grown directly, and zoomed out, where ⌘A outlines thousands, none: the rings are on the frames as before). The dot grid under the shapes (`DotGrid`, the core's) is drawn from tiles of dots filled once, copied at whole device pixels, into a bitmap through a clip of the pixels the dots touch (a bitmap composites a copy's clear pixels as dearly as its dots) and into any other context unclipped (AppKit's context for a view's `draw(_:)` records a display list, which Core Animation renders on the GPU while the window is on screen, making a clip of a rect per dot into a region again for every copy: some 400 ms a frame), in the context's own pixel layout and space (sRGB for one no 8-bit bitmap takes, an extended range's): pixel for pixel the path of an ellipse per dot it replaced wherever a whole number of dots spans whole pixels (100%, and the POC's goldens at 60% and 15%, which hold it), and elsewhere each copy within an eighth of a pixel (a tile for each quarter-pixel step its copies need); kept while the camera doesn't move, some 1 to 1.5 ms of a frame at 1200 × 800 on Retina (the path took 3 to 7), CanvasTests' GridTests against that path (CanvasTesting's `drawGridAsEllipses`, which the benchmark reports beside it) and unclipped against clipped byte for byte (debug builds' `editor.gridClipOverride`, and `gridFramesClipped` and `gridFramesUnclipped` for which a frame did; CanvasUITests' Display suite holds a canvas the window displays to the unclipped one). A shape's shadow (`RenderContext.fillWithShadow`: 6 world units of blur, `Style.shapeShadowBlur`, scaled by the zoom by hand) is left out where its fill hides it (the core's `ShapeShadows`): an opaque color, no pattern, the context's alpha and blend mode left as the fresh state has them (neither can be read back), and a path holding all the context draws into (its clip's box, a device pixel more for an edge's antialiasing; a comparison of rects first, so only a shape bigger than the view does path arithmetic, `CGPath.subtracting`), as a shape zoomed in past the view's edges does; the blur cost what the shape covers on screen, some 24 ms a frame at 800% on a 2× bitmap, where on screen the GPU blurs it (measured on screen, 1200 × 800 at 800%: 60 fps, none dropped, some 0.7 ms of the process's CPU a frame, with an edge or a corner in view too). One with an edge in view still blurs on the CPU (some 8 to 15 ms at 800%, reported); drawing it in tiles, or clipping its path to the view, cut that but cost more on screen (0.5 to 0.7 ms more of the main thread, or 2 to 2.7 ms of the process's CPU a frame), and capping the blur would change the look. Shapes more than 40 world units out of view (`Style.drawingPad`, for a shadow at the edge, grown by the farthest drawing offset, `worldDrawingReach`, for a shape shaken into view) don't draw, and outlines are asked for and stroked only for shapes whose kept extent meets that area (a plugin's rects too), so ⌘A costs what's in view in strokes whatever the board holds: a list of shapes outlined (the selection, the faint ones, a tinted group) of 32 shapes or fewer, or with half or more of them in view, is looked at shape by shape for its kept extent (nothing copied or allocated); a longer one with fewer in view is outlined by asking each shape in view, found in the grid, whether it is in the list (`OutlineMembers`: a set of the list's ids made once and kept while the list is the same, found by comparing the lists, which for the one a frame is handed again is a comparison of the storage they share, the last four kept; `editor.outlines` hands Selection's list over as it is, and the hint is stroked beside it, not appended, so a selection of every shape is never copied for a frame), so a shape out of view costs nothing, selected or not: 100,000 selected, all out of view, add nothing measurable to an empty board's frame (16 ms before), and a pan's frame with all of them selected at 100% costs 10 ms where nothing selected costs 8 to 9 (49 before). A pan works the selection's box out again only while some kind's geometry reads the camera (`editor.geometriesReadCamera`, none of the shipped kinds': [selection](selection.md)). Tinted groups (`Outlines.tinted`, a plugin's own colors: Neighbors' lights; `TintedOutlines`: a color, shapes and a `ring`; `Outlines` and `TintedOutlines` are `Equatable`, so a plugin that keeps what it hands over asks for a frame only when it changed) cost the same, each group a pass of its own, stroked first, under the faint and the full outlines (so a selected shape's accent line shows over its tint) and appended by `editor.outlines` in the order the plugins gave them: `Style.tintedIndicatorWidth` (2 view points, opaque) centered where the selection's line stands (`outlineStandoff` off a closed shape, so a quarter of a point of tint either side of the selection's 1.5), each ring past the first `Style.tintedRingGap` (2) and a line further out (`strokeOutlines`' `outset`, in view points, divided by the zoom, part of the standoff the area reaches: the rings' centers are 4, 8 and 12 off a closed shape, whole pixels at 1× and 2×), a skeleton's ring on its frame grown by the same (a closed outline that isn't a rect is a ring on its frame once it's past the first ring, since growing it costs far more), an open shape on its own path whatever the ring. A shape that's outlined in full too (selected, or the hint) has its tint one ring further out, clear of the selection's line, which lies over most of ring 0, zoomed out too: looked up only for the shapes that meet the area, a few outlined in full compared one by one (as `faint(_:leavingOut:)` does) and many through one set made when first asked, and nothing at all while no group or no full outline is there (`TintedRings`, made once a frame that has a group, and what `OverlayContext` carries). An open shape of a kind drawn as a line (`StrokedShape`, found once by the registry as `skeletonOutlines` is: an arrow, `ArrowStyle.strokeWidth`) is covered, not outlined along its middle: in full its tinted outline is stroked `max(tintedIndicatorWidth, width × zoom + tintedCoverPad (1))` view points wide, round, over its line and its heads (its `indicatorPath` holds them) rather than a stripe inside a thick line at 200% and wider than a thin one at 50%; zoomed out, where its skeleton is a line a point wide, the plain width; the selection's and a hover's stay thin. A faint outline beside tinted ones is the shape they're about, and is `Style.tintedFaintIndicatorOpacity` (0.85) instead of `faintIndicatorOpacity` (0.6) so it reads above them. `OverlayContext.tintedOutset(of:)` is how far the outermost tinted ring round a closed shape stands beyond ring 0 this frame, in view points (a ring is `tintedRingPitch`, 4, counting the one further out of a selected shape's tint; none for a shape with one ring or none, or an open one), worked out the first time any shape is asked, one pass over what's tinted, so an overlay that decorates outside the outline stands outside them (Validation's ring: [validation](validation.md)), and `greatestTintedOutset` is the most it could be, one pass over the groups, for culling; 10,000 lit shapes out of view add some 0.35 ms to a frame and in view at 10% some 7 ms, ⌘A's rings' cost. Below 35% every shape but the one being edited is its skeleton (`CustomSkeleton`, else the default): a few fills, no text, shadows or pictures of its own, reading nothing that parses or lays out text, since a frame there draws thousands (what skeletons saved, as measured: `docs/plugin-system.md`, "Skeletons"). Shipped: a note's frame in its color's paper (`context.colors(of:)?.paper`, the one lookup a shape; yellow without a color; a type's note in its white paper whatever its color, resolved once for the appearance drawn, and in the light a hairline just inside its edge standing for the shadow, which parted its white from a frame's: [types](types.md)) with its text greeked by the core's `SkeletonTextBars` (paragraphs' lines counted, never laid out, by any kind's text: 0.52 em a column as `SkeletonText` counts them, calibrated once against string drawing, `SkeletonTextMetrics`; runs of text and inline runs a number of columns wide, `SkeletonTextRun`, which a kind's own runs say they are, `SkeletonTextRunConvertible`: a mention as wide as its pill by its stored label, Mentions' `MentionRun`, in columns, read only as far as a pill grows; blank or all-space paragraphs blank lines, CR, LF or CR LF ending one, a paragraph read only until it fills the lines left once something in it shows, text typed in the app (bridged UTF-16) read 64 code units at a time on the stack, at most the box's lines (clamped to 100,000, so any height counts in an `Int`), each bar over its line's x-height, 6.5 to 15 of 19), a subboard's fills and preview, an arrow's arc ([arrows follow tldraw](arrows.md)). Draw what's per shape zoomed out in batches of rects or lines, in paint order a run of one look at a time: Core Graphics fills those in one path for a fraction of each alone, while many curves in one path, or rounded rects stroked together, cost more than each alone (Validation and the core fill rings). The editor keeps each shape's geometry and extent, and the paint order (`ShapeIndex`), and redoes them for what each commit's `ChangeSet` names; it keeps the board's bounds too, and where each commit moved or reshaped shapes (damage: where the shapes it touched, and those whose geometry followed them, were and are), for pictures of the board kept from frame to frame (host API: `shapeBounds`, `damageMark`, `damage(since:)`). A render may also read the editor's services (`RenderContext.service(_:)`, as `EditContext.service(_:)` lends an editing view's; made once per frame, `lend`), and nothing observes a render, so a lending service calls `editor.setNeedsDisplay()` when what it lends changes (CanvasTests' RenderLendTests). So a definition looks at other shapes only through its `ShapeContext` (`shape(_:)`, `geometry(of:)`, `hitTest(_:shape:)`: they're tracked, all of `document` or `camera` isn't); a `hitTest` that reaches past the outline declares `hitMargin`; a paint anchor follows from its shape's props and the document's order; per-frame code (overlays) goes through `editor.paintOrder(in:)` with `context.visibleRect`; and a service keeps per-shape state and redoes the shapes a change set names, as `ValidationEngine`, `ContentsState` and `AdornmentState` do.

## The minimap

- The minimap (CanvasUI's `MinimapView`, which `CanvasView.showsMinimap` hosts in the bottom-left corner of the part of the canvas the host's chrome leaves clear (`editor.coveredEdges`: beside the sidebar while it shows, gliding to the window's corner with it as it goes, `editorCoveredEdgesDidChange`; `MinimapStyle.frame(in:)`), floating as the window's chrome does (`ChromeStyle`: its frame the chrome's surface, a float's corners, a hairline and the float's shadow, the board drawn in a well of the canvas's own color 3 points in, its corners concentric; `MinimapStyle.boardArea(in:)`, the well less 3 points of padding, is what the board is fitted to: 6 points in from the minimap's edge, as before the well, since a kept picture is held pixel for pixel to a fresh one at that fit); View › Show Minimap, ⌃⌘M, remembered per board in `BoardViewState` and `BoardViewMemory`; hidden, its corner the canvas's, while the board has nothing to show, `shapeBounds` null, and faded in with the first shape, once the press that placed it under the minimap, or its editing, is done, at once on another board) works as tldraw's: the board and the viewport fitted together; a click beside the viewport eases the camera there over 320 ms, a drag moves it within tldraw's clamp, a double-click centers exactly. It draws every shape as a rough silhouette of its geometry in one color (the core's `Silhouettes`; a container, a frame, its outline, so what it holds shows), never its render (no text, no colors), into a bitmap of tiles kept from frame to frame (`MinimapPicture`): damage makes tiles stale, and each frame draws again those stale longest, within its budget (4 ms, and a tile at least), so a frame spends at most that on the picture however big the board, and whatever goes stale every frame, every tile has its turn. The selection shows in the accent color, and a hover's faint outline lighter, through `editor.outlines` (host API), and what a plugin outlines in colors of its own (`Outlines.tinted`) in those, each group's silhouettes first, under both, in the order they came, so what's lit off screen shows there. While any group is drawn the rest recedes and what's lit stands out: the minimap's background is laid over the picture at 40% (`MinimapStyle.litDim`), and what's outlined (tinted, faint and selected) is drawn after the viewport's veil, not under it, so a lit fill off screen isn't dimmed by the veil; with nothing tinted, neither (the picture and the order are as they were, pixel for pixel). A shape in several groups shows each, as the canvas draws a ring for each: every group's fill for it is inset a step further than the one before, a step `MinimapStyle.litInset` (1.6 minimap points) and never more than `litInsetMost` (30%) of its smaller side on a side, so one both pointed to and pointing back is a frame of one color round a block of the other (a tinted group's steps are its ring; a selected or hovered shape's, one more than the outermost ring that tints it, as the canvas draws its tint one ring out, so its accent shows inside its tint; a closed outline is scaled in about the middle of its bounds, so a disc stays a disc; an open shape is a line, and the later group's is over the earlier's) (10,000 lit add some 4 ms to the minimap's drawing, as 10,000 selected do, and some 8 when all 10,000 are selected too, each one's accent inset inside its tint: a set of the selected and a smaller fill for each, reported by the benchmark, not budgeted). On another board it starts afresh, never from the old board's picture, and keeps up to 8 boards' pictures (those on the path longest, as the editor keeps their indexes), taken back while `editor.isCurrent` says their marks are. Keep it that cheap.
- The zoom above it (CanvasUI's `ZoomIndicator`, `CanvasView.zoomIndicator`, always hosted, hidden at rest): "44%", `editor.zoomPercentage` (where the camera's headed, so a zoom command's target from its first frame), in the look of the minimap's frame (a capsule, as short as it is), 8 points above the minimap with their left edges in line, or in its corner while it's off or hidden. A change of zoom on the board shown shows it at once (a pinch, a ⌘-scroll, a zoom command, a fit, a reveal); it stays while the zoom goes on changing, fades out over 300 ms a second after the last change (on `editor.scheduler`), and is hidden once faded, so a snapshot of the canvas leaves it out. A pan doesn't show it, nor another board shown (it hides at once), nor a change before the canvas has a size, nor a window opening at its remembered zoom (`BoardDocument` sets the camera before the window makes its canvas, which takes the zoom it's made at as no change). It's placed again on every redraw while it shows, so it follows the minimap showing or hiding. When it begins to fade, VoiceOver hears the zoom it came to ("Zoom 44%"), once. It takes no clicks (`hitTest` is nil). There's no zoom menu: zooming is the gestures, and View's items and their keys. The canvas asks it with every redraw (`zoomMayHaveChanged`, a comparison).

## The views the canvas hosts

- The views `CanvasView` hosts, the minimap and the editing view, have the pointer where they are: over one the pointer isn't over the canvas, though AppKit tells the canvas it moved (its tracking area covers them), so the tool hears it leave (`pointerExited`), once, as it moves or is released there (the press that began editing), and nothing more until it's back on the canvas. So the select tool hovers no shape under a note's text (`editor.hovered` is only what another plugin sets there), `editor.pointerOverCanvas` is nil there, and the canvas leaves the view's cursor alone. Once editing ends, where the editing view was is the canvas again. So do the views a host puts over the canvas (the path's pill and a crumb's menu): the canvas asks the window what's at the pointer (`hitTest`), and where that isn't the canvas or a view it hosts the pointer is off it, under the arrow cursor, and back on the canvas the tool's cursor is set again.

## Testing

- For the minimap, CanvasUITests put a `CanvasView` with `showsMinimap` in an unshown window, send `canvas.minimap` mouse events, and hold its picture, kept through edits, to a fresh `MinimapView`'s, pixel for pixel. A shape outlined in a color of its own is filled in that color, wherever it is, under the faint and selected ones, one in several groups showing each, inside the one before (the Minimap suite's tinted tests: the dim to 40% of the background while anything is lit and none otherwise, a lit fill off screen whole and a selected one over the veil only while something is lit, and the insets by 1.6 points a step, the accent inside a tint, and the cap on a few points across). Their Zoom indicator suite sets `editor.camera` and reads `canvas.zoomIndicator` (`isShowing`, `title`, `isHidden`, its frame and the layer's opacity animation), the fade on `h.wait`. - For board previews, drive a `BoardPreviews` over a `CanvasFile` (`preview(of:in:)`, `build(in:until:)` with a deadline already past for one chunk, far off for all; `waiting`, `keptKeys`, the picture's pixels, read back through an sRGB context of the test's own; CanvasTests' PreviewTests), or render twice with `renderBitmap`: the first frame asks, and with `editor.boardPreviews.frameBudget` lengthened, the next (after `h.wait(0)`) draws them (SubboardsTests).
