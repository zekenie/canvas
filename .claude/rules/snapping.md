# Snapping

Snapping what a drag moves, and the sides a resize moves, to the frames of the shapes in view: targets, ties, modifiers, guides and cost.

Related: [editor](editor.md), [selection](selection.md), [plugins and kinds](plugins-and-kinds.md) ([every topic](../../CLAUDE.md#map)).

In the guide, `docs/plugin-system.md`: [Snapping](../../docs/plugin-system.md#snapping).

## Architecture

- Snapping (platform, a thing you do, on the core alone; `docs/plugin-system.md`, "Snapping"): what a drag moves (a `TranslatingSession`: the selection dragged, a shape just placed) snaps to the frames of the shapes in view, as one rect (the frames it moves, together, as the drag began), along each axis on its own, to the nearest within 8 view points (tldraw's `snapThreshold`) of: an edge or its middle onto another frame's (tldraw's corners and center); its middle onto the middle of the space between two neighbors, if it fits there; its far or near edge where the space between two neighbors would repeat before or after them (tldraw's bounds snapping, but spaces only between neighbors, each frame's nearest past either edge that overlaps it across the axis, within 64 frames along it, and only for what shares their stretch across by more than the threshold, so a snap along the other axis never leaves it without its guide; `SnapTargets`, pure, world units). Ties go to edges and middles, then the near edge. It lands exactly on the line (the core's translation adjuster moves each box by exactly what it says on a changed axis), and along the other axis follows the pointer exactly. Always on, unlike tldraw's (on only while ⌘ is held), once the pointer is past the drag threshold from the press, so a click's lone box moves no more than the pointer; ⌘ or ⌃ held once the drag is under way lets what moves follow the pointer exactly, at once (Keynote's ⌘; either frees an arrow's end too; at the press ⌘ on a shape starts a marquee and ⌃ is the secondary click), and ⇧, which keeps the drag to one axis (the core's), snaps and guides along that axis alone (`SnapTargets.snap(_:movedBy:within:only:)`). Kinds with a frame (`BoxShapeProps`) snap and are snapped to by the frame they are laid out in (`context.frame(of:)`, [editor](editor.md): a note grown by its type snaps by its laid-out edges, and others to them; the frames it moves are taken together from one `editor.shapeContext`), locked ones too (they stay put, and aren't part of what moves); arrows and lines neither (tldraw's arrows can't be snapped to, `canSnap`). What's in view is read when the drag first snaps, again once the camera moves, a shape the drag doesn't move changes (`SnapState`, a service) or shapes are laid out again with no commit (`editor.layoutRevision`, checked at each event, which also takes the frames it moves together again); no view (`viewportSize` zero, headless), nothing to snap to. Guides (`editor.snapGuides`: `.alignment`, a line through the corners and centers on it with a ✕ at each; `.spacing`, equal spaces side by side with ticks, those in a row too; tldraw's look, in its snap red) draw with the session. Reading 10,000 frames in view costs some 6 ms, each event after 0.12 ms (`./build.sh bench`).
- A resize snaps too (`SnapResize`, the core's resize adjuster, `registrar.resizeAdjuster`, which Selection's `ResizingSession` asks for as a handle's drag begins: [editor](editor.md), [selection](selection.md)): the sides the drag moves, along the ways it changes the box (`ResizeScaling.changesWidth`, `changesHeight`), go onto the nearest edge or middle of the frames in view but its own within the same 8 view points, no spaces (tldraw's resize snapping, `SnapTargets.snap(_:resizedFrom:about:along:inProportion:minimum:room:within:)`, pure). A side moves when it isn't at the anchor (`ResizeScaling.anchor`, the point of the box as it began that stays put): the dragged side, or with ⌥ both about the middle, the one nearer a line snapping and the other mirroring it. Each way snaps on its own; in proportion (⇧, `keepsProportions`) one scale, so the side nearest a line of all that move goes onto it and the rest follow. A line that would leave it under the minimum along a way it changes, or past its room (inside its container's edge), isn't snapped to, and a further one within the threshold may be. Ties go to x, then the near side. Guides are alignments of its two edges along each way it changes, the side that stays too (tldraw's). ⌘ and ⌃ free it as they free a drag; the threshold is behind it (the handle's press waits for it); what's in view (`FramesInView`, the drag's too) is read again once the camera or layout moves or a shape other than the one resized changes (`SnapState`, which holds the drag or the resize, one at a time). A frame's own contents are targets: its edge on one exactly leaves it inside, and the release's settle leaves it be. ⌥ and an arrow resize unsnapped, as nudges move.

## Testing

- For snapping, SnappingTests hold `SnapTargets` pure and drag CanvasTesting's boxes with a move tool of their own (`snapHarness`, `movePlugin(moves)`: a press starts a `TranslatingSession` from the press, the select tool's lone box, since SnappingTests can't use Selection) in a 1000 × 800 view at 100% (a test that snaps needs `viewportDidResize`: with no view nothing is in view), reading `editor.snapGuides`; a key let go counts as held until `h.wait(150)`. SnappingTests' ResizeTargetsTests hold the resize snapping pure (by a side, a corner, from the center, in proportion, its minimum and its room). CanvasTests' TranslationAdjusterTests hold the core's hook with a fixture adjuster and the resize adjuster's claim, SelectionTests' ResizeTests the resize asking it (`resizerPlugin`), and CompositionTests' SnappingShippedTests the shipped pieces through the select tool (frames resized by a side and a corner among them).
