# Moving

Moving: `/move-to` and `/move-near`, slash commands that move the shapes they're typed for (the shape whose text they're typed in, or the shapes selected, several as one block) into a frame, onto a subboard's board, or beside a shape, on any board of the file; Move To ▸, and the table of contents' drops that move shapes into a frame, onto a subboard's board or out of frames (`ContentsMoves`); what each takes, where it lands (`canvas draw`'s placing), the commit, the arrows a move keeps through reference pills, and going where the shapes went.

Related: [placing](placing.md), [slash commands](slash-commands.md), [table of contents](table-of-contents.md), [mentions](mentions.md), [command line](command-line.md), [containers](containers.md), [boards in a file](boards-in-a-file.md), [transform](transform.md), [locking](locking.md), [arrows](arrows.md), [references](references.md) ([every topic](../../CLAUDE.md#map)).

In the guide, `docs/plugin-system.md`: [Moving](../../docs/plugin-system.md#moving).

## Architecture

- Moving (platform, a thing you do: no kind opts into it and nothing imports it, as with Jump To and `Examples/Reordering`; plugin id `moving`) is two slash commands on the kinds with a box ([slash commands](slash-commands.md)), and the table of contents' `ContentsMoves` (below), on SlashCommands, ContextMenus, TableOfContents, Draw, Boards (Move To's paths), Selection (going where the shapes went) and Canvas: `MovingPlugin` (its only public type) registers them in `registrar.forShapes(adopting: .box) { $0.slashCommands { … } }`, on the kinds whose props are `BoxShapeProps` (the kinds a drag moves; the core's capability `.box`, "with a box"; a link, a tag or an arrow offers neither), `SlashCommand.together("move-to", argument: "@frame or subboard", kinds: { $0.containingKinds.union($0.boardOwningKinds) }, shapes:, step: "Move", perform:)` and `SlashCommand.together("move-near", shapes:, step: "Move", perform:)` (reach `together`, [slash commands](slash-commands.md): each is handed every shape it acts on at once, since several are moved as one block, which the one-shape form run on each shape would not do), so the report's line is `moving /move-to /move-near → every shape with a box · CanvasContextMenu → every shape with a box · ContentsMoves`, a note's `/` list ends `title, lock, public, move-to, move-near` (`MovingPlugin()` is right before `ReorderingPlugin()` in the shipped list, after every plugin with a command for other kinds), and both steps are "Move", a drag's name (`MovingStyle.stepName`). A list with it needs `SlashCommandsPlugin()` and `TableOfContentsPlugin()` (`ownerNotInstalled`). Typed in a note's editor, the `@` picker on the line lists what the command takes ([mentions](mentions.md)): for `/move-to` only the titled frames and subboards (its `kinds`, members kept as a scope's, so it costs what they are), with nothing typed every one it takes on every board (the board shown's nearest first, then the boards it's inside, then the others, the nearest board first), for `/move-near` every shape; Return picks, a second Return runs the line. Both work across the file's boards, never only the board shown. Typed in the command bar for the shapes selected (`/` on the canvas, its field Mentions' text, so `@` names the place there too: [slash commands](slash-commands.md)), they act on the selected shapes that have a box, as one block, the `@` picker listing what takes them all, nearest their middle first: a selected shape with none (a link, an arrow) is not among them (`1 of 2`), nor is a read-only one (neither command is a declared one, so a locked shape in a selection is never offered them, and never moves).
- **What each takes** (the `shapes:` rule, `Moving.swift`, also what the picker lists), given the shapes the command acts on (`owners`: the one a text's line is on, or the selected shapes it reaches): `/move-to`'s is `admitsInto(_:for:in:)`, asked only of frames and subboards: the target is a place (`movePlace(of:in:)`, which is `contentsPlace(of:on:in:)`, the function Move To ▸ makes its places with too, of a shape anywhere in the file the app can show, `editor.shape(inFile:)`, not kept aside: a shape that owns a board is that board, else a shape that holds shapes is the inside of it on the board it is on, as draw's `in` prefers a subboard; a frame on another board is a place on that board) and `Drop(owners, to: place, in:)` is not nil. So the command, Move To ▸ and the table of contents' drops refuse and land alike, for one shape and for several; `Drop`'s refusals are below. What `Drop` does that `/move-to` did otherwise for one shape, before it went by this rule: a shape nested in the frame it is moved to leaves the nested one, beside it, kept in the frame it is moved to (it used to be put at that frame's top-left, clear of what it holds); the subboard whose board is shown takes a shape inside a frame there, out beside the outermost frame (and still none on the board directly); the frame's size is asked only of shapes coming in from elsewhere; and a block that the in-or-out rule would leave out of the place, put down over a frame nested in it with no room clear of that frame, is not moved at all (it used to end up in the nested frame). `/move-near`'s is `admitsNear(_:for:in:)`, which the picker asks of every shape as the person types (no kinds), so it costs next to nothing: not one of the shapes and, only for the shapes among them that hold shapes or own a board (`takesAlong`, rare: a note does neither), nothing that goes with them (one set of what moves, those shapes and what they hold at any depth, against the target and the file's path to its board: `goesWith(_:onto:moving:in:)`); a read-only target is fine (it doesn't change), and whether its board takes shapes is the move's to say. Each `perform` asks its rule again (`Move.into` is `Drop`'s own, `Move.near` asks `admitsNear`): a target it no longer takes, or one gone, moves nothing. What `/move-to`'s rule costs, asked of every titled frame and subboard a picker ranks: two `Block`s and a few lookups a frame (about 17 µs for one shape and 100 µs for ten in a debug build), where `/move-near`'s is a set of ids.
- **Where it lands** is [placing](placing.md)'s (Draw's placement for plugins), Moving's solver moved out so any plugin can use it (`Landing.swift` keeps only the `Block` glue: `Surroundings(on:moving: block, in:)`, which hands it the block's box size, its shapes as the ones carried and what they hold as `held`). `/move-to` asks `inside` (a frame) or `anywhere` (a subboard's board), `/move-near` `near`, the drops all three; what each does, and why, is there.
- **The commit** (`Move.swift`, `Block.commit`): one `editor.transactWhole("Move")` with the core's tools as draw's commit uses them, the shape moved by where it lands less where it is (what it holds goes along, read-only or not). Landing on the board shown: `translate([id], by:)`, `move(_:above:)` each zone and F that paints above it, then `settle([id])`. On another board: the arrows that would cross are kept through pills first (below), then `move([id], to:)`, so what's bound to it across the edge and could not be kept lets go where it was drawn (an arrow's end stays where the shape was, as after a cut, not where it lands, a spot on the board shown it never reaches), then `translate([id], by:on:)` there (the core's, for this) and `settle([id], on:)` of the shapes alone, then `settle` of the new pills on their own, so that however many pills a move brings they never change where the shapes belong (a block dropped into a narrow frame stays in it). Anything refused (the shape read-only, a read-only container settling would put it in, the read-only owner of the board it goes onto, a read-only shape among what a container would take onto another board, which `translate` carries on its board but `move(_:to:)` refuses) gives the whole move up (`editor.transactWhole`), so nothing commits and no step opens, and the refused shapes are heard (`editor.refuseChanges(to:)`: they shake), as Transform's turns are. After a commit on the board shown: a shape being edited that moved is put down (`endEditing(discarding: false)`, as a `canvas draw` that moves it ends it), then what moved glides from where it was (`editor.glide`: the shape and what it holds by the translation, plus settle's nudges). Onto another board: the shape is gone from the board shown (the core ends its editing), nothing glides, and the step, made on the board shown, holds both boards whole, so Undo Move puts them back exactly (undo shows that board first). Then the editor goes where the shapes went (below). A move that lands where the shape is commits nothing: no step, editing goes on, and the editor goes nowhere (`editor.revision` unchanged).
- **Arrows kept through pills** (`Block.commit` asks Draw's `CrossingArrows`, `Sources/Draw/DrawMoves.swift`, which Clipboard's cut and paste can ask later; the owner's decision 13 of the engineering kit, a departure from tldraw: [arrows](arrows.md)): a move onto another board lets go of an arrow bound to a shape that moves and one that stays, as tldraw's does, unless it is kept: **an arrow goes where its start goes**. Only the connector's arrows count (the kind `drawEdges` names, bound at both ends: Moving names no kind), and not one with an end at a part (a key at a row: `boundParts`; a pill would stand for the whole shape and lose the row, so the move lets it go as tldraw's does, until pills stand for rows, K10), judged against what moves with what it carries (`Block.carried`). If the start moves, the arrow moves with it and its end becomes a reference pill for the shape that stays (read as what it stands for if it is a pill itself, since a pill never stands for a pill), put down beside the start where it lands, right of it, as `canvas draw` and ⌘L's pick put one (`StandInRoute`: the landing against the start's planned box, `Surroundings.landing(of:at:planned:avoiding:)`, clear of what moves and of the pills planned already, a second pill for the same start in its column below the first); if the end moves, the arrow stays and its end becomes a pill for the shape that moved, beside the start on the board it stays on, with the movers left out of what is in the way. A pill for the same shape already on the board the arrow ends up on is reused (in the same container the start is in, `Sharing.any`), and so is the shape itself if it is there (an end that was a pill for a shape on the destination goes straight to the shape), rather than making another; one pill serves every arrow to the same shape in the move. A move never makes a shape public (the kind stands for it only as it is): an end no pill may stand for, a read-only arrow, a pill that would sit in a read-only frame, or a board shown that cannot take the new pill, lets go as it did before this. It is the one "Move" transaction, nothing in the core: `rebind(in:)` writes before the move (the pills in, on the board shown and then on the board the arrow is on; the arrow's end re-pointed with `remappingReferences`, which keeps the binding's anchor; for an arrow that goes with its start, the pill goes with it, so it is bound only to shapes that move and the core's carry rule moves it, `rodeAlong`), and `Rebound.finish(in:)` right after it re-points an arrow that rode on a stand-in to the pill or shape the destination already had, takes the stand-in away, and puts the pills that stay on the origin down by the in-or-out rule, with the shapes gone (before the move a container that moves could take one with it, and the arrow would let go); the pills made for the destination are in the move's `along` and in `placed`: already where they land, so the commit translates none of them and settles them with the shapes. Undo Move takes it all back on every board, exactly. Moving asks only for a pill's label (Mentions', as ⌘L's pick does). The pills are placed as a pick's are (`StandInRoute.spot` and `landing`) but not through `placePill`, which reads the board shown and a start that is on it, where a move's pill is for a board the shapes have not reached; a pill made for a shape that moves is fitted to the place the shape has now, and the place line, drawn live, folds to the width it has. A pill a move leaves with no arrow stays (a hand-made one cannot be told apart).
- **Shapes that move together** (`Block`, `Move.swift`): on one board the app can show (any, not only the board shown), each with a box and not kept aside, back to front, less those another of them holds (it takes them along, `carried`), placed as one by the box around them all (`box`: the frames they are laid out in, `editor.frame(inFile:)`, which a shape on a board that isn't shown has too, so a note grown by its type is landed with room for what is drawn, and the delta is between origins, which a laid-out frame shares with its stored box), so they keep where they are to each other; a command's are the shapes it acts on (`Move`, `Move.swift`: those ids, and a `Block` of them, which `/move-near` lands; `/move-to` hands the ids to `Drop`, which makes its own). `Surroundings(on:moving:in:)` (Landing.swift) reads the destination with the block's shapes left out on their own board and what they hold carried. `Block.commit(_:finish:)` is the commit above for any of them from any board: landing on their own board (shown or not), `translate(_:by:on:)`, raised over the zones (`move(_:above:on:)`: when any of them is below one, all of them, together, in their order), `settle(_:on:)`; onto another, `move(_:to:)` from the board shown or `move(_:from:to:)` from another, then the same there; `finish` last in the transaction (the table of contents' restack), which throws to drop it all; glides only from the board shown onto it; then goes where they went. Moving passes boxes to `move`, and writes no rule for what rides: an arrow bound only to shapes that move goes with them, the core's rule in `move(_:to:)` ([boards in a file](boards-in-a-file.md)), so two related notes moved together keep their arrow; the core says what rode (`transaction.rodeAlong`) and the commit translates those with the boxes (`translate(ids.union(transaction.rodeAlong), …)`), so an arrow's free end lands as far from its note as it was. ArrowsTests' `ArrowMovingOntoBoardsTests` hold it with the real arrow for a move off a board that is not shown, onto the board shown or a third; CompositionTests' `movingShippedTests` hold it through `/move-to` (the table of contents' drops share `Block.commit`).
- **The table of contents' drops** (`Dropping.swift`, `Drop`; [table of contents](table-of-contents.md)): `registrar.contentsMoves(admits:move:)` with `Drop(shapes, to: place, in:)`, which is nil for shapes not all on one board, kept aside or with no box (`Block`), read-only, all in the place already, a place that goes with them (its container or neighbor one of them or in one, its board under one of them), a board the editor can't show or that's read-only (a locked subboard's, at any depth: `editor.isReadOnly(board:)`), or a container (not opaque, `isContainer`, not read-only) too small for the block coming in from elsewhere (by the block's laid-out frames). `/move-to` is this rule (`Move.into` is `Drop(ids, to: place, in:).commit()`), and so is Move To ▸. Those not in the place (`block`) land one way (`Way`): **into** the container as `/move-to` puts a shape in a frame (`inside`, the container on the place's board), **onto** another board as `/move-to` puts one on a subboard's (`anywhere`), or **beside** the outermost container they leave (`outermostLeft`: going to a container they're in at any depth, or the board, from inside one; the first's chain decides for the block) as `/move-near` puts one beside it, kept within the container they go to if they go to one (`near(_:within:)`: each side's problem held by it, directly; no side placed there, `inside` it), on their own board; then every shape dropped (`dropped`) is restacked where the drop says (`ContentsPlace.restack`), in the same step, Move, unless the in-or-out rule left what moved out of the place (no room there clear of what it holds, put down over a frame in it, which takes them): then the step throws (`Transaction.Refused`, naming none) and nothing changes. `goesWith(_:onto:moving:in:)` is the one check of what goes with a move, the commands' and the drops'. Asked as a drag moves over the rows, `Drop.init` lays nothing out; `commit` does.
- **Move To ▸** (`MoveToMenu.swift`): the selection's context menu, which is also the menu bar's Shape menu, holds a Move To submenu on every selection of shapes with boxes (`contextMenuForCanvas` under `forShapes(adopting: .box)`, its own group `MoveToMenuGroup`, `.before(StateMenuGroup.self)`: after what changes what a shape is, right before Lock and Delete; Moving imports ContextMenus). It lists the places `Drop(shapes, to: place, in:)` admits for the selection, the same refusals as a table of contents drop: the frames and subboards of the board shown, nearest the selection's middle first, then the boards that enclose the one shown, innermost first, at most `MovingStyle.menuLimit` (20); no submenu where none admits it. A frame is named as its row is (`tableOfContents.name(of:)`; an untitled one as every path names it, `registry.containerName(of:)`: "Untitled Subboard", "Frame", the placeholder its row shows), an enclosing board by its owner (named as a row is, though its owner is on none of the boards shown),  the root by the file's name; two places called alike (case apart) say their path instead, the names of the shapes each is inside, outermost first, then its own name, as every path says them ("Pen 800 › Pen 300": Boards' `ShapePaths`, Topology's containers, each named by `registry.containerName(of:)` and joined by `ShapePath.separator`, from the root, never trimmed to the board shown, which would leave two places on different levels alike again: a frame in a subboard and the board above it), so a frame called Checkout in two frames is two items; without TopologyPlugin they keep their names. Choosing one is `Drop.commit()`, the table of contents' own move and landing, one step, Move, several shapes together; `/move-to` takes exactly the places the menu lists for the same shapes (the menu caps the list and orders it). A plain `NSMenu`, which the keys walk and type to select in (principle 10 of [keyboard navigation](keyboard-navigation.md); the submenu's place in the Shape menu is its key). Settling on a nudge is not this menu's.
- **Going where the shapes went** (`Block.follow`, Move.swift; the owner's ask, 2026-11-03: "being left somewhere after the object you moved is gone is not the right move"): after every move that commits (the commands, Move To ▸ and the table of contents' drops alike, since all commit through `Block.commit`), the person is taken to the shapes, so they're never left where the shapes were. Onto another board, that board is shown with them selected there (`showBoard(_:selecting:)`); on the board shown, they're selected. Either way what was selected stays so where it holds them all (an arrow, or a link, that rode along with them too: the selection before the move, those of it now on the board they landed on), else they alone are, back to front; then they're brought into view if they aren't in it (`revealIfOffscreen`: centered at the zoom the camera has, farther out only where they don't fit, never closer: a move isn't a link followed to read what it names, which `goTo` brings to a readable 70%, and a board organized zoomed out stays zoomed out), on another board from the camera it was left with (or its first visit's, which a landing `anywhere` is in the middle of). It's one step back (`editor.navigate(from:)` of `editor.navigationStart`, taken before the transaction: the board, the selection and the camera before the move), so ⌘[ returns to where the move was made, and Selection's Back goes back to a place by its camera where only the camera moved ([selection](selection.md)): a move on the board shown that stays in view, with nothing more selected, moves nothing and makes no step. Without the Selection plugin nothing is selected and there is no step back: their board is shown and they're brought into view. Undo Move shows the board the step was made on and leaves the camera, as every undo does.
- Not built: `/move-near` naming the side (`/move-near @X below`).
